Fact sheet

Slapped Cheek (Fifth Disease)

Bright red cheeks, as if slapped, then a lacy rash on the arms and legs. It’s a mild virus in children. The odd twist: by the time the rash appears, they’re usually no longer contagious.

What it is

  • An infection with parvovirus B19, spread by coughs and sneezes. Common in school-aged children, often in late winter and spring.
  • Starts like a mild cold: runny nose, low fever, aches.
  • A few days later, bright red cheeks appear, then a lacy, pink rash on the arms, legs and body.
  • The rash can fade and come back with heat, sun, baths or exercise, and usually clears in 7 to 10 days.
  • Older children and adults may get sore, swollen joints.

Care

  • No specific treatment. Rest, fluids and paracetamol if needed.
  • Children are usually contagious before the rash, so there’s usually no need to keep a child with just the rash home if they feel well. Check your childcare or school’s policy.

Pregnancy

  • About half of adults are already immune.
  • Most pregnant women who catch it, and their babies, are fine. Rarely, it causes anaemia in the baby or miscarriage, mostly in the first half of pregnancy. It doesn’t cause birth defects.
  • If you’re pregnant and have been around slapped cheek, or have a rash, tell your doctor or midwife. A blood test can show whether you’re immune.

See a doctor urgently if

  • Your child has a blood disorder (such as sickle cell, thalassaemia or another anaemia) or a weakened immune system, and has been exposed.
  • Your child is pale, very tired, breathless or seems very unwell.

Common questions

Is slapped cheek contagious once the rash appears?

Usually not. Children are most contagious in the cold-like stage before the rash shows.

Is slapped cheek dangerous in pregnancy?

Usually not, and about half of adults are already immune. Rarely it affects the baby, mostly in the first half of pregnancy. If you’re pregnant and exposed, tell your doctor or midwife.

Why does the slapped cheek rash keep coming back?

The lacy rash can flare with heat, sun, exercise or a warm bath for a few weeks. It isn’t a sign of new infection.

Is slapped cheek the same as fifth disease?

Yes. Slapped cheek, fifth disease and erythema infectiosum are all names for parvovirus B19 infection.
