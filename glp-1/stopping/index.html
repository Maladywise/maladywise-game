Stopping a GLP-1 medicine: what happens to your weight?

Made with AI tools and overseen by an Australian GP · Updated 8 November 2026

The awkward truth that social media skips: these medicines treat weight while you take them. Stop, and appetite usually comes back, and so does much of the weight.

Quick answer: Most people regain weight after stopping. A year after stopping semaglutide, people in the STEP 1 extension had regained about two-thirds of the weight they’d lost. In SURMOUNT-4, people switched from tirzepatide to placebo regained 14% of their weight in a year, while those who continued kept losing. Plan any stop with your doctor.

What the trials found

  • STEP 1 extension (semaglutide): people lost 17.3% of their weight over 68 weeks. One year after stopping, they’d regained 11.6 percentage points, leaving them 5.6% below their starting weight.
  • STEP 4 (semaglutide): after 20 weeks on treatment (10.6% lost), people who continued lost a further 7.9% over 48 weeks. Those switched to placebo regained 6.9%.
  • SURMOUNT-4 (tirzepatide): after 36 weeks (20.9% lost), people who continued lost a further 5.5% over a year. Those switched to placebo regained 14.0%.
  • In SURMOUNT-4, 89.5% of people who stayed on tirzepatide kept at least 80% of their weight loss, versus 16.6% on placebo.

Health benefits fade too

  • In the STEP 1 extension, improvements in blood pressure, blood fats and blood sugar mostly drifted back towards where they started once semaglutide stopped.
  • If you have diabetes, your blood sugar may rise again, so your doctor may need to adjust your other treatment.

Why it happens

  • Obesity is a long-term (chronic) condition. The trial authors concluded that ongoing treatment is needed to keep the weight and health gains.
  • Regain after stopping isn’t a willpower failure. It’s what the medicine stopping looks like.
  • One caveat: in the STEP 1 extension, the lifestyle support also stopped at the same time.

If you’re thinking of stopping

  • Talk to your doctor first, so you can plan follow-up checks of weight, blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol.
  • Keep up strength training and protein: muscle helps you stay strong whatever the scales say. See muscle loss and protein.
  • Planning a pregnancy? There are minimum gaps before trying to conceive. See pregnancy and contraception.

See your GP before stopping if you take a GLP-1 medicine for type 2 diabetes, so your blood sugar plan can be adjusted. Get urgent help (000 in Australia, or your local emergency number) for very high blood sugar with vomiting, drowsiness or confusion.

Common questions

Will I regain all the weight if I stop?

Not necessarily all of it, but most people regain a lot. One year after stopping semaglutide in the STEP 1 extension, people had regained about two-thirds of their lost weight and were 5.6% below their starting weight on average.

How fast does weight come back after stopping Mounjaro?

In SURMOUNT-4, people switched from tirzepatide to placebo regained 14% of their body weight over the following year.

Do the health benefits go away when I stop?

Mostly, yes. In the STEP 1 extension, improvements in blood pressure, blood fats and blood sugar largely went back towards where they started within a year of stopping.

Do I have to take a GLP-1 medicine forever?

Not necessarily, but the trials show the benefits last only while you take it. How long to continue is a decision to make with your doctor, weighing benefits, side effects and cost.

Is regaining weight my fault?

No. Regain after stopping is expected from how these medicines work: appetite returns when the medicine stops. It happened to most people in carefully run trials.

Evidence and more information

  • Wilding et al, Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism 2022: STEP 1 trial extension, weight regain after stopping semaglutide
  • Rubino et al, JAMA 2021: STEP 4, continuing versus stopping semaglutide
  • Aronne et al, JAMA 2024: SURMOUNT-4, continuing versus stopping tirzepatide
  • Wilding et al, NEJM 2021: STEP 1, semaglutide 2.4 mg for weight management (weight loss, side-effect and gallbladder rates)
