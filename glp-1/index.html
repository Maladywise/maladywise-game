GLP-1 medicines: the evidence, minus the hype

Made with AI tools and overseen by an Australian GP · Updated 9 November 2026

Semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy), tirzepatide (Mounjaro) and their relatives copy gut hormones that control blood sugar and appetite. They’re genuinely effective. They’re also not magic, not cheating, and not for everyone.

Quick answer: GLP-1 medicines are weekly injections (and some tablets) that copy gut hormones, lowering blood sugar and appetite. They’re used for type 2 diabetes and, for some brands, weight management. In the main trials, people lost about 15% (semaglutide) to 21% (tirzepatide) of their weight. Gut side effects are common, and weight usually returns after stopping.

What they are

  • Semaglutide: Ozempic (type 2 diabetes) and Wegovy (weight management).
  • Tirzepatide: Mounjaro. It copies two gut hormones, GIP and GLP-1.
  • Oral semaglutide: Wegovy tablets for weight management, newly approved in Australia (see below).
  • Older relatives: liraglutide and dulaglutide (Trulicity).
  • Brand names are listed so you can recognise them, not as a recommendation.

New: oral semaglutide tablets (Wegovy)

  • The TGA approved oral semaglutide for weight management (brand name Wegovy tablets), a once-daily tablet, on 3 November 2026. They’re the first GLP-1 tablets approved for weight management in Australia.
  • Strengths: 1.5 mg, 4 mg, 9 mg and 25 mg, taken once a day on an empty stomach.
  • Approved uses: the same as injectable Wegovy, according to the Department of Health: weight management alongside a reduced-calorie diet and more physical activity, and lowering the risk of heart attack and stroke in eligible adults.
  • Not in pharmacies yet: the TGA still has to check packaging and manufacturing before release. Novo Nordisk expects supply before the end of 2026.
  • Cost: not on the PBS, and no Australian price has been announced. See PBS and cost.
  • It’s the same medicine as the injection in a different form, so the same side effects and cautions apply. A tablet is a choice about convenience, not a gentler drug.

How they work

  • They boost insulin when blood sugar is high.
  • They slow stomach emptying, so you feel full sooner and for longer.
  • They act on the brain’s appetite centres, turning down hunger and “food noise”.

Who they’re for

  • Type 2 diabetes, alongside diet and activity.
  • Weight management in adults with obesity, or overweight with a weight-related condition (trials enrolled people with a BMI of 30 or more, or 27 or more with a related condition).
  • Heart protection: in SELECT, semaglutide cut heart attacks, strokes and heart deaths by 20% (6.5% vs 8.0%) in people with heart disease and overweight but no diabetes.
  • Sleep apnoea with obesity: tirzepatide is TGA-approved for this.
  • Not for: pregnancy or breastfeeding, or people with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2.

What results look like

  • Semaglutide 2.4 mg (STEP 1): 14.9% average weight loss vs 2.4% on placebo over 68 weeks.
  • Tirzepatide (SURMOUNT-1): 15.0–20.9% vs 3.1% over 72 weeks, depending on dose.
  • Head to head (SURMOUNT-5): 20.2% on tirzepatide vs 13.7% on semaglutide. Full comparison: semaglutide vs tirzepatide.
  • These are averages. Some people lose much more, some much less.

Not a miracle, not cheating. It’s a hormone medicine for a long-term condition. It turns down hunger; protein, sleep, movement and follow-up still do a lot of the work. Whether one suits you is a conversation with your doctor.

Common questions

What is a GLP-1 medicine?

A medicine that copies GLP-1, a gut hormone released after eating. It boosts insulin when blood sugar is high, slows stomach emptying and reduces appetite. Semaglutide, tirzepatide, liraglutide and dulaglutide are examples.

Is Ozempic the same as Wegovy?

Both contain semaglutide. Ozempic is used for type 2 diabetes and Wegovy for weight management.

Which GLP-1 medicine causes the most weight loss?

On current evidence, tirzepatide. In the head-to-head SURMOUNT-5 trial, people lost 20.2% of their weight on tirzepatide versus 13.7% on semaglutide over 72 weeks.

Do I have to take a GLP-1 medicine forever?

The benefits last only while you take it. In trials, most people regained much of the weight within a year of stopping. How long to continue is a decision for you and your doctor.

Are GLP-1 medicines on the PBS?

Only for type 2 diabetes (Ozempic and Trulicity, when criteria are met) as of November 2026. None are subsidised for weight loss, and Mounjaro isn’t on the PBS for any use.

Is there a GLP-1 weight-loss pill in Australia?

The TGA approved oral semaglutide tablets (Wegovy) for weight management on 3 November 2026, in 1.5 mg, 4 mg, 9 mg and 25 mg strengths. They aren’t in pharmacies yet, aren’t on the PBS and have no announced Australian price.
