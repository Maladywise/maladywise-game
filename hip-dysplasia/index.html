Hip Dysplasia in Babies (DDH)

A hip joint that’s loose, shallow or out of place. Found early, it’s usually fixed with a soft harness and no surgery. That’s why every baby’s hips are checked, and why swaddling should leave the legs free.

What it is

  • In developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH), the ball of the thigh bone doesn’t sit snugly in a well-formed socket. It can be loose, partly out or fully dislocated.
  • It can affect one or both hips, and it isn’t painful in babies.

Who is more at risk

  • Girls, and first-born babies.
  • Breech position in the last weeks of pregnancy.
  • A parent or sibling with hip dysplasia.
  • Tight swaddling with the legs straight and pressed together.

Checks

  • Your baby’s hips are examined at birth, at about 1 week and 6 weeks, at 6 months and when they start walking.
  • Babies with risk factors such as breech or a family history are usually offered a hip ultrasound at about 6 weeks.
  • Signs in older babies: uneven leg skin creases, one leg looking shorter, one leg that won’t open out as far at nappy changes, or a limp once walking.

Treatment

  • Most babies are treated with a soft brace, the Pavlik harness, which holds the hips bent and apart so the socket can deepen. It’s usually worn for several months.
  • If that doesn’t work, or DDH is found later, the hip may need to be put back under anaesthetic and held in a cast.
  • Found after about 6 months, surgery is more likely. Early detection makes treatment much simpler.

Hip-healthy swaddling

  • If you wrap your baby, keep the wrap firm around the chest but loose around the hips, so the legs can bend up and out like a frog.
  • Carriers should support the thighs with the knees higher than the bottom.

Common questions

How is hip dysplasia found in babies?

By routine hip checks at birth and in the first months, and by an ultrasound at about 6 weeks for babies with risk factors such as breech position or a family history.

Does a Pavlik harness hurt my baby?

No. It holds the hips in a safe position. Babies usually adapt quickly. Your team will show you how to care for your baby in it.

Can swaddling cause hip dysplasia?

Tight swaddling with the legs straight and together increases the risk. Wrap loosely around the hips so the legs can bend up and out.

Do girls get hip dysplasia more often?

Yes. Being a girl, being first-born, breech position and a family history all raise the risk.
