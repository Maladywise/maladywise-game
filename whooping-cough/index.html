Fact sheet

Whooping Cough (Pertussis)

A long, hacking cough that can last for months in older children and adults, but can be life-threatening for young babies. Vaccinating in pregnancy is the best way to protect a newborn.

What it is

  • A bacterial infection (Bordetella pertussis) that spreads through coughs and sneezes.
  • Starts like a cold, then becomes bouts of severe coughing, sometimes ending in a ‘whoop’ when breathing in, or vomiting. It can last weeks to months.
  • Babies may not whoop. Instead they may stop breathing, go blue, or struggle to feed.

Why babies are at risk

  • Babies under 6 months are too young to be fully protected by their own vaccines, and are most at risk of hospital, pneumonia, seizures and death.
  • In more than half of babies who catch it, the source is a parent or someone at home.

Vaccination

  • In pregnancy: a whooping cough vaccine in every pregnancy, ideally at 20 to 32 weeks. Antibodies pass to the baby and protect them in the first months. It’s free in Australia.
  • Babies: doses at 2 (from 6 weeks), 4 and 6 months, then boosters at 18 months and 4 years, and in early high school.
  • Adults around babies, such as partners and grandparents, can have a booster if it’s been 10 years or more.

Treatment

  • Antibiotics stop it spreading if started early, but may not shorten the cough once it’s well established.
  • Stay away from childcare, school, work and babies until you’ve had 5 days of antibiotics, or as your doctor advises.
  • Close contacts, especially babies and pregnant women, may need antibiotics too.

Get help urgently if

  • A baby has pauses in breathing, goes blue or pale during coughing, or can’t feed.
  • Anyone is struggling to breathe or seems very unwell. Call 000 in an emergency.

Common questions

When should I have the whooping cough vaccine in pregnancy?

In every pregnancy, ideally between 20 and 32 weeks. It’s free, and it protects your baby in the first months of life.

Why is whooping cough dangerous for babies?

Babies under 6 months aren’t fully protected yet and can stop breathing, get pneumonia or have seizures. Most catch it from someone at home.

How long does whooping cough last?

The cough can last for weeks to months, which is why it’s called the 100-day cough.

Do grandparents need a whooping cough booster?

Adults who will be around a new baby can have a booster if it’s been 10 years or more. Ask your GP or pharmacist.
