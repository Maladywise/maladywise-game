Fact sheet

Warts

Rough skin growths from a common virus (HPV). Harmless, and most vanish on their own: about half within a year in children, most within 2 years. Treatment just speeds things up.

The warts ladder

Doing nothing is a legitimate first step. Whatever you choose, give it 12 weeks before calling it a failure.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careLeave it aloneMost warts go away without treatment, especially in children.
    Works
    About half clear within a year in children, and about two-thirds within 2 years
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    Months
    Watch for
    Picking or biting: spreads warts

    The cheapest treatment with the best side-effect profile.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacySalicylic acid paint or gelSoak, file off the dead skin, dry, apply, cover. Protect nearby skin with petroleum jelly.
    Works
    About half clear with daily use for 12 weeks, versus about a quarter with nothing
    Cost
    About $10–20
    How long
    Daily for up to 12 weeks
    Watch for
    Sore, red skin: stop for a few days, then restart. Not on the face

    Consistency beats strength. Daily, boringly, for weeks.

  3. Step 3 · PharmacyPharmacy freezing sprayA home freezing kit. Not as cold as a doctor’s liquid nitrogen.
    Works
    No good evidence it works
    Cost
    About $25–40
    How long
    A few applications, weeks apart
    Watch for
    Blisters, pain. Keep away from healthy skin

    Cold, yes. Cold enough, often not.

  4. Step 4 · ProcedureFreezing by a GP or podiatristLiquid nitrogen every 2 to 3 weeks, often several sessions.
    Works
    Hand warts: about half clear in 13 weeks vs 1 in 12 waiting. Foot warts: no better than salicylic acid
    Cost
    GP: often bulk-billed or about $40–90 out of pocket. Podiatrist: about $80–120 a session
    How long
    2–4 sessions or more
    Watch for
    Pain, blisters, and sometimes a pale or dark mark

    Hurts more than the paint. Works about the same. Choose your pain.

  5. Step 5 · SpecialistDermatologist treatmentsFor stubborn warts: scraping and burning, laser, or other prescription treatments.
    Works
    Small trials, mixed results; helps some stubborn warts, but they can return
    Cost
    About $150–300 a visit with a Medicare rebate when referred
    How long
    One to several visits
    Watch for
    Scarring, pain, and warts that come back anyway

    For warts that have outstayed their welcome by years, not weeks.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. Results are typical figures from trials: your wart didn’t read them.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Duct tapeNo better than a dummy treatment in larger trials. Your toolbox can stay in the shed.
  • Apple cider vinegarNo trials, and there are reports of chemical burns. The wart may leave; a scar may move in.
  • Garlic, banana peel and other kitchen curesNo good evidence, and garlic can burn the skin. Save them for dinner.
  • Homeopathic wart remediesNo better than a dummy treatment in a trial. Very dilute, very expensive water.

What you can do

  • Wear thongs in shared showers and don’t share towels or nail files.
  • Don’t cut or burn warts at home: infection and scarring aren’t worth it.
  • Most treatments take 6 to 12 weeks. Patience is part of the treatment.

See your GP if

  • Warts are on the face, genitals or around the nails
  • You have diabetes, poor circulation or numb feet (don’t self-treat foot warts)
  • A growth bleeds, changes quickly or you’re not sure it’s a wart
  • You have a weakened immune system

See a doctor promptly if a treated area becomes hot, swollen and increasingly painful, or you develop a fever.

Common questions

Do warts go away on their own?

Most do. In children, about half clear within a year, and most within 2 years.

What is the best treatment for warts?

Salicylic acid paint or gel from the pharmacy, daily for up to 12 weeks, clears about half of warts. Freezing by a doctor works about as well but hurts more.

Does duct tape work on warts?

It was no better than a dummy treatment in larger trials.

Should I treat foot warts myself if I have diabetes?

No. If you have diabetes, poor circulation or numb feet, see your GP rather than self-treating foot warts.
