Fact sheet

Warts

Rough skin growths caused by a common virus (HPV). Plantar warts on the soles grow inwards and can hurt to walk on. Harmless, and most clear without treatment: about half within 6 months in children, and most within 2 years.

Treatment options

Ratings show how strong the research is.

  • Moderate evidenceSalicylic acid paint or gelPharmacy treatment, daily for up to 12 weeks. Soak, file off the dead skin, dry, apply, then cover. Protect nearby skin with petroleum jelly. A modest but definite benefit.
  • Limited evidenceFreezing by a doctorLiquid nitrogen every 2 to 3 weeks, often several sessions. Works about as well as salicylic acid but hurts more and can blister.
  • Limited evidencePharmacy freezing spraysNot as cold as a doctor’s liquid nitrogen. Little evidence.
  • Limited evidenceDuct tapeNo better than placebo in larger trials.
  • Limited evidenceSpecialist treatmentsFor stubborn warts: scraping and burning, laser or injections. Higher risk of scarring, and warts can return.
  • Not recommendedCutting or burning warts at homeRisk of infection and scarring.

What you can do

  • Leaving warts alone is reasonable, especially in children.
  • Most treatments take 6 to 12 weeks.
  • Don’t pick or bite warts. Wear thongs in shared showers and don’t share towels or nail files.
  • If skin gets sore or red, stop for a few days, then restart.

See your GP if

  • Warts are on the face, genitals or around the nails
  • You have diabetes, poor circulation or numb feet (don’t self-treat foot warts)
  • A growth bleeds, changes quickly or you’re not sure it’s a wart
  • You have a weakened immune system

See a doctor promptly if a treated area becomes hot, swollen and increasingly painful, or you develop a fever.

Common questions

Do warts go away on their own?

Most do. In children, about half clear within 6 months, and most within 2 years.

What is the best treatment for warts?

Salicylic acid paint or gel from the pharmacy, daily for up to 12 weeks, gives a modest but definite benefit. Freezing by a doctor works about as well but hurts more.

Does duct tape work on warts?

It was no better than placebo in larger trials.

Should I treat foot warts myself if I have diabetes?

No. If you have diabetes, poor circulation or numb feet, see your GP rather than self-treating foot warts.
