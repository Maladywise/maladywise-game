Coeliac Disease Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on coeliac disease: what gluten does, how it’s diagnosed (and why you keep eating gluten for testing), and living gluten free.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What is coeliac disease?

    1. An allergy to wheat that causes hives
    2. An autoimmune reaction to gluten that damages the small bowel
    3. A bowel infection
    4. A food intolerance with no damage
    Show answer

    Answer: An autoimmune reaction to gluten that damages the small bowel. Gluten triggers the immune system to flatten the villi that absorb nutrients.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    Which grains contain gluten?

    1. Rice and corn
    2. Potato and tapioca
    3. Quinoa and buckwheat
    4. Wheat, barley and rye
    Show answer

    Answer: Wheat, barley and rye. Oats are a special case: see later in the quiz.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    About how many Australians have coeliac disease?

    1. About 1 in 70
    2. 1 in 5
    3. 1 in 1 million
    4. 1 in 10,000
    Show answer

    Answer: About 1 in 70. Most don’t know yet. Symptoms can be mild or absent.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    Which blood test is used first?

    1. Liver function tests
    2. Cholesterol
    3. Coeliac antibodies (tTG-IgA) with total IgA
    4. Thyroid tests
    Show answer

    Answer: Coeliac antibodies (tTG-IgA) with total IgA. Total IgA is checked because low IgA can make the main test falsely negative.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    Before testing, what should you be eating?

    1. Only rice
    2. Gluten every day for about 6 weeks
    3. Nothing for 24 hours
    4. A strict gluten-free diet
    Show answer

    Answer: Gluten every day for about 6 weeks. Going gluten free first can make tests falsely normal. Coeliac Australia suggests at least 4 slices of wheat bread a day (or equivalent) for 6 weeks before testing.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    Which is a common non-gut sign of coeliac disease?

    1. Iron deficiency anaemia
    2. High blood pressure
    3. Gout
    4. Hearing loss
    Show answer

    Answer: Iron deficiency anaemia. Others: tiredness, mouth ulcers, low bone density, abnormal liver tests and an itchy blistering rash.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    How is coeliac disease confirmed in adults?

    1. Gene test alone
    2. A stool test
    3. A skin prick test
    4. Small bowel biopsy at gastroscopy
    Show answer

    Answer: Small bowel biopsy at gastroscopy. Positive blood tests are confirmed with biopsies showing damaged villi.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    What does a negative HLA-DQ2/DQ8 gene test mean?

    1. You definitely have it
    2. You need a biopsy straight away
    3. Coeliac disease is very unlikely
    4. Nothing useful
    Show answer

    Answer: Coeliac disease is very unlikely. About 1 in 3 people carry the genes, so a positive test is common. A negative test virtually rules it out.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    Can people with coeliac disease eat oats?

    1. Only if cooked twice
    2. Only porridge
    3. Not in Australia, unless their specialist advises it
    4. Yes, freely
    Show answer

    Answer: Not in Australia, unless their specialist advises it. Australian standards don’t allow oats to be labelled gluten free. Oats can be contaminated, and some people react to oat protein itself.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    What is dermatitis herpetiformis?

    1. Eczema from wheat flour
    2. A cold sore virus
    3. A sunburn reaction
    4. An itchy blistering rash caused by coeliac disease
    Show answer

    Answer: An itchy blistering rash caused by coeliac disease. Usually on elbows, knees and buttocks. It’s treated with a gluten-free diet.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    Who should be offered testing?

    1. First-degree relatives of someone with coeliac disease
    2. Everyone with a cold
    3. Only people who feel bloated
    4. Only children
    Show answer

    Answer: First-degree relatives of someone with coeliac disease. About 1 in 10 parents, siblings and children are also affected.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Why does lifelong strict gluten avoidance matter?

    1. It only matters if you have symptoms
    2. It heals the gut and lowers the risk of osteoporosis and some cancers
    3. It cures the immune system after a year
    4. It prevents colds
    Show answer

    Answer: It heals the gut and lowers the risk of osteoporosis and some cancers. Even small amounts cause damage. A follow-up biopsy may be done to check healing.

Common questions

Is coeliac disease the same as gluten intolerance?

No. Coeliac disease causes measurable bowel damage. Non-coeliac gluten sensitivity causes symptoms without that damage.

Can it start in adults?

Yes. Coeliac disease can be diagnosed at any age, including in older adults.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. Don’t start a gluten-free diet before talking to your GP about testing.
