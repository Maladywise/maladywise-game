    On standard ECG paper (25 mm/s), how long is one small square?

    2. 0.1 seconds (100 ms)
    3. 0.02 seconds (20 ms)
    4. 0.2 seconds (200 ms)
    Show answer

    Answer: 0.04 seconds (40 ms). One small square is 40 ms and one big square (five small) is 200 ms, so five big squares make one second.

  12. Question 12 of 15

    What is a normal PR interval?

    1. Over 300 ms (more than 7.5 small squares)
    2. 120 to 200 ms (3 to 5 small squares)
    3. 40 to 80 ms (1 to 2 small squares)
    4. 200 to 300 ms (5 to 7.5 small squares)
    Show answer

    Answer: 120 to 200 ms (3 to 5 small squares). Longer than 200 ms (one big square) is first-degree AV block. Shorter than 120 ms raises the possibility of a pre-excitation pathway such as WPW.

  13. Question 13 of 15

    The rhythm is regular with 4 big squares between R waves. What is the heart rate?

    1. 100 a minute
    2. 60 a minute
    3. 50 a minute
    4. 75 a minute
    Show answer

    Answer: 75 a minute. For a regular rhythm, rate = 300 ÷ big squares between R waves. 300 ÷ 4 = 75. For irregular rhythms, count QRS complexes in 10 seconds and multiply by 6.

  14. Question 14 of 15

    A QRS complex counts as broad when it is longer than:

    1. 40 ms (1 small square)
    2. 120 ms (3 small squares)
    3. 80 ms (2 small squares)
    4. 200 ms (5 small squares)
    Show answer

    Answer: 120 ms (3 small squares). Normal QRS is under about 120 ms. Broad complexes mean the impulse is travelling the slow way: bundle branch block, a ventricular rhythm, pacing or hyperkalaemia.

Common questions

How do you read an ECG?

Use the same order every time: check the patient details and calibration, then the rate, the rhythm (regular or not, P waves before every QRS?), the axis, the PR interval, the QRS width, the ST segments and T waves, and the QT interval. Finish by comparing with an old ECG if there is one.

How do you work out the heart rate from an ECG?

For a regular rhythm, divide 300 by the number of big squares between two R waves (or 1,500 by the number of small squares). For an irregular rhythm, count the QRS complexes on a 10-second strip and multiply by 6.

What does a normal ECG look like?

Sinus rhythm at 60 to 100 a minute, a P wave before every QRS, a PR interval of 120 to 200 ms, a narrow QRS under about 120 ms, no significant ST elevation or depression, upright T waves in most leads and a normal QTc.
