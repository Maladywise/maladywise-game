Fact sheet

Gambling problems

Gambling harm is common and treatable. Free counselling is available 24/7, and tools like BetStop and bank gambling blocks make it much harder to bet on impulse.

Warning signs

  • Betting more than you can afford, or chasing losses.
  • Needing to bet more to get the same buzz; feeling restless when trying to stop.
  • Hiding gambling, borrowing money or selling things to gamble.
  • Gambling affecting your relationships, work, mood or sleep.
  • Gambling disorder is a recognised mental health condition.

Blocking tools

  • BetStop: the free national self-exclusion register that blocks you from all licensed Australian online and phone betting providers (betstop.gov.au).
  • Bank gambling blocks: most major banks let you block gambling transactions in their app.
  • Venue self-exclusion for pubs, clubs and casinos.
  • Blocking software on phones and computers; delete betting apps.

What helps

  • Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) has the best evidence.
  • Financial counselling to deal with debt (free through the National Debt Helpline: 1800 007 007).
  • Hand over control of money for a while to someone you trust.
  • Naltrexone has some evidence for reducing gambling urges; ask your GP.
  • Treat what comes with it: depression, anxiety, alcohol use.

Where to get help

  • Gambling Help: 1800 858 858 (free, 24/7)
  • Gambling Help Online: 24/7 chat and email (gamblinghelponline.org.au)
  • Lifeline 13 11 14 if you’re in crisis

Common questions

How do I ban myself from betting apps in Australia?

Register with BetStop, the free national self-exclusion register. It blocks you from all licensed Australian online and phone gambling providers.

Can my bank block gambling?

Yes. Most major Australian banks offer gambling transaction blocks in their app or on request.

Is there free help for gambling?

Yes. Call Gambling Help on 1800 858 858 or use Gambling Help Online for 24/7 chat and counselling.

Is gambling addiction a mental illness?

Gambling disorder is a recognised condition, and treatments like CBT work.
