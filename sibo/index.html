Fact sheet

SIBO

SIBO means too many bacteria in the small intestine, causing bloating, wind, diarrhoea and sometimes poor absorption. It’s real and treatable in people with clear risk factors. It’s also heavily overdiagnosed with unreliable breath tests, at considerable cost.

If an online quiz diagnoses you with SIBO and then sells you the herbs, that’s not a test. That’s a shop.

Who really gets it

  • People who’ve had bowel or stomach surgery, especially loops or bypasses
  • Slow gut movement: scleroderma, diabetes with nerve damage, intestinal pseudo-obstruction
  • Very low stomach acid, older age, cirrhosis, some immune problems
  • Long-term opioids or strong acid suppression may add to risk
  • Its role in everyday IBS is debated

Bloating after a big meal is digestion, not a disease.

Symptoms

  • Bloating, wind, tummy discomfort and diarrhoea
  • In more severe cases: weight loss, low B12, iron or fat-soluble vitamins, fatty pale stools

See your GP soon for any of these: weight loss you can’t explain, vomiting that won’t settle, trouble swallowing, black or bloody stools, low iron or anaemia, a family history of bowel or stomach cancer, or new symptoms after 60.

Testing

  • Breath tests (glucose or lactulose) are widely used but often wrong: false positives with fast gut transit, false negatives otherwise.
  • The gold standard, sampling fluid from the small bowel, is rarely done outside research.
  • In people with strong risk factors, doctors sometimes treat without a test.
  • Also check for coeliac disease, IBD, pancreatic problems and bile acid diarrhoea, which cause similar symptoms.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Repeated breath tests to “track” SIBOThey’re unreliable and don’t guide treatment well.
  • Long-term restrictive “SIBO diets”Can harm nutrition and gut bacteria diversity. Short low-FODMAP trials with a dietitian are better evidenced for IBS.
  • Herbal antimicrobial protocolsOne small study; quality and dosing vary. Not proven.
  • Elemental dietsSome evidence, but very hard to tolerate and expensive. Specialist-guided only.

Common questions

What is SIBO?

Too many bacteria in the small intestine, usually because of bowel surgery, slow gut movement or low stomach acid. It causes bloating, wind and diarrhoea, and sometimes vitamin deficiencies.

Are SIBO breath tests accurate?

Not very. They give false positives and false negatives. Results should be interpreted with your symptoms and risk factors, and other causes considered.

Is rifaximin on the PBS for SIBO?

No. In Australia rifaximin is PBS-listed for a liver condition (hepatic encephalopathy), so for SIBO it’s a private prescription. Older antibiotics are cheaper alternatives.
