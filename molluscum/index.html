Fact sheet

Molluscum Contagiosum

Small, pearly bumps with a tiny central dimple, from a common skin virus. Mostly in kids, harmless, and gone on its own in 6 to 18 months. Patience is the main treatment.

The molluscum ladder

Most children need nothing but time. No treatment has clearly beaten waiting in trials, so climb only for a good reason.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careLeave it aloneThe immune system clears the virus itself. Spots often go red and angry just before they vanish: that’s the good part.
    Works
    About 7 in 10 clear by 18 months (average about 13 months); about 1 in 8 still have spots at 2 years
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    6–18 months
    Watch for
    Picking and scratching spread it. New spots can keep appearing for a while.

    The most effective treatment is a calendar.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacyTreat itchy eczema around the spotsMoisturiser, plus a mild steroid cream on itchy, inflamed skin nearby.
    Works
    Not tested in trials; low risk, and less itch means less scratching and spreading
    Cost
    About $10–25 for moisturiser or hydrocortisone cream
    How long
    Steroid cream a few days to 2 weeks; moisturiser ongoing
    Watch for
    Stinging on broken skin. No strong steroids on the face without advice.

    Scratching is molluscum’s favourite travel agent.

  3. Step 3 · PharmacyWart paint (salicylic acid) on a few spotsIrritates the spot so the body notices it. Protect the skin around it with petroleum jelly.
    Works
    Evidence weak: no treatment clearly beat waiting in a Cochrane review; may speed clearing a little
    Cost
    About $10–20 a bottle
    How long
    A few weeks
    Watch for
    Redness, stinging, blistering. Not on the face, genitals or broken skin.

    Aim small. Painting the whole child is not the plan.

  4. Step 4 · ProcedureFreezing or scraping by a doctorFor a few stubborn spots in older children or adults.
    Works
    Removes the spots treated; no good trials show faster overall clearing than waiting
    Cost
    GP visit, often bulk-billed or a gap of about $40–90
    How long
    1–3 visits, a few weeks apart
    Watch for
    Pain, blistering, scarring or pale marks. Not a toddler favourite.

    Great for an adult with three spots. Less great for a five-year-old with thirty.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by pharmacy. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Tea tree oil and other “natural” remediesOne small trial, and they can cause skin irritation or allergy. Mostly tea tree oil and hope.
  • Imiquimod creamNo better than a dummy treatment in children in good trials, with more side effects.
  • Cimetidine tabletsAn old heartburn tablet that did no better than a dummy treatment in a trial.
  • Apple cider vinegarNo trials, and it can cause chemical burns on young skin.

What you can do

  • Keep nails short. No scratching, picking or shaving over spots.
  • Don’t share towels, baths or clothes. Cover spots for contact sports.
  • No need to miss childcare, school or swimming.
  • Moisturise dry skin.

See your GP if

  • Spots near the eye or on the eyelid
  • Spots are painful, swelling or oozing pus
  • An adult with spots on the genitals (a sexual health check is worthwhile)
  • Many large spots, or a weakened immune system

See a doctor the same day for a red, painful eye, spreading hot red skin with fever, or a child who seems unwell.

Common questions

How long does molluscum last?

It clears on its own, usually within 6 to 18 months, sometimes longer.

Should molluscum be treated?

Leaving it alone is the best approach for most children. No treatment has clearly beaten waiting in trials, and picking or squeezing spreads it.

Can my child go to school or swimming with molluscum?

Yes, there’s no need to miss childcare, school or swimming. Don’t share towels, baths or clothing, and cover spots for contact sports.

Why have the molluscum spots gone red?

Spots often go red and inflamed just before they clear, which is a good sign. See your GP if they are painful, swelling or oozing pus.
