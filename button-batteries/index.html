Fact sheet

Button batteries

A coin-sized lithium button battery stuck in a child’s food pipe can cause a severe chemical burn within 2 hours. Children may show no symptoms at first.

If you think a child has swallowed or put a button battery in their nose or ear, go to the emergency department straight away. Call the Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26 on the way for advice, or 000 if they have trouble breathing. Don’t make them vomit. Don’t wait for symptoms.

Possible signs

  • Often none at first
  • Gagging, drooling, coughing or trouble swallowing
  • Vomiting (sometimes with blood), chest pain, refusing food
  • Black or bloody poo later
  • A battery missing from a device or packet

Where they hide, especially at Christmas

  • Musical cards, flashing decorations, novelty toys and Christmas jumpers
  • Flameless candles and fairy lights
  • Remote controls, car key fobs, kitchen and bathroom scales
  • Hearing aids, thermometers, watches and calculators
  • Loose spare batteries in drawers and the bin

Keeping children safe

  • Since June 2022, Australian law requires products with button batteries to have a secure battery compartment and the batteries to come in child-resistant packaging, with warnings.
  • Check secondhand, imported and novelty products: they may not meet the rules. Tape compartments shut if they’re loose.
  • Store spare batteries up high and locked away. Tape over used batteries and put them straight in an outside bin or a battery recycling point: ‘flat’ batteries can still burn.
  • Teach older children that batteries aren’t toys.

Common questions

What happens if a child swallows a button battery?

If it lodges in the food pipe, it can create a current that causes a severe chemical burn within 2 hours, which can be fatal. Go to the emergency department straight away, even if the child seems well.

What should I do if my child swallowed a button battery?

Go to the nearest emergency department immediately. Call the Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26 for advice on the way, or 000 if they’re struggling to breathe. Don’t make them vomit.

Are flat button batteries still dangerous?

Yes. ‘Dead’ batteries often hold enough charge to cause a burn. Tape them and put them straight in an outside bin or recycling point.

Which products contain button batteries?

Remotes, key fobs, musical cards, flameless candles, novelty toys and decorations, hearing aids, scales, watches and thermometers, among many others.
