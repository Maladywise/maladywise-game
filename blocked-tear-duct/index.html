Blocked Tear Duct in Babies

A watery, sticky eye in an otherwise happy baby. About 1 in 20 babies are born with a blocked tear duct, and it usually clears on its own by the first birthday.

What it is

  • Tears drain from the inner corner of the eye down a small tube into the nose. In some babies, a thin membrane at the nose end hasn’t opened yet.
  • Tears back up, so the eye waters even when the baby isn’t crying, and the lashes get crusty or sticky, especially after sleep.
  • The white of the eye usually stays white, and the baby is otherwise well. It may not be noticed until about a month of age.

What you can do

  • Clean the eye with cooled boiled water or salt water (1 teaspoon of salt in 250 mL of cooled boiled water) on a clean cotton ball, wiping from the inner corner outwards. Use a fresh ball for each wipe.
  • Massage: with a clean fingertip, press firmly on the side of the nose where the upper and lower lids meet, and slide down 1 to 2 cm. Repeat 5 times, twice a day. Your GP or child health nurse can show you.
  • If the skin gets sore, a thin layer of white soft paraffin helps.
  • Antibiotic drops are only needed if there’s a true infection, such as a red eye with lots of discharge.

See a doctor promptly if

  • There’s a lot of sticky discharge in the first 2 weeks of life: newborn eye infections need checking the same day.
  • A firm, swollen lump at the inner corner of the eye, or red or bluish skin there.
  • The white of the eye is red, or the eyelids are swollen.
  • The eye looks cloudy or larger than the other, or your baby is very sensitive to light and keeps the eye shut. These can be signs of rare but serious eye problems such as glaucoma.

If it doesn’t clear

  • If it’s still blocked after about 12 months, an eye specialist can open the duct with a fine probe, as a short procedure under general anaesthetic.
  • Probing usually works first time.

Common questions

How long does a blocked tear duct last in a baby?

Most clear on their own within the first year, often in the first few months. Massage and gentle cleaning help in the meantime.

Is a blocked tear duct the same as conjunctivitis?

No. A blocked tear duct gives a watery, sticky eye with a white eyeball. Conjunctivitis makes the white of the eye red. Either needs a doctor in the first 2 weeks of life.

Do babies with a blocked tear duct need antibiotics?

Usually not. Cleaning is enough for crusting. Antibiotic drops are only needed for a real infection.

When does a blocked tear duct need surgery?

Usually only if it’s still blocked after about 12 months. A short probing procedure opens it.
