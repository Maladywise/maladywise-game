Ankle Sprain: exercise plan

Rolled it? The pain fades fast, but the wobbliness doesn’t unless you train it. A few short balance sessions a week cuts your chance of doing it again.

How to use this
  • First few days: protect the ankle for 1–3 days, raise it above the level of your heart, and use a compression bandage or tape. Then start putting weight on it and moving it as pain allows.
  • Pain rules: in the first few weeks, exercises should be close to pain-free. Let pain guide you: if an exercise makes the ankle worse, ease back.
  • How often: gentle movement daily from the first few days. Then balance and strength work 3 times a week, up to 30 minutes a session, for 8 weeks.
  • How long: most people are back at work and sport within days to a few weeks. Finish the 8 weeks of balance work even once it feels fine: in a trial it cut repeat sprains by about a third.
  • Progress when: you can do a level with good control and no limp. Move through movement → balance → strength → hopping.
  • Based on: the 2021 US physiotherapy ankle sprain guideline (JOSPT), a BJSM 2017 summary of the review evidence (Doherty), the 8-week home balance program (Hupperets, BMJ 2009) and PEACE & LOVE (BJSM 2020).

Your exercises

  1. Ankle movement (alphabet)Sitting with your foot off the ground, ‘write’ the alphabet with your big toe. Progress to a standing calf stretch once you can take weight.A few minutes, several times a day, from the first few days
  2. Single-leg balanceStand on the injured leg near a bench. Start with fingertip support; progress to no hands, eyes closed, a pillow, then catching a ball.Each session, 3 times a week, for 8 weeks
  3. Balance reachStand on the injured leg and tap the other foot forwards, sideways and backwards as far as you can with control. Progress by reaching further.Each session, 3 times a week
  4. Heel raisesRise up on your toes and lower slowly. Start on both feet; progress to the injured leg only, then on a step edge.Each session, 3 times a week
  5. Band ankle strengthLoop a band around your forefoot and push your foot outwards (and inwards) against it. Progress with a stronger band.Each session, 3 times a week
  6. Hop progressionOnce balance and heel raises feel easy: two-foot hops on the spot, then single-leg hops, then forward and side-to-side hops, then sport drills.In the later weeks, 3 times a week

Back to sport: when you can hop, land and change direction on the injured leg without pain or giving way. After a sprain, wearing a brace for sport, together with balance exercises, lowers the chance of another.

Stop and see your GP or physio if

  • You couldn’t take 4 steps straight after the injury or have bony tenderness (needs an X-ray check)
  • Pain or swelling getting worse after the first few days
  • Your ankle keeps giving way after 6 weeks of exercise
  • Numbness, a cold foot, or calf pain and swelling

Want a supervised program?

A physio can guide a supervised, progressive program, which speeds recovery and helps prevent another sprain, especially for sport or if you’ve sprained it before. If you have a chronic condition, ask your GP about a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan (GPCCMP): it gives a Medicare rebate for up to 5 allied health visits a year (physio, exercise physiologist or podiatrist).
