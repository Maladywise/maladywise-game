Fact sheet

Ankle Sprain

A stretched or torn ligament, usually from rolling your ankle outwards. Most people walk on it within days and recover in 2 to 6 weeks. Skip the rehab and it often comes back.

Do I need an X-ray? Most sprains don’t. Doctors use the Ottawa ankle rules: you need one if you couldn’t take 4 steps straight after the injury and still can’t, or if there is bone tenderness at the back edge or tip of either ankle bone, at the base of the outer long bone of the foot, or over the bony bump on the inner midfoot.

The ankle sprain ladder

Move early, load gradually. Long rest is a step backwards here.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careProtect, then walkA couple of easy days with ice and elevation, then walk as soon as pain allows. Crutches briefly if needed.
    Works
    Early walking: back to work and sport sooner than in a cast. Ice and elevation: weak evidence, may ease early swelling
    Cost
    Free, or about $10–25 for an ice pack
    How long
    Protect for 2–3 days; walk within days
    Watch for
    Heat, alcohol, running and massage in the first 2–3 days can boost swelling

    Couch-and-cast is out. Pain-guided walking is in.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacyAnti-inflammatory gel or tabletsFor pain and swelling in the first few days.
    Works
    Gel: about 1 extra person in every 2–4 gets good pain relief within a week, with fewer side effects. Tablets: modest relief
    Cost
    About $5–20
    How long
    A few days, up to a week
    Watch for
    Tablets aren’t for everyone (stomach ulcers, kidney problems, some heart conditions, pregnancy). Check with your pharmacist

    A short-term helper, not a reason to skip rehab.

  3. Step 3 · PharmacyLace-up brace or tapeSupports the ankle while the ligaments heal.
    Works
    Cuts repeat sprains by about half when worn for sport. Better than a bandage
    Cost
    About $30–80 for a brace; tape is cheaper but needs skill
    How long
    Early on, then for sport for at least 6 months
    Watch for
    A tubular bandage alone does very little

    A crepe bandage is a hug, not a brace.

  4. Step 4 · Allied healthExercise and balance training (physio)Ankle circles, calf raises and single-leg balance, building to hopping and changing direction.
    Works
    Cuts repeat sprains by about a third or more: the best prevention
    Cost
    Physio about $90–130 a session (private health extras may help). Home programs work too
    How long
    Start in the first week; keep going for 3 months
    Watch for
    Stopping once it feels fine. That’s exactly when sprains come back

    Stand on one leg while you brush your teeth. Cheapest insurance you’ll ever buy.

  5. Step 5 · PrescriptionShort-term boot or castFor a severe sprain, a doctor may suggest one for up to 10 days, then rehab.
    Works
    In one large trial, faster early recovery than a compression bandage in severe sprains
    Cost
    Boot about $80–200; often supplied by the hospital
    How long
    Up to 10 days
    Watch for
    Stiffness and calf weakness if worn too long; clot risk in some people

    A short holiday for your ankle, not a long-term lease.

  6. Step 6 · SpecialistSurgeryFor an ankle that keeps giving way despite a full rehab program.
    Works
    Good results for most with ongoing instability, but no good trials. Rarely needed after a first sprain
    Cost
    Free in public hospitals (waits apply) or private with an out-of-pocket gap
    How long
    Months of rehab afterwards
    Watch for
    Infection, nerve irritation, stiffness

    Only after you’ve actually done the rehab. Actually done it.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Recovery times are averages. Your ankle didn’t read them.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Ultrasound therapyTrials show no real benefit for ankle sprains. The machine hums; the ankle doesn’t care.
  • Coloured kinesiology tapeLittle better than sham taping in trials. Looks sporty, does little.
  • Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) injectionsSmall studies, no clear benefit, and costly.
  • Days of icingMay numb pain briefly, but there’s little evidence it speeds healing. Walking does more.
  • An X-ray for every sprainMost don’t need one. The Ottawa ankle rules pick out the few that do.

What you can do

  • Use pain as a guide: mild discomfort with exercise is fine.
  • Return to sport once you can hop and change direction without pain.
  • Keep up balance exercises for 3 months, even when it feels better.
  • Brace or tape for sport for at least 6 months after a sprain.
  • Print our ankle sprain exercise handout and stick it on the fridge.

See your GP if

  • You can’t walk 4 steps, or have bone tenderness (see the X-ray rules above)
  • Not improving after 1 week, or still painful or unstable after 6 weeks
  • The ankle keeps giving way
  • Pain is high on the shin, or at the back of the heel (Achilles)

Go to the emergency department if the ankle or foot looks deformed, is cold, pale or numb, or a bone is visible.

Common questions

How long does a sprained ankle take to heal?

Most people can walk on it within days and recover in 2 to 6 weeks, though severe sprains take longer. Keep up balance exercises for 3 months to lower the chance of another sprain.

Do I need an X-ray for a sprained ankle?

Most sprains don’t. You need one if you couldn’t take 4 steps straight after the injury and still can’t, or if there is tenderness over specific spots on the ankle or foot bones.

Should I rest a sprained ankle or walk on it?

Walk on it as soon as pain allows, using crutches briefly if needed. Early movement leads to faster recovery than rest, and long periods in a cast slow things down.

Should I use ice or heat on a sprained ankle?

Wrapped ice for 15 to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours may ease pain and swelling in the first 2 days. Avoid heat, alcohol and massage for the first 2 to 3 days.
