Migraine

What it is

Migraine is a common brain condition that causes repeated attacks of moderate to severe headache, often with nausea and sensitivity to light and sound. It often runs in families and is more common in women.

The four stages

  • Early warning (prodrome): yawning, cravings, mood changes or a stiff neck, up to a day or two before.
  • Aura (in about 1 in 4 people): flashing lights, zigzags, blind spots or tingling, usually lasting 5 to 60 minutes.
  • Headache: often throbbing and one-sided, worse with movement.
  • Hangover (postdrome): tired or foggy for a day afterwards.

Common triggers

  • Stress, and the let-down after stress
  • Hormone changes, such as periods
  • Skipped meals and dehydration
  • Poor or irregular sleep
  • Bright lights, strong smells, alcohol and weather changes

A headache diary can help you spot your own pattern.

What helps day to day

  • Regular sleep and meal times
  • Drink water through the day
  • Limit caffeine to 1 to 2 cups a day
  • Regular exercise, such as walking
  • Screen breaks and a good desk setup
  • Relaxation, such as breathing exercises or yoga

Treating an attack

  • Take pain relief early, at the first sign of headache. Aspirin, ibuprofen or paracetamol can work well. Follow the packet directions.
  • Rest in a dark, quiet room. A cold pack on the forehead can help.
  • Your GP can prescribe migraine-specific tablets and anti-nausea medicine if needed.
  • Using pain relief more than 2 to 3 days a week can cause rebound headaches.

Prevention

If you have frequent attacks, your GP can discuss daily preventive treatment. Some people find magnesium, riboflavin (vitamin B2) or coenzyme Q10 helpful. Try one for 3 months and check with your GP or pharmacist first.

Call 000 or go to the emergency department if you have a sudden, severe "worst ever" headache, a headache with fever and a stiff neck, new weakness, confusion, trouble speaking, or a headache after a head injury.
