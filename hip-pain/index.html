Fact sheet

Outer Hip Pain

Pain on the outside of the hip, often worse lying on that side, on stairs or getting up from a chair. Usually caused by irritated buttock (gluteal) tendons rather than "bursitis". Most common in women over 40. It often takes a few months to settle.

Treatment options

Ratings show how strong the research is.

  • Strong evidenceEducation and exerciseA physio-guided program that gradually strengthens the hip muscles and reduces squashing of the tendons. In the Australian LEAP trial it worked better than a steroid injection at 2 months and at 1 year. Allow at least 12 weeks.
  • Moderate evidenceSteroid injectionEases pain in the short term, but at 1 year fewer people had improved than with exercise. Repeated injections may weaken the tendon.
  • Limited evidenceAnti-inflammatoriesTablets or gel for a short time when pain is bad or disturbs sleep. Not suitable for everyone, so check with your pharmacist.
  • Limited evidenceShockwave therapyMay help if you haven’t improved with exercise. Usually given by a physio.
  • Limited evidenceSurgeryRarely needed. Only for a torn tendon or long-lasting pain after other treatment.
  • Not recommendedStretching the sore sideStretches that pull the leg across your body squash the tendon and often make pain worse.

What you can do

  • Don’t cross your legs, sit in low chairs or stand with your weight on one hip.
  • Sleep on your back, or on the good side with a pillow between your knees.
  • Break up long walks and limit hills and stairs for now. Swimming or cycling may be easier.
  • Mild soreness with exercise is fine if it settles by the next morning.
  • If you are above a healthy weight, losing some reduces load on the tendons.

See your GP if

  • No better after 6 to 8 weeks of exercise
  • Groin pain, a limp or a stiff hip (could be the hip joint itself)
  • Pain spreading down the leg with numbness or tingling

See a doctor the same day for a hot, swollen, very painful hip with fever, or if you can’t put weight on your leg after a fall.

Common questions

Is outer hip pain bursitis?

Usually it’s irritated buttock (gluteal) tendons rather than bursitis, though both affect the same area. It’s most common in women over 40 and often takes a few months to settle.

What is the best treatment for outer hip pain?

A physio-guided education and exercise program. In the Australian LEAP trial it worked better than a steroid injection at 2 months and at 1 year. Allow at least 12 weeks.

Should I have a cortisone injection for hip bursitis?

It eases pain in the short term, but at 1 year fewer people had improved than with exercise. Repeated injections may weaken the tendon.

How should I sleep with outer hip pain?

Sleep on your back, or on the good side with a pillow between your knees. Avoid crossing your legs and sitting in low chairs.
