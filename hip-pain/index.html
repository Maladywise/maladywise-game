Fact sheet

Outer Hip Pain

Pain on the outside of the hip, worse lying on that side, on stairs or rising from a chair. Usually cranky buttock (gluteal) tendons rather than “bursitis”. Slow to settle, but very fixable.

The outer hip pain ladder

Start at the bottom, climb only if you need to. Tendons are slow: give exercise at least 12 weeks before you judge it.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careTake the squash off the tendonNo leg crossing, no low chairs, no hanging on one hip. Sleep with a pillow between your knees, and break up long walks.
    Works
    Eases pain for many and makes every other step work better
    Cost
    Free (maybe a decent pillow)
    How long
    Start now; most improve over 2–3 months
    Watch for
    Stretches that pull the leg across your body squash the tendon and usually make it worse.

    Standing with all your weight on one hip is a pose, not a posture.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacyAnti-inflammatory gel or a short course of tabletsDiclofenac or ibuprofen gel, or ibuprofen or naproxen tablets, for bad patches or poor sleep.
    Works
    Takes the edge off pain; doesn’t fix the tendon
    Cost
    Gel about $15–30; tablets about $5–15
    How long
    A week or two around bad patches
    Watch for
    Tablets: stomach upset, kidney and heart risks. Not for everyone: check with your pharmacist.

    A painkiller is a bridge, not a destination.

  3. Step 3 · Allied healthEducation and exercise with a physioA program that gradually strengthens the hip muscles and teaches you to stop squashing the tendon.
    Works
    In the Australian LEAP trial, about 3 in 4 people improved at 8 weeks and at 1 year: better than an injection
    Cost
    About $90–130 a session; Medicare rebates with a GP care plan
    How long
    At least 12 weeks, mostly done at home
    Watch for
    Mild soreness is fine if it settles by the next morning.

    The boring answer that actually wins. Annoying, we know.

  4. Step 4 · Allied healthShockwave therapyPulses of sound energy to the tendon, usually from a physio, for pain that hangs on.
    Works
    May help if exercise alone hasn’t; evidence is limited
    Cost
    About $60–120 a session, usually 3–5 sessions
    How long
    Weekly for 3–5 weeks
    Watch for
    Sore during and after. An add-on to exercise, not a replacement.

    Loud, a bit zappy, and still needs you to do your exercises.

  5. Step 5 · ProcedureSteroid (cortisone) injectionAn injection near the tendon, often with ultrasound guidance.
    Works
    Good short-term relief; at 1 year, no better than waiting it out in LEAP
    Cost
    About $0–250 depending on the clinic; sometimes bulk-billed
    How long
    Relief for weeks to a few months
    Watch for
    Pain flare for a day or two, skin thinning or lightening, a temporary rise in blood sugar if you have diabetes. Repeated injections may weaken the tendon.

    Great at buying time. Spend the time on exercises.

  6. Step 6 · SpecialistSurgeryFor a torn tendon, or long-lasting pain despite good rehab. Rarely needed.
    Works
    Helps selected people
    Cost
    Free in the public system (expect a wait); private out-of-pocket costs often run to thousands
    How long
    Months of rehab afterwards
    Watch for
    Usual surgical risks. Needs an orthopaedic assessment and usually an MRI first.

    The last rung, not a shortcut past the physio.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by clinic and pharmacy. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials: your hip didn’t read the trials.

What you can do

  • Uncross your legs. Yes, you just did it again.
  • Sleep on your back, or on the good side with a pillow between your knees.
  • Swap hills and stairs for swimming or cycling for now.
  • If you are above a healthy weight, losing some takes load off the tendons.

See your GP if

  • No better after 6 to 8 weeks of exercise
  • Groin pain, a limp or a stiff hip (could be the hip joint itself)
  • Pain spreading down the leg with numbness or tingling

See a doctor the same day for a hot, swollen, very painful hip with fever, or if you can’t put weight on your leg after a fall.

Common questions

Is outer hip pain bursitis?

Usually it’s irritated buttock (gluteal) tendons rather than bursitis, though both affect the same area. It’s most common in women over 40 and often takes a few months to settle.

What is the best treatment for outer hip pain?

A physio-guided education and exercise program. In the Australian LEAP trial, about 3 in 4 people improved at 8 weeks and at 1 year, more than with a steroid injection. Allow at least 12 weeks.

Should I have a cortisone injection for hip bursitis?

It eases pain in the short term, but at 1 year it was no better than waiting it out in the LEAP trial. Repeated injections may weaken the tendon. Use the relief to get going with exercise.

How should I sleep with outer hip pain?

Sleep on your back, or on the good side with a pillow between your knees. Avoid crossing your legs and sitting in low chairs.
