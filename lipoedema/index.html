Fact sheet

Lipoedema

A long-term condition where fat builds up in a symmetrical, often painful way on the legs, hips and sometimes arms, almost always in women. The feet and hands are spared. It isn’t caused by overeating, and dieting shrinks the rest of the body much more than the affected areas.

What it looks and feels like

  • Both legs (and often both arms) enlarged in the same way, out of proportion to the waist and upper body.
  • The feet and hands are spared, so there’s a ‘cuff’ or ‘bracelet’ of fat just above the ankles or wrists.
  • The fat is often tender or painful to touch or pressure, and bruises easily.
  • Legs feel heavy and achy, worse later in the day, after standing, and in hot weather.
  • Skin may feel cool, soft or lumpy, with small spider veins.

Who gets it

  • Almost only women. It usually starts or worsens with hormone changes: puberty, pregnancy or menopause.
  • It often runs in families.
  • It affects people of every size, including slim people. How common it is isn’t known: estimates range from under 1% to about 1 in 10 women, because it’s often missed or mislabelled as obesity.

Lipoedema, obesity or lymphoedema?

  • Obesity: fat is spread across the body, including the feet, and usually isn’t painful. It shrinks with weight loss.
  • Lymphoedema: fluid swelling, usually one-sided at first, and it includes the foot (you can’t pinch the skin on top of the second toe).
  • Lipoedema: symmetrical, spares the feet, tender, bruises easily, and resists dieting.
  • They can overlap: many people with lipoedema also have obesity, and late lipoedema can damage lymph drainage (lipo-lymphoedema).

Stages

  • Stage 1: smooth skin, soft fat with small nodules.
  • Stage 2: uneven, dimpled skin with larger lumps.
  • Stage 3: large, overhanging lobes of fat, especially around the thighs and knees.
  • Some add a stage 4 for lipoedema with lymphoedema. Not everyone progresses.

Diagnosis

  • There’s no blood test or scan for it. Diagnosis is clinical: the history, the shape, the spared feet and the tenderness.
  • Your GP may check for other causes of leg swelling (heart, kidney, liver, thyroid, veins, medicines) and refer to a lymphoedema therapist, vascular or plastic surgeon.

What helps

  • Compression garments fitted by a trained therapist: often ease pain, heaviness and swelling.
  • Movement, especially in water (swimming, aqua aerobics, walking in a pool): the water pressure acts like compression.
  • Manual lymphatic drainage (a gentle massage technique): some find it eases symptoms; evidence is limited.
  • Weight management helps the non-lipoedema fat and protects joints, even though lipoedema fat itself resists diets. Crash dieting isn’t recommended.
  • Liposuction (special lymph-sparing techniques) can reduce pain, bruising and improve mobility in selected people when other measures haven’t worked. It isn’t a cure, compression is usually still needed, and it can be costly.
  • Look after mental health too: years of being told to ‘just lose weight’ take a toll.

See your GP if

  • Your legs or arms are enlarging in a symmetrical, painful way that doesn’t match the rest of your body.
  • New swelling of one leg, especially with pain, redness or warmth (rule out a clot), or swelling that now includes the feet.

Common questions

Is lipoedema just obesity?

No. Lipoedema fat is symmetrical, spares the feet and hands, is often tender and bruises easily, and doesn’t shrink much with dieting. Some people have both.

Can you lose lipoedema fat by dieting?

Not much. Diet and exercise reduce other body fat and help symptoms and fitness, but lipoedema fat tends to stay. Compression and, for some, specialised liposuction help more.

What causes lipoedema?

The cause isn’t fully known. It runs in families and starts with hormone changes, so genes and oestrogen are thought to play a part.

Is lipoedema the same as lymphoedema?

No. Lymphoedema is fluid build-up and usually includes the foot. Late lipoedema can damage the lymph system and lead to both (lipo-lymphoedema).
