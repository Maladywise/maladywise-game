Anxiety self-check (GAD-7)

Need help now? In Australia call Lifeline on 13 11 14 (24/7) or 000 in an emergency. Elsewhere, see support lines by country or findahelpline.com.

The GAD-7 is a 7-question checklist used by GPs around the world to screen for symptoms of anxiety and to track how they change. It takes about 2 minutes. It’s a screening check, not a diagnosis.

For adults (18 and over). For young people, talk to a GP, or contact Kids Helpline on 1800 55 1800 or headspace (Australia).

Private by design: your answers stay in this browser tab. They aren’t saved, sent or tracked, and they disappear when you close or refresh the page. (Like every page on Malady Wise, we count anonymous page visits, without cookies, but never your answers or score.)

Over the last 2 weeks, how often have you been bothered by the following problems?

1. Feeling nervous, anxious or on edge
2. Not being able to stop or control worrying
3. Worrying too much about different things
4. Trouble relaxing
5. Being so restless that it is hard to sit still
6. Becoming easily annoyed or irritable
7. Feeling afraid as if something awful might happen
If you checked off any problems, how difficult have these problems made it for you to do your work, take care of things at home, or get along with other people? (optional, not scored)

What to do next

In Australia

  • See your GP. Take your score with you if it helps start the conversation. Your GP can talk through how you’ve been, check for physical causes (such as thyroid problems, anaemia or medicine side effects) and discuss options.
  • Mental Health Treatment Plan. If it would help, your GP can prepare a Mental Health Treatment Plan. Under the Better Access program, this gives Medicare rebates for up to 10 individual sessions a calendar year with a psychologist or another eligible mental health professional. Since 1 November 2025, the plan is made by your usual GP or at the practice where you’re registered with MyMedicare.
  • Free help without a referral: MindSpot (free online and phone assessment and courses for adults), Medicare Mental Health (formerly Head to Health; phone 1800 595 212, weekdays) and Beyond Blue (24/7 phone and web chat).

Outside Australia

  • See your GP or family doctor and mention your score.
  • UK: in England you can refer yourself to NHS Talking Therapies without seeing a GP first.
  • US: the SAMHSA National Helpline (1-800-662-4357) offers free, confidential treatment referral 24/7, and 988 is there for anyone in distress.
  • New Zealand: your GP, or call or text 1737 to talk with a trained counsellor.

Things that often help

  • Tell one person you trust how you’re really going.
  • Keep regular sleep and wake times, move your body most days, and go easy on alcohol.
  • Plan one small, doable thing each day that you enjoy or that gives a sense of achievement.
  • Read more in our depression and anxiety fact sheets.

Support lines

If you or someone else is in danger right now, call your emergency number. To talk to someone, any time:

  • Australia: Lifeline 13 11 14 (24/7, or text 0477 13 11 14), Suicide Call Back Service 1300 659 467, Beyond Blue 1300 22 4636, 13YARN 13 92 76 for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people. Emergency: 000.
  • Young people in Australia: Kids Helpline 1800 55 1800 (24/7, ages 5 to 25) or headspace (ages 12 to 25).
  • New Zealand: call or text 1737. Emergency: 111.
  • UK and Ireland: Samaritans 116 123. Emergency: 999 (or 112).
  • US and Canada: call or text 988. Emergency: 911.
  • Anywhere else: findahelpline.com lists free helplines in more than 100 countries.

How it works

  • Each of the 7 questions scores 0 (not at all) to 3 (nearly every day), for a total of 0 to 21.
  • Standard score ranges: 0–4 minimal, 5–9 mild, 10–14 moderate, 15–21 severe.
  • A score of 10 or more is the usual point where a doctor would suggest a fuller check. In the original study, this cut-off picked up about 89 in 100 people with generalised anxiety disorder.
  • The GAD-7 was designed for generalised anxiety, but it also picks up many people with panic disorder, social anxiety and PTSD. It can’t tell these apart, which is one reason it isn’t a diagnosis.

Common questions

Is this an official anxiety test?

The GAD-7 is a real, widely used questionnaire that GPs and researchers use to screen for anxiety and track symptoms. It can’t diagnose an anxiety disorder on its own. That takes a conversation with a health professional.

What does my GAD-7 score mean?

Scores of 0–4 suggest minimal anxiety symptoms, 5–9 mild, 10–14 moderate and 15–21 severe. A score of 10 or more is a good reason to see your GP for a fuller check.

Can I do this for my teenager?

This version is for adults filling it in about themselves. Teenagers need a check with a GP or a youth service. In Australia, headspace (ages 12 to 25) and Kids Helpline (1800 55 1800, ages 5 to 25) can help.

Are my answers saved?

No. Your answers stay in this browser tab and disappear when you close or refresh the page. Nothing is stored or sent.
