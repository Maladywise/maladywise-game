Low Sperm Count

A semen test with fewer sperm than expected. It’s common, often improves, and a low count doesn’t mean you can’t have children. The single biggest fixable cause many men don’t know about: testosterone or steroids switch sperm production off.

What a semen test measures

  • Count: the World Health Organization’s 2021 lower reference limit is 16 million sperm per mL, or 39 million in the whole sample.
  • Movement (motility): at least 30% swimming forwards.
  • Shape (morphology): at least 4% normal-looking sperm. Most sperm look ‘abnormal’, even in fertile men.
  • Volume: at least 1.4 mL.
  • These are lower limits of normal, not pass/fail lines. Men below them can still father children, and men above them can still have trouble.
  • Oligospermia means a low count. Azoospermia means no sperm in the sample, which always needs specialist assessment.

One test isn’t the answer

  • Results vary a lot from week to week. A fever, illness or heavy drinking in the past 3 months can drop the numbers.
  • An abnormal result is repeated, usually after a few weeks, with 2 to 7 days without ejaculating beforehand.

Common causes

  • Testosterone or anabolic steroids, including gym supplements that contain them. They tell the brain to stop making the hormones that drive sperm production.
  • Varicocele: enlarged veins around a testicle.
  • Smoking, heavy alcohol, cannabis, obesity, and heat (saunas, hot tubs, laptops on laps).
  • Some medicines, past infections like mumps, undescended testes, injury, chemotherapy or radiotherapy.
  • Genetic causes, such as Klinefelter syndrome or Y chromosome changes, mainly with very low or zero counts.
  • Often no cause is found.

What helps

  • Sperm take about 3 months to make, so give changes 3 months before re-testing.
  • Stop smoking, cut alcohol, avoid cannabis, aim for a healthy weight, and keep the testicles cool.
  • Stop testosterone or steroids under medical care. Most men’s sperm return, often within 6 to 12 months, though it can take longer.
  • Treat a cause if one is found, such as a hormone problem or, in some men, a varicocele.
  • Many antioxidant supplements are marketed for sperm, but good evidence that they lead to more babies is lacking.
  • Even very low counts can lead to pregnancy with IVF and ICSI, where a single sperm is injected into each egg. Sometimes sperm can be retrieved surgically when none are in the semen.

See a doctor if

  • You and your partner haven’t conceived after 12 months of trying, or 6 months if she is 35 or older. Test the man early: it’s a cheap, simple test.
  • Your semen test showed no sperm, or a very low count.
  • You notice a lump or swelling in a testicle, small or soft testicles, low sex drive or erection problems.

Common questions

What is a normal sperm count?

The World Health Organization’s lower reference limit is 16 million sperm per mL, or 39 million per sample. Below this doesn’t mean you can’t have children, and results vary a lot between tests.

Can a low sperm count be improved?

Often, yes. Stopping smoking, cutting alcohol, losing weight, keeping cool and stopping testosterone or steroids can all help. Sperm take about 3 months to make, so re-test after 3 months.

Does testosterone increase sperm count?

No. Testosterone or anabolic steroids usually lower sperm count, sometimes to zero, because they switch off the brain’s signals to the testicles. Most men recover after stopping, but it can take months.

Can I still have a baby with a low sperm count?

Many men do, naturally or with help. With IVF and ICSI, even very low counts can lead to pregnancy.
