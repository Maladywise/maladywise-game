Fact sheet

Hay Fever

An allergy to pollen, dust mites, mould or pets: sneezing, runny or blocked nose, itchy eyes. About 1 in 5 Australians has it. A daily nasal spray beats a cupboard full of tablets.

The hay fever ladder

Start low, but don’t skip step 3: a daily steroid spray is the single most effective treatment.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careDodge the pollen and rinse it outCheck pollen forecasts, wraparound sunglasses, shower after time outside, saline rinses.
    Works
    Saline rinses ease nose symptoms by about a quarter in small trials; avoidance helps on bad days
    Cost
    Free to about $10–25 for a saline rinse kit
    How long
    Through your season
    Watch for
    Use sterile or cooled boiled water for rinses.

    Drying sheets on the line in spring is a pollen collection service.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacyNon-drowsy antihistamine tablets (cetirizine, loratadine, fexofenadine)For sneezing, itch, runny nose and watery eyes.
    Works
    Modest benefit over a dummy treatment in trials: good for itch and sneezing, little help for a blocked nose
    Cost
    About $10–25 for 2–3 months of generic tablets
    How long
    Work within an hour; take daily in season or as needed
    Watch for
    Some people still feel a bit drowsy, especially on cetirizine.

    Generic and brand-name tablets are the same medicine in a different box.

  3. Step 3 · PharmacySteroid nasal spray (budesonide, fluticasone, mometasone)Calms the inflamed nose lining.
    Works
    Beat antihistamine tablets for nose symptoms across 16 trials; the most effective single treatment
    Cost
    About $15–25 a bottle (1–2 months)
    How long
    A few days to start working, up to 2 weeks for full effect. Daily through the season.
    Watch for
    Nosebleeds and dryness. Aim towards the outer side of the nostril, not the middle.

    It’s not a rescue spray. Daily, even on good days.

  4. Step 4 · PharmacyCombination spray (azelastine with fluticasone)A steroid and an antihistamine in one spray.
    Works
    Beats either spray alone: about 50% more benefit over a dummy treatment than the steroid spray alone in trials
    Cost
    About $30–35 a bottle
    How long
    Works within an hour; use daily
    Watch for
    Bitter taste, nosebleeds, mild drowsiness.

    For when one spray isn’t pulling its weight.

  5. Step 5 · SpecialistAllergen immunotherapy (desensitisation)Tablets or drops under the tongue, or injections, to retrain the immune system. For proven allergy not controlled by sprays.
    Works
    Symptoms and medicine use about 20–30% lower than a dummy treatment; benefits last years after a 3-year course
    Cost
    About $100–150 a month for tablets or drops (not on the PBS), plus specialist fees
    How long
    3–5 years
    Watch for
    Itchy mouth or throat early on. Rarely, severe allergic reactions: first doses are supervised.

    The only treatment that changes the allergy itself. A marathon, not a sprint.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials: your nose didn’t read them.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • The ‘hay fever injection’A steroid shot risks weaker bones, high blood sugar and mood changes, and safer options work. One jab, months of side effects.
  • HomeopathyA major Australian review (NHMRC) found no health condition where it works better than a dummy treatment. Expensive water.
  • Local honeyThe one small trial found it no better than a dummy treatment. Lovely on toast, useless against pollen.
  • Old sedating antihistamines (e.g. promethazine)No better than modern non-drowsy ones, and the drowsiness affects driving, work and school.
  • Unproven allergy tests (IgG, kinesiology, hair analysis)Not valid for diagnosing allergy and often lead to pointless diets. Skin prick or IgE blood tests do the job.

What you can do

  • Start your steroid spray a couple of weeks before your usual season.
  • Decongestant sprays: 3 days max, or your nose gets more blocked than before.
  • Antihistamine eye drops help itchy, watery eyes.
  • Dust mites: wash bedding weekly in hot water and use mite-proof covers.

See your GP if

  • Symptoms aren’t controlled after a few weeks of daily steroid spray
  • Cough, wheeze or tight chest, especially in spring: you may have asthma
  • Blocked nose on one side only, nosebleeds, or loss of smell
  • You’d like allergy testing or to ask about immunotherapy

Call 000 for trouble breathing, wheeze or a tight chest that isn’t quickly relieved by a reliever puffer. Thunderstorm asthma can start fast during or after a spring storm, even if you’ve never had asthma. If you have asthma, take your preventer every day through pollen season and always carry your reliever.

Common questions

What is the most effective hay fever treatment?

A steroid nasal spray used every day, especially for a blocked nose. Full effect takes a few days to 2 weeks. A combined steroid and antihistamine spray works better than either alone.

Do antihistamines help a blocked nose?

Not much. Non-drowsy antihistamines help sneezing, itch and watery eyes, but do little for a blocked nose.

Is a hay fever injection safe?

Steroid injections aren’t recommended. Risks include weakened bones, raised blood sugar and mood changes, and safer treatments work. Allergen immunotherapy is different: it’s a long-term desensitisation treatment.

What is thunderstorm asthma?

Sudden asthma that can start during or after a spring storm on high-pollen days, even in people who’ve never had asthma. Call 000 for trouble breathing that isn’t quickly relieved by a reliever puffer.
