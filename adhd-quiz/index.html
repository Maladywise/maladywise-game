ADHD Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on what ADHD is (and isn’t), how it’s diagnosed in children and adults, treatment options and the myths that won’t go away.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What are the three core features of ADHD?

    1. Sadness, worry and fear
    2. Low intelligence, poor sleep and laziness
    3. Inattention, hyperactivity and impulsivity
    4. Fever, rash and joint pain
    Show answer

    Answer: Inattention, hyperactivity and impulsivity. People can have mostly inattentive, mostly hyperactive-impulsive, or combined presentations.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    By what age must symptoms have started for a diagnosis?

    1. Any age
    2. Before age 30
    3. Before age 3
    4. Before age 12
    Show answer

    Answer: Before age 12. Adults can be diagnosed, but there should be signs from childhood, even if missed at the time.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Can adults be diagnosed with ADHD?

    1. Only in men
    2. Yes
    3. Only if diagnosed as a child
    4. No, it always goes away
    Show answer

    Answer: Yes. Many adults are diagnosed after their child is, or when life demands outgrow their coping strategies.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    Why is ADHD often missed in girls?

    1. They more often have the quiet, inattentive type
    2. Girls can’t have ADHD
    3. Tests only work on boys
    4. Girls grow out of it by age 5
    Show answer

    Answer: They more often have the quiet, inattentive type. Daydreaming and disorganisation get less attention than disruptive behaviour.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    What is usually the first-line medicine?

    1. A sleeping tablet
    2. An antipsychotic
    3. An antibiotic
    4. A stimulant such as methylphenidate or lisdexamfetamine
    Show answer

    Answer: A stimulant such as methylphenidate or lisdexamfetamine. Stimulants help about 7 in 10 people. They work within an hour or so.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    What is a common side effect of stimulants?

    1. Hair loss
    2. Low blood pressure
    3. Reduced appetite and trouble sleeping
    4. Weight gain
    Show answer

    Answer: Reduced appetite and trouble sleeping. Taking them with or after breakfast and avoiding late doses helps.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    Which is a non-stimulant ADHD medicine?

    1. Guanfacine or atomoxetine
    2. Ritalin
    3. Caffeine
    4. Dexamfetamine
    Show answer

    Answer: Guanfacine or atomoxetine. Useful when stimulants don’t suit, or with tics or anxiety.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    Does sugar cause ADHD?

    1. Only chocolate
    2. No
    3. Yes, always
    4. Only in teenagers
    Show answer

    Answer: No. Controlled studies don’t show sugar causes ADHD or makes children hyperactive.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    How much of ADHD risk is genetic?

    1. About 10%
    2. About 70 to 80%
    3. Almost none
    4. 100%, always from the father
    Show answer

    Answer: About 70 to 80%. ADHD is one of the most heritable conditions in psychiatry. Parents often recognise themselves.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    Which conditions commonly occur alongside ADHD?

    1. None, it always occurs alone
    2. Asthma only
    3. Diabetes
    4. Autism, anxiety and learning difficulties
    Show answer

    Answer: Autism, anxiety and learning difficulties. Screen for them: treating only one can leave the person struggling.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    What should be checked before starting a stimulant?

    1. Vision
    2. Brain MRI
    3. Blood pressure, heart rate and any heart history
    4. Liver biopsy
    Show answer

    Answer: Blood pressure, heart rate and any heart history. Recheck blood pressure and heart rate, plus weight and height in children, while on treatment.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Is ADHD caused by bad parenting?

    1. No, it’s a neurodevelopmental condition
    2. Yes, mostly
    3. Only in single-parent families
    4. Only with too much screen time
    Show answer

    Answer: No, it’s a neurodevelopmental condition. Parenting strategies help, but they don’t cause ADHD.

Common questions

Does ADHD go away?

Symptoms often change with age. Hyperactivity may ease, while inattention and disorganisation often continue into adulthood.

Who can diagnose ADHD in Australia?

Usually a paediatrician or psychiatrist. Some GPs share care once a diagnosis is made.

Is this quiz a diagnosis?

No. It’s general education. Talk to your GP if you’re concerned.
