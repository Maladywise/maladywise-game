Endometriosis Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on endometriosis: common symptoms, why diagnosis takes so long, and the treatments that help pain and fertility.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What is endometriosis?

    1. A type of uterine cancer
    2. An infection of the uterus lining
    3. Tissue similar to the uterus lining growing outside the uterus
    4. A deficiency of oestrogen
    Show answer

    Answer: Tissue similar to the uterus lining growing outside the uterus. It causes inflammation, scarring and pain, often around periods.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    About how many Australian women and girls are affected?

    1. 1 in 100,000
    2. About 1 in 7
    3. 1 in 1,000
    4. 1 in 2
    Show answer

    Answer: About 1 in 7. It’s common, though many go years without a diagnosis.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Which symptom is common?

    1. Period pain that stops you doing usual activities
    2. Hair loss on the scalp
    3. Earache during periods
    4. Painless heavy periods only
    Show answer

    Answer: Period pain that stops you doing usual activities. Also pain with sex, bowel or bladder pain, and tiredness. Period pain that stops you going to school or work isn’t normal.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    What is the average delay to diagnosis in Australia?

    1. About 6 months
    2. About 20 years
    3. About 1 week
    4. About 6 to 8 years
    Show answer

    Answer: About 6 to 8 years. Symptoms are often dismissed. The national action plan aims to shorten this.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    Which imaging test can now detect deep endometriosis?

    1. Specialised pelvic ultrasound
    2. Brain MRI
    3. Bone scan
    4. Chest X-ray
    Show answer

    Answer: Specialised pelvic ultrasound. A normal ultrasound doesn’t rule out milder disease.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    What is a reasonable first treatment?

    1. Hysterectomy straight away
    2. Anti-inflammatories plus hormones, e.g. the pill
    3. A long course of antibiotics
    4. Nothing until fertility testing
    Show answer

    Answer: Anti-inflammatories plus hormones, e.g. the pill. Hormonal options include the combined pill, progestogens and the hormonal IUD.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    Can endometriosis affect fertility?

    1. Never: fertility isn't affected
    2. It always causes infertility
    3. Only after the age of 60
    4. Yes, but many people still conceive
    Show answer

    Answer: Yes, but many people still conceive. Surgery or IVF can help when pregnancy doesn’t happen.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    What is a laparoscopy?

    1. A swab taken from the cervix
    2. A type of pelvic ultrasound
    3. Keyhole surgery to see and remove endometriosis
    4. A blood test for endometriosis
    Show answer

    Answer: Keyhole surgery to see and remove endometriosis. It can confirm the diagnosis and treat it at the same time.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    Which medicine (Visanne) is a progestogen used for endometriosis?

    1. Dienogest
    2. Doxycycline
    3. Metformin
    4. Tamoxifen
    Show answer

    Answer: Dienogest. It’s taken daily and often stops periods.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    Can endometriosis come back after surgery?

    1. Only if you become pregnant
    2. Never, once it has been removed
    3. Only if the surgery was keyhole
    4. Yes, often, especially without hormonal treatment
    Show answer

    Answer: Yes, often, especially without hormonal treatment. Hormonal treatment after surgery lowers the chance of recurrence.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    What is adenomyosis?

    1. A polyp in the large bowel
    2. Endometriosis that has spread to the lungs
    3. Lining-like tissue growing into the uterus muscle
    4. A benign lump in the breast
    Show answer

    Answer: Lining-like tissue growing into the uterus muscle. It causes heavy, painful periods and a bulky uterus, and often coexists with endometriosis.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Which non-medicine options also help pain?

    1. Cold showers only
    2. Pelvic floor physio, heat and exercise
    3. Avoiding all movement
    4. Fasting on painful days
    Show answer

    Answer: Pelvic floor physio, heat and exercise. Pain care is multidisciplinary. Many people benefit from a physio and a GP-led plan.

Common questions

Does pregnancy cure endometriosis?

No. Symptoms may ease during pregnancy but often return.

Is period pain normal?

Some cramping is common. Pain that stops you doing usual things, or doesn’t settle with simple treatment, should be checked.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. See your GP if you have painful periods.
