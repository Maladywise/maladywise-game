Fact sheet

Klinefelter Syndrome (XXY)

An extra X chromosome in males. It’s common, around 1 in 580 males, yet most are never diagnosed. Many first find out when trying for a baby. Testosterone treatment and support make a real difference at any age.

What it is

  • Males usually have one X and one Y chromosome (XY). In Klinefelter syndrome there’s an extra X (XXY). It happens by chance, not from anything parents did.
  • The testicles make less testosterone and usually little or no sperm.
  • It varies a lot. Many have subtle features and live typical lives without knowing.

Signs by age

  • Childhood: sometimes later walking or talking, learning or attention difficulties, low muscle tone, being tall.
  • Puberty: small, firm testicles, less facial and body hair, some breast growth, long legs.
  • Adults: infertility (often the first clue), low libido, tiredness, low mood, thinning bones.

Diagnosis

  • A chromosome blood test (karyotype) confirms it.
  • Hormone tests usually show low testosterone with high LH and FSH.
  • Small testicles on examination are the most consistent sign, so a doctor’s exam matters.

Treatment and support

  • Testosterone, usually started around puberty, supports puberty, energy, mood, muscle and bone. It’s usually lifelong.
  • Speech, learning, occupational therapy and psychology support help children and teens who need it.
  • Breast tissue that doesn’t settle can be removed surgically.

Fertility

  • Most men with XXY have no sperm in their semen, but sperm can sometimes be found in the testicles with a surgical procedure (micro-TESE) and used for IVF with ICSI.
  • Testosterone treatment lowers sperm production, so talk to a fertility specialist before or early in treatment if you want children.

Long-term health

  • Higher risk of osteoporosis, type 2 diabetes, blood clots, heart disease, some autoimmune conditions and male breast cancer.
  • Regular checks of bone density, weight, blood sugar and mood are worthwhile.

Common questions

How common is Klinefelter syndrome?

It affects about 1 in 580 males, making it the most common chromosome difference in males. Most are never diagnosed.

Can men with Klinefelter syndrome have children?

Some can, with help. Sperm can sometimes be retrieved from the testicles and used for IVF with ICSI. Talk to a fertility specialist early, ideally before starting testosterone.

Is Klinefelter syndrome inherited?

Usually not. The extra X comes from a random event when the egg or sperm forms.

What is the treatment for Klinefelter syndrome?

There’s no cure, but testosterone treatment, learning support and regular health checks help a lot.
