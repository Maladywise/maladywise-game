Fact sheet

Medicinal cannabis and driving

In most of Australia it’s an offence to drive with any detectable THC, even if it’s prescribed and you don’t feel affected. CBD-only products are generally fine to drive on if you don’t feel impaired.

The short version

  • THC-containing products: don’t drive. Roadside tests detect THC, not impairment, and a prescription isn’t a defence in most states.
  • CBD-only products: generally fine to drive, as long as you don’t feel drowsy or impaired. Check the label: some ‘CBD’ products contain a little THC.
  • Victoria (from 1 March 2025) and Tasmania: a magistrate can consider your prescription and whether you were impaired when deciding whether to cancel your licence. You can still be charged.
  • Police can order you off the road: after a positive roadside test, drivers are usually told not to drive for a period (often 12 hours or more).

How long does THC stay detectable?

It varies a lot. Saliva tests usually pick up THC for hours after use, sometimes longer in regular users. There’s no safe waiting time that guarantees a negative test, so many doctors advise not driving at all while using THC products.

Work and special licences

  • THC products are generally not suitable for commercial, heavy vehicle, bus, taxi, dangerous goods or emergency vehicle drivers, or other safety-critical work.
  • Workplace drug testing may detect THC. Talk to your employer and doctor before starting.
  • Your doctor should discuss driving before prescribing, and may suggest a CBD-only product if you need to drive.

If you feel affected

Even legal medicines can make driving dangerous. Don’t drive if you feel drowsy, dizzy, slow or ‘high’, especially when starting or changing a dose, or with alcohol or other sedating medicines.

Common questions

Can I drive if I have a prescription for medicinal cannabis?

Not if it contains THC, in most of Australia. Driving with any detectable THC is an offence, and a prescription isn’t a defence. Victoria and Tasmania let a court consider your prescription, but you can still be charged.

Can I drive on CBD oil?

Generally yes, if it’s a CBD-only product and you don’t feel impaired. Check it doesn’t contain THC.

Do roadside drug tests detect medicinal cannabis?

They detect THC, whether it came from a prescription or not. They don’t measure impairment.
