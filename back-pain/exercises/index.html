Lower Back Pain: exercise plan

Backs are strong, and sore is not the same as damaged. The fastest way out is to keep moving, gently at first, then more.

How to use this
  • Pain rules: some pain while moving is normal and isn’t a sign of damage. Start at an amount you can manage and build up step by step, rather than stopping each time it hurts. If a flare-up lasts more than a day or two, drop back a step, then build up again.
  • How often: walk most days; do the exercises once a day.
  • How long: new back pain usually gets better within a few weeks. For back pain lasting more than 3 months, exercise is one of the main treatments. Once you’ve recovered, a walking program built up over 6 months nearly doubled the time before the next bout of back pain.
  • Progress when: it feels manageable. Add a few minutes of walking or a few more repeats at a time. Get back to work and your usual activities as soon as you can; you don’t need to wait until the pain has gone.
  • Based on: the UK NICE back pain guideline (NG59), the Lancet 2018 back pain series, the Cochrane review of exercise for long-term back pain (2021), which found no one type of exercise works best, and the Australian WalkBack trial (Lancet 2024).

Your exercises

  1. WalkingStart with what you can manage, even a few minutes at a time. Add a little each week.Most days; build to at least 30 minutes, 5 times a week
  2. Knee rocksLie on your back, knees bent, and gently let both knees fall to one side then the other. Start small; make the movement bigger as it eases.A few each side to start, adding more as it eases. Once a day
  3. Cat and camelOn hands and knees, slowly round your back up, then let it sag gently. Move within comfort; go further as it loosens.A few slow repeats to start, adding more as it eases. Once a day
  4. BridgeLie on your back, knees bent, and lift your hips by squeezing your bottom. Start with small lifts; progress to higher lifts and short holds.A few to start, building up gradually. Once a day
  5. Bird dogOn hands and knees, reach one arm forward and the opposite leg back, then swap. Start with just a leg or just an arm; progress to both.A few each side to start, building up gradually. Once a day
  6. Sit to standStand up from a chair and sit back down with control. Progress to a lower chair or holding a weight.A few to start, building up gradually. Once a day

Pick what you’ll keep doing: all types of exercise (walking, strength, stretching, yoga, Pilates) help about the same, so choose the ones you enjoy. Bed rest slows recovery: stay as active as you can.

Stop and see your GP or physio if

  • New numbness around your bottom or genitals, or trouble passing urine or controlling your bowels: go to an emergency department now
  • Leg weakness or foot drop that is getting worse
  • Fever, unexplained weight loss, a history of cancer, or pain after a significant fall
  • Severe pain at night that doesn’t ease with any position
  • No improvement after 4–6 weeks

Want a supervised program?

A physio or exercise physiologist can build a graded activity program and help you get back to the things you’ve been avoiding. Group exercise classes are a good option. For ongoing pain, programs that combine exercise with a psychological approach (such as cognitive behavioural therapy) can help when other treatment hasn’t. If you have a chronic condition, ask your GP about a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan (GPCCMP): it gives a Medicare rebate for up to 5 allied health visits a year (physio, exercise physiologist or podiatrist).
