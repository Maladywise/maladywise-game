Fact sheet

Anal Fissure

A small tear at the anus, usually from passing a hard poo. Sharp pain during and after, often bright red blood on the paper. Most heal in 6 to 8 weeks once stools stay soft.

The anal fissure ladder

Soft stools are the foundation of every step. Ointments buy the muscle time to relax; surgery is for the stubborn ones.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careFibre, water, stool softener and warm bathsKeeps stools soft so the tear can finally heal. Sit in warm water for 10–15 minutes after a bowel motion.
    Works
    Most new fissures heal with this alone (about 5–9 in 10). Ongoing fibre cuts repeat tears a lot
    Cost
    Psyllium or a stool softener about $10–20 a tub
    How long
    Daily; 6–8 weeks to heal, then keep the fibre going
    Watch for
    Bloating if you add fibre too fast. Build up over a week and drink plenty of water

    Your bottom is begging for a softer delivery. Give it one.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacyNumbing ointment (lidocaine)Short-term pain relief before a bowel motion.
    Works
    Eases pain short term; in one trial healed fewer than fibre and baths (6 vs 9 in 10)
    Cost
    About $10–20
    How long
    Days to a few weeks
    Watch for
    Stinging or skin irritation. Not for long-term use

    A painkiller, not a repair kit.

  3. Step 3 · PharmacyGlyceryl trinitrate (GTN) 0.2% ointmentRelaxes the tight anal muscle and boosts blood flow. Pharmacist-only.
    Works
    Heals about 5 in 10 long-standing fissures (vs about 3–4 in 10 on a dummy treatment)
    Cost
    About $35–45 for 30 g
    How long
    2–3 times a day as directed, with a 12-hour break overnight; usually several weeks
    Watch for
    Headache (common, especially early on), light-headedness. Never use with erection medicines such as sildenafil or tadalafil: dangerous drop in blood pressure. Not for use in pregnancy or breastfeeding

    A pea-sized dab, not a generous smear. Your head will thank you.

  4. Step 4 · PrescriptionDiltiazem ointmentSame muscle-relaxing idea, made up by a compounding pharmacy.
    Works
    Heals about as many as GTN (around half), with far fewer headaches
    Cost
    About $30–60 a tub on a private script
    How long
    2–3 times a day for 6–8 weeks
    Watch for
    Itch or local irritation

    GTN’s calmer cousin.

  5. Step 5 · SpecialistBotulinum toxin injectionTemporarily relaxes the muscle for 2–3 months, from a colorectal or general surgeon.
    Works
    Heals roughly 5–7 in 10; some come back
    Cost
    Free in public hospitals (waits apply); private costs vary, often several hundred dollars or more
    How long
    One quick procedure; effect lasts 2–3 months
    Watch for
    Temporary leaking of wind or stool in a few people

    A temporary truce with your sphincter.

  6. Step 6 · ProcedureLateral internal sphincterotomyA small, precise cut in the anal muscle to stop it spasming.
    Works
    About 9 in 10 heal: the most effective option
    Cost
    Free in public hospitals (waits apply); private gap varies
    How long
    Day surgery; most heal within weeks
    Watch for
    Small risk of long-term leaking of wind or, less often, stool

    The big fix, saved for when gentler options have had a fair go.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Healing rates are from trials. Your bottom has its own opinions.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Steroid haemorrhoid creamsDid no better than fibre and warm baths in trials, and long-term steroid thins delicate skin.
  • Manual anal stretchAn old operation that causes more leakage (incontinence) than modern surgery. Largely abandoned, and good riddance.
  • Scented wipes and vigorous wipingFragrance and friction irritate a raw tear. Pat dry with plain water or soft paper.

What you can do

  • Aim for 25 to 30 g of fibre a day: legumes, wholegrains, fruit and vegetables.
  • Go when you feel the urge. Don’t strain, and don’t scroll your phone on the loo.
  • Paracetamol helps. Avoid codeine, which causes constipation.
  • Raise your knees on a footstool and pat dry gently.

See your GP if

  • Pain is severe or not improving after 2 weeks
  • Not healed after 8 weeks, or keeps coming back
  • The tear isn’t at the front or back midline, or there are several (these need checking for other causes)
  • You also have diarrhoea, weight loss or tummy pain, or you’re over 45 with bleeding or a change in bowel habit

See a doctor the same day for a hot, swollen, very painful lump near the anus, fever, or heavy bleeding.

Common questions

How long does an anal fissure take to heal?

Most heal within 6 to 8 weeks once stools are kept soft. See your GP if it hasn’t healed after 8 weeks or keeps coming back.

Does GTN ointment work for a fissure?

It helps: it heals about half of long-standing fissures, compared with about a third on a dummy treatment. It’s used a few times a day for several weeks, and headaches are common. Diltiazem ointment works about as well with fewer headaches.

Is it a fissure or haemorrhoids?

A fissure typically causes sharp pain during and for hours after a bowel motion, with bright red blood on the paper. Haemorrhoids more often cause painless bleeding, itch or a lump. Your GP can check which it is.

When is surgery needed for an anal fissure?

Only for fissures that don’t heal with fibre and ointments. A small cut in the anal muscle heals about 9 in 10, but carries a small risk of leaking wind or stool.

Can an anal fissure heal on its own?

Yes, most new (acute) fissures heal within 6 to 8 weeks with soft, regular poos, fibre, plenty of fluid and pain relief. Fissures lasting longer than about 8 weeks are called chronic and usually need a prescribed ointment or other treatment.

What does an anal fissure feel like?

A sharp, tearing pain while doing a poo, often described as like passing glass, followed by a burning or throbbing ache that can last minutes to hours. You may see a little bright red blood on the toilet paper.

Does Botox work for an anal fissure?

Botulinum toxin injected into the anal muscle relaxes it for about 3 months, giving the fissure time to heal. It heals roughly 6 to 8 in 10 chronic fissures, and a few people have short-term leakage of wind or poo.
