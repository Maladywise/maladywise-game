Fact sheet

Varicocele

Enlarged veins around a testicle, usually the left. About 1 in 5 men have one, and most never know or need treatment. It matters mainly when it causes pain, or when a couple is struggling to conceive and the semen test is abnormal.

What it is

  • The veins that drain the testicle become stretched and swollen, like varicose veins in the leg.
  • About 20 to 25% of adult men have one. Most are on the left, because of the way that vein drains.
  • Large ones look or feel like a soft ‘bag of worms’ above the testicle, more obvious when standing and gone when lying down.
  • Most cause no symptoms. Some cause a dull, dragging ache, worse after standing or exercise.

Fertility

  • Varicoceles are more common in men with fertility problems. The leading theory is that the pooled blood warms the testicle.
  • But fewer than half of men with a varicocele have lower sperm count or quality.
  • In men with an abnormal semen test, repairing it improves the semen in about two-thirds. The effect on pregnancy rates is less certain.

When treatment is considered

  • Ongoing pain or ache.
  • Infertility with an abnormal semen test and a varicocele you can feel.
  • In teenagers, if the testicle on that side is smaller or not growing properly.
  • A varicocele only seen on ultrasound, with a normal semen test, usually doesn’t need treatment.

Treatment

  • Surgery ties off the enlarged veins through a small cut in the groin, usually as a day procedure.
  • Embolisation: a radiologist blocks the vein from inside with a tiny coil, through a vein in the groin or neck.
  • Supportive underwear and pain relief can ease mild aches.
  • Sperm take about 3 months to make, so semen tests are repeated 3 to 6 months after treatment.

See a doctor promptly if

  • A varicocele appears suddenly in an older man, is only on the right, or doesn’t empty when lying down. These need a scan to check for something pressing on the veins.
  • Any hard lump in the testicle itself.

Common questions

Is a varicocele serious?

Usually not. Most cause no symptoms and need no treatment. It matters mainly for pain, or for fertility problems with an abnormal semen test.

Does a varicocele cause infertility?

It can lower sperm count or quality in some men, but fewer than half of men with a varicocele have abnormal semen. Many father children without treatment.

Why is a varicocele usually on the left?

The left testicle’s vein drains at a right angle into a larger vein, so pressure builds more easily. A new right-sided varicocele should be checked with a scan.

Does varicocele surgery improve sperm count?

In men with an abnormal semen test, about two-thirds see improvement. The effect on pregnancy rates is less certain.
