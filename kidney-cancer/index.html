Kidney Cancer

About 4,900 Australians are diagnosed with kidney cancer each year, twice as many men as women. Most are now found by accident on a scan done for something else, often early, and 83% of people are alive after 5 years.

The kidney’s version of a surprise party: found on a scan you had for your back.

See your GP soon if you see blood in your urine, even once, or have a lasting pain in your side or a lump in your tummy, or weight loss, fevers or night sweats you can’t explain.

The numbers

  • About 4,900 new cases a year: about 3,300 men and 1,600 women
  • About 1 in 66 people are diagnosed by age 85
  • 83% are alive 5 years after diagnosis, up from 51% in the late 1980s

Signs

  • Often none: most are found on an ultrasound or CT done for another reason
  • Blood in the urine
  • Pain in the side or back that doesn’t go away
  • A lump in the tummy, tiredness, weight loss or fevers

Risk factors

  • Smoking
  • Being above a healthy weight, and high blood pressure
  • Long-term kidney failure, especially on dialysis
  • Rare inherited conditions such as von Hippel–Lindau disease

Kidney ‘cleanses’ and detox teas do nothing for cancer risk. Not smoking and a healthy blood pressure do.

Tests and treatment

  • Tests: CT or MRI scans; sometimes a biopsy
  • Small tumours: careful watching with scans, removing part of the kidney, or freezing or heating the tumour
  • Larger tumours: removing the whole kidney. One healthy kidney does the job
  • Advanced cancer: immunotherapy and targeted medicines have greatly improved survival in the last decade

Common questions

What are the first signs of kidney cancer?

Usually none. Most are found by chance on a scan. Signs can include blood in the urine, a lasting pain in the side and a lump in the tummy.

Can you live with one kidney?

Yes. After removing a kidney, most people live a normal life with the remaining one, with regular checks of kidney function and blood pressure.

Is kidney cancer curable?

Often, when found early. About 83% of people are alive 5 years after diagnosis.
