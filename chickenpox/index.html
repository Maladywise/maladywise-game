Fact sheet

Chickenpox

Itchy spots that turn into blisters, then scabs. Chickenpox is usually mild in children, but it can be serious for newborns, pregnant women and people with weak immune systems. A vaccine prevents most cases.

How it goes: a day or two of fever, tiredness and going off food, then red spots that become blisters and crust over. New spots keep coming for 3 to 5 days. Most are crusted by about 10 days. Symptoms start 2 to 3 weeks after catching it.

How it spreads: very easily, through the air and blister fluid. People are infectious from about 2 days before the rash until every blister has crusted over.

Complications: the most common is a bacterial skin infection of the spots, including from strep bacteria. Rarer ones include pneumonia and brain inflammation. Adults, teenagers, newborns and people with weak immune systems are more at risk.

The vaccine: in Australia, children get one dose of a combined measles, mumps, rubella and chickenpox vaccine at 18 months, free on the National Immunisation Program. People aged 14 and over who haven’t had chickenpox need 2 doses, at least 4 weeks apart.

The chickenpox ladder

Most children only need comfort care. Antiviral medicine is for people at higher risk of complications. Avoid ibuprofen.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careParacetamol, fluids and cool comfortParacetamol for fever or soreness, plenty of fluids (ice blocks count), loose clothes, oat baths and patting skin dry. Keep nails short, and try mittens at night to stop scratching.
    Works
    Eases symptoms while the virus runs its course. In healthy children aged 1 week to 12 years, no specific treatment is needed
    Cost
    Free, plus a few dollars for paracetamol
    How long
    About 10 days until all spots are crusted
    Watch for
    Spots that get redder, hotter and more painful can mean infection.

    Scratching: tempting, but it’s how the scars happen.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacyItch reliefAsk a pharmacist about soothing creams or gels and an antihistamine suitable for your child’s age to help with itch and sleep.
    Works
    Can ease itch and help sleep; doesn’t change the course of the illness
    Cost
    About $10–20
    How long
    Until the spots crust over
    Watch for
    Some antihistamines cause drowsiness; check the age limit on the pack.

    Sleep is the real treatment for everyone in the house.

  3. Step 3 · GPAntiviral tablets (aciclovir) for someA doctor may suggest antiviral tablets for people at higher risk, such as unvaccinated children over 12, people with severe eczema or chronic lung disease, children on steroid tablets, and second cases in the same household.
    Works
    Given to reduce the chance of complications in higher-risk groups; most healthy children don’t need it
    Cost
    Prescription; cost varies
    How long
    About a week
    Watch for
    If you’re in a higher-risk group, see a doctor early.

    Not for every spotty kid, but worth asking about if you’re in a risk group.

  4. Step 4 · HospitalAntiviral dripFor newborn babies, people with weak immune systems, and anyone with complications such as pneumonia.
    Works
    Used to treat or prevent serious complications
    Cost
    Free in Australian public hospitals
    How long
    Days
    Watch for
    Pregnant women exposed to chickenpox should see a doctor within 24 hours.

    Serious chickenpox is rare. Hospitals take it seriously when it happens.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • IbuprofenAvoid it in chickenpox. It’s linked to a higher risk of serious strep skin infections. Use paracetamol instead.
  • AspirinNever give aspirin to children. It’s linked to Reye syndrome, a rare but serious illness.
  • Antibiotics for ordinary spotsChickenpox is a virus. Antibiotics only help if the skin becomes infected.

What you can do

  • Keep your child home until every spot has crusted over.
  • Keep away from newborns, pregnant women who haven’t had chickenpox, and people with weak immune systems.
  • Use paracetamol, not ibuprofen.
  • Cut nails short and use cotton mittens at night.
  • Check your child’s vaccines are up to date.
  • Adults who had chickenpox can later get shingles from the same virus.

See your GP if

  • Your child is in a higher-risk group, or is over 12 and unvaccinated
  • You’re pregnant and have been near someone with chickenpox (see a doctor within 24 hours)
  • Spots become red, hot, swollen or more painful
  • New spots are still appearing after 7 days, or the rash spreads to the eyes
  • A fever of 38°C or more lasts more than 3 days, or comes back

See a doctor urgently if

  • A newborn baby, or anyone with a weak immune system, who gets chickenpox
  • Breathing faster or harder than usual
  • Signs of dehydration, such as a dry mouth or not weeing
  • Your child is getting worse overall

Call 000 (Australia) or your local emergency number if someone with chickenpox is struggling to breathe, has pale, blue or blotchy skin, is very hard to wake or confused, has a fit, or has a rash that doesn’t fade when you press a glass on it.

Common questions

How long is chickenpox contagious?

From about 2 days before the rash appears until every blister has crusted over, usually around 5 to 10 days after the rash starts.

Why shouldn’t I give ibuprofen for chickenpox?

Ibuprofen is linked to a higher risk of serious strep skin infections in chickenpox. Paracetamol is the safer choice for fever and pain.

Can you get chickenpox if you’re vaccinated?

It’s possible, but it’s usually much milder. In Australia, children get one dose at 18 months, and people aged 14 and over need 2 doses.

What if I’m pregnant and exposed to chickenpox?

If you haven’t had chickenpox or the vaccine, see a doctor within 24 hours of contact, at any stage of pregnancy. Treatment can lower the risk to you and your baby.
