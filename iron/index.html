Fact sheet

Iron Deficiency

Low iron stores, which can leave you tired, breathless and eventually anaemic. Common with heavy periods, pregnancy and low-iron diets. Easy to fix, but the cause matters as much as the iron.

The iron deficiency ladder

Start at the bottom, climb only if you need to. Tablets work for most people; finding why you’re low is not optional.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careIron-rich foodRed meat, chicken, fish, eggs, legumes, tofu and iron-fortified cereals.
    Works
    Not tested as a fix in trials; helps keep stores up, but rarely enough alone for a real deficiency
    Cost
    Your usual grocery bill
    How long
    Ongoing
    Watch for
    Tea and coffee with meals block iron absorption.

    Spinach is fine, but it isn’t Popeye-level iron. Plant iron is poorly absorbed.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacyIron tabletsIdeally every second day, on an empty stomach, with tea, coffee, milk, calcium and antacids kept 2 hours away.
    Works
    About 7 in 10 respond within 2 weeks; haemoglobin should rise about 20 g/L by 4 weeks
    Cost
    About $10–20 a month, less on alternate days
    How long
    At least 3 months, then a repeat blood test
    Watch for
    Dark stools (harmless), constipation, nausea. Keep away from children: an overdose can be fatal.

    Every second day often beats daily: better absorbed, kinder to your gut.

  3. Step 3 · PrescriptionTreat the cause: heavy periodsOptions include tranexamic acid, a hormonal IUD or the pill.
    Works
    Tranexamic acid or the pill cut bleeding by about a third to a half; a hormonal IUD by up to about 90%
    Cost
    Tranexamic acid $25 on the PBS ($7.70 concession); hormonal IUD $25 on the PBS plus insertion fees
    How long
    Months to years
    Watch for
    Tranexamic acid isn’t for people with a history of clots.

    Topping up a leaking bucket works better once you fix the leak.

  4. Step 4 · PrescriptionIron infusionA drip of 15 to 30 minutes, for when tablets fail or aren’t tolerated, poor absorption, later pregnancy, or when iron is needed fast.
    Works
    Refills stores in one or two visits; haemoglobin rises over 2–4 weeks, a little faster than with tablets in trials
    Cost
    Iron is $25 on the PBS ($7.70 concession); clinic fees vary, from bulk-billed to a few hundred dollars
    How long
    One or two visits, then a blood test
    Watch for
    Headache, aches or flushing for a day or two. Report stinging or swelling at the drip site at once: leaks can cause permanent brown staining. Can lower phosphate, mainly after repeats.

    Faster, not fancier. Tablets still win for most people.

  5. Step 5 · SpecialistFinding hidden blood loss (gastroscopy and colonoscopy)Looks for bleeding or poor absorption in the gut, including coeliac disease.
    Works
    Finds the cause in many men and people past menopause; bowel or stomach cancer in about 1 in 10 to 12
    Cost
    Free in the public system (expect a wait); private often several hundred dollars after rebates
    How long
    A day procedure
    Watch for
    Essential if you are a man, past menopause, or have bowel symptoms or weight loss.

    In men and after menopause, low iron is a clue, not a diagnosis.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by pharmacy and clinic. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials: your blood didn’t read the trials.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • A vitamin C chaserA 2020 trial found no added benefit to taking it with iron tablets. Save the orange juice for breakfast.
  • Iron ‘fish’ and cast-iron pansThe randomised trial of an iron cooking fish found no rise in haemoglobin, and pans add an unpredictable trickle. Fine for cooking, not for refilling empty stores.
  • Tonics and multivitaminsMost contain a fraction of the iron in a proper iron tablet: fine as a top-up, far too little to fix a deficiency.
  • Pricey ‘gentle’ or slow-release ironSome are kinder on the stomach, but slow-release types can let iron slip past where it’s best absorbed. Try a standard tablet every second day first.

What you can do

  • Take tablets every second day, away from tea, coffee and milk.
  • Expect dark stools. Black, sticky, tar-like ones are different: see your GP.
  • Store iron tablets out of children’s reach.

See your GP if

  • Black or bloody stools, or a change in bowel habits
  • Weight loss you can’t explain
  • No better after a few weeks of treatment

Call 000 for chest pain, severe breathlessness or fainting.

Common questions

Is an iron infusion better than tablets?

Not for everyone. Tablets are the usual first step and work for most people. An infusion is used when tablets don’t work or aren’t tolerated, with poor absorption, in later pregnancy, or when iron is needed quickly.

How long do iron tablets take to work?

Haemoglobin usually rises within 3 to 4 weeks, but refilling stores takes at least 3 months, then a repeat blood test. Taking iron every second day is absorbed better and causes fewer side effects for many people.

When is the best time to take iron tablets?

On an empty stomach. Keep tea, coffee, milk, calcium and antacids at least 2 hours away. Adding vitamin C gave no extra benefit in a 2020 trial.

What are the side effects of an iron infusion?

A headache, aches or flushing for a day or two are common. Rarely, iron leaking from the drip causes permanent brown skin staining, so report stinging or swelling at once. It can also lower phosphate, mainly after repeat infusions.
