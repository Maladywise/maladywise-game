Iron deficiency

What it is

Your body uses iron to make haemoglobin, which carries oxygen in your blood. Iron deficiency means your stores are low. If it goes on long enough, it causes anaemia (a low blood count).

Common symptoms

  • Tiredness and low energy
  • Breathlessness or a racing heart with exercise
  • Pale skin, headaches, poor concentration
  • Restless legs, brittle nails, hair thinning

Some people have no symptoms at all.

Common causes

  • Heavy periods
  • Pregnancy
  • Not enough iron in your diet
  • Poor absorption, such as coeliac disease
  • Slow bleeding from the stomach or bowel

Tests

A blood test checks your ferritin (iron stores) and blood count. Your GP may also check for the cause, especially if you’re a man or have finished menopause.

Treatment

  • Iron tablets are the usual first step. Take them once a day or every second day, as your GP advises.
  • Take them on an empty stomach, or with orange juice or other vitamin C.
  • Keep tea, coffee, milk, calcium and antacids at least 2 hours away from your iron dose.
  • Iron can cause constipation, an upset stomach and dark stools. This is common.
  • If tablets don’t work or you can’t take them, your GP may suggest an iron infusion (a drip).
  • Most people need treatment for at least 3 months. Your GP will recheck your blood.

What you can do

Eat iron-rich foods: red meat, chicken, fish, eggs, legumes, tofu, leafy greens and iron-fortified cereals. Iron from meat is absorbed best.

See your GP soon if you have

  • Blood in your stools, or black, sticky, tar-like stools
  • Weight loss you can’t explain, or a change in your bowel habits
  • Periods that are getting heavier
  • Symptoms that don’t improve after a few weeks of treatment

Call 000 if you have chest pain, severe breathlessness or you faint.
