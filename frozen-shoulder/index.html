Frozen Shoulder

The capsule around the shoulder joint becomes inflamed, then thick and tight, so the shoulder hurts and then stiffens in every direction. It’s slow and frustrating, but it gets better on its own for most people over 1 to 3 years.

Symptoms. A deep ache in the shoulder and upper arm, often worse at night and when lying on it, then growing stiffness. Turning the arm out and reaching behind your back or up to a shelf get hard, even when someone else tries to move the arm for you.

The three stages. Freezing (painful, about 2–9 months), frozen (stiff but less painful, about 4–12 months) and thawing (movement slowly returns, about 5–24 months). The stages overlap and timing varies a lot.

Who gets it. Mostly people aged 40 to 60, women a little more often than men. It’s more common with diabetes (about 5 times more likely), thyroid disease, and after a shoulder injury, surgery or a spell of keeping the arm still. Often there’s no clear cause.

How it’s diagnosed. From how your shoulder moves when your GP or physio examines it. An X-ray may be used to rule out arthritis, which can look similar. With diabetes or no clear cause, your GP may check your blood sugar.

The frozen shoulder ladder

Frozen shoulder gets better on its own; treatment eases the pain and helps you keep and regain movement while it does.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careKeep it moving, gentlyUse the arm for light daily tasks and do gentle range-of-motion exercises within comfort, little and often. Heat before stretching can help.
    Works
    Doesn’t change the overall course, but gentle beats forceful: in a Dutch study (not randomised), 89% of people exercising within their pain limits had a normal or near-normal shoulder at 2 years vs 63% with intensive stretching
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    Daily, for months
    Watch for
    Don’t rest it in a sling or force painful stretches in the early stage.

    Little and often. The shoulder doesn’t respond to bullying.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacyPain relief (paracetamol, ibuprofen or anti-inflammatory gel)To make sleep and gentle movement possible.
    Works
    Short-term pain relief; doesn’t shorten the condition
    Cost
    About $5–15 a pack
    How long
    Days to weeks at a time
    Watch for
    Anti-inflammatory tablets: stomach, kidney and blood pressure risks. Check with a pharmacist if you have other conditions.

    Painkillers buy you sleep and a window to move.

  3. Step 3 · Allied healthPhysio and a home exercise programStage-matched stretches, hands-on mobilisation and advice on how hard to push.
    Works
    In a 2020 review of 65 trials, adding home exercises to a steroid injection may have given extra benefit over the following months
    Cost
    Free at home with our handout; physio about $80–120 a session (Medicare rebates with a GP care plan)
    How long
    Months
    Watch for
    Stretching should leave only mild discomfort that settles within about 30 minutes.

    The program that suits your stage beats the hardest one.

  4. Step 4 · ProcedureSteroid injection into the joint (sometimes with hydrodilatation)An injection into the shoulder joint, often guided by ultrasound. Hydrodilatation adds a larger volume of fluid to stretch the tight capsule.
    Works
    Injection: the best-supported treatment for pain in the first 12 weeks, about 1 point lower on a 10-point pain scale than no treatment, best given in the first year. Hydrodilatation: short-term benefit for pain and movement in a Cochrane review; unclear if it beats an injection alone
    Cost
    Medicare rebates; out-of-pocket fees vary, especially with imaging
    How long
    One injection, sometimes repeated
    Watch for
    Short-lived flare, skin thinning at the site, and raised blood sugar for a few days with diabetes.

    A window of relief to get moving, not a cure.

  5. Step 5 · SpecialistManipulation under anaesthetic or keyhole capsular releaseFor severe, stubborn stiffness despite the steps above, usually after months of trying.
    Works
    In the UK FROST trial (503 people), physio plus injection, manipulation and keyhole release scored within about 3 points of each other on a 48-point shoulder score at 1 year, smaller than a difference that matters. Keyhole release had the most serious complications (8 vs 2 with manipulation)
    Cost
    Public waiting lists; private fees vary
    How long
    Recovery over weeks, with physio afterwards
    Watch for
    Anaesthetic risks; rarely fracture or nerve injury with manipulation.

    Bigger isn’t automatically better. Ask what each option adds.

Costs are rough Australian prices in November 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials, not promises.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Forceful stretching in the painful stagePushing hard into pain early on did worse than gentle exercise within pain limits in a 2-year study. Save firmer stretches for the stiff stage.
  • Rushing to surgeryIn the FROST trial, surgery did no better than physio plus an injection at a year, with more complications and cost. It’s for the stubborn minority.
  • Resting it in a slingKeeping the arm still is a known trigger for stiffness. Keep using it for light tasks.

What you can do

  • Follow our frozen shoulder exercise handout: six gentle stretches, with what to do at each stage.
  • Keep using the arm for light tasks below shoulder height.
  • Sleep on your back or good side with a pillow hugging the sore arm.
  • With diabetes, keep sugars well controlled, and ask your GP about a blood sugar check if you haven’t had one.
  • Ask about an injection early if pain is stopping you sleeping or moving.

See your GP if

  • Shoulder pain and stiffness aren’t easing after a few weeks
  • Pain is waking you most nights
  • Weakness lifting the arm, especially after a fall (this may be a tendon tear)
  • Pain in both shoulders and hips with morning stiffness, especially if you’re over 50

Get urgent help for shoulder or arm pain with chest pain, breathlessness, sweating or nausea, which can be a heart attack: call 000 (Australia) or your local emergency number. See a doctor the same day for a hot, swollen shoulder with fever, or a shoulder that looks out of place after a fall.

Common questions

What are the first signs of frozen shoulder?

A deep, aching shoulder pain, often worse at night, followed by stiffness in every direction. Turning the arm out (like opening a door) and reaching behind your back are usually the first to go, and even with help the arm won’t move far.

What are the stages of frozen shoulder?

Freezing (painful, about 2–9 months), frozen (stiff but less painful, about 4–12 months) and thawing (movement returns, about 5–24 months). The stages blur into each other and vary a lot between people.

How long does frozen shoulder last?

Usually 1 to 3 years. In a UK study, about 6 in 10 people had a normal or near-normal shoulder after an average of 4.4 years, and nearly all the rest had only mild symptoms.

Is frozen shoulder linked to diabetes?

Yes. People with diabetes are about 5 times more likely to get it, and about 13 in 100 people with diabetes get it. Around 3 in 10 people with frozen shoulder have diabetes, so your GP may suggest a blood sugar test.

Does a cortisone injection help frozen shoulder?

It’s the treatment with the best evidence for easing pain in the first few months, especially early on. It doesn’t cure the condition, and works best combined with gentle home exercises.

Can frozen shoulder come back?

It rarely comes back in the same shoulder, but about 1 in 5 people get it in the other shoulder at some point.
