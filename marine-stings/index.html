Fact sheet

Marine Stings

Most stings in Australian waters are painful but not dangerous. A few, mainly in tropical northern waters, can be life-threatening. The right first aid depends on what stung you. This sheet follows the current (2025) ANZCOR first aid guideline.

Treatment options

Ratings show how strong the research is.

  • Strong evidenceBluebottle: hot waterPick off tentacles and rinse with seawater, then soak in hot water (about 45°C, as hot as can be tolerated without burning) for 20 to 30 minutes. A hot shower works. Use ice in a bag if hot water isn’t available or doesn’t help.
  • Strong evidenceBox jellyfish: 000 and CPRIn tropical waters, get the person out of the water and call 000. Start CPR if they’re unresponsive and not breathing normally. Antivenom is available.
  • Moderate evidenceBlue-ringed octopus, cone shell, sea snake: pressure bandageBites can be painless but cause paralysis. Call 000, apply a firm pressure bandage over the bite and whole limb, keep the person still, and give CPR if breathing stops until help arrives.
  • Moderate evidenceStonefish, stingray or other fish spines: hot waterSoak the limb in water as hot as the rescuer can comfortably bear, and get medical care. Don’t pull out a large embedded barb: pad around it. Stonefish antivenom is available.
  • Limited evidenceBox jellyfish: vinegarDouse the sting for 30 seconds, then pick off tentacles. The evidence is mixed but it is still advised. Use ice for pain. Don’t use fresh water.
  • Not recommendedBluebottle: vinegarDon’t use it on bluebottle stings.
  • Not recommendedRubbing, fresh water or urineRubbing and fresh water can fire more stinging cells. Urine doesn’t help.

What you can do

  • Swim between the flags. In tropical waters in stinger season (about November to May), wear a full-body stinger suit and swim in netted areas.
  • Wear shoes on reefs and in rock pools. Never pick up an octopus or cone shell.
  • Shuffle your feet in shallow water to warn stingrays.
  • For advice, call the Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26.

See your GP if

  • A sting or wound becomes red, hot, swollen or oozing in the following days
  • A spine or barb may still be in the skin
  • An itchy rash returns at the sting site a week or two later

Call 000 for trouble breathing, collapse, a sting covering half a limb or more, or a sting near the eye. Also for severe back or muscle pain, sweating or vomiting starting 20 to 40 minutes after a minor tropical sting (Irukandji syndrome).

Common questions

Should you put vinegar on a bluebottle sting?

No. Pick off tentacles, rinse with seawater, then soak in hot water (about 45°C, as hot as can be tolerated without burning) for 20 to 30 minutes.

What is the first aid for a box jellyfish sting?

Get the person out of the water and call 000. Start CPR if they’re unresponsive and not breathing normally. Douse the sting with vinegar for 30 seconds, then pick off tentacles.

Does urine help a jellyfish sting?

No. Rubbing and fresh water can also fire more stinging cells, so avoid them.

What is Irukandji syndrome?

A reaction to a tiny tropical jellyfish, with severe back or muscle pain, sweating or vomiting starting 20 to 40 minutes after a minor sting. Call 000.
