Fact sheet

Marine Stings

Most stings in Australian waters hurt a lot and harm a little. A few, mainly in tropical northern waters, can kill. First aid depends on the culprit. This follows the 2025 ANZCOR guideline.

The marine sting ladder

This ladder climbs by danger, not by price. Steps 1 and 2 cover most beach days; steps 4 to 6 are emergencies.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careAny jellyfish sting: out, rinse, pickGet out of the water. Rinse with seawater and pick off tentacles (fingers are fine). Don’t rub.
    Works
    Stops more stinging cells firing
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    Straight away
    Watch for
    Fresh water and rubbing fire more stingers. Urine does nothing.

    Urine has never helped anyone, in any situation, on any beach.

  2. Step 2 · Self-careBluebottle: hot waterSoak in hot water (about 45°C, as hot as can be tolerated without burning) for 20 to 30 minutes. A hot shower works. Use ice in a bag if hot water isn’t available or doesn’t help.
    Works
    Eases pain in most people; beat ice in an Australian trial
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    20 to 30 minutes
    Watch for
    Test the water on unstung skin first, especially for kids. No vinegar on bluebottles.

    Vinegar is for chips and box jellyfish. Bluebottles want a hot bath.

  3. Step 3 · Self-careStonefish, stingray or other fish spines: hot water, then a doctorSoak in water as hot as the rescuer can comfortably bear, and get medical care.
    Works
    Hot water eases the pain; wounds and spines need checking
    Cost
    Free first aid; public hospital care is free with Medicare
    How long
    Soak until pain eases, then get seen
    Watch for
    Don’t pull out a large embedded barb: pad around it. Call 000 for a stingray wound to the chest or belly.

    Shuffle your feet in the shallows. Stingrays hate surprises as much as you do.

  4. Step 4 · Self-careBox jellyfish (tropical): 000, vinegar, CPRGet the person out of the water and call 000. Start CPR if they’re unresponsive and not breathing normally. Douse the sting with vinegar for 30 seconds, then pick off tentacles.
    Works
    Fast CPR and hospital care save lives; vinegar evidence is mixed but still advised
    Cost
    Free first aid; ambulance fees vary by state
    How long
    Until help arrives
    Watch for
    Don’t use fresh water. Ice in a bag for pain.

    Vinegar bottles on tropical beaches aren’t for chips.

  5. Step 5 · Self-careBlue-ringed octopus, cone shell, sea snake: pressure bandageCall 000. Apply a firm pressure bandage over the bite and the whole limb, splint it and keep the person still. Give CPR if breathing stops.
    Works
    Slows venom spread; breathing support saves lives while paralysis wears off
    Cost
    Free first aid; an elastic bandage for the kit costs about $10–20
    How long
    Until help arrives
    Watch for
    Bites can be painless. Paralysis can start within minutes.

    Cute, small, pretty. Do not pick it up.

  6. Step 6 · SpecialistHospital careAntivenom (box jellyfish, stonefish, sea snake), strong pain relief, breathing support, care for Irukandji syndrome, and wound care.
    Works
    Highly effective when needed
    Cost
    Free in public hospitals with Medicare
    How long
    Hours to days
    Watch for
    Irukandji: severe back or muscle pain, sweating or vomiting 20 to 40 minutes after a minor tropical sting.

    The bit where the experts take over. Your job was steps 1 to 5.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by state and service. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Ambulance fees depend on your state and cover; most first aid costs nothing but a cool head.

What you can do

  • Swim between the flags. In tropical waters in stinger season (about November to May), wear a full-body stinger suit and swim in netted areas.
  • Wear shoes on reefs and in rock pools. Never pick up an octopus or cone shell.
  • Shuffle your feet in shallow water to warn stingrays.
  • For advice, call the Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26.

See your GP if

  • A sting or wound becomes red, hot, swollen or oozing in the following days
  • A spine or barb may still be in the skin
  • An itchy rash returns at the sting site a week or two later

Call 000 for trouble breathing, collapse, a sting covering half a limb or more, or a sting near the eye. Also for severe back or muscle pain, sweating or vomiting starting 20 to 40 minutes after a minor tropical sting (Irukandji syndrome).

Common questions

Should you put vinegar on a bluebottle sting?

No. Pick off tentacles, rinse with seawater, then soak in hot water (about 45°C, as hot as can be tolerated without burning) for 20 to 30 minutes.

What is the first aid for a box jellyfish sting?

Get the person out of the water and call 000. Start CPR if they’re unresponsive and not breathing normally. Douse the sting with vinegar for 30 seconds, then pick off tentacles.

Does urine help a jellyfish sting?

No. Rubbing and fresh water can also fire more stinging cells, so avoid them.

What is Irukandji syndrome?

A reaction to a tiny tropical jellyfish, with severe back or muscle pain, sweating or vomiting starting 20 to 40 minutes after a minor sting. Call 000.
