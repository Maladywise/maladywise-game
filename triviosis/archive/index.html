  1. #20 · Friday 6 November 2026 Hard

    Theme: Doctor on the court

    1. James Naismith, born on this day in 1861, invented basketball in 1891 in which city?

      Answer: Springfield, Massachusetts

      At a YMCA training school, with two peach baskets nailed to a balcony rail.

    2. In 1898, Naismith also graduated in medicine from a medical school in?

      Answer: Denver

      Gross Medical College in Denver, Colorado. He then spent decades at the University of Kansas.

    3. In 1892, Senda Berenson adapted Naismith’s new game for women students at?

      Answer: Smith College

      The first women’s college game followed at Smith in March 1893.

    4. A ball jamming the fingertip can cause mallet finger, an injury to the?

      Answer: Extensor tendon at the end joint

      The fingertip droops. Usual treatment is a splint holding the end joint straight, day and night, for about 6–8 weeks.

    5. Jumper’s knee, common in basketball and volleyball, is tendinopathy of the?

      Answer: Patellar tendon

      Pain sits just below the kneecap and builds with jumping and landing.

    6. In a rolled ankle (inversion sprain), which ligament is injured most often?

      Answer: Anterior talofibular ligament

      The ATFL is the weakest of the lateral ankle ligaments and gives way first.

    7. A patient lies face down and you squeeze their calf. The foot doesn’t move. This suggests a ruptured?

      Answer: Achilles tendon

      The Simmonds–Thompson test. A normal calf squeeze points the foot.

    8. Commotio cordis is cardiac arrest caused by?

      Answer: A blow to the chest at a vulnerable moment in the heartbeat

      Usually a ball or elbow to the chest wall. Fast CPR and an AED save lives.

    9. Osgood–Schlatter disease, common in jumping teenagers, causes a sore lump at the?

      Answer: Tibial tuberosity

      Where the patellar tendon pulls on a growth plate. It settles once growth finishes.

    10. Drinking far more than you sweat out during a long event can cause?

      Answer: Exercise-associated hyponatraemia

      Low blood sodium can cause confusion, seizures and collapse. Advice now is to drink to thirst.

  2. #19 · Thursday 5 November 2026 Medium

    Theme: After the wave

    1. Today is World Tsunami Awareness Day. In START mass-casualty triage, anyone who can walk is tagged?

      Answer: Green (minor)

      START stands for Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment. Walking wounded go green and are reassessed later.

    2. In adult START triage, a breathing patient is tagged red (immediate) if their respiratory rate is over?

      Answer: 30 a minute

      Over 30, a capillary refill over 2 seconds (or no radial pulse), or not obeying commands all mean red.

    3. A limb trapped under rubble for hours is finally freed. The most immediate threat to life is?

      Answer: Hyperkalaemia and cardiac arrest

      Potassium and myoglobin flood out of crushed muscle on release. Rescuers start IV fluids before freeing the limb when they can.

    4. Bywaters and Beall described crush syndrome and its kidney failure in 1941, in people pulled from?

      Answer: Bombed buildings in the London Blitz

      Their BMJ paper made the link between crushed muscle and acute kidney injury.

    5. Most of the roughly 230,000 deaths in the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami were caused by?

      Answer: Drowning

      The wave hit with little warning. Many victims were women and children, who were less likely to be able to swim or outrun it.

    6. ‘Tsunami lung’ is?

      Answer: Aspiration pneumonia from inhaling dirty seawater

      The water carries mud, sewage and soil bugs, so infections can be mixed and unusual.

    7. After floods, which infection spread through water contaminated with rat urine rises sharply?

      Answer: Leptospirosis

      Think fever, muscle aches (especially calves) and red eyes after wading through floodwater. Severe cases cause jaundice and kidney failure (Weil’s disease).

    8. The 2004 tsunami was triggered by a magnitude 9.1 earthquake off which island?

      Answer: Sumatra

      The quake was off northern Sumatra in Indonesia. Waves reached as far as East Africa.

    9. Weeks after big floods in the tropics, standing water often drives a surge in which mosquito-borne virus?

      Answer: Dengue

      Aedes mosquitoes breed in water left in tyres, buckets and rubble. Clearing containers matters as much as clean water.

    10. A survivor has a dirty wound and can’t remember their tetanus jabs. The safest plan is?

      Answer: Tetanus vaccine plus tetanus immunoglobulin

      A tetanus-prone wound with an unknown history gets both. Tetanus cases spiked in Aceh after 2004.

  3. #18 · Wednesday 4 November 2026 Medium

    Theme: Kidneys

    1. What is the functional unit of the kidney?

      Answer: The nephron

      About a million per kidney.

    2. eGFR estimates?

      Answer: How well the kidneys filter

      eGFR estimates how much blood the kidneys filter each minute, worked out from blood creatinine, age and sex.

    3. What is the most common type of kidney stone?

      Answer: Calcium oxalate

      About three-quarters of kidney stones are calcium-based, mostly calcium oxalate. Drinking plenty of water is the best prevention.

    4. Which hormone from the kidneys drives red cell production?

      Answer: Erythropoietin

      Erythropoietin (EPO) is made by the kidneys, which is why kidney failure so often causes anaemia.

    5. Adult polycystic kidney disease is inherited how?

      Answer: Autosomal dominant

      Adult polycystic kidney disease is autosomal dominant: each child of an affected parent has a 50% chance of inheriting it.

    6. Heavy protein in the urine, swelling and low albumin suggest?

      Answer: Nephrotic syndrome

      Nephrotic syndrome is leaky kidney filters: heavy protein loss in the urine, low albumin and puffy swelling.

    7. The kidneys sit in which space?

      Answer: The retroperitoneum

      The kidneys sit behind the abdominal lining, in the retroperitoneum, tucked against the back muscles.

    8. Extreme exercise, like an ultramarathon, can flood the kidneys with myoglobin from damaged muscle. This is?

      Answer: Rhabdomyolysis

      Myoglobin is toxic to kidney tubules, the CK soars and urine can turn cola-coloured. A modest CK rise is normal after any marathon, such as the New York City Marathon on 1 November.

    9. Which electrolyte is the big danger in kidney failure?

      Answer: Potassium

      High potassium can stop the heart.

    10. Cola-coloured urine a few weeks after strep throat suggests?

      Answer: Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis

      Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis is an immune reaction one to three weeks after a strep throat or skin infection, inflaming the kidney filters.

  4. #17 · Tuesday 3 November 2026 Easy

    Theme: Horses, not zebras (Melbourne Cup day)

    1. ‘When you hear hoofbeats, think of horses, not zebras’ was coined by?

      Answer: Theodore Woodward

      Woodward taught it to students at the University of Maryland in the late 1940s: common things are common. Rare diagnoses have been ‘zebras’ ever since.

    2. ‘Cauda equina’, the bundle of nerve roots at the bottom of the spinal canal, literally means?

      Answer: Horse’s tail

      Below the end of the spinal cord the nerve roots fan out like a horse’s tail.

    3. Numbness in the ‘saddle’ area plus new bladder trouble is a red flag for?

      Answer: Cauda equina syndrome

      Compression of those nerve roots, often by a large disc prolapse, is a surgical emergency.

    4. A ‘charley horse’ is?

      Answer: A sudden muscle cramp

      Usually a painful calf cramp. The origin of the name is uncertain, but it’s been in American slang since the 1880s.

    5. The hippocampus, the brain’s memory hub, is named after its resemblance to a?

      Answer: Seahorse

      From the Greek hippos (horse) and kampos (sea monster). Anatomist Julius Caesar Arantius named it in 1587.

    6. A horseshoe kidney is when?

      Answer: The two kidneys are joined at their lower poles

      It’s the commonest kidney fusion, in about 1 in 500 people, and is usually found by chance on a scan.

    7. Many antivenoms, and the first diphtheria antitoxin, were made using which animal?

      Answer: Horses

      Horses are given small, safe doses of venom or toxin and make antibodies, which are purified from their plasma. Most Australian snake antivenoms are still made this way.

    8. A classic menopause hormone therapy, Premarin, takes its name from?

      Answer: Pregnant mares’ urine

      PREgnant MARes’ urINe is the source of its conjugated oestrogens. Women’s health owes the horse more than most people realise.

    9. Which animal is the symbol of Ehlers-Danlos syndromes and many rare-disease groups?

      Answer: Zebra

      Patients adopted it to say: sometimes the hoofbeats really are a zebra. Rare diseases are rare one by one, but together affect millions.

    10. Phar Lap, winner of the 1930 Melbourne Cup, is famous for having a heart that weighed about?

      Answer: 6.2 kg

      About twice the 3.2 kg of an average horse’s heart. It’s on display at the National Museum of Australia in Canberra.

  5. #16 · Monday 2 November 2026 Easy

    Theme: Pathology Week: the lab behind the diagnosis

    1. In the standard H&E stain, haematoxylin colours cell nuclei?

      Answer: Blue-purple

      Haematoxylin (from the logwood tree) turns nuclei blue-purple; eosin turns cytoplasm pink. It’s been the workhorse of histology for well over a century.

    2. Which white cells are normally the most numerous in adult blood?

      Answer: Neutrophils

      Neutrophils make up roughly 40–75% of white cells and are first on the scene in bacterial infection.

    3. Tissue frozen and cut during an operation, so the pathologist can report within minutes, is a?

      Answer: Frozen section

      A frozen section lets the surgeon know mid-operation whether a margin is clear or a lump is cancer. The full report on fixed tissue follows days later.

    4. Gram-negative bacteria stain which colour on a Gram stain?

      Answer: Pink-red

      Gram-positive bacteria hold the crystal violet and look purple; Gram-negatives lose it and pick up the pink counterstain. Danish scientist Hans Christian Gram invented the method in 1884.

    5. HbA1c reflects average blood glucose over roughly the past?

      Answer: 2–3 months

      Glucose sticks to haemoglobin for the life of the red cell, about 120 days, so HbA1c shows the longer-term picture.

    6. Which blood test is the key marker of heart muscle damage?

      Answer: Troponin

      Troponins are proteins of heart muscle. A rise and fall in troponin is central to diagnosing a heart attack.

    7. ‘Autopsy’ literally means?

      Answer: Seeing for yourself

      From the Greek autos (self) and opsis (sight). ‘Necropsy’ means seeing the dead.

    8. Rudolf Virchow, father of modern pathology, popularised the idea that every cell comes from?

      Answer: Another cell

      ‘Omnis cellula e cellula’ (1855). It buried the idea that life springs from non-living matter and grounded disease in cells.

    9. Target cells, sickle cells and schistocytes are all abnormal shapes of which cells on a blood film?

      Answer: Red blood cells

      Red cell shape tells a story: sickle cells in sickle cell disease, target cells in thalassaemia and liver disease, fragments (schistocytes) when cells are sliced up in small vessels.

    10. The Pap smear is named after Georgios Papanicolaou. Who was his main test subject for 21 years?

      Answer: His wife, Andromachi (Mary)

      Andromachi Papanikolaou let him take a sample from her cervix almost every day for 21 years, which helped him map normal cell changes. Few medical ‘thank yous’ have been more overdue.

    4. ‘QID’ on a prescription means?

      Answer: Four times a day

      Quater in die.

    5. The prefix ‘dys-’ means?

      Answer: Difficult or abnormal

      As in dyspnoea, dysphagia, dysuria.

    6. ‘-aemia’ at the end of a word refers to?

      As in anaemia and septicaemia.

    7. The ‘cholecyst’ is the?

      Answer: Gallbladder

    8. ‘Pyrexia’ means?

      Answer: Fever

      Pyrexia (from the Greek for fire) is the medical word for fever, usually a temperature of 38°C or more.

    9. Testing for ‘dysdiadochokinesia’ checks the ability to?

      Answer: Do rapid alternating movements

      A cerebellar sign. Also excellent Scrabble.

    10. ‘Stat’ comes from the Latin statim, meaning?

      Answer: Immediately

      ‘Stat’ is short for statim, Latin for immediately. In practice it means drop everything.

  1. #14 · Saturday 31 October 2026 Hard

    Theme: Spooky medicine

    1. In 1985 a chemist suggested which rare disease might explain vampire legends?

      Answer: Porphyria

      Congenital erythropoietic porphyria, said David Dolphin. Historians and doctors were not convinced.

    2. ‘Werewolf syndrome’ is a nickname for?

      Answer: Hypertrichosis

      Excess hair growth all over. Clinical lycanthropy is the belief you're turning into an animal.

    3. A ‘zombie’ state of staring, stiff stillness and no response can be?

      Answer: Catatonia

      It often responds well to treatment.

    4. Rigor mortis happens because muscles run out of?

      Answer: ATP

      Without ATP, myosin can't let go of actin.

    5. Goosebumps are caused by tiny muscles called?

      Answer: Arrector pili

      Left over from furrier ancestors puffing up to look scary.

    6. Botulinum toxin works by blocking the release of?

      Answer: Acetylcholine

      This causes a descending flaccid paralysis. Tiny doses are used in medicine.

    7. Tetrodotoxin is found in which animal?

      Answer: Pufferfish

      It blocks sodium channels. One ethnobotanist claimed it was in Haitian ‘zombie powder’, a disputed idea.

    8. Cyanide is classically said to smell of?

      Answer: Bitter almonds

      Not everyone can smell it. Rotten eggs is hydrogen sulphide.

    9. Methanol poisoning can permanently cause?

      Answer: Blindness

      Its breakdown product, formic acid, damages the optic nerve.

    10. The adult skull is made of how many bones, not counting the ear ossicles?

      Answer: 22

      Eight cranial and fourteen facial.

    1. Most strokes are caused by?

      Answer: A blocked artery (ischaemic)

      Roughly 8 in 10 are ischaemic.

      Answer: 1 in 4

  4. #11 · Wednesday 28 October 2026 Medium

    Theme: Hormones

    1. Which gland is called the ‘master gland’?

      Answer: Pituitary

      The pituitary, a pea-sized gland under the brain, sends hormones that boss around the thyroid, adrenals, ovaries and testes.

    2. Graves’ disease causes?

      Answer: An overactive thyroid

      Often with bulging eyes.

    3. HbA1c reflects average blood glucose over roughly?

      Answer: 3 months

      About the life of a red cell.

    4. Hot flushes around menopause are mainly driven by falling levels of?

      Answer: Oestrogen

      Most women get them at some point. Hormone therapy and non-hormonal options both help.

    5. Too much growth hormone in adults causes?

      Answer: Acromegaly

      In children, before growth plates close, it causes gigantism.

    6. In diabetes, hypoglycaemia is usually treated when blood glucose drops below?

      Answer: 4 mmol/L (about 70 mg/dL)

      ‘Four is the floor.’

    7. Diabetes insipidus is a problem with which hormone?

      Answer: ADH (vasopressin)

      Lots of dilute urine. Nothing to do with sugar.

    8. Addison’s disease is failure of the?

      Answer: Adrenal cortex

      In Addison’s disease the adrenal cortex fails, so the body runs short of cortisol and aldosterone.

    9. Iodine deficiency can cause a swollen thyroid called a?

      Answer: Goitre

      Without enough iodine the thyroid can’t make its hormones, so it enlarges trying to keep up. Iodised salt has made goitre far rarer.

    10. Which hormone peaks around waking and follows a daily rhythm?

      Answer: Cortisol

      Melatonin does the opposite, rising at night.

      Answer: Optogenetics

      Answer: Electrical activity of the brain

      An EEG uses scalp electrodes to record the brain’s electrical activity, and is key for diagnosing epilepsy.

    9. Nerve cells pass a signal to the next cell using?

      Answer: Neurotransmitters

      Chemicals such as dopamine and serotonin.

    10. Today, optogenetics is mainly?

      Answer: A research tool

      Early human trials exist, including one where a man with retinitis pigmentosa regained partial sight. Still experimental.

  7. #8 · Sunday 25 October 2026 Genius
