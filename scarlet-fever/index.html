Scarlet Fever

A strep throat with a rough, red rash. It sounds old-fashioned, but it’s still around and easily treated with penicillin. Finishing the full course matters, to protect against rheumatic fever.

What it is

  • An infection with group A streptococcus (strep), the same bacteria that causes strep throat. Most common in children aged 5 to 15.
  • Fever, sore red throat, swollen neck glands, and a red bumpy strawberry tongue.
  • A red rash that feels like sandpaper starts on the neck and chest and spreads, usually within 1 to 2 days. It fades after 3 to 4 days, and skin may peel, especially on the hands and feet.

Treatment

  • See your GP. A throat swab can confirm it.
  • Penicillin for 10 days is usual. Children often feel better within 2 days, but finish the whole course.
  • Paracetamol or ibuprofen for fever and sore throat, and plenty of fluids.

Back to school

  • Children can return once they’ve had at least 2 days of antibiotics (some guidance says 24 hours) and feel well enough. Check your school’s policy.

Why finishing antibiotics matters

  • Untreated strep can lead to acute rheumatic fever, which can damage the heart (rheumatic heart disease). It’s rare in most of Australia but still far too common in some Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities.
  • Rarely, strep spreads into the blood or deep tissues (invasive group A strep). Get help urgently for a child who is very drowsy, has severe pain, difficulty breathing, or a rapidly spreading red, painful patch of skin.

Common questions

Is scarlet fever serious?

Usually not when treated. Antibiotics clear it quickly. Finishing the full course helps prevent rare complications like rheumatic fever.

How long is scarlet fever contagious?

Until about a day or two after starting antibiotics. Children can usually return to school after at least 2 days of treatment if they feel well.

What does the scarlet fever rash feel like?

Rough, like sandpaper. It often starts on the neck and chest, and the skin may peel as it fades.

Can adults get scarlet fever?

Yes, but it’s much more common in school-aged children.
