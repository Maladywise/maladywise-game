Fact sheet

Cough and Acute Bronchitis

Most coughs come from colds and chest viruses, and acute bronchitis is basically a chest cold. The cough often hangs around for about 3 weeks, which is normal and annoying. Antibiotics rarely help.

The cough ladder

Most coughs need time, honey and fluids. See a doctor if it’s dragging past 3 weeks or you have warning signs. Antibiotics are for pneumonia, not chest colds.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careHoney, fluids and restA spoonful of honey (straight or in a warm drink), plenty of fluids, rest, and no smoke.
    Works
    In a 2020 review of 14 trials, honey eased how often and how badly people coughed more than usual care. In children it beat a dummy treatment for up to 3 nights
    Cost
    About $5–10
    How long
    As needed
    Watch for
    No honey for babies under 12 months.

    Cheap, tasty and actually evidence-based. Rare combo.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacyParacetamol or ibuprofenFor fever, aches and a sore chest from coughing.
    Works
    Ease fever and aches; they don’t stop the cough
    Cost
    About $3–10
    How long
    A few days
    Watch for
    Ibuprofen: avoid with stomach ulcers, kidney disease or some heart conditions. No aspirin for under 16s.

    Won’t fix the cough. Will fix the misery.

  3. Step 3 · PharmacyCough medicines (adults)Such as dextromethorphan (to quieten a dry cough) or guaifenesin (to loosen phlegm).
    Works
    Mixed results: a Cochrane review found no good evidence for or against them. Reasonable to try if they help you
    Cost
    About $10–20
    How long
    A few days
    Watch for
    Not for children under 6 in Australia; ages 6–11 only on advice from a doctor, pharmacist or nurse. Some cause drowsiness.

    Read the label. Some “night-time” mixes are an antihistamine in a trench coat.

  4. Step 4 · GPGP checkListens to your chest and checks for pneumonia, asthma, whooping cough or other causes.
    Works
    Not a trial question: finds the coughs that need something different
    Cost
    GP visit often bulk billed or about $40–80 gap
    How long
    One visit; sometimes a chest X-ray or breathing test
    Watch for
    Mention smoking, vaping, reflux, hay fever and any blood pressure tablets.

    Three weeks of coughing earns you a stethoscope.

  5. Step 5 · PrescriptionAntibiotics, only when they’ll helpFor pneumonia or another bacterial infection, or for some people at higher risk.
    Works
    For acute bronchitis they shorten the cough by only about half a day, and about 1 in 24 people get side effects. For pneumonia they’re essential
    Cost
    Under the $25 PBS co-payment
    How long
    Usually 5 days, as prescribed
    Watch for
    Diarrhoea, rash, thrush, and more resistant bugs next time.

    The right tool for pneumonia. A blunt one for a chest cold.

  6. Step 6 · SpecialistLung specialist (respiratory physician)For a cough lasting more than 8 weeks without a clear cause, or abnormal tests.
    Works
    Not a trial question: tests for asthma, lung disease and other causes
    Cost
    Specialist fees vary; Medicare rebates with a GP referral
    How long
    Waits can be weeks
    Watch for
    Keep a cough diary: triggers, time of day, phlegm.

    Detective work for lungs.

Costs are rough Australian prices in November 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials, not promises.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Antibiotics for a chest cold (acute bronchitis)About half a day less coughing, with side effects for some. Not worth it for most people.
  • Asthma reliever inhalers if you don’t have asthma or wheezeIn adults without narrowed airways they didn’t ease cough, and about half got shaky or tremulous.
  • Cough and cold medicines for children under 6Little evidence they help and a risk of harm, so they shouldn’t be used in this age group.
  • Antihistamine cough syrupsNo better than a dummy treatment for cough in trials, and they can cause drowsiness.

What you can do

  • Take honey in a warm drink (for anyone over 12 months).
  • Drink plenty, rest, and give it time. A cough lasting 3 weeks after a cold is common.
  • Stop smoking or vaping. See our quit smoking page.
  • Cover your cough, wash your hands, and stay home while you have a fever.
  • Keep vaccines up to date, including flu, COVID-19 and whooping cough, especially in pregnancy and around babies.

See your GP if

  • A cough lasting more than 3 weeks, or not improving
  • Coughing up blood or blood-stained phlegm
  • Chest pain when breathing or coughing, or feeling short of breath
  • You’re over 65, pregnant, or have a long-term heart, lung or kidney condition, diabetes or a weak immune system, and feel unwell
  • Coughing fits ending in a whoop or vomiting
  • You smoke and your cough has changed, or you’re losing weight without trying

Call 000 or your local emergency number if someone is struggling to breathe or can’t speak in full sentences, has blue, grey or very pale lips or skin, is suddenly confused, or is a baby who is floppy or can’t be woken.

Common questions

What’s the difference between bronchitis and pneumonia?

Acute bronchitis is inflammation of the airways, usually from a virus, with a cough that can last weeks but normal breathing otherwise. Pneumonia is infection in the lung tissue itself: you’re usually more unwell, with fever, fast or difficult breathing and sometimes chest pain.

Why does my cough last so long?

Airways stay irritated after the virus has gone. A cough after a chest infection usually improves within 3–4 weeks without antibiotics.

Does green phlegm mean I need antibiotics?

Not on its own. Yellow or green phlegm is common with viral infections too. Your doctor decides based on how unwell you are and what they hear in your chest.

How is the cause of a cough diagnosed?

Most short-term coughs are diagnosed from symptoms and a chest check. A cough lasting more than 3 weeks may need a chest X-ray, breathing tests or other tests.

What causes a long-lasting cough?

Common causes are asthma, reflux, postnasal drip from hay fever or sinus problems, smoking, and some blood pressure medicines (ACE inhibitors). Less often, it’s whooping cough, lung disease or cancer, which is why a cough over 3 weeks deserves a check.

How do I stop coughing at night?

A teaspoon of honey in warm water or on its own (not for babies under 1) can ease night coughing a little. Raising your head on an extra pillow, warm drinks and avoiding smoke may also help. A cough that wakes you every night for weeks should be checked.

Do cough medicines work?

There’s little good evidence that over-the-counter cough medicines help much. In Australia they shouldn’t be given to children under 6, and children aged 6 to 11 should only have them on advice from a doctor, pharmacist or nurse practitioner.

When should I worry about a cough?

See a doctor promptly if you cough up blood, are short of breath, have chest pain or high fever, or are losing weight. A cough that lasts more than 3 weeks, or any persistent cough in a smoker, should also be checked.
