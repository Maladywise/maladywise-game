Fact sheet

Rosacea

Flushing, redness, little visible blood vessels and sometimes pimple-like bumps. Common after 30, not contagious, not from poor hygiene, and very manageable.

The rosacea ladder

Start with sun protection and gentle skin care. Creams take 8 to 12 weeks, so judge them by the calendar, not the mirror on day 3.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careSunscreen, gentle skin care and trigger-spottingSPF 50+ every day, soap-free cleanser, lukewarm water. A diary to find your triggers.
    Works
    Not tested in trials, but reduces flares for many; the foundation for every other step
    Cost
    About $10–25 for sunscreen and cleanser
    How long
    Every day, for good
    Watch for
    Scrubs, toners and alcohol-based products: they sting

    Your 12-step skincare routine is 10 steps too many for rosacea.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacyAzelaic acid gelCalms bumps and some redness. Available from the pharmacy.
    Works
    Bumps fall about 50–60% vs 40% on plain gel in trials; for mild to moderate bumps
    Cost
    About $25–35 a tube
    How long
    8–12 weeks to judge
    Watch for
    Stinging and itching for the first few weeks; usually settles

    The sting is temporary. The temptation to quit in week 1 is the real enemy.

  3. Step 3 · PrescriptionIvermectin or metronidazole creamOnce- or twice-daily creams for bumps and pimples. Ivermectin is often the most effective.
    Works
    Ivermectin clears or nearly clears bumps in about 4 in 10 by 12 weeks (vs 1–2 in 10 on plain cream)
    Cost
    Private script: about $60–65 (ivermectin) or $30–50 (metronidazole) a tube
    How long
    8–12 weeks to judge; often used long term
    Watch for
    Mild burning, dryness. A brief flare when starting ivermectin

    Not a quick fix, but the steadiest rung on the ladder.

  4. Step 4 · PrescriptionLow-dose doxycycline tabletsFor more widespread bumps, often with a cream.
    Works
    Bumps roughly halve by 16 weeks (vs about a quarter on a dummy treatment)
    Cost
    Under the $25 PBS co-payment, about $20–25 a month
    How long
    A few months, then step back to a cream
    Watch for
    Sun sensitivity, heartburn (take upright with water). Not in pregnancy or under 8

    Treats bumps, not redness. Redness needs a different rung.

  5. Step 5 · PrescriptionBrimonidine gel for rednessShrinks blood vessels to reduce redness for about 12 hours.
    Works
    Big drop in redness on the day in 1 in 4 to 1 in 3 users (vs 1 in 10 on plain gel)
    Cost
    Private script, about $60–90 a tube
    How long
    On the day; best kept for occasions
    Watch for
    Rebound redness when it wears off, sometimes worse than before

    A party trick, not a daily driver.

  6. Step 6 · ProcedureVascular laser or IPLThe best option for persistent redness and visible blood vessels.
    Works
    Clear benefit in small trials; often reduces redness and visible vessels after a few sessions
    Cost
    About $200–500 a session; usually no Medicare rebate
    How long
    Usually 3–4 sessions; touch-ups as redness returns
    Watch for
    Bruising, swelling, short-term redness. Rarely blistering or skin colour change

    The one thing that actually tackles stubborn redness, at a price.

  7. Step 7 · SpecialistDermatologist treatmentsLow-dose isotretinoin for stubborn bumps, or laser or surgery for a thickened nose.
    Works
    Low-dose isotretinoin: about 6 in 10 get bumps 90% cleared (vs 1 in 10 on a dummy treatment)
    Cost
    Private script, about $30–80 a month, plus specialist fees
    How long
    Months, with regular check-ups
    Watch for
    Dry lips and skin. Isotretinoin causes severe birth defects: no pregnancy during and for 1 month after

    Big guns, used carefully.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by pharmacy and clinic. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials: your cheeks didn’t read them.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Steroid creams on the faceThey make rosacea much worse, especially when stopped. Can cause harm.
  • Scrubs, peels and exfoliating acidsRosacea skin is already irritated, and scrubbing makes it angrier. No evidence they help.
  • Tea tree oilSmall, low-quality studies, and it often irritates sensitive skin. Essential oils are not essential.
  • Apple cider vinegar tonersNo evidence, and acid on inflamed skin stings for a reason. Keep it in the salad.

What you can do

  • Common triggers: sun, heat, hot drinks, alcohol, spicy food, exercise and stress. You don’t need to avoid them all, just yours.
  • Mineral (zinc or titanium) sunscreens are often better tolerated.
  • Green-tinted make-up hides redness.
  • Keep cool: sip cold water and use a fan when exercising.

See your GP if

  • Redness or bumps aren’t controlled by sun protection and gentle skin care
  • Your eyes are often red, gritty or sore (warm compresses and lid cleaning help; drops if needed)
  • The skin on your nose is thickening
  • It’s affecting your confidence or mood

See a doctor the same day for a painful red eye, sensitivity to light or blurred vision, as rosacea can damage the eye.

Common questions

What triggers rosacea flares?

Common triggers are sun, heat, hot drinks, alcohol, spicy food, exercise and stress. A diary helps find yours.

What is the best cream for rosacea?

For bumps and pimples, prescription ivermectin cream is often the most effective. Azelaic acid (from the pharmacy) or metronidazole also help. Allow 8 to 12 weeks.

What helps rosacea redness?

Vascular laser or intense pulsed light (IPL) is the best option for persistent redness and visible blood vessels. Brimonidine gel reduces redness for about 12 hours, but redness can rebound.

Can rosacea affect the eyes?

Yes, about half of people get dry, gritty or sore eyes. See a doctor the same day for a painful red eye, sensitivity to light or blurred vision.
