Fact sheet

Carpal Tunnel Syndrome

A squeezed nerve at the wrist causing tingling, numbness and pain in the thumb and first fingers, often waking you at night. Splints and injections help many; surgery usually fixes it for good.

The carpal tunnel ladder

Mild symptoms often respond to steps 1–3. Constant numbness or a weak, wasting thumb means moving up the ladder sooner, before nerve damage becomes permanent.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careEase off what sets it offCut down on repeated wrist bending, hard gripping and vibrating tools; take regular breaks and keep wrists straight at the keyboard.
    Works
    Helps symptoms settle; no trials in numbers. Carpal tunnel in pregnancy usually goes after the birth
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    Ongoing
    Watch for
    If you work with vibrating tools or repetitive gripping, talk to your workplace about changes.

    Your wrists aren’t lazy. They’re overbooked.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacyNight-time wrist splintA brace that holds the wrist straight while you sleep, so the nerve isn’t squashed by bent wrists.
    Works
    Improves symptoms and hand function within weeks in trials
    Cost
    About $25–50
    How long
    Every night for up to 6 weeks to see if it helps
    Watch for
    It needs to keep the wrist straight, not bent back. A pharmacist or hand therapist can check the fit.

    The world’s least glamorous nightwear. Also one of the most useful.

  3. Step 3 · PrescriptionSteroid injectionA single injection into the wrist to reduce swelling around the nerve.
    Works
    In a UK trial, one injection beat a night splint at 6 weeks and matched it at 6 months
    Cost
    Medicare rebates; out-of-pocket fees vary
    How long
    One injection; relief often lasts months, and symptoms may come back
    Watch for
    Short-lived soreness, skin thinning or paler skin at the site, and raised blood sugar for a few days in diabetes.

    Often the quickest relief. Not always the last word.

  4. Step 4 · SpecialistCarpal tunnel release surgeryA day procedure under local anaesthetic to cut the ligament roofing the tunnel and free the nerve.
    Works
    Better than splinting at 3 months and at a year in trials; usually cures it
    Cost
    Public waiting lists; private fees vary, with Medicare rebates
    How long
    About 20 minutes; around a month to get back to normal activities
    Watch for
    A tender scar and wrist stiffness for a while; infection is less common. Severe, long-standing numbness may not fully recover.

    Small cut, big relief, for most. Don’t wait until the thumb muscle has gone.

Costs are rough Australian prices in November 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials, not promises.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Vitamin B6Small trials found no clear benefit for symptoms. And too much B6 can itself damage nerves.
  • Anti-inflammatory tablets and fluid tablets (diuretics)No better than a dummy pill for carpal tunnel in trials. Fine for short-term pain, but they don’t treat the squeezed nerve.
  • Magnets and laser acupunctureNo better than placebo or control in trials.
  • Special keyboards and miceTrials give mixed results. Good posture and breaks are free.

What you can do

  • Wear a wrist splint at night for up to 6 weeks and keep wrists straight, not curled under your pillow.
  • Shake or move your hands when tingling wakes you, then reset the splint.
  • Try gentle hand and wrist exercises if they help you; the evidence is limited, but they’re safe.
  • Keep a healthy weight and get diabetes or thyroid problems checked and treated; they raise the risk.
  • Pregnant? Splints are the main treatment, and symptoms usually settle after the birth.

See your GP if

  • Symptoms are getting worse, or not improving after 6 weeks of splinting
  • Numbness is constant, rather than coming and going
  • You’re dropping things, or your grip or thumb is weak
  • The muscle at the base of your thumb looks flatter or thinner

See a doctor the same day for sudden severe numbness or weakness after a wrist injury or fracture. Get urgent help for sudden weakness or numbness of the face, arm or leg, or trouble speaking, which can be a stroke: call 000 (Australia) or your local emergency number. From a mobile, 112 works in most countries.

Common questions

What are the symptoms?

Tingling, numbness, pain or burning in the thumb, index, middle and half of the ring finger, often worse at night or first thing in the morning. Later, grip can weaken and you may drop things. It can affect both hands.

What causes it?

Swelling in the tight tunnel at the wrist squeezes the median nerve. Risks include pregnancy, being overweight, diabetes, an underactive thyroid, arthritis, wrist injury, repetitive gripping or vibrating tools, and having close family with it. It’s most common in women aged 40–60.

How is it diagnosed?

Usually from your symptoms and an examination of your hand. If it’s unclear or surgery is being considered, a nerve conduction test measures how well the nerve is working, and blood tests may check for diabetes or thyroid problems.

Could it be my neck?

Sometimes. A pinched nerve in the neck can cause similar tingling, usually with neck pain or symptoms further up the arm. Your GP can check.

Does carpal tunnel go away on its own?

Sometimes. Mild symptoms settle in roughly a third of people over a year or two, especially if triggers ease. Carpal tunnel in pregnancy usually improves within weeks to months after the birth. Ongoing numbness or weakness needs treatment, because long-term nerve pressure can cause lasting damage.

Why is carpal tunnel worse at night?

Many people sleep with their wrists bent, which raises pressure on the nerve, and fluid shifts overnight add to it. A wrist splint that keeps the wrist straight at night eases symptoms for many people within a few weeks.

How long does carpal tunnel surgery take to recover from?

Most people use the hand for light tasks within days. Desk work is often possible after 1 to 2 weeks and heavy manual work after about 4 to 6 weeks. Night tingling usually improves quickly, while grip strength takes around 3 months to return.
