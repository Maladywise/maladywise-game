Asthma Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, following the Australian Asthma Handbook: what happens in the airways, which puffer does what, asthma first aid and when to call 000.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What happens in the airways during an asthma flare?

    1. They swell, tighten and fill with mucus
    2. They fill with blood
    3. They collapse permanently
    4. They become infected every time
    Show answer

    Answer: They swell, tighten and fill with mucus. Inflammation, muscle tightening (bronchoconstriction) and extra mucus narrow the airways. That’s why preventers treat inflammation and relievers relax the muscle.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    Which medicine is the classic ‘blue puffer’ reliever?

    1. Prednisolone
    2. Salbutamol
    3. Fluticasone
    4. Montelukast
    Show answer

    Answer: Salbutamol. Salbutamol relaxes airway muscle within minutes. It doesn’t treat the underlying inflammation.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    What is asthma first aid in Australia?

    1. Wait 30 minutes before doing anything
    2. 1 big puff and lie flat
    3. 4 separate puffs of reliever via a spacer, wait 4 minutes, repeat if needed
    4. Breathe into a paper bag
    Show answer

    Answer: 4 separate puffs of reliever via a spacer, wait 4 minutes, repeat if needed. One puff at a time, 4 breaths after each. If no better after two rounds, or at any time it’s severe, call 000 and keep giving 4 puffs every 4 minutes.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    Needing a reliever on more than how many days a week suggests poorly controlled asthma?

    1. Every day is normal
    2. 5 days a week
    3. Only if it’s at night
    4. 2 days a week
    Show answer

    Answer: 2 days a week. Regular reliever use is a warning sign. It usually means more preventer is needed, not more blue puffer.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    Why is relying on a blue reliever alone risky?

    1. It damages the heart in any dose
    2. It stops working after a month
    3. It eases symptoms but doesn’t treat the inflammation
    4. It’s addictive
    Show answer

    Answer: It eases symptoms but doesn’t treat the inflammation. Relying on reliever alone is linked to more severe attacks. Most adults now use an inhaled steroid-containing preventer or a combined preventer-reliever.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    Which test confirms asthma?

    1. Spirometry before and after a reliever
    2. ECG
    3. Chest X-ray
    4. Blood test for IgE
    Show answer

    Answer: Spirometry before and after a reliever. Spirometry shows narrowing that improves after a bronchodilator. A normal X-ray doesn’t rule asthma out.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    What does a spacer do?

    1. Only helps children
    2. Gets more medicine into the lungs and less in the mouth
    3. Replaces the need for a preventer
    4. Makes the medicine stronger
    Show answer

    Answer: Gets more medicine into the lungs and less in the mouth. A puffer plus spacer works as well as a nebuliser in most flares, even for adults.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    Why rinse your mouth after using a preventer?

    1. It’s not needed
    2. To stop it working too fast
    3. To protect your teeth from the blue colour
    4. To prevent oral thrush and a hoarse voice
    Show answer

    Answer: To prevent oral thrush and a hoarse voice. Inhaled steroids left in the mouth can cause thrush. Rinse, gargle and spit.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    What caused the 2016 Melbourne thunderstorm asthma event?

    1. Smoke from bushfires
    2. Mould in air conditioners
    3. Ryegrass pollen broken up by storm winds and rain
    4. A chemical leak
    Show answer

    Answer: Ryegrass pollen broken up by storm winds and rain. Tiny pollen fragments got deep into the lungs. Many people affected had hay fever and no asthma diagnosis. Grass pollen season (October to December) is the risk time.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    Which sign means call 000 straight away?

    1. Can’t speak in full sentences, or blue lips
    2. Wheeze after running
    3. A mild cough at night
    4. Needing a reliever once today
    Show answer

    Answer: Can’t speak in full sentences, or blue lips. Also: drowsiness, a quiet chest, or symptoms getting worse fast. Keep giving reliever while you wait.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    Which common painkillers can trigger asthma in some adults?

    1. Lidocaine patches
    2. Aspirin and other anti-inflammatories (NSAIDs)
    3. Paracetamol
    4. Codeine
    Show answer

    Answer: Aspirin and other anti-inflammatories (NSAIDs). About 1 in 14 adults with asthma react to NSAIDs, often with nasal polyps too. Paracetamol is usually safe.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    What should everyone with asthma have?

    1. An annual chest X-ray
    2. A home oxygen tank
    3. Antibiotics on standby
    4. A written asthma action plan
    Show answer

    Answer: A written asthma action plan. It says what to take every day, how to spot a flare and what to do in an emergency. Ask your GP to update it each year.

Common questions

Can adults get asthma for the first time?

Yes. Asthma can start at any age, sometimes after a chest infection or with workplace triggers.

Is asthma first aid the same for children?

Yes, 4 separate puffs via a spacer, 4 breaths per puff, wait 4 minutes, repeat. Use a mask with the spacer for young children.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. Follow your own asthma action plan and see your GP to review it.
