Fact sheet

Prediabetes

Blood sugar that’s higher than normal but not high enough to be diabetes. About 1 in 3 people with prediabetes develop type 2 diabetes within 10 years. The good news: modest weight loss and more movement can roughly halve that risk.

Symptoms

  • Usually none. It’s found on a blood test, often during a check-up.
  • If you have thirst, frequent urination or repeated infections, get tested for diabetes itself.

Causes and risk factors

  • The same things that lead to type 2 diabetes: family history, getting older, extra weight around the waist and not moving much.
  • Diabetes in a past pregnancy, or polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS).
  • Higher rates in some communities, including Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander, Pacific Islander and South Asian people.

How it’s diagnosed

  • HbA1c (3-month sugar average) of 6.0–6.4%.
  • Or a fasting glucose of 6.1–6.9 mmol/L (impaired fasting glucose).
  • Or a 2-hour result of 7.8–11.0 mmol/L on a glucose tolerance test (impaired glucose tolerance).
  • In Australia, the AUSDRISK questionnaire estimates your 5-year diabetes risk; a high score means it’s time for a blood test.
  • Once you know you have prediabetes, recheck HbA1c every year.

The prediabetes ladder

Lifestyle change is the main treatment and beats tablets in trials. Medicines are an add-on for some people, not a shortcut.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careLose 5–10% of your weight and move moreAim for 150–300 minutes a week of moderate activity plus some strength work, and sit less. For a 90 kg person, 5–10% is 4.5–9 kg.
    Works
    Losing 5–10% of body weight halves the risk of developing type 2 diabetes
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    Ongoing
    Watch for
    Crash diets rebound. Slow and sustainable wins.

    You don’t need a new body. You need about 5% less of the current one.

  2. Step 2 · ProgramA structured lifestyle programGroup or online coaching on food, activity and habits over months, often with a health coach or dietitian.
    Works
    In the US Diabetes Prevention Program, intensive lifestyle coaching cut new diabetes by 58% over about 3 years
    Cost
    Some programs are free or subsidised; costs vary; dietitian visits rebated with a GP care plan
    How long
    Usually 6–12 months
    Watch for
    Ask your GP what’s available locally; options differ by state and country.

    Accountability is the secret ingredient. Turns out we behave better when someone checks.

  3. Step 3 · PrescriptionMetforminA diabetes tablet sometimes used to prevent diabetes in people at highest risk (off-label in Australia).
    Works
    Cut new diabetes by 31% in the same trial: helpful, but less than lifestyle change
    Cost
    Under the $25 PBS co-payment; $7.70 concession
    How long
    Long term, with yearly checks
    Watch for
    Nausea and diarrhoea; take with food. Low B12 over years.

    The understudy. Good, but lifestyle is still the lead.

  4. Step 4 · PrescriptionWeight-loss medicines (GLP-1 medicines)Weekly injections that reduce appetite, for people with obesity or extra weight plus a related condition.
    Works
    Average weight loss of about 8–15% or more over 1 to 1.5 years, depending on the medicine; weight loss lowers diabetes risk
    Cost
    Not on the PBS for weight loss, so full private price
    How long
    Long term; weight usually returns after stopping
    Watch for
    Nausea, vomiting, constipation, gallstones.

    A helpful tool for some, not a substitute for the basics.

  5. Step 5 · SpecialistWeight-loss (bariatric) surgeryFor people with severe obesity, after other approaches.
    Works
    Prevents progression to diabetes better than usual care in studies
    Cost
    Free in the public system (long waits); private costs often run to thousands
    How long
    One operation, lifelong follow-up
    Watch for
    Needs lifelong vitamin supplements and follow-up.

    A big intervention for a big problem. Not the first rung.

Costs are rough Australian prices in November 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials, not promises.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Home fingerprick testingNot recommended for prediabetes: sugar rarely goes too low, and a yearly HbA1c tells you more. Save the strips.
  • Blood-sugar “balancing” supplementsNo supplement or tablet is approved to prevent diabetes. Walking is free and has the trials.
  • Waiting to see what happensAbout a third progress within 10 years. The window to change course is now.

What you can do

  • Pick one change you can keep: a daily walk, water instead of soft drink, smaller portions.
  • Ask your GP about a structured lifestyle program near you.
  • Recheck HbA1c every year, and know your blood pressure and cholesterol too: prediabetes raises heart risk.
  • Sleep counts: poor sleep makes weight and sugar harder to manage. See trouble sleeping.
  • If you smoke, quitting helps your heart and your sugar.

See your GP if

  • You score high on the AUSDRISK test
  • You had diabetes in pregnancy, or have PCOS and haven’t had a sugar test lately
  • Your HbA1c hasn’t been rechecked in over a year
  • You have symptoms such as thirst, frequent urination or repeated infections

See a doctor promptly if you’re very thirsty, passing lots of urine, losing weight without trying, or feeling very unwell: that may be diabetes rather than prediabetes.

Common questions

Is prediabetes reversible?

Often, yes. Many people bring their sugar back into the normal range with weight loss and activity, and lower their risk of diabetes even if it doesn’t fully normalise.

How much weight do I need to lose?

Around 5 to 10% of your body weight. That’s enough to halve the risk in studies.

Do I need to cut out carbs completely?

No. Cutting sugary drinks, refined snacks and portion sizes, and choosing high-fibre carbs, matters more than strict rules you can’t keep.

Can I get NDSS supplies for prediabetes?

No. The NDSS is for people diagnosed with diabetes. People with prediabetes aren’t eligible.

What are the symptoms of prediabetes?

Usually none, which is why it’s usually found on a routine blood test. Risk factors include being over 40, a family history of diabetes, extra weight around the waist, high blood pressure, past gestational diabetes and PCOS.

What HbA1c level is prediabetes?

In Australia, an HbA1c of 6.0 to 6.4% (42 to 46 mmol/mol) suggests prediabetes, and 6.5% or more suggests diabetes. Fasting glucose of 6.1 to 6.9 mmol/L or a borderline glucose tolerance test can also show it.

How likely is prediabetes to turn into diabetes?

Without changes, a large share of people with prediabetes develop type 2 diabetes within about 10 years. In a major trial, a lifestyle program with modest weight loss and 150 minutes of activity a week cut the risk by 58% over 3 years.
