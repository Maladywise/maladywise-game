Trendelenburg sign

Trendelenburg sign points to weak hip abductors (gluteus medius) on the standing side.

How it’s done
Standing on one leg, the pelvis drops on the other side.
What a positive result points to
Weak hip abductors (gluteus medius) on the standing side
Who it’s named after
Friedrich Trendelenburg, a German surgeon, in 1895. He has a position named after him too.
Good to know
Weak hip abductors (gluteus medius) on the standing leg let the opposite hip drop.

How accurate is it?

In studies, it picks up about 73% of people with a gluteal tendon problem (outer hip pain), and correctly clears about 77% of people without it. Respectable both ways, from one small study. Not bad for standing on one leg.

Source: BestBETs: clinical tests for greater trochanteric pain
