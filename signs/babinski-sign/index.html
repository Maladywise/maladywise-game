Babinski sign

Babinski sign points to an upper motor neurone lesion (normal in babies).

How it’s done
Stroking the sole makes the big toe go up and the other toes fan out.
What a positive result points to
An upper motor neurone lesion (normal in babies)
Who it’s named after
Joseph Babinski, a French neurologist, in 1896.
Good to know
Normal in babies up to about age 1 to 2, before the nerve pathways finish maturing.

How accurate is it?

In studies, it picks up about 51% of people with damage to the brain or spinal cord’s movement wiring, and correctly clears about 99% of people without it. When it’s there, believe it. When it’s not, shrug: it missed about half.

Source: Accuracy of the Babinski sign, 2014 (J Neurol Sci abstract)
