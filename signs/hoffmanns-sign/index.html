Hoffmann’s sign

Hoffmann’s sign points to upper motor neurone lesion, e.g. cervical cord compression.

How it’s done
Flicking the nail of the middle finger makes the thumb and index finger curl.
What a positive result points to
Upper motor neurone lesion, e.g. cervical cord compression
Who it’s named after
Johann Hoffmann, a German neurologist, in the late 1800s.
Good to know
The upper-limb cousin of the Babinski sign; MRI of the neck confirms cord compression.

How accurate is it?

In studies, it picks up about 59% of people with cervical myelopathy (spinal cord squeezed in the neck), and correctly clears about 84% of people without it. Misses about half of people with cord compression, and some healthy people have it. A clue, not a verdict: MRI decides.

Source: Jiang et al. 2023, Global Spine Journal: clinical signs in degenerative cervical myelopathy (systematic review)
