Hoover’s sign

Hoover’s sign points to functional (non-organic) leg weakness.

How it’s done
A ‘paralysed’ leg pushes down firmly when the patient lifts the good leg against resistance.
What a positive result points to
Functional (non-organic) leg weakness
Who it’s named after
Charles Franklin Hoover, an American physician, in 1908.
Good to know
Shows the leg can work, which helps explain functional weakness to patients. It isn’t a test for faking.

How accurate is it?

In studies, it picks up about 63% of people with functional (non-organic) leg weakness, and correctly clears about 100% of people without it. When it’s positive, it’s very convincing. When it’s negative, it proves little. And it never means the person is faking.

Source: McWhirter et al. 2011, J Psychosom Res (prospective cohort, suspected stroke)
