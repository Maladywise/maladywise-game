Homans’ sign

Homans’ sign points to classically DVT, but it is unreliable and no longer recommended.

How it’s done
Calf pain when the ankle is bent upwards.
What a positive result points to
Classically DVT, but it is unreliable and no longer recommended
Who it’s named after
John Homans, a Boston surgeon, in 1941. He later warned against relying on it himself.
Good to know
Poorly accurate and largely abandoned. Use the Wells score, D-dimer and ultrasound.

How accurate is it?

In studies, it picks up about 32% of people with a clot in the leg (DVT). Old-school and unreliable: it shows up in anywhere from under 1 in 10 to about half of people with a clot. More than half of people without a clot have it too. Scores, D-dimer and ultrasound took over.

Source: GPnotebook: Homans’ sign (citing Joshua 2005, Intern Med J)
