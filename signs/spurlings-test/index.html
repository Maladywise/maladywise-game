Spurling’s test

Spurling’s test points to cervical nerve root compression (radiculopathy).

How accurate is it?
50%picks up94%correctly clears

In studies, it picks up about 50% of people with a pinched nerve in the neck (cervical radiculopathy), and correctly clears about 94% of people without it. A classic rule-in test: a positive result is strong evidence. A negative one doesn’t clear you.

Source: Thoomes et al. 2017, The Spine Journal (systematic review)

How it’s done
Tilting and pressing down on the extended head reproduces pain running down the arm.
What a positive result points to
Cervical nerve root compression (radiculopathy)
Who it’s named after
Roy Glenwood Spurling and William Scoville, American neurosurgeons, in 1944.
Good to know
Good for ruling in a pinched nerve; poor for ruling it out.
