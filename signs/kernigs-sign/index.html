Kernig’s sign

Kernig’s sign points to meningeal irritation, e.g. meningitis.

How it’s done
With the hip bent to 90 degrees, straightening the knee is painful and resisted.
What a positive result points to
Meningeal irritation, e.g. meningitis
Who it’s named after
Vladimir Kernig, a Russian physician, in 1882.
Good to know
Very specific but misses most cases of meningitis. A normal exam never rules it out: if in doubt, treat.

How accurate is it?

In studies, it picks up about 5% of people with meningitis, and correctly clears about 95% of people without it. Famous, and nearly useless for ruling out. It missed 19 in 20 cases. A negative Kernig’s means nothing; worry anyway.

Source: Thomas 2002 (Clin Infect Dis), summarised in American Family Physician
