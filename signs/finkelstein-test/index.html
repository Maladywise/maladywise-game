Finkelstein test

Finkelstein test points to de Quervain tenosynovitis.

How it’s done
Thumb tucked into a fist, then the wrist bent towards the little finger: sharp pain at the thumb side of the wrist.
What a positive result points to
De Quervain tenosynovitis
Who it’s named after
Harry Finkelstein, a New York surgeon, in 1930.
Good to know
De Quervain’s is common in new parents from lifting babies (‘mummy’s thumb’).
