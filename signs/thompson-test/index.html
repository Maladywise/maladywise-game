Thompson test

Thompson test points to achilles tendon rupture.

How it’s done
Squeeze the calf of a patient lying face down: the foot doesn’t point down.
What a positive result points to
Achilles tendon rupture
Who it’s named after
Franklin Simmonds described it in 1957, Thompson and Doherty in 1962. Also called the Simmonds–Thompson test.
Good to know
Patients can often still point their foot weakly using other muscles, so don’t rely on that alone.

How accurate is it?

In studies, it picks up about 96% of people with a torn Achilles tendon, and correctly clears about 93% of people without it. A ten-second squeeze that almost never gets it wrong. Rare for a bedside test.
