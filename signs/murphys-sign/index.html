Murphy’s sign

Murphy’s sign points to acute cholecystitis.

How it’s done
Press under the right ribs and ask for a deep breath: the patient stops breathing in sharply, mid-breath.
What a positive result points to
Acute cholecystitis
Who it’s named after
John Benjamin Murphy, a Chicago surgeon, around 1903. He also invented the ‘Murphy button’ for joining bowel.
Good to know
Often absent in older people, so a negative sign doesn’t rule out cholecystitis. Ultrasound confirms.

How accurate is it?

In studies, it picks up about 65% of people with an inflamed gallbladder (cholecystitis), and correctly clears about 87% of people without it. Better at ruling in than out: it misses about 1 in 3. Hence the ultrasound.

Source: Trowbridge 2003 JAMA review, teaching summary (University of Florida)
