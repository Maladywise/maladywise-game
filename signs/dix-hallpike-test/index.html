Dix–Hallpike test

Dix–Hallpike test points to benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV).

How it’s done
Lying the patient back quickly with the head turned brings on brief vertigo and a twisting eye flicker.
What a positive result points to
Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV)
Who it’s named after
Margaret Dix and Charles Hallpike, British otologists, in 1952.
Good to know
If positive, the Epley manoeuvre usually fixes posterior canal BPPV in one or two goes.

How accurate is it?

In studies, it picks up about 79% of people with BPPV (loose-crystal vertigo), and correctly clears about 75% of people without it. Good, not perfect: a negative doesn’t fully rule out BPPV. Bonus: a positive leads straight into the head-turning manoeuvre that treats it.

Source: Halker 2008 review (PubMed abstract)
