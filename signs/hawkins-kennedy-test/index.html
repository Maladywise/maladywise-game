Hawkins–Kennedy test

Hawkins–Kennedy test points to subacromial impingement.

How it’s done
With the shoulder and elbow bent to 90°, rotating the arm inwards causes shoulder pain.
What a positive result points to
Subacromial impingement
Who it’s named after
Richard Hawkins and J. C. Kennedy, Canadian orthopaedic surgeons, in 1980.
Good to know
Sensitive but not specific: many shoulder problems hurt with it.

How accurate is it?

In studies, it picks up about 79% of people with shoulder impingement, and correctly clears about 59% of people without it. Neer’s near-twin, with the same problem: lots of false alarms.

Source: Hegedus 2008 meta-analysis (Br J Sports Med abstract)
