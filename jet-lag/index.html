Fact sheet

Jet Lag

Tiredness, poor sleep, brain fog and a grumpy gut after crossing 3+ time zones: your body clock is still at home. Usually worse flying east. Settles by itself, about a day per time zone.

The jet lag ladder

Light is the main lever. Most people never need past step 3; medicines are for long eastward trips or when you must perform on arrival.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careShift your sleep before you goFor 2 to 3 days before leaving, go to bed and get up an hour earlier each day if flying east, or later if flying west.
    Works
    Not well tested in travellers; lab studies show it shifts your clock ahead of time
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    2–3 days before departure
    Watch for
    Not worth it for trips of 2 days or less: staying on home time may be easier.

    Cheaper than any “jet lag kit” sold at the airport.

  2. Step 2 · Self-careDaylight at the right timeThe strongest signal for resetting your body clock. Flew east? Get morning light. Flew west? Get afternoon and evening light.
    Works
    No good traveller trials, but light is the clock’s strongest reset signal in lab studies; the most useful single step
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    First few days at your destination
    Watch for
    Mistimed light can shift your clock the wrong way. Sunglasses help when you need to avoid it.

    Your body clock runs on sunshine, not on willpower.

  3. Step 3 · Self-careCaffeine and smart napsCaffeine for daytime alertness; naps under 30 minutes; eat and sleep on local time.
    Works
    Small benefit for daytime alertness in trials; helps you function but doesn’t reset your clock
    Cost
    The price of a coffee
    How long
    First few days
    Watch for
    No caffeine in the 6 hours before bed. Alcohol worsens sleep quality, on the plane and after.

    A three-hour “nap” at 3pm is just a second night with worse timing.

  4. Step 4 · PrescriptionMelatoninA low dose (0.5 to 5 mg) at local bedtime for a few days. Needs a prescription in Australia, except slow-release tablets from a pharmacist for people over 55.
    Works
    Reduced jet lag in 9 of 10 trials, with the biggest benefit flying east across 5 or more time zones
    Cost
    About $20–40 a pack, private script or pharmacist
    How long
    A few nights after arrival
    Watch for
    Drowsiness, headache. Don’t drive soon after a dose. Check interactions if you take blood thinners or epilepsy medicines.

    Timing beats dose: more melatonin isn’t more reset.

  5. Step 5 · PrescriptionShort-acting sleeping tabletA doctor may suggest one for a few nights on arrival.
    Works
    Small trials show longer, better sleep on arrival; doesn’t reset your body clock
    Cost
    Private script, about $15–30
    How long
    2–3 nights at most
    Watch for
    Next-day drowsiness and unsteadiness. Not with alcohol. Avoid on the plane: a deep sleep in a cramped seat raises clot risk.

    Knocks out the night, not the jet lag.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by pharmacy and clinic. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials: your body clock didn’t read the trials.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Homeopathic jet lag tabletsHomeopathy hasn’t beaten dummy treatment for any condition in reliable studies. Sugar pills with a boarding pass.
  • “Feast and fast” anti-jet-lag dietsBuilt on small, older studies and hard to stick to. Eating on local time from the first meal is simpler and free.
  • Light-therapy glasses and gadgetsThey can nudge the body clock in lab studies, but real-world jet lag trials are few and small. The sun does the same job for nothing.
  • Sedating antihistamine sleep aidsNot properly tested for jet lag, and next-day grogginess feels suspiciously like jet lag.
  • Alcohol to sleep on the planeKnocks you out, then wrecks sleep quality and dries you out. A nightcap at 35,000 feet is still a nightcap.

What you can do

  • Set your watch to destination time when you board, and sleep or stay awake to match.
  • Drink water and walk the aisle on long flights.
  • On arrival, live on local time from the first meal.
  • Avoid big decisions on day one. Ordering a third dessert counts.

See your GP if

  • You take medicines at set times, such as insulin or the pill, and need a plan for time zones
  • You’d like to discuss melatonin or sleeping tablets before you travel
  • Sleep problems last more than 2 weeks after you return

See a doctor urgently for a painful, swollen calf, chest pain or sudden breathlessness after a long flight, as these can be signs of a blood clot. Call 000 if severe.

Common questions

How long does jet lag last?

Roughly a day for each time zone crossed, a little faster flying west. It settles on its own, and daylight at the right time speeds things up.

Does melatonin help jet lag?

It reduces jet lag in most trials, especially flying east across 5 or more time zones. A low dose (0.5 to 5 mg) at local bedtime for a few days is typical. It needs a prescription in Australia, except slow-release tablets for people over 55.

Is jet lag worse flying east or west?

Usually east. After flying east, get morning daylight. After flying west, get afternoon and evening light.
