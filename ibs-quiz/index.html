IBS Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on irritable bowel syndrome: what it is (and isn’t), warning signs that need tests, and the treatments with real evidence.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What does IBS stand for?

    1. Inflammatory bowel syndrome
    2. Internal bleeding syndrome
    3. Intestinal blockage syndrome
    4. Irritable bowel syndrome
    Show answer

    Answer: Irritable bowel syndrome. Not the same as inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn’s and colitis).

  2. Question 2 of 12

    Which symptom is central to IBS?

    1. Recurring tummy pain linked to bowel habits
    2. Unexplained weight loss
    3. Fever and night sweats
    4. Vomiting blood
    Show answer

    Answer: Recurring tummy pain linked to bowel habits. Bloating, diarrhoea, constipation or both are common.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Roughly how common is IBS?

    1. Almost everyone at some point
    2. About 1 in 10,000 people
    3. About 1 in 10 to 1 in 20 people
    4. Mainly elite athletes
    Show answer

    Answer: About 1 in 10 to 1 in 20 people. Strict Rome IV criteria give about 4% worldwide; older, looser criteria give about 10%. It’s more common in women and usually starts before 50.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    How is IBS best understood now?

    1. Imaginary or exaggerated symptoms
    2. A disorder of gut–brain interaction
    3. A long-lasting bowel infection
    4. A hidden food allergy
    Show answer

    Answer: A disorder of gut–brain interaction. The gut is more sensitive and moves differently, with two-way signalling between gut and brain.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    Which symptom is a red flag needing tests?

    1. Pain relieved by a bowel motion
    2. Blood in the poo or unexplained weight loss
    3. Symptoms worse with stress
    4. Bloating after large meals
    Show answer

    Answer: Blood in the poo or unexplained weight loss. Also: new symptoms after 50, anaemia, waking at night to open bowels, or a family history of bowel cancer or IBD.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    Which blood test is recommended to rule out a common mimic?

    1. Troponin
    2. Thyroid antibodies
    3. Coeliac serology
    4. PSA
    Show answer

    Answer: Coeliac serology. Coeliac disease can look just like IBS. Calprotectin in poo helps rule out inflammation.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    Which diet has good evidence, done with a dietitian?

    1. Carnivore diet
    2. Fasting for days
    3. Cutting out all fibre
    4. Low-FODMAP, then reintroduction
    Show answer

    Answer: Low-FODMAP, then reintroduction. Developed at Monash University. It’s a short elimination then a structured reintroduction, not a lifelong diet.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    Which fibre helps IBS most?

    1. Soluble fibre such as psyllium
    2. No fibre at all
    3. Insoluble fibre supplements
    4. Wheat bran
    Show answer

    Answer: Soluble fibre such as psyllium. Psyllium helps both constipation and diarrhoea for many people; bran can worsen bloating.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    Which over-the-counter remedy has trial evidence for IBS pain?

    1. Charcoal tablets
    2. Colon cleanses
    3. Peppermint oil capsules
    4. Apple cider vinegar
    Show answer

    Answer: Peppermint oil capsules. Enteric-coated peppermint oil relaxes gut muscle.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    What is gut-directed hypnotherapy?

    1. A type of bowel surgery
    2. A psychological therapy shown to reduce IBS symptoms
    3. A colonoscopy under sedation
    4. A stage hypnosis show
    Show answer

    Answer: A psychological therapy shown to reduce IBS symptoms. Gut-directed hypnotherapy and CBT work for many people, including via apps and online programs.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    Why might a low-dose tricyclic like amitriptyline be used?

    1. It calms gut nerve pain signalling
    2. To cause constipation on purpose
    3. To treat depression only
    4. To kill bacteria
    Show answer

    Answer: It calms gut nerve pain signalling. The ATLANTIS trial showed low-dose amitriptyline helped IBS symptoms in primary care.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Can IBS start after gastro?

    1. Only in children
    2. Only after food poisoning from chicken
    3. No, never
    4. Yes, post-infection IBS is well described
    Show answer

    Answer: Yes, post-infection IBS is well described. About 1 in 10 people develop IBS after an episode of infectious gastroenteritis.

Common questions

Does IBS cause cancer?

No. IBS doesn’t raise your risk of bowel cancer, but new or changing symptoms with red flags should still be checked.

Is IBS caused by stress?

Stress can worsen symptoms, but IBS has real physical mechanisms in the gut and nerves.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. See your GP about your own symptoms or results.
