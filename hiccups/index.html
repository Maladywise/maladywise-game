Fact sheet

Hiccups

Your diaphragm having a twitchy moment, with your voice box snapping shut for the “hic”. Almost every bout stops on its own within minutes to hours. Here’s what helps, and when to worry.

The hiccups ladder

Most people never get past step 2. Steps 3 to 5 are for hiccups that keep coming back or last more than 2 days.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careBreath-hold “top-up”Breathe in fully and hold for 10 seconds. Without breathing out, sip in a little more air and hold 5 seconds, once more for 5, then breathe out slowly.
    Works
    Not tested in trials; often stops a bout in small reports
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    Under a minute; repeat once or twice
    Watch for
    Light-headedness if you overdo it. Sit down to try it.

    Cheaper than every hiccup gadget on the internet, and about as proven.

  2. Step 2 · Self-careOther harmless tricksSip or gargle ice-cold water, swallow a teaspoon of sugar, hug your knees to your chest, or bear down as if straining.
    Works
    Not tested in trials; helps some people, evidence is anecdotal
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    Minutes
    Watch for
    Skip pressing on the eyeballs: it can damage the eye. Scaring people isn’t proven either.

    Pick one and commit. All five at once is a party trick, not a treatment.

  3. Step 3 · PharmacyReflux treatment (antacid or a proton pump inhibitor such as omeprazole)For hiccups that keep coming back, reflux is a common culprit.
    Works
    No good trials for hiccups; helps when reflux is behind it, no use for a one-off bout
    Cost
    About $10–25 for a pharmacy pack
    How long
    2–4 weeks to judge
    Watch for
    Heartburn with trouble swallowing, vomiting or weight loss needs a GP, not just a tablet.

    Sometimes the hiccup is just your oesophagus lodging a complaint.

  4. Step 4 · PrescriptionMedicines for persistent hiccups (baclofen, gabapentin or metoclopramide)For hiccups lasting more than 2 days that won’t quit, once causes are looked for.
    Works
    Small trials only; helps some people, evidence is weak
    Cost
    Private script, about $15–40
    How long
    Days to a few weeks
    Watch for
    Drowsiness and dizziness: no driving until you know how you react. Metoclopramide can rarely cause muscle spasms.

    Reserved for hiccups that outstay their welcome by days, not minutes.

  5. Step 5 · SpecialistFinding the causeBloods, chest imaging or a gastroscopy for hiccups lasting weeks, to find reflux, medicine side effects or other illness.
    Works
    No trials; treating the cause usually fixes the hiccups
    Cost
    Public is free; private specialist about $150–300 a visit, part-rebated by Medicare with a referral
    How long
    Depends on the cause
    Watch for
    Weight loss, trouble swallowing or new nerve symptoms (see below).

    Rare, but weeks-long hiccups deserve detective work, not more sugar.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by pharmacy and clinic. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Evidence for hiccup cures is mostly stories: your diaphragm didn’t read them either.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Scares, eyeball pressing and other internet curesNo evidence, and pressing on the eyes can hurt them. Nobody needs a surprise scare or a thumb in the eye.
  • Breathing into a paper bagNo good evidence it beats a simple breath-hold, and it’s a bad idea with heart or lung problems.
  • Special hiccup strawsThe only evidence is a survey of users with no comparison group, and the inventor was an author. Might work; we’d like a trial that isn’t a fan club.
  • AcupunctureOnly small, low-quality studies for stubborn hiccups. Unproven, and not a reason to skip finding the cause.

What you can do

  • Eat slowly, in smaller meals. Go easy on fizzy drinks, alcohol and smoking.
  • New medicine and new hiccups? Ask your pharmacist: steroids and some sedatives can cause them.
  • If they keep recurring, note when they happen and what you ate or drank.

See your GP if

  • Hiccups last more than 2 days, or keep coming back
  • They stop you eating, sleeping or talking
  • You also have heartburn, trouble swallowing, tummy pain, vomiting or weight loss
  • You started a new medicine shortly before

Call 000 if hiccups come with chest pain, shortness of breath, sudden weakness or numbness, trouble speaking, confusion or a severe headache.

Common questions

What is the fastest way to stop hiccups?

Try the breath-hold “top-up”: breathe in fully and hold, then sip in a little more air twice before breathing out slowly. Sipping ice-cold water, swallowing a teaspoon of sugar or hugging your knees to your chest are also harmless to try.

What causes hiccups?

Common triggers are eating too fast or too much, fizzy drinks, alcohol, smoking, and excitement or stress. Reflux and some medicines can cause hiccups that keep coming back.

When should I worry about hiccups?

See your GP if they last more than 2 days or keep coming back. Call 000 if hiccups come with chest pain, shortness of breath, sudden weakness, trouble speaking or confusion.
