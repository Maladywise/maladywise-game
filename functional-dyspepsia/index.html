Fact sheet

Functional Dyspepsia

Ongoing upper tummy pain, burning, early fullness or uncomfortable fullness after meals, with tests that look normal. It’s a real gut–brain problem, not imagination, and about 1 in 14 people have it. Treatments work best stepped and combined.

Symptoms

  • Fullness type: feeling uncomfortably full after normal meals, or full after a few bites
  • Pain type: burning or pain in the upper tummy, not only after meals
  • Many people have both, and many also have IBS or reflux
  • Symptoms come and go, often worse with stress, big or fatty meals

See your GP soon for any of these: weight loss you can’t explain, vomiting that won’t settle, trouble swallowing, black or bloody stools, low iron or anaemia, a family history of bowel or stomach cancer, or new symptoms after 60.

Why it happens

  • The stomach doesn’t relax properly to receive food, or empties unevenly
  • The stomach and duodenum are oversensitive: normal stretching feels painful
  • Mild inflammation in the duodenum, sometimes after a gut infection
  • The gut–brain connection amplifies signals, especially with stress, anxiety or poor sleep

Tests

  • A breath or stool test for Helicobacter pylori
  • Blood tests: full blood count, iron, coeliac serology
  • A gastroscopy for red flags or new symptoms after 60
  • Repeated scopes and scans don’t help once the diagnosis is made

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • IgG food intolerance testsThey measure normal exposure, not intolerance. No value for dyspepsia.
  • Removing the gallbladder for normal-gallbladder painDoesn’t help functional dyspepsia unless there’s proven gallbladder disease.
  • Repeated scopesOnce a good gastroscopy is normal, repeating it rarely changes anything.

Common questions

What is functional dyspepsia?

Ongoing upper tummy pain, burning or early fullness with normal tests such as a gastroscopy. It’s caused by an oversensitive, slow-to-relax stomach and gut–brain signalling, not by an ulcer or cancer.

How is functional dyspepsia treated?

Small, low-fat meals, H. pylori treatment if positive, a short course of acid tablets, and if needed low-dose amitriptyline, gut-directed hypnotherapy or medicines that help the stomach empty.

Is functional dyspepsia serious?

It doesn’t cause cancer or shorten life, but it can affect quality of life. New symptoms after 60, weight loss, vomiting, bleeding or anaemia need checking.
