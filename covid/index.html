Fact sheet

COVID-19

COVID is now a familiar winter (and summer) virus, but it still hits older and immunocompromised people hard. If you’re at higher risk, test early: antivirals work best within 5 days of symptoms starting.

Symptoms

  • Sore throat, runny nose, cough, fever, tiredness, headache and aches
  • Loss of smell or taste is less common than early in the pandemic
  • Most people recover within 1–2 weeks

Call 000 for trouble breathing, chest pain or pressure, confusion, blue lips, or fainting.

‘Just a cold’ is a diagnosis made by a test, not by hope.

Testing

  • Rapid antigen tests (RATs) work; a negative test early on may need repeating 24–48 hours later.
  • Combined COVID and flu RATs are available from pharmacies.
  • A PCR test may be needed to access antivirals or if you’re at high risk.

What helps

Antivirals are for people at higher risk of severe illness. Ask early: they work best within 5 days.

Protecting others

  • Stay home while you have symptoms.
  • For 7 days, avoid visiting aged care, hospitals and people at high risk; wear a mask in crowded indoor places.
  • Open windows and good ventilation help.

Vaccines

  • Boosters are recommended every 6 months for people 75 and over, and yearly for people 65–74; other adults can have a yearly dose.
  • Severely immunocompromised people may need extra doses.
  • Updated vaccines target recent variants. Check the latest ATAGI advice with your GP or pharmacist.

Ivermectin is a brilliant horse dewormer. For COVID, the trials said no.

After COVID

  • Symptoms lasting beyond 3 months may be long COVID. See your GP; there are treatments for many symptoms.

Common questions

Who can get COVID antivirals in Australia?

Mainly older people and those with risk factors for severe illness or a weakened immune system. Eligibility rules change, so ask your GP or pharmacist early. Treatment should start within 5 days of symptoms.

How long should I stay home with COVID?

Stay home while you have symptoms. For 7 days, avoid aged care, hospitals and high-risk people, and wear a mask in crowded indoor places.

How often should I get a COVID booster?

Every 6 months for people 75 and over, and every 12 months for people 65–74. Younger adults can have a yearly dose, and some immunocompromised people need more.
