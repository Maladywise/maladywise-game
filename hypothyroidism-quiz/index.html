Hypothyroidism Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on the underactive thyroid: what it does, how it’s tested, taking thyroxine properly and when doses change.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What does the thyroid gland mainly control?

    1. Salt balance
    2. Blood sugar
    3. Blood clotting
    4. The body’s metabolic rate
    Show answer

    Answer: The body’s metabolic rate. Thyroid hormone sets the pace for almost every cell, so too little slows everything down.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    Which symptom fits an underactive thyroid?

    1. Bulging eyes
    2. Racing heart and sweating
    3. Tiredness, feeling cold and constipation
    4. Weight loss and tremor
    Show answer

    Answer: Tiredness, feeling cold and constipation. Others: dry skin, weight gain, low mood, heavy periods. The racing, sweaty list is an overactive thyroid.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Which blood test is the best first check?

    1. Calcium
    2. TSH
    3. Thyroid antibodies alone
    4. Full blood count
    Show answer

    Answer: TSH. TSH rises when the thyroid is underactive, because the pituitary is shouting at a sluggish gland.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    What is the most common cause in Australia?

    1. Hashimoto’s thyroiditis (autoimmune)
    2. Thyroid cancer
    3. A virus caught from pets
    4. Iodine overdose
    Show answer

    Answer: Hashimoto’s thyroiditis (autoimmune). The immune system slowly damages the gland. Positive TPO antibodies support it.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    How should thyroxine usually be taken?

    1. Only when tired
    2. With a big breakfast
    3. On an empty stomach, the same way every day
    4. With calcium or iron tablets
    Show answer

    Answer: On an empty stomach, the same way every day. Food, coffee, calcium and iron all reduce absorption. Consistency matters more than the exact time.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    How long after a dose change should TSH be rechecked?

    1. About 6 weeks
    2. After 2 years
    3. After 1 week
    4. The next morning
    Show answer

    Answer: About 6 weeks. TSH takes about 6 weeks to settle after a change, so earlier tests mislead.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    What happens to thyroxine needs in pregnancy?

    1. They never change
    2. They usually go up, often by about 25–30%
    3. They stop being needed
    4. They halve
    Show answer

    Answer: They usually go up, often by about 25–30%. Women on thyroxine should tell their GP as soon as they’re pregnant. Many increase the dose straight away.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    What does ‘subclinical hypothyroidism’ mean?

    1. Normal tests but symptoms
    2. Low TSH with high T4
    3. A thyroid lump
    4. High TSH with normal T4
    Show answer

    Answer: High TSH with normal T4. Many people don’t need treatment, especially if TSH is under 10 and they feel well. Repeat the test first.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    Which medicine can cause an underactive thyroid?

    1. Salbutamol
    2. Paracetamol
    3. Lithium or amiodarone
    4. Amoxicillin
    Show answer

    Answer: Lithium or amiodarone. Both affect thyroid function, so people on them have regular thyroid tests.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    Which other condition is more common with Hashimoto’s?

    1. Kidney stones
    2. Gout
    3. Migraine
    4. Coeliac disease and other autoimmune conditions
    Show answer

    Answer: Coeliac disease and other autoimmune conditions. Autoimmune conditions cluster. Type 1 diabetes, pernicious anaemia and vitiligo are also more common.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    Why does over-replacement with thyroxine matter in older adults?

    1. It raises the risk of atrial fibrillation and bone loss
    2. It stops working
    3. It causes hair to grow
    4. It causes diabetes
    Show answer

    Answer: It raises the risk of atrial fibrillation and bone loss. A suppressed TSH from too much thyroxine is not harmless. The dose is set by blood tests, not by how energetic you’d like to feel.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    What is myxoedema coma?

    1. An allergic reaction to thyroxine
    2. A rare, life-threatening extreme of untreated hypothyroidism
    3. A type of migraine
    4. A deep sleep after thyroid surgery
    Show answer

    Answer: A rare, life-threatening extreme of untreated hypothyroidism. Low temperature, confusion and slow breathing, often triggered by infection or cold in older people. It needs hospital care.

Common questions

Can hypothyroidism be cured?

Hashimoto’s is usually lifelong, but thyroxine replaces the missing hormone well. Most people feel normal once the dose is right.

Are natural thyroid extracts better?

Not on current evidence. Thyroxine gives steady, measurable levels. Talk to your GP before changing.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. See your GP about your own results.
