Stroke Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on spotting a stroke fast, the time windows for treatment, mini-strokes, and preventing the next one.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What does FAST stand for?

    1. Fall, Arm, Sight, Touch
    2. Face, Abdomen, Skin, Temperature
    3. Fever, Ache, Sweat, Tired
    4. Face, Arms, Speech, Time
    Show answer

    Answer: Face, Arms, Speech, Time. Drooping face, weak arm, slurred speech: call 000. Time lost is brain lost.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    What causes most strokes?

    1. A bleed in the brain
    2. A brain infection
    3. A blocked artery (ischaemic stroke)
    4. A migraine
    Show answer

    Answer: A blocked artery (ischaemic stroke). About 8 in 10 are ischaemic. The rest are bleeds, which are treated very differently.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    What must be done before giving clot-busting drugs?

    1. A brain scan to rule out a bleed
    2. An ECG to check the heart rhythm
    3. A lumbar puncture to check for infection
    4. A blood test for cholesterol
    Show answer

    Answer: A brain scan to rule out a bleed. Clot-busters in a bleed would be disastrous. CT is done within minutes of arrival.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    Up to how many hours after onset can clot-busting (thrombolysis) usually be given?

    1. 30 minutes
    2. 4.5 hours
    3. 3 days
    4. 24 hours
    Show answer

    Answer: 4.5 hours. The earlier the better. Clot retrieval through a catheter can work up to 24 hours later for some people.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    What is a TIA (mini-stroke)?

    1. Stroke symptoms that fully go away, usually within an hour
    2. A small bleed that heals by itself
    3. A stroke that only affects sleep
    4. A migraine with harmless symptoms
    Show answer

    Answer: Stroke symptoms that fully go away, usually within an hour. A TIA is a warning: the risk of a full stroke is highest in the next few days. Same-day assessment.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    Which heart rhythm raises stroke risk about five times?

    1. Sinus rhythm
    2. Atrial fibrillation
    3. First-degree heart block
    4. Sinus arrhythmia
    Show answer

    Answer: Atrial fibrillation. Clots form in the fibrillating heart. Anticoagulants cut that risk by about two thirds.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    What is the biggest modifiable risk factor for stroke?

    1. Being tall
    2. Left-handedness
    3. High blood pressure
    4. Drinking coffee
    Show answer

    Answer: High blood pressure. Lowering blood pressure prevents more strokes than anything else.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    Why is a swallow check done before a stroke patient eats or drinks?

    1. To test their memory and understanding
    2. To meet hospital policy, not for safety
    3. To check for new food allergies
    4. To prevent food or drink going into the lungs
    Show answer

    Answer: To prevent food or drink going into the lungs. Swallowing is often affected. Aspiration pneumonia is a major cause of death after stroke.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    Neck pain after a sudden neck movement, then stroke symptoms in a 35-year-old: what might it be?

    1. A migraine with aura
    2. A pinched nerve in the neck
    3. Artery dissection (a tear in a neck artery)
    4. A panic attack from the pain
    Show answer

    Answer: Artery dissection (a tear in a neck artery). A common cause of stroke in younger adults. Trauma, sport and sudden twisting can trigger it.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    What improves survival and recovery more than almost anything after admission?

    1. A private room
    2. Care in a dedicated stroke unit
    3. Strict bed rest for a month
    4. High-dose vitamins
    Show answer

    Answer: Care in a dedicated stroke unit. Stroke units cut death and disability through expert teams, early mobility and rehab.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    How long after a stroke before most people can drive a private car in Australia?

    1. At least 1 year, for everyone
    2. The next day, if they feel well
    3. Never again, after any stroke
    4. At least 4 weeks (2 weeks after a TIA)
    Show answer

    Answer: At least 4 weeks (2 weeks after a TIA). Then only if there’s no ongoing problem with vision, thinking or limb control.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    What is ‘time is brain’ based on?

    1. About 1.9 million brain cells die each minute
    2. It's a slogan with no science behind it
    3. Brain cells regrow quickly after a stroke
    4. Strokes nearly always get better alone
    Show answer

    Answer: About 1.9 million brain cells die each minute. Every minute saved in getting treatment means more independence afterwards.

Common questions

Can young people have strokes?

Yes. About 1 in 4 strokes happen in people under 65.

What should I do if I see FAST signs?

Call 000 immediately, note the time symptoms started, and don’t give food, drink or aspirin.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. If you think someone is having a stroke, call 000 now.
