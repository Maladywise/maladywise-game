Autism: Support and Treatment Algorithm

For health professionals General practice

Autism isn’t a disease to cure. The GP’s job is a timely, respectful diagnosis, support that fits the person, and treating the co-occurring conditions that cause the most distress. Graded options, built on the Australian national guidelines.

Evidence grades: A strong · B moderate · C weak or mixed · D anecdotal · X avoid.

Step 0 · Recognise and refer

  • Children: speech delay, limited joint attention or pointing, loss of skills, repetitive play, strong sensory responses, parental concern. Adults: lifelong social differences, burnout, anxiety or depression not responding, family history.
  • Girls and women are under-recognised and often mask; ask about camouflaging and exhaustion.
  • Refer for assessment following the national guideline (paediatrician, psychiatrist or psychologist; multidisciplinary when needed). Hearing test in children.
  • Check co-occurring conditions: ADHD, anxiety, depression, sleep, epilepsy, GI problems, intellectual disability, eating problems, hypermobility.

Step 1 · Supports (children)

Goals set with the family; neurodiversity-affirming.

  1. B · ModerateParent-mediated and naturalistic developmental behavioural interventionse.g. PACT, JASPER: build communication through everyday play.
    Evidence
    RCTs show gains in social communication; PACT effects persisted 6 years later
    How
    Speech pathologists and early intervention services; NDIS early childhood approach
    Watch for
  2. B · ModerateSpeech pathology and occupational therapyCommunication (including AAC) and sensory or daily-living goals.
    Evidence
    Moderate evidence for targeted goals
    How
    Allied health; NDIS for eligible children
    Watch for
  3. B · ModerateSchool supportsVisual schedules, quiet spaces, predictable routines.
    Evidence
    Guideline consensus
    How
    School learning support plans
    Watch for

Step 2 · Co-occurring conditions

Treat what causes distress; most medicines target these, not autism itself.

  1. A · StrongADHD medicationAs in the ADHD algorithm; start lower, go slower.
    Evidence
    Methylphenidate helps about half (RUPP), with more side effects than in ADHD alone
    How
    See /algorithms/adhd/
    Watch for
    Irritability, appetite.
  2. B · ModerateAdapted CBT for anxietyConcrete, visual, interest-based CBT.
    Evidence
    Moderate evidence in autistic children and adults
    How
    Psychologist with autism experience
    Watch for
  3. B · ModerateMelatonin for sleepAfter sleep hygiene and routine.
    Evidence
    RCTs show faster sleep onset and longer sleep in autistic children
    How
    Prolonged-release paediatric melatonin (Slenyto) 2–5 mg
    Watch for
    Morning drowsiness.
  4. A · StrongRisperidone or aripiprazole for severe irritability or aggressionWhen behaviour support hasn’t worked and there’s risk of harm.
    Evidence
    RCTs show large reductions in irritability
    How
    Low dose; specialist-guided
    Watch for
    Weight gain, metabolic effects, sedation, prolactin (risperidone).
  5. C · Weak / mixedSSRIs for repetitive behaviours
    Evidence
    Trials in children negative; some benefit for anxiety or depression in adults
    How
    Start very low
    Watch for
    Activation and agitation.
  6. X · AvoidChelation, hyperbaric oxygen, MMS (bleach), stem cells, secretin, strict special diets
    Evidence
    No benefit; some cause serious harm
    How
    Avoid
    Watch for

Adults

  • Psychoeducation and connection with autistic-led community help self-understanding and wellbeing.
  • Reasonable adjustments at work or study; written communication; sensory accommodations.
  • Recognise autistic burnout: reduce demands, sensory load and masking; it isn’t depression, though both can coexist.
  • Treat anxiety, depression, sleep and ADHD as usual, with adapted approaches and lower starting doses.
  • Higher suicide risk: ask directly and plan safety.

Common questions

Is there medication for autism?

Not for autism itself. Medicines treat co-occurring conditions such as ADHD, anxiety, sleep problems and severe irritability or aggression.

What therapies help autistic children?

Parent-mediated and naturalistic developmental approaches (such as PACT and JASPER), speech pathology and occupational therapy, chosen around the child’s and family’s goals.
