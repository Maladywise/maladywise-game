Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on Ehlers-Danlos syndromes: bendy joints, the Beighton score, the types that matter most and how they’re managed.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What do Ehlers-Danlos syndromes affect?

    1. Red blood cells
    2. Connective tissue, such as collagen
    3. Nerves only
    4. Hormone glands
    Show answer

    Answer: Connective tissue, such as collagen. Connective tissue holds joints, skin and blood vessels together.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    Which feature is common in most types?

    1. Stiff fused joints
    2. Brittle hair
    3. Very flexible (hypermobile) joints
    4. Extra fingers
    Show answer

    Answer: Very flexible (hypermobile) joints. Joints may dislocate or partly dislocate easily.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    What does the Beighton score measure?

    1. Joint hypermobility, out of 9
    2. Heart rate
    3. Skin stretch
    4. Bone density
    Show answer

    Answer: Joint hypermobility, out of 9. Little fingers, thumbs, elbows, knees and palms to the floor. You can print one at our printables page.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    Which type is most common?

    1. Kyphoscoliotic EDS
    2. Dermatosparaxis EDS
    3. Vascular EDS
    4. Hypermobile EDS (hEDS)
    Show answer

    Answer: Hypermobile EDS (hEDS). hEDS has no gene test yet, so it’s diagnosed on the 2017 clinical criteria.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    Which type is the most dangerous?

    1. Hypermobile EDS
    2. They are all identical
    3. Classical EDS
    4. Vascular EDS
    Show answer

    Answer: Vascular EDS. It can cause artery, bowel or uterine rupture. A COL3A1 gene test confirms it.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    Which skin feature suggests classical EDS?

    1. Very stretchy skin with wide, thin scars
    2. Thick leathery skin
    3. Bright red palms
    4. No scars ever
    Show answer

    Answer: Very stretchy skin with wide, thin scars. Wounds may split and heal with papery scars.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    Which condition often occurs with hEDS?

    1. Kidney stones always
    2. POTS (a fast heart rate on standing)
    3. Type 1 diabetes always
    4. Gout
    Show answer

    Answer: POTS (a fast heart rate on standing). Gut symptoms, fatigue and anxiety are also common alongside hEDS.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    What is the main treatment for hEDS?

    1. Bed rest
    2. Joint fusion surgery for everyone
    3. Physio-led strengthening and pacing
    4. Antibiotics
    Show answer

    Answer: Physio-led strengthening and pacing. Building muscle around joints improves stability and pain.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    What is hypermobility spectrum disorder?

    1. A muscle infection
    2. Symptomatic hypermobility that doesn’t meet hEDS criteria
    3. A form of vascular EDS
    4. A gene test result
    Show answer

    Answer: Symptomatic hypermobility that doesn’t meet hEDS criteria. It’s managed in the same way as hEDS.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    Why tell your dentist or surgeon you have EDS?

    1. They won’t treat you
    2. No reason
    3. It makes surgery faster
    4. Local anaesthetic may work less well and healing can be slower
    Show answer

    Answer: Local anaesthetic may work less well and healing can be slower. Many people with EDS report numbing medicines wearing off quickly.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    How is EDS (other than hEDS) usually inherited?

    1. Mostly autosomal dominant, so a 50% chance of passing it on
    2. Always from fathers only
    3. Only from grandparents
    4. Never inherited
    Show answer

    Answer: Mostly autosomal dominant, so a 50% chance of passing it on. Some rarer types are recessive. Genetic counselling helps families.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Which activity advice is usually given in vascular EDS?

    1. Avoid all walking
    2. Train for powerlifting
    3. Avoid heavy lifting and contact sports
    4. No changes needed
    Show answer

    Answer: Avoid heavy lifting and contact sports. Sudden strain can stress fragile arteries. Gentle regular activity is still encouraged.

Common questions

Is being double-jointed the same as EDS?

No. Many people are flexible with no problems. EDS involves other features and symptoms.

Is there a test for hEDS?

Not yet. It’s diagnosed with a clinical checklist; gene tests help rule out other types.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. See your GP about your own symptoms.
