Reflux in Babies

Milk coming back up is very common in babies, and most are ‘happy spitters’ who grow well. It usually settles by about 12 months as the stomach valve matures and babies sit up.

What it is

  • The valve at the top of a baby’s stomach is still immature, so milk easily comes back up (gastro-oesophageal reflux, or GOR).
  • Spilling or posseting after feeds is normal. It often looks like more than it is.
  • GORD (reflux disease) is when reflux causes problems such as poor weight gain, feeding refusal or breathing problems. It’s much less common.

What helps

  • Smaller, more frequent feeds, and burping during feeds.
  • Hold your baby upright for a while after feeds.
  • Keep the nappy loose around the tummy.
  • Always put your baby to sleep on their back on a flat cot mattress. Raising the cot or using wedges isn’t recommended.
  • Thickened feeds may help some formula-fed babies; ask your GP or child health nurse first.

About medicines

  • Acid-blocking medicines don’t reduce crying or spilling in otherwise well babies, and they can have side effects.
  • They may be used for proven GORD, under a doctor’s care.

See a doctor promptly if

  • Vomit is green or yellow (bile) or has blood: same day.
  • Forceful, projectile vomiting after every feed, usually between 2 and 8 weeks of age, with a hungry baby: a blocked stomach outlet (pyloric stenosis) needs urgent checking.
  • Vomiting starts suddenly, or with fever, a swollen tummy or blood in the poo.
  • Poor weight gain, refusing feeds, fewer wet nappies, or breathing problems, coughing or choking with feeds.

Common questions

When does reflux in babies stop?

Most babies improve as they grow, and it usually settles by about 12 months.

Is it normal for babies to spit up after every feed?

Often, yes. If your baby is growing well and happy, it’s usually normal reflux and doesn’t need treatment.

Do reflux medicines help babies who cry a lot?

Usually not. Acid blockers don’t reduce crying in otherwise well babies and can have side effects. They’re used for proven reflux disease.

What vomiting in a baby is an emergency?

Green or bloody vomit, forceful projectile vomiting after every feed, or vomiting with a swollen tummy, fever, lethargy or fewer wet nappies. See a doctor the same day.
