Fact sheet

PMS and PMDD

Physical and mood symptoms in the 1 to 2 weeks before a period that ease once it starts. PMDD is the severe form, affecting about 1 in 20. Both are real, and both are treatable.

The PMS ladder

Track symptoms for 2 cycles first: it confirms the pattern and shows whether treatment works. Give each step 2 to 3 cycles.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careSymptom diary, exercise and sleepA daily diary, regular exercise across the whole month, decent sleep, and less alcohol and caffeine before your period.
    Works
    The diary is key to diagnosis; exercise may help mood and sleep (evidence is limited)
    Cost
    Free (an app or the IAPMD diary)
    How long
    2–3 cycles
    Watch for
    Exercising only in the bad week. It works best all month.

    Book the big stuff for your good weeks. That’s strategy, not weakness.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacyCalcium1,000–1,200 mg a day, from food or a supplement.
    Works
    May ease mood and bloating; evidence is limited
    Cost
    About $10–20 a month
    How long
    2–3 cycles
    Watch for
    Constipation. Count the dairy you already eat before adding tablets.

    Cheap, safe, worth a go. Evening primrose oil, sadly, did no better than placebo.

  3. Step 3 · PrescriptionContraceptive pill containing drospirenoneTaken continuously or with a short break, it smooths out the hormone swings.
    Works
    Reduces symptoms for many with PMDD; evidence is moderate
    Cost
    $25 on the PBS for 3 months ($7.70 concession)
    How long
    2–3 cycles to judge
    Watch for
    Nausea, spotting, small clot risk. Not with migraine with aura, a past clot, or smoking over 35.

    Skipping the sugar pills is allowed. A monthly bleed on the pill isn’t medically needed.

  4. Step 4 · PrescriptionSSRI antidepressant (such as fluoxetine or sertraline)The best-proven treatment for PMDD and severe PMS. Taken daily, or just in the 2 weeks before a period.
    Works
    About 6 in 10 improve, often within days
    Cost
    Under the $25 PBS co-payment, about $18–25 a month
    How long
    Works within days; judge over 2 cycles
    Watch for
    Nausea, sleep changes, lower libido. Tell your doctor if mood gets worse.

    Part-month dosing is a legitimate option, not a cheat code.

  5. Step 5 · Allied healthCBT with a psychologistCoping skills that reduce distress and help you plan around your cycle.
    Works
    Reduces distress and improves coping; evidence is moderate
    Cost
    About $150–250 a session; Medicare rebates with a mental health care plan
    How long
    Usually 6–10 sessions
    Watch for
    Needs practice between sessions.

    Tools you keep, whatever your cycle gets up to.

  6. Step 6 · SpecialistMedicines that pause the cycle, with hormone “add-back”For severe PMDD when other options fail, through a gynaecologist.
    Works
    Effective for most people with confirmed PMDD
    Cost
    Specialist fees, plus medicines that can cost more if not PBS-subsidised for this use
    How long
    A trial of 3–6 months; longer use needs bone monitoring
    Watch for
    Menopause-like symptoms and bone thinning, which add-back hormones reduce.

    A serious option for a serious problem. Specialist-only for good reason.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by pharmacy. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card.

What you can do

  • Track symptoms daily for 2 to 3 cycles, using an app or the free IAPMD diary.
  • Plan demanding tasks and big events for your better weeks where you can.
  • Prioritise sleep, and cut back on alcohol and caffeine before your period.
  • Go easy on supplement aisles: vitex and vitamin B6 have mixed evidence, and high-dose B6 can damage nerves.

See your GP if

  • Symptoms affect work, study or relationships
  • Low mood or anxiety lasts all month, not just before periods
  • You also have heavy or very painful periods

If you have thoughts of self-harm or suicide, even only before your period, call Lifeline on 13 11 14, or 000 in an emergency. Tell your GP: this is treatable.

Common questions

What is the difference between PMS and PMDD?

PMS is physical and mood symptoms in the 1 to 2 weeks before a period that ease once it starts. PMDD is a severe form, affecting about 1 in 20, with marked low mood, anxiety or irritability.

Do antidepressants help PMDD?

SSRIs such as fluoxetine or sertraline help about 6 in 10 people with PMDD or severe PMS. They can be taken daily or only in the 2 weeks before a period, and often work within days.

Can the pill help PMS?

A pill containing drospirenone, taken with a short break or continuously, can reduce symptoms. It isn’t suitable for everyone, for example with migraine with aura or a past blood clot.

Do supplements help PMS?

Calcium may ease mood and bloating. Vitex and vitamin B6 have mixed evidence (keep B6 to low doses), and evening primrose oil is no better than placebo.
