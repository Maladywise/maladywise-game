Christmas food safety

Summer heat, big batches of food and long lunches make the festive season peak time for food poisoning. A few habits prevent most of it.

The big rules

  • Keep cold food cold: fridge at 5 °C or below. Don’t overpack it before Christmas; use eskies with ice for drinks.
  • The 2-hour rule: put perishable food back in the fridge within 2 hours, less on a hot day.
  • Thaw in the fridge, not on the bench. A large frozen turkey can take several days.
  • Don’t wash raw poultry: it splashes bacteria around the sink. Cook it through instead: 75 °C in the thickest part, checked with a meat thermometer.
  • Separate raw meat from salads and cooked food, with different boards and utensils.
  • Leftovers: cool, cover and refrigerate quickly; eat within 2 to 3 days; reheat until steaming hot.
  • Seafood: buy prawns and oysters last, keep them on ice, and refrigerate straight away.

Extra care if you’re pregnant, older or have low immunity

  • Listeria risk: avoid deli meats, cold cooked chicken, pre-cut melon, soft and blue cheeses, cold smoked salmon, raw seafood and pre-made salads unless freshly made or heated until steaming.
  • Avoid raw egg in homemade desserts, eggnog and mayonnaise.

See a doctor if

  • Signs of dehydration: very little urine, dizziness, a dry mouth, or a baby with fewer wet nappies
  • Blood in the poo, high fever or severe tummy pain
  • Vomiting that stops you keeping fluids down, or diarrhoea lasting more than a few days
  • You’re pregnant, elderly or have low immunity

Common questions

How long can food be left out at Christmas?

Put perishable food back in the fridge within 2 hours, and sooner on a hot day. Food left out longer should be thrown away.

Should I wash the turkey?

No. Washing raw poultry spreads bacteria around the sink. Cook it until the thickest part reaches 75 °C instead.

How long are Christmas leftovers safe?

Refrigerate them quickly, eat within 2 to 3 days, and reheat until steaming hot all the way through.

What should pregnant people avoid at Christmas?

Foods with a listeria risk: deli meats, cold cooked chicken, pre-cut melon, soft and blue cheeses, cold smoked salmon, raw seafood and pre-made salads, plus raw egg desserts.
