Fact sheet

Tennis Elbow

Pain on the outside of the elbow when gripping, lifting or twisting. Usually from work or everyday tasks, not tennis. It’s a tendon overload problem, and most people get better.

The tennis elbow ladder

Time is the main treatment: most people recover within a year, often sooner. The ladder makes the wait less painful.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careAdjust your load and give it timeKeep using the arm, but ease off tasks that cause more than mild pain and change how you do them.
    Works
    About 8–9 in 10 are better by a year with wait-and-see in trials, often within months
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    Weeks to months
    Watch for
    Complete rest: tendons get weaker, not better

    Your tendon wants less load, not a sling.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacyAnti-inflammatory gel and simple pain reliefSuch as diclofenac gel, paracetamol or a short course of anti-inflammatory tablets.
    Works
    Gel: about 7 in 10 improve vs 5 in 10 on a dummy treatment over a few weeks (low-quality evidence)
    Cost
    About $10–25
    How long
    Short term, while you sort the load
    Watch for
    Skin irritation from gel. Tablets: stomach upset; not for everyone. Check with your pharmacist

    Rub it in, then go fix your mouse grip.

  3. Step 3 · PharmacyForearm braceA strap just below the elbow to take load off the tendon during activities.
    Works
    Evidence weak but low risk; may ease pain during activities
    Cost
    About $20–40
    How long
    Wear during aggravating tasks
    Watch for
    Too tight: numb fingers. Loosen it

    Cheap, safe, and worth a go.

  4. Step 4 · Allied healthPhysio-guided strengtheningA program that slowly builds up load on the forearm muscles and tendon.
    Works
    More people improve by 6 weeks than with wait-and-see; no difference by a year
    Cost
    About $90–130 a session; Medicare rebates with a GP care plan
    How long
    6–12 weeks or more, mostly done at home
    Watch for
    Some discomfort during exercises is expected; sharp pain means back off

    The exercises only work if you do them, not if you photograph the handout.

  5. Step 5 · ProcedureShockwave therapySound waves applied to the tendon over a few sessions.
    Works
    Little or no benefit over sham in better-quality trials
    Cost
    About $60–120 a session, usually 3–5 sessions
    How long
    A few weeks
    Watch for
    Pain during treatment, bruising

    Loud, popular, and less impressive than the noise suggests.

  6. Step 6 · SpecialistSurgeryOnly considered if pain lasts more than 6 to 12 months despite good non-surgical care.
    Works
    No better than sham surgery in a small trial; benefit uncertain
    Cost
    Specialist and hospital fees vary widely; private cover helps
    How long
    Weeks to months of recovery
    Watch for
    Infection, stiffness, ongoing pain

    Rarely needed. The calendar usually wins first.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. Results are typical figures from trials: your elbow didn’t read them.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Cortisone injectionEases pain for a few weeks, but people are less likely to be better at 1 year and far more likely to relapse (54% vs 12% in a large Australian trial).
  • PRP and blood injectionsNo better than a dummy treatment, and costly.
  • Scans (ultrasound or MRI)Rarely change treatment for typical tennis elbow. A picture of a grumpy tendon is still a grumpy tendon.
  • Therapeutic ultrasoundReviews find little or no benefit. A warm wand and a hefty invoice.
  • Laser therapySmall, low-quality studies with mixed results. Not worth the appointments.

What you can do

  • Lift with your palm facing up, use both hands, and use thicker grips on tools and racquets.
  • Take regular breaks from repetitive tasks and keyboard or mouse work.
  • Do the exercises in our tennis elbow exercise handout: a few minutes a day, building up slowly.
  • Return to sport gradually. A coach can check your technique and grip size.

See your GP if

  • Pain isn’t improving after 6 to 12 weeks
  • Pain stops you working or sleeping
  • You have numbness, tingling or weakness in your hand, or neck pain spreading down your arm
  • Pain started after a fall or injury

See a doctor the same day for a hot, red, swollen elbow, especially with fever, or if you can’t bend or straighten your elbow after an injury.

Common questions

How long does tennis elbow last?

It usually settles within 12 to 18 months, often sooner. See your GP if pain isn’t improving after 6 to 12 weeks.

Should I get a cortisone injection for tennis elbow?

It isn’t recommended. It eases pain for a few weeks, but people are less likely to be better at 1 year and far more likely to relapse.

What exercises help tennis elbow?

A physio-guided strengthening program that slowly builds up load on the forearm muscles and tendon over 6 to 12 weeks or more.

Does a tennis elbow brace work?

A strap just below the elbow may ease pain during activities. The evidence is weak, but it’s cheap and safe to try.
