Sleep Apnoea Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on obstructive sleep apnoea: the warning signs, how sleep studies work, and treatments from CPAP to splints.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What happens in obstructive sleep apnoea?

    1. The brain forgets to sleep
    2. The throat repeatedly narrows or closes during sleep
    3. The lungs fill with fluid
    4. The heart stops beating at night
    Show answer

    Answer: The throat repeatedly narrows or closes during sleep. Breathing pauses until the brain briefly wakes to reopen the airway, many times an hour.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    Which symptom is common?

    1. Loud snoring with pauses in breathing
    2. Sleeping too lightly to snore
    3. Ringing ears
    4. Itchy skin
    Show answer

    Answer: Loud snoring with pauses in breathing. Partners often notice gasping or choking. Daytime sleepiness and morning headaches are common.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Which screening questionnaire is often used?

    1. APGAR
    2. Glasgow Coma Scale
    3. STOP-Bang
    4. MMSE
    Show answer

    Answer: STOP-Bang. Snoring, Tiredness, Observed apnoeas, blood Pressure, BMI, age, neck size and gender.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    How is sleep apnoea diagnosed?

    1. A hearing test
    2. A blood test
    3. A chest X-ray
    4. A sleep study (at home or in a sleep lab)
    Show answer

    Answer: A sleep study (at home or in a sleep lab). Home studies are common in Australia for straightforward cases.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    What does AHI measure?

    1. How loud the snoring is
    2. Average heart rate
    3. Breathing pauses and shallow breaths per hour of sleep
    4. Hours of deep sleep
    Show answer

    Answer: Breathing pauses and shallow breaths per hour of sleep. Roughly: 5–14 mild, 15–29 moderate, 30 or more severe in adults.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    What is the most effective treatment for moderate to severe OSA?

    1. Drinking warm milk
    2. CPAP (a mask that keeps the airway open with air pressure)
    3. Sleeping pills
    4. Nasal spray alone
    Show answer

    Answer: CPAP (a mask that keeps the airway open with air pressure). It works when used: most of the night, most nights.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    What is a mandibular advancement splint?

    1. A neck brace
    2. A tongue piercing
    3. A nasal tube
    4. A mouthguard that holds the lower jaw forward
    Show answer

    Answer: A mouthguard that holds the lower jaw forward. An option for mild to moderate OSA or people who can’t tolerate CPAP, fitted by a dentist.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    Which lifestyle change can greatly improve OSA in people with obesity?

    1. Weight loss
    2. Sleeping on the back
    3. Taking sedatives
    4. Drinking alcohol before bed
    Show answer

    Answer: Weight loss. Alcohol and sedatives relax throat muscles and worsen apnoea. Side-sleeping helps some people.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    Which condition is linked to untreated OSA?

    1. Short-sightedness
    2. High blood pressure
    3. Hay fever
    4. Gout only in children
    Show answer

    Answer: High blood pressure. Also atrial fibrillation, stroke risk, depression and motor vehicle crashes.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    Why does OSA matter for driving?

    1. It improves reaction times
    2. It only matters for cyclists
    3. Sleepiness raises crash risk
    4. It doesn’t
    Show answer

    Answer: Sleepiness raises crash risk. Australian fitness-to-drive standards cover sleep disorders, especially for commercial drivers.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    Which feature of children’s OSA differs from adults’?

    1. Large tonsils and adenoids are the common cause
    2. Children can’t get OSA
    3. It only causes weight gain
    4. It’s always genetic
    Show answer

    Answer: Large tonsils and adenoids are the common cause. Children may be hyperactive rather than sleepy. Tonsil and adenoid removal often helps.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Which newer medicine has been approved overseas for OSA in people with obesity?

    1. Melatonin
    2. Caffeine tablets
    3. Penicillin
    4. Tirzepatide
    Show answer

    Answer: Tirzepatide. The SURMOUNT-OSA trials showed large AHI reductions with weight loss. Check local availability and funding.

Common questions

Is snoring always sleep apnoea?

No. Many people snore without apnoea, but snoring with gasping, pauses or daytime sleepiness should be checked.

Can sleep apnoea be cured?

Sometimes, with major weight loss or surgery in selected people. For most it’s managed long term, like blood pressure.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. See your GP about your own symptoms or results.
