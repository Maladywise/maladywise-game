Dry Eye

Gritty, burning, tired eyes, especially after a day at a screen. Dry eye is very common and usually long term, but it can be kept comfortable. Blinking, lid care and the right drops do most of the work.

What it is: your eyes don’t make enough tears, or the tears evaporate too quickly. Eyes can feel dry, gritty, sore or tired, look red, and sometimes water a lot as they try to make up for it.

Common causes: getting older, hormone changes, screens and reading (we blink less), wind, sun, air conditioning and smoke, contact lenses, laser eye surgery and some medicines.

Related conditions: blepharitis (inflamed eyelids) is a common partner. Rosacea, Sjögren’s syndrome and some types of arthritis can also cause dry eye.

What you can do

  • Take regular screen breaks and remember to blink.
  • Turn air-conditioning vents and fans away from your face.
  • Drink enough water and avoid smoke.
  • Remove eye make-up every night.
  • If you have red cheeks and flushing, read our rosacea fact sheet; it can affect the eyes.
  • Get regular eye checks with an optometrist.

See your GP if

  • Drops haven’t helped after 6 to 8 weeks
  • Symptoms change or get worse
  • You also have a very dry mouth, joint pain or swelling
  • You think a medicine might be causing it

See a doctor or optometrist the same day if you have a painful red eye, sensitivity to light, or any change in your vision. These are not typical of dry eye.

Common questions

What causes dry eyes?

Ageing, hormone changes, screen use, air conditioning, wind, contact lenses, some medicines and conditions such as blepharitis. Often several at once.

Which eye drops are best for dry eyes?

There is no single best drop. Lubricating drops such as hypromellose, carmellose or hyaluronate all help. Try one for 6 to 8 weeks. Preservative-free drops are better if you use them often or wear contact lenses.

Does fish oil help dry eyes?

Probably not much. A 2019 Cochrane review found omega-3 supplements may make little or no difference to symptoms compared with placebo.

Can dry eye be cured?

Usually not, but it can be kept comfortable with drops, lid care and changes to your environment and screen habits.

Can dry eye cause blurry vision?

Yes. Dry eye often causes blurring that comes and goes and clears when you blink, especially during screen use or reading. Blurring that doesn’t clear, a painful red eye or sensitivity to light needs a prompt eye check.

How often can I use lubricating eye drops?

Preservative-free drops can be used as often as you need. Drops with preservatives are best kept to about 4 to 6 times a day, as preservatives can irritate the eye surface with frequent use.

Why do my eyes water if they’re dry?

When the eye surface dries, it triggers a reflex flood of watery tears. These tears lack the oily layer that stops evaporation, so they run off without fixing the dryness. Treating the dry eye often reduces the watering.
