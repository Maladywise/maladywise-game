Eczema Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on eczema (atopic dermatitis): why skin flares, how to use moisturisers and steroid creams properly, and when to see a doctor.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What is the medical name for common eczema?

    1. Rosacea
    2. Acne vulgaris
    3. Atopic dermatitis
    4. Psoriasis
    Show answer

    Answer: Atopic dermatitis. It often runs in families with asthma and hay fever.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    What is the main symptom?

    1. Itch
    2. Sweating
    3. Numbness
    4. Hair loss
    Show answer

    Answer: Itch. Scratching damages the skin barrier, which makes it itchier: the itch–scratch cycle.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Where does eczema often appear in older children and adults?

    1. Only on the scalp
    2. In the elbow and knee creases
    3. Only on the soles
    4. Only inside the mouth
    Show answer

    Answer: In the elbow and knee creases. In babies it often starts on the cheeks and outer arms and legs.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    What is the cornerstone of daily care?

    1. Long, hot showers to soothe itching
    2. Scrubbing the skin to remove scales
    3. Scented soap to keep the skin clean
    4. Moisturiser every day, even when skin looks fine
    Show answer

    Answer: Moisturiser every day, even when skin looks fine. Thick, fragrance-free moisturisers applied generously, often twice a day.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    How should steroid creams be used for a flare?

    1. A tiny dab once a week, at most
    2. Only once the flare has settled
    3. On inflamed skin until it clears, then reduced
    4. Never: they always thin the skin
    Show answer

    Answer: On inflamed skin until it clears, then reduced. Under-treatment from steroid fear is a bigger problem than side effects when creams are used as directed.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    What is a fingertip unit?

    1. A disease of the fingernails
    2. A fungal skin infection
    3. A type of skin allergy test
    4. A measure of how much cream to use
    Show answer

    Answer: A measure of how much cream to use. A strip from the fingertip to the first crease covers an area about two adult palms.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    Which bath or shower habit helps?

    1. Scrubbing with a loofah daily
    2. Short, lukewarm washes with soap-free wash
    3. Bubble baths with scented oils
    4. Long hot baths to ease the itch
    Show answer

    Answer: Short, lukewarm washes with soap-free wash. Pat dry and moisturise straight after.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    Which sign suggests infected eczema?

    1. Weeping, crusting or sudden painful worsening
    2. Skin that’s lighter after healing
    3. Dry, slightly flaky skin
    4. Mild itch at night
    Show answer

    Answer: Weeping, crusting or sudden painful worsening. Staph is common. Painful punched-out blisters may be eczema herpeticum, which needs urgent same-day care.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    What are wet wraps?

    1. A type of food allergy skin test
    2. A hair treatment for scalp eczema
    3. A swimming technique for eczema
    4. Damp bandages over moisturiser for a bad flare
    Show answer

    Answer: Damp bandages over moisturiser for a bad flare. They cool, hydrate and reduce scratching, especially in children.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    Does eczema mean a food allergy is the cause?

    1. Only if it's on the face
    2. Usually not; food is rarely the main driver
    3. Yes, always
    4. Only in adults
    Show answer

    Answer: Usually not; food is rarely the main driver. Strict diets without testing can cause harm. Babies with severe early eczema are at higher risk of food allergy, so early allergen introduction is recommended.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    What is dupilumab?

    1. An injected biologic for moderate to severe eczema
    2. A thick moisturiser
    3. An antibiotic cream
    4. A steroid tablet
    Show answer

    Answer: An injected biologic for moderate to severe eczema. It blocks IL-4 and IL-13 signalling. JAK inhibitor tablets are another newer option for severe disease.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Which cream is a steroid-free option for the face and eyelids?

    1. Zinc oxide sunscreen
    2. Hydrocortisone tablets
    3. Pimecrolimus (a calcineurin inhibitor)
    4. Benzoyl peroxide gel
    Show answer

    Answer: Pimecrolimus (a calcineurin inhibitor). Often used for thin skin areas where longer steroid use is less suitable.

Common questions

Will my child grow out of eczema?

Many children improve a lot as they get older, though skin can stay sensitive and flare at times.

Are steroid creams safe?

Yes, when used as directed for flares. The right strength for the right site, for the right time, is safe and effective.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. See your GP about your own symptoms or results.
