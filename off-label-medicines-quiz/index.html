Off-Label Medicines Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on medicines with surprising second lives, and which ones the evidence backs.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What does ‘off-label’ mean?

    1. Using a medicine bought from overseas
    2. Using a medicine outside its officially approved uses
    3. Using a medicine past its expiry date
    4. Using a generic instead of the brand
    Show answer

    Answer: Using a medicine outside its officially approved uses. Different condition, dose, age group or route from the approved product information. Legal with a good reason and consent.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    Which drug began as an angina treatment before its famous side effect changed its career?

    1. Sildenafil
    2. Isosorbide mononitrate
    3. Alprostadil
    4. Finasteride
    Show answer

    Answer: Sildenafil. Developed for chest pain in the 1990s. It’s also used for pulmonary arterial hypertension.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Minoxidil was first used as…

    1. An acne cream
    2. An anti-nausea drug
    3. A strong blood pressure tablet
    4. A thyroid medicine
    Show answer

    Answer: A strong blood pressure tablet. Hair growth was a side effect. Low doses by mouth are now used off-label for hair loss.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    In which country is diamorphine (medical heroin) a licensed painkiller?

    1. Japan
    2. Australia
    3. New Zealand
    4. The United Kingdom
    Show answer

    Answer: The United Kingdom. It’s licensed for severe pain after surgery, heart attacks and at the end of life. Heroin is prohibited in Australia.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    Which diuretic is used off-label for hormonal acne in women?

    1. Hydrochlorothiazide
    2. Furosemide
    3. Indapamide
    4. Spironolactone
    Show answer

    Answer: Spironolactone. The 2023 SAFA trial showed better acne quality of life than placebo. Not for use in pregnancy.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    Propranolol is used off-label for performance nerves because it…

    1. Blunts the racing heart and tremor
    2. Sedates the brain like a sleeping pill
    3. Lowers cortisol levels
    4. Boosts serotonin quickly
    Show answer

    Answer: Blunts the racing heart and tremor. It blocks adrenaline’s effects on the body, not the worry itself. Avoid with asthma.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    Oral tranexamic acid is used off-label for which skin problem?

    1. Rosacea
    2. Psoriasis
    3. Melasma
    4. Vitiligo
    Show answer

    Answer: Melasma. About 12 weeks of tablets lightens patches. Not for people with a history of blood clots.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    A large 2018 trial of prazosin for PTSD nightmares in 304 veterans found…

    1. It worked only in women
    2. No benefit over placebo
    3. It worsened blood pressure in most
    4. Nightmares halved
    Show answer

    Answer: No benefit over placebo. Earlier small trials looked positive. Bigger trials often shrink or erase early wins.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    Low-dose amitriptyline helped which condition in the 2023 ATLANTIS trial?

    1. Insomnia in older adults
    2. Irritable bowel syndrome
    3. Migraine
    4. Restless legs
    Show answer

    Answer: Irritable bowel syndrome. 10–30 mg at night improved IBS symptoms in UK primary care.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    Which non-hormonal options reduce menopausal hot flushes?

    1. Antihistamines and decongestants
    2. Metformin and statins
    3. Gabapentin and clonidine
    4. Paracetamol and ibuprofen
    Show answer

    Answer: Gabapentin and clonidine. Useful when hormone therapy isn’t suitable. Paroxetine and some newer drugs also help.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    Thalidomide is now approved again for…

    1. Morning sickness, at lower doses
    2. Insomnia in older people
    3. Nothing: it’s banned worldwide
    4. Myeloma, under strict pregnancy prevention
    Show answer

    Answer: Myeloma, under strict pregnancy prevention. Its birth-defect disaster led to modern drug safety laws; it returned for myeloma and a leprosy complication.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Why might an off-label prescription cost more in Australia?

    1. The PBS usually subsidises only approved uses
    2. Pharmacists must compound every one
    3. Off-label medicines carry a special tax
    4. Off-label scripts expire after a week
    Show answer

    Answer: The PBS usually subsidises only approved uses. Off-label use is often private, at the full price. Ask before you fill it.
