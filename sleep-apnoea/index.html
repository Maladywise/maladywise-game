Fact sheet

Obstructive Sleep Apnoea

Your throat narrows or closes many times a night, pausing your breathing. Loud snoring, gasping and daytime sleepiness are the clues. A sleep study confirms it, often at home.

The sleep apnoea ladder

Mild sleep apnoea can start at the bottom. Moderate to severe usually goes straight to CPAP, with the lifestyle steps alongside. Medicare covers a sleep study if a screening questionnaire shows you’re at risk.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careWeight loss, less alcohol, no smokingIf you’re above a healthy weight, losing weight is the biggest lever you control.
    Works
    Each 10% of body weight lost cuts breathing pauses by about a quarter; sometimes cures it
    Cost
    Free (or the cost of support programs)
    How long
    Months; keep it up
    Watch for
    Alcohol within 3 hours of bed, sleeping tablets and opioids relax the throat and make it worse

    The nightcap is doing your throat no favours.

  2. Step 2 · Self-careSleeping on your sideHelps if your apnoea happens mostly on your back. A positional device or backpack trick can keep you there.
    Works
    About 7 fewer breathing pauses an hour than no treatment in back-only apnoea; less than CPAP, and many stop using devices
    Cost
    Free, or about $50–300 for a device
    How long
    Every night
    Watch for
    Doesn’t help if your apnoea happens in every position

    The old tennis-ball-in-the-pyjamas trick, now with a price tag.

  3. Step 3 · Allied healthCPAP (continuous positive airway pressure)A quiet machine blows air through a mask to keep your throat open. The most effective treatment for moderate to severe sleep apnoea.
    Works
    Stops breathing pauses in almost everyone while worn; daytime sleepiness improves and blood pressure drops a little (about 2–3 points)
    Cost
    About $1,000–3,000 to buy, or about $60–120 a month to hire. Masks about $150–300
    How long
    All night, every night, long term
    Watch for
    Dry mouth, mask leaks, sore nose. A well-fitted mask and humidifier fix most problems

    It only works when it’s on your face, not in the cupboard.

  4. Step 4 · Allied healthMouth splint (mandibular advancement splint)A custom mouthguard from a dentist with sleep training that holds your lower jaw forward.
    Works
    Roughly halves breathing pauses in mild to moderate apnoea; less than CPAP, but people often use it more
    Cost
    About $1,500–3,000 for a custom splint; some private health cover
    How long
    Every night; check it works with a follow-up sleep study
    Watch for
    Jaw ache, drooling, tooth movement over years. Pharmacy ‘boil and bite’ devices work less well

    The fallback for people who’d rather wear a mouthguard than a mask.

  5. Step 5 · PrescriptionTirzepatide injectionA weekly weight-loss injection approved in Australia for moderate to severe sleep apnoea in adults with obesity.
    Works
    Cut breathing pauses by about half to two-thirds over a year (dummy treatment about 5–6%), mainly through weight loss
    Cost
    Not PBS-subsidised for this: about $300–600 a month
    How long
    Weekly, long term; benefit fades if stopped
    Watch for
    Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation. Not in pregnancy

    Pricey and weekly, but it treats the weight and the breathing together.

  6. Step 6 · ProcedureSurgery or a nerve stimulator implantTonsil and adenoid removal is the main treatment for children with large tonsils. For adults: throat or jaw surgery, or a tongue-nerve stimulator, after sleep specialist review.
    Works
    Children: sleep study normal in about 8 in 10 after tonsil surgery vs 5 in 10 waiting. Adults: mixed results in selected people
    Cost
    Specialist and hospital fees vary widely; private cover helps
    How long
    Weeks of recovery
    Watch for
    Pain, bleeding, and results that may fade in adults

    For the few, not the first try.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials: your snoring didn’t read them.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Mouth tapingLittle evidence it helps, and it may be risky if your nose is blocked. A social media trend, not a treatment.
  • Anti-snoring spraysNo good evidence they treat sleep apnoea. They may quieten the noise while the breathing pauses carry on.
  • Nasal strips and dilatorsCan reduce snoring a little, but don’t treat sleep apnoea. Silence is not the same as oxygen.
  • ‘Anti-snore’ pillowsNo good evidence beyond helping you sleep on your side, which a cheaper trick can do.
  • Boil-and-bite mouthguardsLess effective and less comfortable than a custom splint from a dentist. Cheap now, wasted later.

What you can do

  • Don’t drive when sleepy. Take a break every 2 hours on long trips, and pull over if you feel drowsy.
  • Treat a blocked nose: it makes CPAP and sleep easier.
  • Give CPAP a few weeks: most people need time to get used to it. Ask your provider about mask fit and a humidifier.
  • Tell your driver licensing authority if sleep apnoea affects your driving. Most people can keep driving once treated.

See your GP if

  • You snore loudly, stop breathing or gasp in your sleep, or wake unrefreshed
  • You feel sleepy during the day, especially while driving
  • You have high blood pressure that’s hard to control, heart rhythm problems or type 2 diabetes
  • A child snores most nights, breathes through their mouth, or has trouble with behaviour or concentration

Stop driving and see your GP soon if you have nodded off at the wheel or had a crash or near miss from sleepiness. Call 000 for chest pain, severe breathlessness, or if someone can’t be woken.

Common questions

What are the signs of sleep apnoea?

Loud snoring, breathing pauses or gasping noticed by a partner, waking unrefreshed, morning headaches and daytime sleepiness.

How is sleep apnoea diagnosed?

With a sleep study, often done at home. Medicare covers the study if a screening questionnaire shows you are at risk.

Are there alternatives to CPAP?

A custom mouth splint from a dentist suits mild to moderate sleep apnoea. Weight loss, sleeping on your side and, for adults with obesity, tirzepatide injections can also help.

Can I drive with sleep apnoea?

Don’t drive when sleepy. Tell your driver licensing authority if sleep apnoea affects your driving. Most people can keep driving once treated.
