Fact sheet

Osteoporosis

Thin, fragile bones that break too easily, often first noticed after a minor fall. Silent until it isn’t. The good news: exercise and modern medicines cut fractures a lot.

The osteoporosis ladder

Exercise, calcium and vitamin D are the foundation for everyone. After a minimal-trauma fracture, or with low bone density, medicine is usually the main event.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careStrength, impact and balance exerciseProgressive weights, impact (stepping, jumping if safe) and balance work, most days.
    Works
    Cuts falls by about a quarter; small gains in bone density
    Cost
    Free to a gym fee; exercise physiologist about $80–120 a session (Medicare rebates with a GP care plan)
    How long
    Ongoing
    Watch for
    Start supervised if you have had spine fractures. Avoid heavy forward bending and twisting.

    Walking is lovely. Your bones want you to lift something heavier than a coffee.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacyCalcium and vitamin DFood first (dairy, tinned fish with bones, fortified foods); supplements if you fall short or are low in vitamin D.
    Works
    Cuts hip fractures by about 15%, mostly in aged care (about 9 fewer per 1000 a year); supports medicines
    Cost
    About $10–20 a month
    How long
    Ongoing
    Watch for
    Constipation from calcium tablets. More is not better.

    Supplements aren’t a substitute for a treatment. They’re the scaffolding.

  3. Step 3 · PrescriptionBisphosphonate tablets (alendronate, risedronate)Slow bone breakdown. Weekly or monthly tablet.
    Works
    Cut spine fractures by about half, and hip fractures by up to a half
    Cost
    Under the $25 PBS co-payment, about $20–25 a month
    How long
    Usually 5 years, then review for a break
    Watch for
    Take on an empty stomach with a full glass of water, stay upright for 30 minutes. Heartburn. Rare jaw bone problems and thigh fractures.

    The fussiest breakfast routine in medicine. Worth it.

  4. Step 4 · PrescriptionYearly infusion (zoledronic acid)A bisphosphonate given into a vein once a year.
    Works
    Cuts spine fractures by about two-thirds and hip fractures by about 40%
    Cost
    $25 per script on the PBS ($7.70 concession), plus infusion fees that vary
    How long
    Once a year, often for 3 years, then review
    Watch for
    Flu-like aches for a day or two after the first dose. Needs a kidney and vitamin D check.

    Fifteen minutes once a year. Great for anyone who hates tablets or forgets them.

  5. Step 5 · Prescription6-monthly injection (denosumab)Blocks the cells that break down bone.
    Works
    Cuts spine fractures by about two-thirds and hip fractures by about 40%
    Cost
    $25 per injection on the PBS ($7.70 concession)
    How long
    Every 6 months, on time, long term
    Watch for
    Low calcium, so check levels. Stopping or running late causes rapid bone loss and spine fractures: never stop without a plan for the next medicine.

    The one medicine where being 2 months late really matters. Book the next one before you leave.

  6. Step 6 · SpecialistBone-building medicines (romosozumab, teriparatide)Injections that build new bone, for very high fracture risk. Started by a specialist.
    Works
    Cut new spine fractures by about two-thirds or more
    Cost
    $25 per script on the PBS for those who qualify ($7.70 concession), plus specialist fees
    How long
    Romosozumab 12 months; teriparatide up to 18–24 months; then another medicine to lock it in
    Watch for
    Romosozumab: not after a recent heart attack or stroke. Teriparatide: daily injections, dizziness, leg cramps.

    Bone renovation, not just maintenance. The heavy machinery.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials, not promises. PBS subsidy for osteoporosis medicines depends on age, bone density and fracture history.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Vibration platesSmall effects on bone density at best, and no good evidence they prevent fractures. A pricey way to stand still.
  • Collagen supplementsSmall, low-quality studies, mostly funded by the people selling them.
  • Alkaline diets and alkaline waterTrials show no benefit for bone. Your stomach is acid regardless.
  • Big once-a-year vitamin D dosesOne large trial found a single yearly mega-dose increased falls and fractures. Regular modest doses are safer.
  • Yearly bone density scansBone changes too slowly to measure reliably in a year, so scans are usually spaced 2 or more years apart.

What you can do

  • Had a fracture from a fall at standing height or less? Ask about a bone density scan and treatment.
  • Follow our bone health exercise handout: one printable page with sets and progressions.
  • Make your home trip-proof: lights at night, no loose rugs, rails in the bathroom.
  • Stop smoking, keep alcohol to 2 standard drinks a day or less.
  • See your dentist before starting, and keep teeth in good shape.
  • Don’t quietly stop your medicine. Talk to your GP first, especially denosumab.

See your GP if

  • You’ve broken a bone from a minor fall or bump
  • You’re over 70, went through early menopause, or take long-term steroid tablets
  • New sudden back pain or loss of height
  • New thigh, groin or jaw pain while on treatment

Get urgent help after a fall if you can’t stand or walk, or have severe hip or back pain: call 000 (Australia) or your local emergency number. From a mobile, 112 works in most countries.

Common questions

Who should have a bone density scan?

People who’ve had a minimal-trauma fracture, everyone 70 and over, and younger people with risk factors such as early menopause, long-term steroids or some medical conditions. Medicare covers scans for these groups.

Are osteoporosis medicines safe?

For people with osteoporosis, the fractures they prevent far outnumber the serious side effects. Jaw bone problems and unusual thigh fractures are rare. Good dental care lowers the jaw risk.

Can I stop denosumab injections?

Not without a plan. Stopping or delaying causes rapid bone loss and a risk of several spine fractures. Your doctor will usually switch you to another medicine first.

Is calcium enough to treat osteoporosis?

No. Calcium and vitamin D support treatment, but on their own they do little to prevent fractures in people living at home. Exercise and prescribed medicines do the heavy lifting.

What are the symptoms of osteoporosis?

Usually none until a bone breaks, often the wrist, hip or spine after a minor fall. Spine fractures can cause back pain, loss of height or a curved upper back. That’s why bone density scans are offered to people at higher risk.

Can osteoporosis be reversed?

Bone density can improve, and the main goal, fewer fractures, is very achievable. Common osteoporosis medicines cut the risk of spine fractures by around half or more, alongside exercise, calcium, vitamin D and fall prevention.

What exercise is best for osteoporosis?

A mix of weight-bearing exercise (such as brisk walking or stair climbing), progressive strength training and balance work. Supervised high-intensity strength training has been shown to be safe and to improve bone density in women with low bone mass.
