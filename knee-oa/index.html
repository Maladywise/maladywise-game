Fact sheet

Knee Osteoarthritis

The most common arthritis: knee cartilage and bone change over time, causing pain and stiffness. Not simple “wear and tear”: joints are living tissue, and moving usually makes them feel better.

The knee osteoarthritis ladder

Start at the bottom, climb only if you need to. Steps 1 and 2 are the real treatment at every stage, even if you also need the others.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careExercise and strengtheningLeg strengthening plus walking, cycling or water exercise, most days.
    Works
    Pain down about 13 points out of 100 vs no exercise; about as much as anti-inflammatory tablets, without the side effects
    Cost
    Free, or about $5–20 a session for a pool or gym
    How long
    6–12 weeks to feel it; keep going for life
    Watch for
    Some soreness is normal. Fine if it settles within 24 hours.

    Resting a sore knee feels logical. Unfortunately, knees prefer movement.

  2. Step 2 · Self-careWeight loss, if above a healthy weightEvery kilo off is several kilos less load on the knee with each step.
    Works
    Losing about 10% roughly halved pain in an 18-month trial (diet plus exercise); 5% noticeably eases pain
    Cost
    Free; weight-loss medicines such as semaglutide are private scripts, often several hundred dollars a month
    How long
    Months
    Watch for
    Weight-loss medicines: nausea, constipation; not in pregnancy.

    Your knees do the maths on every staircase.

  3. Step 3 · PharmacyAnti-inflammatory gel (diclofenac or ibuprofen)Rubbed on the knee, up to 3 or 4 times a day.
    Works
    about 1 in 10 more people get good relief than with a dummy gel; far fewer side effects than tablets
    Cost
    About $15–30 a tube
    How long
    2 weeks to judge; use as needed
    Watch for
    Skin redness or itch.

    Best first painkiller for knees. Paracetamol does very little.

  4. Step 4 · PharmacyAnti-inflammatory tablets, short termIbuprofen or naproxen, or celecoxib on prescription, at the lowest dose for the shortest time.
    Works
    Pain down about 1 point out of 10 more than a dummy treatment, short term; useful for flares
    Cost
    Pharmacy about $5–15; celecoxib under the $25 PBS co-payment
    How long
    Days to a couple of weeks
    Watch for
    Stomach bleeds, kidney and heart risks. Not safe for many people: check with your GP or pharmacist.

    A flare tool, not a lifestyle.

  5. Step 5 · Allied healthPhysio, exercise physiology or a GLA:D programA tailored strengthening plan, or a structured group program (about 6 weeks, run by trained physios) that builds skills and confidence.
    Works
    Moderate drop in pain at 3 months, mostly kept at 12 months (GLA:D Australia data, no comparison group); better function
    Cost
    About $90–130 a session; Medicare rebates with a GP care plan. GLA:D about $400–700 for the program
    How long
    6–8 weeks of sessions, then keep going at home
    Watch for
    Choose someone who gets you moving, not just massaged.

    Paying someone to make you do squats is surprisingly good value.

  6. Step 6 · ProcedureSteroid injectionAn injection into the knee for a bad flare.
    Works
    Pain down about 1 point out of 10 vs a dummy treatment; fades over 6 weeks, gone by 6 months
    Cost
    From bulk-billed to a few hundred dollars, depending on the clinic
    How long
    Relief for weeks
    Watch for
    Not a long-term fix. Repeated injections may harm the joint. Rare infection.

    A pause button, not a fix.

  7. Step 7 · SpecialistKnee replacementFor severe pain and disability despite at least 3 months of good non-surgical care.
    Works
    About 85 in 100 improve meaningfully at 1 year (vs 68 in 100 with exercise program alone); about 1 in 5 have ongoing pain
    Cost
    Free in the public system (expect a wait); private out-of-pocket costs vary from hundreds to several thousand dollars
    How long
    Months of rehab
    Watch for
    Clots, infection, stiffness. Being fitter beforehand helps recovery.

    The top rung. The exercises on rung 1 still come with you.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by pharmacy and clinic. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials: your knee didn’t read the trials.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Glucosamine and chondroitinNo better than a dummy treatment in good-quality studies. A pricey monthly habit with nothing to show for it.
  • Hyaluronic acid (“gel”) injectionsNo better than a dummy treatment in good-quality studies, and often hundreds of dollars a course.
  • PRP and stem cell injectionsSmall, low-quality studies; a large good trial of PRP found no benefit over a dummy treatment. Stem cells can cost thousands with no good evidence at all.
  • Arthroscopy (“clean-out”)No better than exercise or sham surgery for arthritic knees, with all the risks of an operation.
  • OpioidsLittle extra pain relief over anti-inflammatories, with more side effects and a real risk of dependence.
  • MRI scansRarely needed. Most knees over 50 show “damage” on MRI, painful or not, and the scan rarely changes treatment.

What you can do

  • Aim for 150 minutes of activity a week. On bad days, do less rather than stopping.
  • Supportive shoes; a walking stick in the opposite hand takes load off the knee.
  • Follow our knee exercise handout: one printable page with sets and progressions.

See your GP if

  • Pain stops you sleeping, working or doing what you enjoy
  • Your knee locks, gives way or keeps swelling
  • You need anti-inflammatory tablets most days
  • Pain keeps getting worse despite 3 months of exercise and weight management

See a doctor the same day for a hot, red, swollen knee, especially with fever, or if you suddenly can’t put weight on your leg.

Common questions

Is exercise safe with knee arthritis?

Yes. It is the most effective treatment at every stage, easing pain about as much as anti-inflammatory tablets. Some soreness is normal, and pain that settles within 24 hours is OK.

Do knee injections work for osteoarthritis?

Hyaluronic acid (“gel”) and PRP injections are no better than a dummy treatment in good-quality studies. A steroid injection can ease a bad flare for a few weeks, but isn’t a long-term fix.

Does glucosamine help knee arthritis?

No. Glucosamine and chondroitin are no better than a dummy treatment in good-quality studies.

When should I consider a knee replacement?

When pain and disability are severe despite at least 3 months of good non-surgical care. Most people have much less pain afterwards, but about 1 in 5 still have some ongoing pain.
