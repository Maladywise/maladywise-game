Newborn Skin Spots

Newborn skin is a work in progress. Tiny white spots, pimples and blotchy red rashes are all common in the first weeks and nearly always clear on their own. The trick is knowing which ones to leave alone.

Harmless and common

  • Milia: tiny white spots on the nose, cheeks and chin, from blocked skin pores. Similar spots on the gums or roof of the mouth are called Epstein pearls. They go by themselves within weeks.
  • Newborn rash (erythema toxicum): red blotches with small yellow-white bumps in the middle, in the first few days, coming and going on the body. Despite the name, it’s harmless and clears within a week or two.
  • Baby acne: small pimples on the cheeks, nose and forehead, often from 2 to 3 weeks. It clears within a few months and isn’t linked to teenage acne.
  • Stork marks (salmon patches): flat pink patches on the eyelids, between the eyebrows or on the back of the neck. Most on the face fade by age 2; neck marks often stay.

What to do

  • Leave them alone: no squeezing, scrubbing, oils or adult acne creams.
  • Wash with plain water and pat dry.
  • Keep your baby comfortably dressed, not overheated, which can bring out heat rash (tiny red bumps in skin folds).

See a doctor if

  • Blisters, pus-filled spots or crusting, especially in the first weeks of life.
  • A rash with fever, or a baby who is unsettled, sleepy or not feeding.
  • Purple or red spots that don’t fade when pressed with a glass: get help immediately.
  • A birthmark that grows quickly, bleeds or is near the eye, lip or nappy area.
  • Dry, itchy, red patches that don’t settle, which may be eczema.

Common questions

What are the little white spots on my newborn’s nose?

Usually milia: tiny blocked pores. They’re harmless and go on their own within a few weeks. Don’t squeeze them.

Should I treat baby acne?

Usually not. Gentle washing with water is enough, and it clears within a few months. Avoid oils and adult acne products. See your GP if it’s severe or lasts beyond a few months.

What is erythema toxicum?

A common, harmless newborn rash of red blotches with small pale bumps, in the first few days of life. It comes and goes and clears within a week or two.

When is a newborn rash an emergency?

If it’s purple or red spots that don’t fade under pressure, or your baby has a fever, is floppy, very sleepy or not feeding. Get help immediately.
