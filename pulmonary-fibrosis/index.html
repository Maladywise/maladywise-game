Fact sheet

Pulmonary Fibrosis

Pulmonary fibrosis means scarring in the lungs, in the thin tissue around the air sacs. The scarring makes the lungs stiff, so it’s harder to get oxygen in. It’s one type of interstitial lung disease (ILD). The most common form, idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), has no known cause.

Breathless walking up hills you used to stroll? Worth a proper listen to the chest, not just a puffer.

Quick answer: Pulmonary fibrosis is scarring of the lungs that causes breathlessness on exertion and a dry cough. It’s diagnosed with a high-resolution CT scan and breathing tests, reviewed by a lung specialist team. It can’t be reversed, but two PBS-subsidised medicines, nintedanib and pirfenidone, slow the scarring by about half, and pulmonary rehab, oxygen and vaccines help people live well with it.

Types and causes

  • Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF): the most common type. No known cause; mostly affects people over 60, more men than women, often past or current smokers. Some families carry genes that raise the risk.
  • Hypersensitivity pneumonitis: a reaction to something breathed in again and again, such as birds and feathers, mould, hot tubs or farm dust.
  • Autoimmune conditions: scleroderma, rheumatoid arthritis, myositis and Sjögren’s can scar the lungs, sometimes before other symptoms.
  • Work dusts: silica (including engineered stone benchtops, banned in Australia from 1 July 2024), asbestos and coal dust.
  • Medicines and treatments: for example amiodarone, nitrofurantoin, methotrexate, some cancer drugs and chest radiotherapy.
  • It isn’t contagious, and it isn’t the same as COPD or asthma, though people can have more than one lung condition.

Symptoms

  • Breathlessness on exertion that slowly gets worse over months or years
  • A dry, tickly cough that won’t go away
  • Tiredness and weight loss
  • Rounded, enlarged fingertips (clubbing) in some people
  • Doctors may hear fine crackles at the bottom of the lungs, often called “Velcro” crackles

Tests

  • Breathing tests (spirometry and gas transfer, DLCO) show how much the lungs can hold and how well oxygen gets into the blood.
  • High-resolution CT scan of the chest: the key test. A normal chest X-ray doesn’t rule it out.
  • Oxygen levels at rest and on a short walk (6-minute walk test).
  • Blood tests for autoimmune conditions, and questions about your work, hobbies, home, birds, mould and medicines.
  • A team of lung specialists, radiologists and sometimes pathologists reviews the results together. A biopsy is only needed when the scan isn’t typical.

Treatment

Scarring can’t be undone, so treatment aims to slow it, ease symptoms and keep you active. What helps depends on the type.

  1. Step 1 · EveryonePulmonary rehabilitation, vaccines and quitting smokingA supervised 8-week exercise and education program. Get flu, pneumococcal, COVID-19 and RSV vaccines, and stop smoking.
    Works
    Rehab improves walking distance (about 40 metres on a 6-minute walk) and quality of life (Cochrane 2021)
    Cost
    Often free through public hospital programs
    How long
    8 weeks, then keep exercising
    Watch for
    Tell the team if you get chest pain or very breathless during exercise.
  2. Step 2 · SpecialistAntifibrotic medicines: nintedanib or pirfenidoneTablets or capsules taken every day to slow scarring in IPF. Nintedanib is also used for other types of fibrosis that keep progressing.
    Works
    Slow the fall in lung function by about half (INPULSIS, ASCEND, INBUILD trials)
    Cost
    PBS co-payment once approved by a lung specialist
    How long
    Long term
    Watch for
    Nintedanib: diarrhoea (common), nausea, liver blood tests. Pirfenidone: nausea, rash and sunburn, so wear sunscreen.

    They slow the scarring; they don’t reverse it.

  3. Step 2 · Other typesAvoid the trigger; steroids or immune medicinesFor hypersensitivity pneumonitis and autoimmune lung disease: remove the trigger (bird, mould, dust), then prednisolone, mycophenolate or similar.
    Works
    Often stabilises or improves inflammatory types. Steroids and azathioprine harm people with IPF, so the type matters
    Cost
    PBS co-payment
    How long
    Months to years
    Watch for
    Infections, raised blood sugar and bone thinning with steroids; regular blood tests.
  4. Step 3 · As neededOxygen, cough relief and lung transplantPortable oxygen if levels drop on walking. Low-dose morphine can ease a stubborn cough. Some people are assessed for a lung transplant.
    Works
    Ambulatory oxygen improved quality of life (AmbOx trial); low-dose morphine cut daytime cough by about 40% (PACIFY trial)
    Cost
    Home oxygen through state programs; transplant through public hospitals
    How long
    Ongoing
    Watch for
    Never smoke or be near flames with oxygen. Morphine can cause constipation and drowsiness.

    Planning ahead for breathlessness and future care is part of good treatment, not giving up.

Results are typical figures from trials, not promises. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Your specialist will tailor treatment to you.

Living with it

  • Keep moving: regular walking and strength exercise help most people feel better.
  • Pace yourself and plan rest; breathlessness is easier to manage when you don’t rush.
  • Treat reflux and sleep apnoea if you have them.
  • Avoid the triggers you know about, and wear proper protection with dusts at work.
  • Patient support groups and Lung Foundation Australia can connect you with others.

See your GP if

  • You’re getting more breathless with activity over weeks or months
  • A dry cough lasts more than 8 weeks
  • You work with stone, silica or asbestos and have any breathing symptoms
  • You have scleroderma, rheumatoid arthritis or myositis and become breathless
  • You get new breathlessness while taking amiodarone, nitrofurantoin or methotrexate

Get urgent help for sudden severe breathlessness, blue lips, chest pain, coughing up blood, or confusion: call 000 (Australia) or your local emergency number.

Outlook. It varies a lot. IPF usually worsens over years; without treatment, median survival has been about 3 to 5 years from diagnosis, and antifibrotic medicines slow the decline. Some people have sudden flare-ups that need hospital care. Many other types, especially when a trigger is removed early, stay stable or improve. Common questions

Is pulmonary fibrosis the same as IPF?

IPF is the most common type of pulmonary fibrosis. Pulmonary fibrosis is the general term for lung scarring, which can also be caused by autoimmune diseases, dusts, medicines or allergies to things breathed in.

Can pulmonary fibrosis be reversed?

Scarring can’t be undone. Medicines can slow it, and some inflammatory types improve when the trigger is removed and treatment starts early.

Is pulmonary fibrosis contagious?

No. You can’t catch it or pass it on.

What is the life expectancy with IPF?

It varies a lot. Without treatment, median survival has been about 3 to 5 years from diagnosis, but some people live much longer, and antifibrotic medicines slow the disease.

Are antifibrotic medicines on the PBS?

Yes. Nintedanib and pirfenidone are PBS-subsidised for IPF, and nintedanib for other progressive fibrosing lung disease, once a lung specialist team confirms the diagnosis.

Does a normal chest X-ray rule it out?

No. Early scarring can be missed on a chest X-ray. A high-resolution CT scan is the key test.

Can engineered stone benchtops cause lung scarring?

Yes. Cutting engineered stone releases silica dust, which causes silicosis. Australia banned engineered stone from 1 July 2024, but anyone who worked with it should see their GP about screening.
