Lupus Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on systemic lupus erythematosus: the many ways it shows up, the tests used, and the treatments that keep it quiet.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What kind of disease is lupus?

    1. An allergy
    2. An autoimmune disease
    3. A cancer
    4. An infection
    Show answer

    Answer: An autoimmune disease. The immune system attacks the body’s own tissues: skin, joints, kidneys, blood and more.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    Who is most often affected?

    1. Women aged 15 to 45
    2. Babies and toddlers
    3. Men over 80
    4. Only elite athletes
    Show answer

    Answer: Women aged 15 to 45. About 9 in 10 people with lupus are women. It’s more common in Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander and Asian people.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Which rash is classic for lupus?

    1. Silvery plaques on the elbows
    2. A rash on the soles only
    3. A target-shaped rash
    4. A butterfly rash across the cheeks
    Show answer

    Answer: A butterfly rash across the cheeks. It often flares with sun. Many people with lupus never get it.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    Which blood test is positive in nearly all people with lupus?

    1. HbA1c
    2. Rheumatoid factor
    3. ANA (antinuclear antibody)
    4. Ferritin
    Show answer

    Answer: ANA (antinuclear antibody). A negative ANA makes lupus very unlikely. But a positive ANA is common in healthy people, so it doesn’t prove lupus.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    Which test is more specific for lupus?

    1. CRP
    2. TSH
    3. Anti-double-stranded DNA (anti-dsDNA)
    4. Vitamin D
    Show answer

    Answer: Anti-double-stranded DNA (anti-dsDNA). Anti-dsDNA and anti-Smith antibodies are much more specific. Anti-dsDNA levels can track kidney activity.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    Which medicine is recommended for almost everyone with lupus?

    1. Allopurinol
    2. Hydroxychloroquine
    3. Warfarin for all
    4. Antibiotics
    Show answer

    Answer: Hydroxychloroquine. It reduces flares and organ damage. Yearly eye checks are needed after the first five years (sooner with risk factors).

  7. Question 7 of 12

    Why does sun protection matter?

    1. Lupus makes skin immune to sunburn
    2. It doesn't matter in lupus
    3. Sun causes lupus in everyone
    4. UV light can trigger rashes and flares
    Show answer

    Answer: UV light can trigger rashes and flares. SPF 50+, hats and covering up are part of treatment.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    Which organ involvement needs regular urine checks?

    1. The kidneys (lupus nephritis)
    2. The appendix (lupus appendicitis)
    3. The gallbladder (lupus cholecystitis)
    4. The tonsils (lupus tonsillitis)
    Show answer

    Answer: The kidneys (lupus nephritis). Protein or blood in the urine can be the first sign. Early treatment protects kidney function.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    What do antiphospholipid antibodies increase the risk of?

    1. Bleeding gums only
    2. High blood sugar
    3. Hair growth
    4. Blood clots and miscarriage
    Show answer

    Answer: Blood clots and miscarriage. People with these antibodies may need blood thinners, especially in pregnancy.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    Which blood count finding is common in active lupus?

    1. Low white cells or platelets
    2. High eosinophils
    3. High haemoglobin
    4. Very high platelets
    Show answer

    Answer: Low white cells or platelets. Low counts and low complement (C3 and C4) often signal activity.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    What is drug-induced lupus?

    1. Lupus that is being treated with medicines
    2. A lupus-like illness from a medicine; settles when stopped
    3. A permanent, more severe form of lupus
    4. Lupus caused by antibiotics, in everyone who takes them
    Show answer

    Answer: A lupus-like illness from a medicine; settles when stopped. Classic causes include hydralazine, procainamide and some TNF inhibitors.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Why plan pregnancy with the lupus team?

    1. Pregnancy is impossible with lupus
    2. Lupus always worsens in pregnancy
    3. 6 months in remission first lowers risks
    4. All medicines must stop beforehand
    Show answer

    Answer: 6 months in remission first lowers risks. Hydroxychloroquine is usually continued. Some medicines, such as mycophenolate, must be stopped well before.

Common questions

Is lupus contagious?

No. It’s an autoimmune disease, not an infection.

Can lupus be cured?

Not yet, but most people live full lives with treatment and regular monitoring.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. See your GP or rheumatologist about your own care.
