POTS Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome: how it’s diagnosed, who gets it, and what actually helps.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What defines POTS in adults?

    1. A heart rate over 50 at rest
    2. Fainting at least once
    3. Heart rate rises 30+ beats a minute within 10 minutes of standing, without a big BP drop
    4. Blood pressure over 160
    Show answer

    Answer: Heart rate rises 30+ beats a minute within 10 minutes of standing, without a big BP drop. In teenagers (12 to 19) the rise needs to be 40 or more. Symptoms must be present for at least 3 months.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    What simple test can a GP do to check for POTS?

    1. Blood glucose
    2. An active stand test (lie, then stand for 10 minutes)
    3. Chest X-ray
    4. A treadmill test
    Show answer

    Answer: An active stand test (lie, then stand for 10 minutes). Measure heart rate and blood pressure lying, then at 1, 3, 5 and 10 minutes standing. Leaning against a wall (the NASA lean test) works too.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Who most commonly gets POTS?

    1. Women aged 15 to 50
    2. Men over 70
    3. Only athletes
    4. Babies
    Show answer

    Answer: Women aged 15 to 50. About 4 in 5 are women. It often starts after a viral illness, surgery or pregnancy.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    Which recent illness has triggered many new cases?

    1. Chickenpox
    2. Hay fever
    3. Tetanus
    4. COVID-19
    Show answer

    Answer: COVID-19. POTS is one of the most common patterns in long COVID.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    What are the first-line lifestyle treatments?

    1. More fluids and salt (if blood pressure allows)
    2. Fluid restriction
    3. A low-salt diet
    4. Strict bed rest
    Show answer

    Answer: More fluids and salt (if blood pressure allows). Often 2 to 3 litres of fluid a day plus extra salt. Check with a doctor if you have high blood pressure or kidney disease.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    Which compression garments help most?

    1. Wrist bands
    2. Waist-high or abdominal compression
    3. Ankle socks
    4. A neck collar
    Show answer

    Answer: Waist-high or abdominal compression. Blood pools in the belly and legs. Squeezing the abdomen helps most.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    How should exercise be started?

    1. Hot yoga
    2. Standing sprints
    3. Lying or seated: rowing, recumbent bike or swimming
    4. Avoid exercise forever
    Show answer

    Answer: Lying or seated: rowing, recumbent bike or swimming. Upright exercise worsens symptoms at first. Gradual reconditioning helps many people improve.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    Which conditions often go along with POTS?

    1. Glaucoma
    2. Kidney stones
    3. Gout
    4. Hypermobility (hEDS) and mast cell activation
    Show answer

    Answer: Hypermobility (hEDS) and mast cell activation. Ask about joint hypermobility, easy bruising, flushing and food reactions.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    When are symptoms usually worse?

    1. Only at night
    2. Only in winter
    3. Never after meals
    4. In heat, after big carb-heavy meals, and in the morning
    Show answer

    Answer: In heat, after big carb-heavy meals, and in the morning. Smaller meals, staying cool, and sitting before standing can help.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    Which is a medicine sometimes used for POTS?

    1. Salbutamol
    2. Ivabradine or midodrine
    3. Insulin
    4. Furosemide
    Show answer

    Answer: Ivabradine or midodrine. Low-dose propranolol and fludrocortisone are also used. Choice depends on the main symptoms.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    Is POTS ‘just anxiety’?

    1. No, it’s a real disorder of the autonomic nervous system
    2. Only in young women
    3. Yes, always
    4. It’s a made-up condition
    Show answer

    Answer: No, it’s a real disorder of the autonomic nervous system. Anxiety and POTS can coexist, but the fast heart rate on standing is measurable and physical.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    What is the outlook for most people with POTS?

    1. It never changes
    2. It’s usually fatal
    3. Many improve over a few years, especially after a viral trigger
    4. It always gets worse
    Show answer

    Answer: Many improve over a few years, especially after a viral trigger. Treatment can make a big difference in the meantime.

Common questions

What does POTS stand for?

Postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome: a fast heart rate when upright, with symptoms such as dizziness, fatigue and brain fog.

Should I add salt if I think I have POTS?

Check with your GP first, especially if you have high blood pressure, heart or kidney disease.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. See your GP for a proper assessment.
