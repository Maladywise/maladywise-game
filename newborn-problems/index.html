Common Newborn and Baby Problems

Most newborn worries are common, harmless and pass with time. This is a quick guide to what’s normal, what helps, and the few signs that need a doctor quickly.

Skin and scalp

  • Newborn skin spots: milia, baby acne, newborn rash and stork marks. Leave them alone.
  • Cradle cap: greasy yellow scale on the scalp. Harmless, gone by about 12 months.
  • Eczema: dry, itchy, red patches. Moisturise often.
  • Nappy rash: frequent changes, nappy-free time and a barrier cream.

Eyes

  • Blocked tear duct: a watery, sticky eye that usually clears by 12 months.
  • Strabismus (squint): a turned eye after 4 months needs checking.
  • Congenital ptosis: a droopy eyelid from birth. Eye checks protect vision.

Feeding, tummy and crying

  • Newborn jaundice: yellow skin in the first week. Pale poo needs checking.
  • Reflux: spilling milk is common and usually settles by 12 months.
  • Crying and colic: peaks at 6 to 8 weeks, settles by 3 to 4 months.
  • Tongue-tie: treat only if it truly affects feeding.
  • Oral thrush: white patches in the mouth that don’t wipe off.

Belly button and hips

  • Belly button problems: cord care, granuloma and umbilical hernia.
  • Hip dysplasia (DDH): routine checks, and leave room for the legs when wrapping.

Get help urgently if your baby

  • Has a temperature of 38°C or more and is under 3 months old.
  • Is floppy, very sleepy, hard to wake or not feeding.
  • Has fewer than 4 to 5 wet nappies a day after the first week.
  • Is breathing fast or with effort, or has blue or pale lips.
  • Has green or bloody vomit, or a rash that doesn’t fade under a glass.
  • Call 000 in an emergency. For advice, call the Pregnancy, Birth and Baby helpline on 1800 882 436.

Common questions

What newborn problems are normal?

Many: skin spots, mild jaundice in the first week, spilling milk, sneezing, hiccups, noisy breathing when asleep, crossing eyes up to 4 months, and lots of crying in the evenings. Most pass with time.

When should I take my newborn to the doctor?

Any fever of 38°C or more under 3 months, a baby who is floppy, very sleepy or not feeding, fewer wet nappies, breathing problems, green or bloody vomit, or a rash that doesn’t fade under pressure. Trust your instincts.

Who can I call for advice about my baby?

Your GP, your child and family health nurse, or the Pregnancy, Birth and Baby helpline on 1800 882 436 (7am to midnight, 7 days). In an emergency, call 000.
