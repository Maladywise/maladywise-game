Marfan Syndrome Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on Marfan syndrome: the connective tissue condition that affects the eyes, skeleton and aorta.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What kind of condition is Marfan syndrome?

    1. An inherited connective tissue disorder
    2. A viral infection
    3. A vitamin deficiency
    4. A type of cancer
    Show answer

    Answer: An inherited connective tissue disorder. It affects fibrillin-1, a protein that gives tissues strength and elasticity.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    Which gene is involved?

    1. BRCA1
    2. FBN1
    3. HFE
    4. CFTR
    Show answer

    Answer: FBN1. Most cases are autosomal dominant; about a quarter arise from a new variant.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Which body build is typical?

    1. Average in every way
    2. Short, with broad hands
    3. Tall and slim with long limbs and fingers
    4. Very muscular
    Show answer

    Answer: Tall and slim with long limbs and fingers. Arm span often exceeds height.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    Which eye problem is characteristic?

    1. Colour blindness
    2. Glaucoma from birth in all cases
    3. Conjunctivitis
    4. A dislocated lens (ectopia lentis)
    Show answer

    Answer: A dislocated lens (ectopia lentis). Short-sightedness is also common.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    What is the most serious complication?

    1. Aortic root dilatation leading to dissection
    2. Kidney failure
    3. Diabetes
    4. Hearing loss
    Show answer

    Answer: Aortic root dilatation leading to dissection. Regular imaging and timely surgery have transformed life expectancy.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    Which test monitors the aorta?

    1. An ECG only
    2. An echocardiogram (and sometimes CT or MRI)
    3. A blood test
    4. A chest X-ray only
    Show answer

    Answer: An echocardiogram (and sometimes CT or MRI). The aortic root size guides when preventive surgery is recommended.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    Which diagnostic criteria are used?

    1. Budapest criteria
    2. Rome IV criteria
    3. Duke criteria
    4. The revised Ghent criteria
    Show answer

    Answer: The revised Ghent criteria. They combine aortic root size, lens dislocation, family history, FBN1 status and a systemic score.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    What are the wrist and thumb signs?

    1. The thumb can't bend at the knuckle
    2. Fingers turn blue and white in the cold
    3. Fingers overlap around the wrist; the thumb pokes past the palm
    4. The wrist clicks loudly when typing
    Show answer

    Answer: Fingers overlap around the wrist; the thumb pokes past the palm. The wrist (Walker–Murdoch) and thumb (Steinberg) signs reflect long, slender fingers and joint laxity.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    Which medicines are used to slow aortic enlargement?

    1. Long-term antibiotics
    2. Beta blockers or ARBs (e.g. losartan)
    3. Antihistamines
    4. Steroid tablets
    Show answer

    Answer: Beta blockers or ARBs (e.g. losartan). They reduce stress on the aortic wall.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    What type of exercise is usually advised against?

    1. Walking
    2. Cycling at an easy pace
    3. Intense isometric and contact sports
    4. Gentle swimming
    Show answer

    Answer: Intense isometric and contact sports. Heavy lifting with breath-holding sharply raises aortic pressure. Individual advice depends on aortic size.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    Why is pregnancy higher risk?

    1. Babies are always born with Marfan syndrome
    2. It isn't: pregnancy is no riskier than usual
    3. Labour always starts very early
    4. Aortic dissection risk rises, more so with a wider aorta
    Show answer

    Answer: Aortic dissection risk rises, more so with a wider aorta. Planning with cardiology and obstetrics before pregnancy is recommended.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Which other condition can look like Marfan but has a different gene?

    1. Loeys–Dietz syndrome
    2. Turner syndrome
    3. Cystic fibrosis
    4. Down syndrome
    Show answer

    Answer: Loeys–Dietz syndrome. TGF-beta pathway variants; aortic disease can be more aggressive, so genetic testing matters.

Common questions

Is Marfan syndrome curable?

No, but with monitoring and timely aortic surgery, life expectancy is now close to normal for many people.

Should family members be tested?

Yes. First-degree relatives are usually offered assessment and genetic testing if a variant is known.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. See your GP about your own symptoms or results.
