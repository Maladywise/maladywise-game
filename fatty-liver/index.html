Fact sheet

Fatty Liver (MASLD)

Extra fat stored in the liver, usually found by accident on a blood test or scan. Common, mostly silent and, in the early stages, reversible. The treatment is unglamorous: weight, movement and less alcohol.

What it is: MASLD (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease) is the new name for NAFLD. It affects up to 1 in 5 people in the UK, and travels with obesity, type 2 diabetes and heart disease.

Why it matters: it can progress from fat, to inflammation, to scarring (cirrhosis) and rarely liver cancer. Fat and inflammation can reverse; cirrhosis can’t. It also raises the risk of diabetes and heart disease, and the risk runs both ways.

Tests: liver blood tests, an ultrasound and often a FibroScan, a painless scan that measures liver stiffness to check for scarring. A biopsy is only for selected cases. Most people have no symptoms.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Liver “detoxes” and cleansesNo good evidence they treat fatty liver. Your liver is the detox.
  • Crash dietsLiver clinics advise against them. Slow, steady loss is what reverses fat.
  • Fruit juices and “healthy” smoothiesThey count as sugary drinks, which you’re advised to cut.

What you can do

  • Aim for 7–10% weight loss if you’re above a healthy weight.
  • Walk most days and add 2 strength sessions a week.
  • Swap sugary drinks and juice for water.
  • Cut back alcohol, ideally to none.
  • Ask your GP to check your blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol.

See your GP if

  • Your liver blood tests are abnormal
  • You have type 2 diabetes or obesity and have never had your liver checked
  • You feel tired with poor appetite, nausea or tummy pain

See a doctor promptly if your skin or the whites of your eyes turn yellow, your urine goes dark, or your tummy swells. These can be signs of advanced liver disease.

Common questions

Is fatty liver reversible?

In the early stages, yes. Losing 7 to 10% of body weight may reverse liver damage, and more than 10% can remove liver fat and inflammation. Scarring that has become cirrhosis does not reverse.

What is the difference between MASLD and NAFLD?

It is the same condition with a new name. MASLD stands for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, reflecting its links with weight, blood sugar and heart disease.

Can I still drink alcohol with fatty liver?

It is best to avoid alcohol where possible. If you drink, stay within low-risk limits, and ask your doctor, as some people with scarring should not drink at all.

Do I need a liver biopsy?

Usually not. Blood tests, an ultrasound and a FibroScan are the usual tests. A biopsy is only for selected cases.

What are the symptoms of fatty liver?

Usually none. Most people find out from a blood test or ultrasound done for something else. Some feel tired or have a dull ache under the right ribs. Symptoms like yellow skin or a swollen tummy mean more advanced liver disease.

How much weight do I need to lose for fatty liver?

Losing about 5% of body weight reduces liver fat, and 7 to 10% can reduce inflammation and even scarring. That’s about 5 to 10 kg for someone weighing 100 kg, lost gradually.

What foods should I avoid with fatty liver?

Cut back on sugary drinks, juice, sweets, alcohol, refined carbs and processed meats. A Mediterranean-style diet with vegetables, legumes, wholegrains, fish and olive oil helps. Coffee, 2 to 3 cups a day, is linked to less liver scarring.
