Myasthenia Gravis Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on myasthenia gravis: why muscles tire, the classic signs, tests, treatments and which medicines can make it worse.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What does myasthenia gravis attack?

    1. The brain's motor cortex
    2. The muscle fibres themselves
    3. The spinal cord's motor pathways
    4. The connection between nerves and muscles
    Show answer

    Answer: The connection between nerves and muscles. Antibodies block acetylcholine receptors at the neuromuscular junction, so signals don’t get through.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    What is the hallmark of myasthenia gravis?

    1. Pain in the muscles without weakness
    2. Weakness that gets worse with use and better with rest
    3. Numbness and pins and needles
    4. Constant weakness that never changes
    Show answer

    Answer: Weakness that gets worse with use and better with rest. ‘Fatigable weakness’: worse at the end of the day or after repeated movements.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    What is the most common first sign?

    1. A rash on the face
    2. Hearing loss in one ear
    3. Droopy eyelids or double vision
    4. Leg cramps at night
    Show answer

    Answer: Droopy eyelids or double vision. Eye muscles are involved first in most people. In some, it stays only in the eyes.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    What bedside test can improve a droopy eyelid?

    1. An ice pack on the eyelid for 2 minutes
    2. A hot water bottle on the eye
    3. Tapping the forehead firmly
    4. Shining a torch in the eye
    Show answer

    Answer: An ice pack on the eyelid for 2 minutes. Cold improves transmission at the neuromuscular junction. A simple, cheap clue.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    Which tumour is linked to myasthenia gravis?

    1. Thymoma
    2. Lymphoma
    3. Meningioma
    4. Melanoma
    Show answer

    Answer: Thymoma. Everyone with MG gets a chest CT. Removing the thymus can help even without a tumour.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    What medicine boosts signals for symptom relief?

    1. Prednisolone eye drops
    2. Pyrimethamine
    3. Pyridostigmine
    4. Propranolol
    Show answer

    Answer: Pyridostigmine. It slows breakdown of acetylcholine. Side effects: cramps, diarrhoea, drooling.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    In a myasthenic crisis, what is the best way to monitor breathing?

    1. Oxygen saturation on its own
    2. Forced vital capacity (FVC), not just oxygen sats
    3. A chest X-ray every few hours
    4. Peak flow, as used in asthma
    Show answer

    Answer: Forced vital capacity (FVC), not just oxygen sats. Sats stay normal until very late. A falling FVC means the breathing muscles are tiring.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    Which medicines can worsen myasthenia gravis?

    1. Vitamin D and calcium
    2. Saline drips and fluids
    3. Paracetamol and ibuprofen
    4. Some antibiotics, magnesium and beta blockers
    Show answer

    Answer: Some antibiotics, magnesium and beta blockers. Always tell prescribers you have MG. Many infections and medicines can trigger a flare.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    Which blood test helps confirm myasthenia gravis?

    1. Troponin
    2. Acetylcholine receptor antibodies
    3. Rheumatoid factor
    4. Thyroid antibodies only
    Show answer

    Answer: Acetylcholine receptor antibodies. Positive in about 85% of generalised MG. Others have MuSK antibodies.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    A similar condition that improves with repeated use and is linked to lung cancer is called?

    1. Multiple sclerosis
    2. Guillain–Barré syndrome
    3. Motor neurone disease
    4. Lambert–Eaton syndrome
    Show answer

    Answer: Lambert–Eaton syndrome. Lambert–Eaton affects calcium channels and often comes with small cell lung cancer.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    What can happen to babies of mothers with myasthenia gravis?

    1. Nothing at all
    2. Permanent deafness
    3. Temporary weakness from the mother’s antibodies
    4. Lifelong myasthenia, always
    Show answer

    Answer: Temporary weakness from the mother’s antibodies. Transient neonatal myasthenia affects about 1 in 10. It fades over weeks as antibodies clear.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Which newer treatments target the antibodies themselves?

    1. FcRn blockers and complement inhibitors
    2. Statins and fish oil
    3. Broad-spectrum antibiotics
    4. Antiviral medicines
    Show answer

    Answer: FcRn blockers and complement inhibitors. Steroids and other immunosuppressants are still the mainstay, but new options are changing care.

Common questions

Is myasthenia gravis inherited?

Usually not. It’s an autoimmune condition, though some rare congenital forms are genetic.

Can people with myasthenia gravis live normally?

With treatment, most people have good control and a normal life expectancy.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. Talk to your doctor about your own care.
