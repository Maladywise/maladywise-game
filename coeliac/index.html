Fact sheet

Coeliac Disease

An immune reaction to gluten that damages the lining of the small bowel. About 1 in 70 Australians have it, most without knowing. Symptoms range from bloating and low iron to nothing at all.

Test before you quit gluten. Keep eating it (adults about 4 slices of wheat bread a day, children about half) for at least 6 weeks. A blood test comes first; a gastroscopy with small bowel samples usually confirms it. A negative gene test rules it out.

What you can do

  • Ask parents, brothers, sisters and children to get tested: about 1 in 10 also have it, often without symptoms.
  • Have blood tests at about 6 and 12 months, then yearly, to check antibodies are falling and levels are normal.
  • Adults may need a bone density scan.
  • Check medicines and supplements for gluten with your pharmacist.

Skip these

  • Going gluten-free before testing: makes the tests unreliable, and you may need weeks back on gluten to be tested properly.
  • “Gluten-digesting” enzyme pills: don’t protect the bowel and are no substitute for the diet.
  • Oats without guidance: some people react even to uncontaminated oats. Only try them with your doctor’s advice.

See your GP if

  • Symptoms continue despite a strict gluten-free diet
  • You have another autoimmune condition, such as type 1 diabetes or thyroid disease, and haven’t been tested
  • A family member has coeliac disease

See a doctor promptly for unexplained weight loss, blood in your poo, trouble swallowing or vomiting that won’t settle. These need checking for other causes.

Common questions

How is coeliac disease tested?

It starts with a blood test. If positive, a gastroscopy (camera test) with small bowel samples usually confirms it. You need to be eating gluten for the tests to be accurate.

Should I go gluten-free before testing?

No. It makes the tests unreliable. Adults need about 4 slices of wheat bread a day (children about half) for at least 6 weeks before testing.

Can people with coeliac disease eat oats?

Some people react to oats even when uncontaminated, and in Australia oats can’t be labelled gluten free. Only try them with your doctor’s guidance.

Should my family be tested for coeliac disease?

Yes. About 1 in 10 parents, brothers, sisters and children of someone with coeliac disease also have it, often without symptoms.
