Fact sheet

Vitamin B12 deficiency

What it is

Vitamin B12 helps make red blood cells and keeps your nerves healthy. When levels are low, you can become anaemic and your nerves can be damaged. Nerve damage may not fully recover if treatment is delayed.

Common symptoms

  • Tiredness and weakness
  • Pins and needles or numbness in the hands or feet
  • Poor balance, especially in the dark
  • A sore, smooth or red tongue
  • Memory problems, low mood or irritability

Common causes

  • A vegan diet, or a vegetarian diet with few eggs or dairy foods
  • Pernicious anaemia, an autoimmune condition that stops B12 being absorbed
  • Long-term metformin (for diabetes) or reflux medicines
  • Stomach or bowel surgery, coeliac disease or Crohn’s disease
  • Heavy alcohol use, and older age

Tests

A blood test checks your B12 level and blood count. Your GP may do more tests to find the cause.

Treatment

  • Injections are usual if you can’t absorb B12 or have nerve symptoms. You’ll have several close together, then one every few months, often for life.
  • High-dose B12 tablets can work when the cause is diet.
  • Many people feel better within weeks. Nerve symptoms can take longer to improve.
  • Don’t stop injections without talking to your GP, even if you feel well.

What you can do

B12 is found in meat, fish, eggs and dairy foods. If you’re vegan, take a B12 supplement or eat fortified foods such as some plant milks and yeast spreads. If you take metformin long-term, ask your GP about checking your B12.

See your GP soon if you have

  • Numbness or tingling that is spreading or getting worse
  • Trouble walking, falls or poor balance
  • New confusion or memory problems
  • Symptoms that don’t improve after treatment starts

If you’re pregnant or planning a pregnancy and eat a vegan diet, check with your GP or midwife that you’re getting enough B12.
