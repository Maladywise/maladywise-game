Heart Attack Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on spotting a heart attack, what to do in the first minutes, how hospitals treat it, and getting back to life afterwards.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What usually causes a heart attack?

    1. A spasm from very low blood sugar
    2. A clot on a cholesterol plaque blocks a heart artery
    3. A heart valve that tears and leaks
    4. A sudden fright that stops the heart
    Show answer

    Answer: A clot on a cholesterol plaque blocks a heart artery. Without blood flow, heart muscle starts dying within minutes. Time is muscle.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    Chest discomfort for how long means call 000?

    1. More than 10 minutes, or severe or getting worse
    2. Wait until the next day and see your GP
    3. Only if you also faint or collapse
    4. More than 2 hours, even if it's mild
    Show answer

    Answer: More than 10 minutes, or severe or getting worse. Heart Foundation advice: don’t drive yourself. Ambulance crews can start treatment on the way.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    What can you take while waiting for the ambulance, unless allergic?

    1. Two paracetamol
    2. An antacid
    3. Chew 300 mg of aspirin
    4. A glass of red wine
    Show answer

    Answer: Chew 300 mg of aspirin. Aspirin stops platelets building the clot bigger. Check with the 000 operator if unsure.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    Which heart attack symptoms are more common in women?

    1. Coughing up blood with a fever
    2. A spreading rash on the chest and arms
    3. Always crushing central chest pain
    4. Breathlessness, nausea, and jaw, neck or back pain
    Show answer

    Answer: Breathlessness, nausea, and jaw, neck or back pain. Women are more likely to have ‘atypical’ symptoms and to be diagnosed late.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    Which blood test shows heart muscle damage?

    1. Ferritin
    2. Lipase
    3. D-dimer
    4. Troponin
    Show answer

    Answer: Troponin. Troponin leaks from damaged heart cells. It’s repeated after a few hours to see if it’s rising.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    What is the best treatment for a STEMI (a fully blocked artery on ECG)?

    1. An emergency stent (angioplasty), ideally within 90 minutes
    2. Bed rest in hospital for a week
    3. Antibiotics through a drip
    4. Oxygen and tablets, then review
    Show answer

    Answer: An emergency stent (angioplasty), ideally within 90 minutes. If a cath lab is too far, clot-busting drugs are given instead, often in the ambulance.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    Which cholesterol medicine do almost all people get after a heart attack?

    1. A vitamin E supplement
    2. A high-intensity statin
    3. No cholesterol medicine is needed
    4. Fish oil only
    Show answer

    Answer: A high-intensity statin. Plus usually two antiplatelets, a beta blocker and an ACE inhibitor.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    What does cardiac rehab do?

    1. Replaces the need for heart medicines
    2. Is only for people aged over 80
    3. Lowers the risk of another heart attack and death
    4. Mainly helps people who were athletes
    Show answer

    Answer: Lowers the risk of another heart attack and death. Supervised exercise, education and support. Many people never get referred: ask for it.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    What is the single biggest change a smoker can make after a heart attack?

    1. Switch to light cigarettes
    2. Cut down by half
    3. Quit smoking
    4. Smoke only on weekends
    Show answer

    Answer: Quit smoking. Quitting roughly halves the risk of another heart attack.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    From what age are Heart Health Checks recommended in Australia?

    1. 65, once you reach retirement age
    2. 45 (30 for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people)
    3. 18, for every adult
    4. Only once symptoms appear
    Show answer

    Answer: 45 (30 for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people). Your GP uses blood pressure, cholesterol, diabetes and smoking status to estimate your 5-year risk.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    How long until most people can drive a private car again after a heart attack?

    1. 6 months
    2. The next day
    3. Never
    4. About 2 weeks
    Show answer

    Answer: About 2 weeks. Under Australia’s fitness-to-drive standards, commercial drivers wait longer. Check with your doctor.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Sudden heart failure after severe emotional stress, with normal arteries, is called what?

    1. Takotsubo (‘broken heart’) syndrome
    2. Pericarditis (inflamed heart lining)
    3. Stable angina (exertion chest pain)
    4. Myocarditis (viral heart muscle)
    Show answer

    Answer: Takotsubo (‘broken heart’) syndrome. Most common in older women. The heart balloons into a pot shape and usually recovers over weeks.

Common questions

Is a heart attack the same as a cardiac arrest?

No. A heart attack is a blocked artery. Cardiac arrest is the heart stopping, which needs CPR and a defibrillator.

Can young people have heart attacks?

Yes, though it’s less common. Smoking, family history, high cholesterol and drugs such as cocaine raise the risk.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. If you think someone is having a heart attack, call 000 now.
