Fact sheet

Shoulder Pain

Usually the rotator cuff tendons complaining (pain lifting the arm, lying on it) or a frozen shoulder (pain, then stiffness). Most settle with time, smart pain relief and the right exercises.

Pain lasting more than 3 months? See our chronic pain hub, with a click-through “What is pain?” explainer.

The shoulder pain ladder

Exercise is the main treatment for rotator cuff pain. Frozen shoulder gets better on its own over 1–3 years; treatment eases the ride.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careKeep it moving, ease off the aggravatorsUse the arm for everyday tasks, avoid heavy overhead work for now, and use heat or a wrapped cold pack for up to 20 minutes.
    Works
    Pain usually starts to ease within about 2 weeks; full recovery can take 6 months or more
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    Weeks to months
    Watch for
    Don’t strap or sling a sore shoulder for long unless told to; it stiffens fast.

    A shoulder kept in cotton wool turns into a frozen one.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacyPain relief (paracetamol, ibuprofen or anti-inflammatory gel)To make movement and sleep possible, not as the treatment itself.
    Works
    Short-term pain relief that helps you keep moving
    Cost
    About $5–15 a pack
    How long
    A week or two at a time
    Watch for
    Anti-inflammatories: stomach, kidney and blood pressure risks. Check with a pharmacist if you have other conditions.

    Painkillers buy you time to do the exercises. Spend it wisely.

  3. Step 3 · Allied healthShoulder strengthening exercisesRotator cuff and shoulder-blade exercises done most days, ideally with a physio to start. Frozen shoulder: gentle stretches within comfort.
    Works
    In a Swedish trial, 1 in 5 doing specific exercises still chose surgery, vs 3 in 5 doing general exercises
    Cost
    Free at home with our handout; physio about $80–120 a session (Medicare rebates with a GP care plan)
    How long
    At least 12 weeks
    Watch for
    Some discomfort (up to 5/10) is fine if it settles by the next day.

    The boring exercises are the ones that work. Sorry.

  4. Step 4 · PrescriptionSteroid injectionInto the space above the rotator cuff, or into the joint for frozen shoulder, often guided by ultrasound.
    Works
    Rotator cuff: modest relief, best at about 2 months and gone by 3. Frozen shoulder: less pain for 3–6 months, best given early
    Cost
    Medicare rebates; out-of-pocket fees vary
    How long
    Usually one, sometimes repeated; not many a year
    Watch for
    Short-lived flare, skin thinning or paler skin at the site, and raised blood sugar for a few days in diabetes.

    A window of relief to get exercising, not a fix on its own.

  5. Step 5 · SpecialistSpecialist proceduresFor frozen shoulder that stays severe: manipulation under anaesthetic or keyhole release. For a tendon tear after a fall or sudden weakness: surgical review.
    Works
    Frozen shoulder: in a UK trial, physio plus injection, manipulation and keyhole release all worked about equally at a year; about 1 in 7 after physio needed more treatment, vs 1 in 25 after surgery
    Cost
    Public waiting lists; private fees vary
    How long
    Recovery over weeks to months, with exercises afterwards
    Watch for
    Surgery has the most complications and costs the most. A sudden tear in a younger, active person needs prompt review.

    Bigger isn’t automatically better. Ask what each option adds.

Costs are rough Australian prices in November 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials, not promises.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Decompression surgery for impingementIn a large UK trial, shaving bone off the shoulder did no better than a fake (look-only) operation. Exercise first.
  • Therapeutic ultrasoundAdds no useful benefit to exercise in trials. The machine hums; the tendon doesn’t care.
  • TENS machinesEvidence is very low quality and uncertain. Fine if it helps you move, but don’t buy one expecting a cure.
  • Repeated injectionsRepeat injections were no better than one in the short term, and relief fades. Use them to start rehab, not instead of it.

What you can do

  • Follow our shoulder impingement exercise handout: one printable page of rotator cuff and shoulder-blade exercises with pain rules. For a tendon tear, or if moving hurts too much to start, try our rotator cuff exercises, which begin with gentle holds.
  • Keep using the arm for light daily tasks below shoulder height.
  • Sleep on the other side with a pillow hugged in front to support the sore arm.
  • At work, bring tasks closer and lower so you’re not reaching overhead for long.
  • With diabetes, keep sugars well controlled; frozen shoulder is more common and can last longer.
  • Stiff in every direction? Read our frozen shoulder fact sheet and frozen shoulder exercises.

See your GP if

  • Pain is getting worse, or not starting to ease after 2 weeks
  • You can’t lift your arm properly, or it’s waking you most nights
  • Pins and needles or pain going down the arm, which may be coming from your neck
  • Pain in both shoulders, or you feel generally unwell with it

Get urgent help for shoulder or arm pain with chest pain, breathlessness, sweating or nausea, which can be a heart attack: call 000 (Australia) or your local emergency number. From a mobile, 112 works in most countries. See a doctor the same day after a fall or injury if the shoulder looks deformed, you can’t move or lift the arm, or the arm is numb, cold or a different colour; or for a hot, swollen shoulder with fever.

Common questions

What are the symptoms of rotator cuff pain?

An ache in the outer upper arm, worse lifting the arm out to the side or overhead, reaching behind your back, or lying on that side at night. Movement is usually still possible, just painful.

How is frozen shoulder different?

It starts with pain, then the shoulder gets stiff in every direction, so even with help it won’t move far. It’s more common in people with diabetes, and usually settles over 1–3 years, sometimes longer.

Do I need a scan?

Usually not to start. Your GP can usually tell from examining your shoulder. Ultrasound or X-ray helps if it isn’t improving, after an injury, or if an injection or surgery is being considered.

What causes it?

Often overload: a sudden increase in lifting, painting, gardening, sport or overhead work, on top of the tendon changes that come with age. Frozen shoulder may follow an injury or surgery, but often has no clear cause.

How long does frozen shoulder last?

Usually 1 to 3 years, moving through painful, stiff and thawing phases. Most people regain most of their movement. An injection in the painful early phase and gentle exercise can ease pain and speed things up.

Why does my shoulder hurt more at night?

Lying on the sore side squashes the irritated tendons, and there’s less movement to keep things comfortable. Sleeping on your back or good side with a pillow hugging the sore arm can help.

Does a cortisone injection help shoulder pain?

It can ease rotator cuff and frozen shoulder pain for several weeks, which can make exercise easier. Benefit beyond a few months is limited, and repeated injections may weaken tendons.
