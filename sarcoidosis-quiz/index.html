Sarcoidosis Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on sarcoidosis: what granulomas are, where they show up and who needs treatment.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What forms in sarcoidosis?

    1. Granulomas: small clumps of inflammatory cells
    2. Cancerous tumours
    3. Small blood clots
    4. Kidney stones only
    Show answer

    Answer: Granulomas: small clumps of inflammatory cells. They can form in any organ but most often the lungs and lymph nodes.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    Which organ is affected in about 9 in 10 people?

    1. The appendix
    2. The lungs and chest lymph nodes
    3. The bladder
    4. The spleen alone
    Show answer

    Answer: The lungs and chest lymph nodes. Often found on a chest X-ray done for another reason.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    What is the classic chest X-ray finding?

    1. A single large lung mass
    2. Fluid around the heart
    3. Swollen lymph nodes at both lung roots
    4. A collapsed lung on one side
    Show answer

    Answer: Swollen lymph nodes at both lung roots. Called bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    What is Löfgren syndrome?

    1. Sarcoidosis affecting the brain and nerves
    2. A form of lung cancer that mimics sarcoid
    3. A drug reaction to sarcoidosis treatment
    4. Fever, red shin lumps, sore ankles, swollen chest nodes
    Show answer

    Answer: Fever, red shin lumps, sore ankles, swollen chest nodes. It usually settles on its own within months.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    Which eye problem can sarcoidosis cause?

    1. Short-sightedness
    2. Colour blindness
    3. Uveitis (inflammation inside the eye)
    4. Cataracts from birth
    Show answer

    Answer: Uveitis (inflammation inside the eye). Red, painful eyes or blurred vision need urgent review. Yearly eye checks are recommended.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    Which blood level can be high in sarcoidosis?

    1. Platelets
    2. Calcium
    3. Potassium
    4. Haemoglobin
    Show answer

    Answer: Calcium. Granulomas make active vitamin D, which raises calcium. Avoid high-dose vitamin D unless your team advises it.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    Which blood test is often raised but not diagnostic?

    1. Troponin
    2. PSA
    3. Lipase
    4. ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme)
    Show answer

    Answer: ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme). It’s non-specific, so the diagnosis rests on symptoms, imaging and biopsy.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    How is the diagnosis usually confirmed?

    1. A biopsy showing non-caseating granulomas
    2. A skin prick test
    3. A stool test
    4. A urine dipstick
    Show answer

    Answer: A biopsy showing non-caseating granulomas. Other causes of granulomas, such as TB and fungal infection, must be excluded.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    Does everyone with sarcoidosis need treatment?

    1. Yes, always lifelong
    2. Yes, with surgery
    3. No. Many improve on their own
    4. Yes, with antibiotics
    Show answer

    Answer: No. Many improve on their own. Treatment is for symptoms or risk to organs such as eyes, heart, nerves or kidneys.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    What is the usual first treatment when needed?

    1. Prednisolone
    2. Amoxicillin
    3. Insulin
    4. Aspirin alone
    Show answer

    Answer: Prednisolone. Methotrexate or other agents may be added to reduce the steroid dose.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    Why does cardiac sarcoidosis matter?

    1. It only causes chest itch
    2. It only lowers blood pressure
    3. It's always harmless
    4. It can cause heart block and dangerous rhythms
    Show answer

    Answer: It can cause heart block and dangerous rhythms. Palpitations, fainting or ECG changes need cardiac MRI or PET scanning.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Which skin lesion on the nose and cheeks is linked to chronic sarcoidosis?

    1. Port-wine stain
    2. Lupus pernio
    3. Acne rosacea
    4. Cold sores
    Show answer

    Answer: Lupus pernio. Purple, swollen plaques on the face. It suggests long-lasting disease.

Common questions

Is sarcoidosis cancer?

No. Granulomas are inflammation, not cancer.

Is it contagious?

No. The cause is unknown, but it doesn’t spread between people.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. See your GP or specialist about your own care.
