Female Infertility

Trying for a baby and nothing’s happening? Most couples conceive within a year, but about 1 in 6 need help. Age matters most, so if you’re 35 or older, see your GP after 6 months, not 12.

When to see your GP

  • After 12 months of trying, or 6 months if you’re 35 or older
  • Straight away with irregular or absent periods, known endometriosis or PCOS, past pelvic infection, or a partner with a known sperm problem
  • Planning ahead? A pre-pregnancy check (folic acid, vaccines, health conditions) is worth it anyway

‘Just relax and it'll happen’ has never once been a fertility treatment.

Common causes

  • Age: egg number and quality fall, especially after 35 and faster after 40
  • Ovulation problems: PCOS is the most common; also thyroid, high prolactin, very low weight or intense exercise
  • Endometriosis
  • Blocked tubes: past chlamydia or pelvic infection, or surgery
  • Uterine problems: some fibroids or polyps
  • Male factor in about half of couples: the semen test is part of your workup
  • Unexplained in about 1 in 4 couples

Tests

  • Progesterone 7 days before an expected period to check ovulation, plus thyroid, prolactin and other hormones
  • Pelvic ultrasound, and a dye test of the tubes (HyCoSy or HSG)
  • Chlamydia test, and a semen analysis for your partner
  • AMH estimates egg number, not quality or your chance of natural pregnancy

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • AMH as a “fertility test”It estimates egg number, not your chance of natural pregnancy. A low AMH doesn’t mean you can’t conceive naturally.
  • Endometrial scratchingA large randomised trial found no increase in live births.
  • Most IVF add-onsEmbryo glue, assisted hatching and immune therapies mostly lack good evidence. Ask what each adds and the proof.
  • Fertility cleanses and detox teasNo evidence, and some contain laxatives or herbs that are unsafe in early pregnancy.

Common questions

How long should we try before seeing a doctor?

12 months if you’re under 35, 6 months if you’re 35 or older, and straight away if you have irregular periods, endometriosis, PCOS or a known sperm problem.

What is the most common cause of female infertility?

Age is the biggest factor overall. Of treatable causes, ovulation problems (most often PCOS) are the most common.

Does a low AMH mean I can’t get pregnant?

No. AMH estimates the number of eggs left, not their quality or your chance of natural pregnancy.
