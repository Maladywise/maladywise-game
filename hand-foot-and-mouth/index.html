Fact sheet

Hand, Foot and Mouth Disease

A common, mild virus in young children: mouth ulcers plus spots or small blisters on the hands, feet and bottom. It usually clears in 7 to 10 days. It has nothing to do with foot-and-mouth disease in animals.

What it is

  • A viral infection, usually from a Coxsackie virus. It spreads easily in childcare through coughs, sneezes, saliva, blister fluid and poo.
  • Starts with a mild fever, tiredness and sore mouth, then mouth ulcers and small spots or blisters on the hands, feet, nappy area and sometimes legs.
  • It usually lasts 7 to 10 days.
  • Some children shed fingernails or toenails a few weeks later. They regrow normally.

Care at home

  • There’s no specific treatment, and antibiotics don’t help a virus.
  • Offer small, frequent sips of fluid. Cold drinks, ice blocks and soft foods are easier on a sore mouth.
  • Paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain. Don’t give aspirin to children.
  • Leave blisters to dry. Don’t pop them.

Stopping the spread

  • Keep your child home from childcare or school until the blisters have dried.
  • The virus can stay in poo for weeks, so wash hands well after nappy changes and toileting.
  • Don’t share cups, cutlery or towels.

See a doctor if

  • Your child isn’t drinking, has fewer wet nappies or seems dehydrated.
  • Spots look infected: larger, red, swollen or oozing pus.
  • Get help urgently if your child is very drowsy, pale, has a stiff neck, trouble breathing, a seizure, or a rash that doesn’t fade when pressed.

Common questions

How long is hand, foot and mouth disease contagious?

It spreads most easily in the first week, but the virus can be in poo for weeks. Keep children home until blisters have dried, and keep up handwashing.

Is it the same as foot-and-mouth disease in animals?

No. They’re different viruses. People don’t catch hand, foot and mouth disease from animals.

Can adults get hand, foot and mouth disease?

Yes, though it’s less common. Adults may have no symptoms or a mild illness.

Why are my child’s nails falling off after hand, foot and mouth?

Nail shedding can happen a few weeks after the illness. It’s harmless and the nails grow back.
