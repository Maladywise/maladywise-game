Addison’s Disease Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on Addison’s disease: what the adrenal glands do, how it’s found, daily treatment and the sick day rules that save lives.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What goes wrong in Addison’s disease?

    1. The kidneys stop filtering
    2. The thyroid stops working
    3. The adrenal glands stop making cortisol and aldosterone
    4. The pancreas makes too much insulin
    Show answer

    Answer: The adrenal glands stop making cortisol and aldosterone. Cortisol handles stress. Aldosterone holds on to salt and water.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    What is the most common cause in Australia?

    1. Too much salt
    2. Autoimmune damage to the adrenal glands
    3. Tuberculosis
    4. Viral hepatitis
    Show answer

    Answer: Autoimmune damage to the adrenal glands. TB is still a common cause worldwide.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Which skin change is a classic sign?

    1. Silver scales
    2. Very pale skin everywhere
    3. Bright yellow skin
    4. Darkening in skin creases, gums and scars
    Show answer

    Answer: Darkening in skin creases, gums and scars. High ACTH levels stimulate pigment cells. It’s often mistaken for a tan.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    Which blood results are typical?

    1. Low sodium and high potassium
    2. High sodium and low potassium
    3. Normal results always
    4. High calcium only
    Show answer

    Answer: Low sodium and high potassium. From losing aldosterone. Low blood sugar can happen too.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    What is the emergency treatment for an adrenal crisis?

    1. Adrenaline only
    2. Insulin
    3. Wait for blood tests
    4. Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV or IM, plus IV fluids
    Show answer

    Answer: Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV or IM, plus IV fluids. Don’t delay treatment for tests. Crisis can kill within hours.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    What is the usual daily replacement?

    1. Hydrocortisone (plus fludrocortisone)
    2. Thyroxine
    3. Metformin
    4. Prednisolone eye drops
    Show answer

    Answer: Hydrocortisone (plus fludrocortisone). Taken in 2 or 3 doses a day to mimic the body’s natural rhythm.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    What are the sick day rules?

    1. Halve the dose
    2. Double or triple the dose with fever, and inject if vomiting
    3. Stop all medicines when sick
    4. Only drink water
    Show answer

    Answer: Double or triple the dose with fever, and inject if vomiting. Everyone with Addison’s should carry an emergency injection kit and know how to use it.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    Which craving is a clue?

    1. Ice
    2. Sugar only
    3. Salt
    4. Spicy food
    Show answer

    Answer: Salt. Losing aldosterone means losing salt.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    Which US president had Addison’s disease?

    1. Richard Nixon
    2. Abraham Lincoln
    3. John F. Kennedy
    4. Barack Obama
    Show answer

    Answer: John F. Kennedy. Kept secret during his presidency. He was treated with steroids.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    Which test confirms the diagnosis?

    1. A Synacthen (ACTH stimulation) test
    2. A urine pregnancy test
    3. A chest X-ray
    4. An MRI of the knee
    Show answer

    Answer: A Synacthen (ACTH stimulation) test. Synthetic ACTH is given. Healthy adrenals respond with a rise in cortisol; in Addison’s they don’t.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    Suddenly stopping long-term steroid tablets can cause what?

    1. Nothing, steroids can always stop suddenly
    2. Adrenal insufficiency, like Addison’s
    3. Diabetes
    4. High blood pressure
    Show answer

    Answer: Adrenal insufficiency, like Addison’s. Long-term steroids switch off your own cortisol production. Taper slowly, with medical advice.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    What should people with Addison’s wear or carry?

    1. Nothing special
    2. A heart monitor
    3. Sunglasses at all times
    4. A medical alert bracelet and an emergency card
    Show answer

    Answer: A medical alert bracelet and an emergency card. Paramedics and ED staff need to know straight away to give hydrocortisone.

Common questions

Is Addison’s disease rare?

Yes. About 1 in 10,000 people. It’s often diagnosed late because early symptoms are vague.

Can people with Addison’s live normally?

Yes. With daily replacement and good sick day plans, most people lead full lives.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. Follow your own endocrinologist’s plan.
