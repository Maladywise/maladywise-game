What the score means

  • 0No ACEs from this list. Doesn’t rule out other hard experiences.
  • 1 to 3Common: about 2 in 3 adults have at least one.
  • 4 or moreAbout 1 in 6 US adults. Linked with higher average risk of some health problems.

What the research shows

  • The original study: over 9,500 adults in a US health plan (1998). People with 4 or more categories had 4 to 12 times the rates of alcohol and drug problems, depression and suicide attempts, and more heart, lung and liver disease.
  • Later reviews: a 2017 meta-analysis (37 studies, about 250,000 people) found the strongest links were with drug problems, violence and self-harm, and weaker links with obesity, inactivity and diabetes.
  • In Australia: a 2023 national study of 8,503 people found 62% had experienced at least one type of child maltreatment, and 39% more than one type.
  • It can’t predict individuals: in two long-running cohorts (UK and New Zealand), ACE scores were poor at picking which person would become unwell, only a little better than a coin toss.

Good to know

  • The score treats every item the same. One severe experience can matter more than several milder ones.
  • It leaves out many adversities, such as bullying, racism, discrimination, poverty, serious illness, war, or being in out-of-home care.
  • Good things count too. Positive childhood experiences, like feeling heard and safe with at least one adult, are linked with better adult mental health, even after ACEs.
  • Trauma-focused therapies help. A GP can talk through options, check how you’re going now, and refer you on.
  • For clinicians: asking about ACEs works best inside trauma-informed care, with time to respond. Don’t use the score on its own to make treatment decisions.

Common questions

What is an ACE score?

A count from 0 to 10 of the types of adverse childhood experience someone had before 18: abuse, neglect, and household problems such as domestic violence, a parent’s mental illness or substance use, separation, or prison.

What is a high ACE score?

A score of 4 or more is usually called high. It’s linked with higher average rates of mental health problems, substance use and some chronic diseases. It doesn’t mean a person will become unwell.

Is the ACE test a diagnosis?

No. It’s a short questionnaire, not a test for any condition. It can’t diagnose trauma, PTSD or anything else, and it’s poor at predicting an individual’s future health.

How common are ACEs?

Very common. In US surveys, about 64% of adults reported at least one ACE and about 17% reported four or more. An Australian national study found 62% had experienced at least one type of child maltreatment.

Can the effects of ACEs be reversed?

Many people with ACEs live well. Safe, supportive relationships, treatment for mental health problems, and trauma-focused therapies all help. A GP can be a good first step.
