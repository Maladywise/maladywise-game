Thallium Poisoning

Thallium is rare today, but it’s a classic poison: odourless, tasteless and slow to show. The combination of very painful nerves followed by hair loss is the clue.

Odourless, tasteless and slow. Agatha Christie used it in a novel, and a real nurse in England spotted a real poisoning because she’d read it.

Symptoms

  • First days: tummy pain, vomiting or diarrhoea, or constipation.
  • Within a week or two: severe burning pain and numbness in the feet and legs, weakness and confusion.
  • After 2–3 weeks: hair loss, often dramatic, and pale lines across the nails.
  • Severe poisoning can affect the heart, breathing and brain, and can be fatal.

Thallium makes hair fall out two or three weeks after poisoning. That delay is what fools people.

Who’s at risk today

  • Deliberate poisoning (still reported worldwide)
  • Some imported products or contaminated herbal or traditional medicines
  • Rare workplace exposure in electronics, optical glass or chemical manufacturing

Diagnosis and treatment

  • A urine thallium test confirms it. Doctors need to think of it, because routine blood tests look normal.
  • Treatment is Prussian blue, a dye taken by mouth that traps thallium in the gut so it’s passed out. Severe cases may need dialysis.
  • Recovery of nerves can take months and may be incomplete.

Call 000 for severe illness. For advice on a possible poisoning, call the Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26.

It's colourless, odourless and tasteless, which is exactly why poisoners liked it.

Weird history

  • In early-1950s Sydney, thallium rat poison was easy to buy during a rat plague. Several notorious poisonings followed, including Caroline Grills, who killed four relatives with thallium-laced treats, and an attempted poisoning of rugby league player Bobby Lulham, who survived.
  • Thallium went back on the NSW poisons list in 1954.
  • Agatha Christie’s novel The Pale Horse described thallium poisoning so accurately that a reader is said to have recognised a real case from it.

Common questions

What are the symptoms of thallium poisoning?

Tummy symptoms in the first days, then severe burning pain and numbness in the legs, followed 2–3 weeks later by hair loss. Severe poisoning can be fatal.

How is thallium poisoning treated?

With Prussian blue, taken by mouth, which binds thallium in the gut so it’s passed out. Severe cases may need dialysis.

Is thallium still used as rat poison?

Not in Australia. It was restricted again in 1954 after a series of poisonings in Sydney, and it is banned for this use in many countries.
