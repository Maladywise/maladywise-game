Fact sheet

Haemorrhoids

Swollen blood vessels in and around the anus. Very common, especially with constipation, straining and pregnancy. They can cause bright red bleeding, itch, a lump or discomfort. Most settle with softer stools.

Treatment options

Ratings show how strong the research is.

  • Strong evidenceRubber band ligationA tiny band on an internal haemorrhoid cuts off its blood supply so it drops off. A quick procedure for ongoing bleeding or prolapse.
  • Strong evidenceSurgeryFor large or persistent haemorrhoids. Most effective long-term, but recovery is more painful.
  • Moderate evidenceFibre and waterAim for 25 to 30 g of fibre a day from fruit, vegetables, legumes and wholegrains, or a supplement such as psyllium. Roughly halves bleeding.
  • Limited evidenceHaemorrhoid creams and suppositoriesPharmacy products with a numbing agent or mild steroid ease itch and pain short-term. Use for no more than a week at a time.
  • Limited evidenceWarm bathsSit in warm water for 10 to 15 minutes, 2 to 3 times a day, to ease pain.
  • Limited evidenceClot removalA sudden, very painful purple lump is a clotted external haemorrhoid. It settles over 1 to 2 weeks, or a doctor can remove the clot in the first few days.
  • Not recommendedCodeine painkillersCause constipation and make haemorrhoids worse.

What you can do

  • Go when you feel the urge. Don’t strain or sit on the toilet scrolling your phone.
  • Raise your knees on a footstool.
  • Paracetamol for pain, and a stool softener if needed.
  • If you’re 45 to 74, do the free bowel screening test every 2 years.

See your GP if

  • You notice bleeding from the bottom that hasn’t been checked
  • Blood is dark or mixed through the stool, or comes with a change in bowel habit, weight loss or tiredness
  • No better after 2 weeks, or pain is severe (could be a fissure or abscess)

Go to the emergency department for heavy bleeding, feeling faint, or a hot, very painful lump with fever.

Common questions

How long do haemorrhoids last?

Most settle once stools are softer. A sudden, very painful purple lump (a clotted haemorrhoid) usually settles over 1 to 2 weeks. See your GP if you’re no better after 2 weeks.

Do haemorrhoid creams work?

Pharmacy creams and suppositories ease itch and pain in the short term. Use them for no more than a week at a time. More fibre and water roughly halves bleeding.

Is bleeding from the bottom always haemorrhoids?

No. Get any bleeding checked, especially dark blood, blood mixed through the stool, or a change in bowel habit, weight loss or tiredness. If you’re 45 to 74, do the free bowel screening test every 2 years.

What is haemorrhoid banding?

A tiny rubber band is placed on an internal haemorrhoid to cut off its blood supply so it drops off. It’s a quick procedure for ongoing bleeding or prolapse.
