Meet the mascots

Every Malady Wise puzzle has a mascot, and every part of the site has a host. Here they all are, with the back stories nobody asked for.

All characters are fictional. Any resemblance to your registrar is a coincidence.

Game mascots

  • Dxtective

    Dex

    A fox detective

    Loves a mystery more than anything. Smug, dramatic and fond of a monologue. Secretly checks her answers twice.

    Read her story

    Dex grew up in the back room of a village library, reading every whodunit twice: once for fun, once to catch the author cheating. She trained as a doctor because patients turned out to be the best mysteries of all, each one a locked room with the clues hidden in plain sight. She keeps a corkboard of unsolved cases at home, complete with red string, and is no longer allowed to bring it to work. The deerstalker was a thank-you gift from her first zebra diagnosis, and she has not taken it off since.

    Says when you win: “Case closed. Elementary.” · “Diagnosis nailed. Frame it.” · “Sharp work, detective.” · “The butler didn't do it. You did.”

  • Wordemic

    Vira

    A word germ

    Chaotic extrovert. Desperate to go viral. Has never once washed her hands of anything.

    Read her story

    Vira was the shyest germ in the Petri dish until a lab tech left the lid off one Friday night. By Monday she had spread to every word game in the building. She collects letters the way other germs collect hosts, and is quietly heartbroken that nobody wants to catch her. She insists she's "not that kind of viral" and has never been sneezed out of anywhere she didn't want to leave.

    Says when you win: “Contagious brilliance.” · “You caught it. Nicely.” · “Spreading the good word.” · “Highly infectious work.”

  • Triviosis

    Echo

    A quiz parrot

    Know-it-all trivia hound. Repeats your mistakes back to you, lovingly.

    Read her story

    Echo spent her early years perched in a university lecture theatre, quietly memorising every slide. She can recite the Krebs cycle, the cranial nerves and every Nobel Prize in Medicine since 1901, but still can't remember where she left her cracker. She once corrected a professor mid-lecture and was asked to leave. She came back the next week wearing a tiny disguise and corrected him again.

    Says when you win: “Squawk! Genius!” · “I'd repeat that score anywhere.” · “Big brain energy.” · “Polly wants your brain.”

  • Glimpsis

    Mira

    A sharp-eyed chameleon

    Quiet watcher. Blends in, then spots the one detail everyone missed.

    Read her story

    Mira grew up in a dermatology waiting room, sitting very still on a pot plant and watching every rash that came through the door. Nobody noticed her, so she noticed everything. She can tell a target lesion from a ringworm at twenty paces and turns a faint pink whenever someone says 'it's probably eczema' without looking properly. She speaks rarely, but when she does, it's the one detail that changes the diagnosis.

    Says when you win: “Eyes everywhere. Including on that.” · “Spotted. Blink and you’d miss it. You didn’t.” · “Inspection: textbook.” · “I change colour. You change outcomes.”

  • Opacitis

    Lumo

    A see-through jellyfish

    Transparent in every sense. Honest to a fault, calm, and glows when you get it right.

    Read her story

    Lumo drifted into a radiology department through a storm drain and decided to stay, because it was dark, quiet and full of other things you could see straight through. She learnt to read films by floating over the radiologists' shoulders. Being transparent means she can't hide a thing, so she tells you the finding plainly and kindly. She glows a soft blue when you get it right, which the radiographers find very helpful at 2am.

    Says when you win: “Clear as a jellyfish. Which is very clear.” · “Read it like a radiologist. Minus the dark room.” · “Opacity spotted. Brilliant.” · “No shadow of a doubt.”

  • Panelysis

    Ruby

    A blood-drop vampire (strictly for science)

    Goth, theatrical, and deadly serious about reference ranges.

    Read her story

    Ruby rose from a centrifuge tube in a hospital lab at 3am and has never once seen daylight on purpose. She wears a cape because the lab was cold, and the fangs are for finding veins, thank you. She can spot a haemolysed sample from across the room and holds very strong opinions about potassium levels drawn through a tiny needle. Her idea of a romantic evening is a full blood count with a film.

    Says when you win: “Results reviewed. Brilliantly.” · “Within normal limits of genius.” · “Flagged H, for hero.” · “Abnormally clever.”

  • Comorbix

    Octavia

    A sorting octopus

    A neat freak who labels everything, including her own arms. Stress-cleans.

    Read her story

    Octavia ran the supply cupboard of a busy coral-reef clinic for twenty years, and nobody has found anything in it since she left. Each of her eight arms has a job, a label and a colour-coded drawer. She invented Comorbix after watching a junior doctor file gout under "toe problems". She relaxes by reorganising other people's fridges, and squirts ink when anyone says "miscellaneous".

    Says when you win: “Eight arms, and you still beat me.” · “All sorted. Chef's kiss.” · “Grouped like a pro.” · “Four groups, zero chaos.”

  • Anatomegaly

    Lottie

    An axolotl with a label gun

    She regrows her own limbs, so she's very relaxed about your mistakes. Labels everything anyway.

    Read her story

    Lottie lived in the specimen jar room of an old anatomy school, where she taught herself every structure by reading the labels on the jars. When the labels started peeling, she bought a label gun and never looked back. Being an axolotl, she can regrow a lost leg, so she is very forgiving when you mix up your nerves. She has labelled her own gills, her friends and once, briefly, the Dean.

    Says when you win: “Labelled to perfection.” · “Textbook anatomy. Literally.” · “Every part in its place.” · “I'd regrow a leg for that score.”

  • Linkase

    Cadi

    A chain snake

    Smooth talker, fiercely competitive. Never lets go of a grudge or a link.

    Read her story

    Cadi grew up coiled around the stethoscope rail in a busy emergency department, listening to doctors talk in chains: this leads to that, which causes this. She started linking everything, then everyone, then refused to let go of a single connection. She is competitive, charming and a sore loser. She once held a grudge against a crossword setter for six years, and she will tell you it was justified.

    Says when you win: “Every link, locked.” · “Sssmooth chaining.” · “Unbroken. Impressive.” · “Sssensational.”

  • Zebritis

    Ziggy

    A baby zebra (secret unicorn)

    Shy, rare and a bit odd. Insists she's a horse. Loves the weird stuff most.

    Read her story

    Ziggy was born with stripes, a stubby little horn under her forelock and a firm belief that she is a perfectly ordinary horse. Doctors are taught 'when you hear hooves, think horses', so she spent her childhood being overlooked. She took it personally and now collects the rare and the odd: syndromes with three-word names, signs nobody has seen in decades, the case report everyone skimmed. The horn stays under the fringe. Mostly.

    Says when you win: “Hooves up for that!” · “Stripes earned.” · “Thinking zebras. Love it.” · “Rare finds only.”

  • Gridectomy

    Tess

    A crossword tortoise

    Unflappable. Very old. Does crosswords in pen, never hurries, is rarely wrong.

    Read her story

    Tess has done the crossword every morning for 147 years, always in pen, always with a cup of tea that's gone cold. She spent most of them as the ward tortoise in a country hospital, where the nurses read her the clues and she answered with a slow, certain nod. She has outlived three hospital buildings and two medical schools. She is never in a hurry and, irritatingly, never wrong.

    Says when you win: “Slow and steady? You were fast.” · “Grid cleared. Shell yeah.” · “Every square, filled.” · “Totally shell-ebrated.”

  • Suturix

    Silk

    A stitching spider

    Perfectionist surgeon with opinions about your knots. Dramatic about neatness.

    Read her story

    Silk was raised in the corner of an operating theatre, where she watched surgeons stitch and quietly judged every knot. She found most of them sloppy. She has eight hands, never trembles, and can close a wound so neatly you'd need a magnifying glass to find the scar. She is dramatic about neatness and has fainted, twice, at a granny knot. Her web, naturally, is a work of art.

    Says when you win: “Neat stitches. No scars.” · “Sewn up beautifully.” · “Tidy work, surgeon.” · “Web of genius.”

  • Morphemia

    Bea

    A nurse bee

    The heart of the ward. Runs on tea, notices everything, firm but very kind.

    Read her story

    Bea was the head nurse of a very busy hive before she ever set foot on a ward. She ran the night shift, fed the babies, spotted the sick ones early and kept the queen's paperwork in order. When she moved to a hospital, it felt like home: busy, warm and full of people who needed looking after. She runs on honey and tea, notices everything, and will tell you kindly but firmly to sit down and drink some water.

    Says when you win: “Bee-autiful work.” · “Buzzing about that.” · “Sweet as honey.” · “Queen bee energy.”

  • Sensitosis

    Sunny

    A meerkat on lookout duty

    Always on watch. First question about any test: what's the false-positive rate?

    Read her story

    Sunny was the lookout for a big meerkat family in the Kalahari, where a false alarm meant everyone dived into the burrow for nothing and a missed eagle meant something much worse. That's how she learnt sensitivity and specificity, before she knew the words. Now she stands guard over every test and asks the awkward question first: how often is it wrong? She has never trusted a screening test that 'catches everything'.

    Says when you win: “Spotted. Every true positive.” · “Sharp eyes. Sharper stats.” · “Sensitive and specific. Rare combo.” · “No false alarms here.”

  • Sudokrine

    Iris

    A night-owl endocrinologist

    A precise night owl who loves a good feedback loop. Can't stand sloppy logic.

    Read her story

    Iris works nights in an endocrine lab, which suits an owl perfectly. She fell in love with hormones because they run on rules: too much of this, so less of that, and everything settles back into balance. Sudoku is the same, which is why she keeps a grid on her desk at all times. She has no patience for sloppy logic and once rewrote a guideline flowchart in red pen, at 4am, for fun.

    Says when you win: “Perfectly balanced. Like a good thyroid.” · “Hoo’s a clever one? You are.” · “Every gland in its place.” · “Homeostasis achieved.”

  • Arrhythmix

    Riff

    A heart with headphones

    A drummer at heart. Keeps perfect time and can't stand a skipped beat.

    Read her story

    Riff started life as a heart in a school band, keeping time on the drums at a steady 72 beats a minute. One day the bass player's heart went into atrial fibrillation mid-song, and Riff noticed before anyone. She has worn headphones ever since and listens for skipped beats everywhere: in music, in conversation, in the queue at the coffee shop. Nothing irritates her more than a drummer who rushes.

    Says when you win: “You’ve got the beat.” · “Calipers? Never needed them.” · “Every P wave accounted for.” · “Read in perfect time.”

  • Cryptoplexy

    Cipher

    A masked cat

    Mysterious. Speaks in riddles. Steals pens.

    Read her story

    Cipher arrived at the hospital one night through a window nobody remembered leaving open, and nobody has seen her face since. She speaks in riddles, leaves cryptic clues on the whiteboard and steals every pen in the doctors' room. Some say she was a code-breaker once. Others say she's just a cat. She doesn't confirm either, but the crossword on Saturday always has her pawprints on it.

    Says when you win: “Cracked it. Purr-fect.” · “Nine lives, one crossword.” · “Devious. I approve.” · “Cryptic? Not to you.”

  • Grand Rounds

    Locum

    A night-shift bat

    Sleep-deprived night-shifter with a very dry sense of humour. Powered by vending machine coffee.

    Read her story

    Locum has worked so many night shifts that she now sleeps upside down out of habit. She did her training on the overnight medical roster, where the vending machine coffee kept her going and the 3am admissions kept her sharp. She has a very dry sense of humour and an encyclopaedic memory for everything that happened on the ward this week. Every Sunday she runs the big quiz, then goes to bed until Tuesday.

    Says when you win: “Up all night for that score.” · “Consultant-level rounds.” · “Batting a thousand.” · “Night shift hero.”

  • Deep dives

    Pebble

    A deep-diving penguin who brings you a pebble of knowledge

    Earnest, a little nerdy, and overdressed for every dive. Hates fluff. Shows affection by bringing you pebbles.

    Read her story

    Pebble grew up on a cold, windy island where penguins give each other pebbles as a sign of love. She started bringing pebbles of evidence instead: a trial here, a meta-analysis there, the one study everyone else missed. She dives deeper than most and always comes up with something worth keeping. She wears a tiny diving suit for every dive, even though penguins really don't need one. She says it's professional.

    Says when you win: “Deep enough for you?” · “I brought you a pebble. It’s evidence.” · “Holding my breath for the next one.” · “Down we go.”

  • Stackulus

    Brix

    A wombat tradie in a hard hat

    Tradie energy. Plans every stack, measures twice, drops once. Very proud of his big, reinforced wombat bum (real wombats block their burrows with theirs).

    Read his story

    Brix is a wombat from the Hunter who was a builder for twenty years before he discovered he could stack organs. He measures twice, plans every drop and keeps a pencil behind his ear at all times. Like all wombats, he has a reinforced back end that can block a burrow, and he is extremely proud of it. His smoko is at 10am sharp. Nobody is allowed to touch his hard hat.

    Says when you win: “Built like a pro.” · “Level and plumb.” · “Not one wonky organ.” · “Site inspection: passed.”

Hosts and topic mascots

They look after hubs, sections and health topics.

  • Dental and oral health pages

    Kiri

    Shark

    Cheerful, slightly terrifying smile, flosses after every meal. Grows a new set of teeth whenever she likes and won't stop bragging about it.

    Read her story

    Kiri is a shark who has replaced about 30,000 teeth in her lifetime, so she takes dental hygiene very personally. She trained as a dental nurse after realising nobody else at the reef was brushing. She carries a toothbrush everywhere, counts your teeth when you smile, and is on a lifelong mission to convince humans that their one spare set is not a lot to work with.

  • Emergency and first aid pages

    Zola

    Anglerfish

    Calm in a crisis, brilliant in the dark. Her headlamp has never once gone flat.

    Read her story

    Zola grew up in the deep sea, where it is always 3am and something is always on fire. That made her a natural emergency nurse. Her glowing lure doubles as a torch for checking pupils, finding veins and reading tiny print on medicine labels. She has a scary grin and a gentle voice, and she will tell you exactly when to call an ambulance and when a cold pack will do.

  • Immunisation and vaccine pages

    Tembi

    Elephant

    Kind, patient and never forgets a due date. Gives the gentlest jabs in the business.

    Read her story

    Tembi is an elephant, so she remembers every vaccine schedule, every booster date and every child who was brave last time. She became an immunisation nurse after seeing how quickly measles can spread through a herd. Her trunk is perfect for steady hands, and she keeps a pocket full of stickers for anyone who needs one, adults included.

  • Men's health pages

    Magnus

    Walrus

    Big, gruff and secretly soft. Gets men to see a doctor by pretending it was their idea.

    Read his story

    Magnus is a walrus who avoided doctors for forty years, until a check-up caught a problem early and changed his mind. Now he's on a mission to drag every bloke he knows to a GP, gently, with his tusks if necessary. He talks about prostates, mental health and blood pressure the way other walruses talk about the weather: loudly, often, and without embarrassment.

  • Mental health pages

    Otto

    Golden retriever puppy

    Warm, steady and a very good listener. Knows when to talk and when to just sit with you.

    Read his story

    Otto started out as a therapy puppy at a children's hospital and discovered he was good at noticing who needed company. He doesn't fix things. He sits beside you, listens properly and reminds you that asking for help is brave, not weak. He still gets excited about tennis balls, but he will always put the ball down if you need to talk.

  • Nutrition and diet pages

    Fig

    Hedgehog

    Practical, food-loving dietitian. No food is banned in her kitchen, except detox tea.

    Read her story

    Fig is a hedgehog who ran a tiny café before training as a dietitian. She got tired of customers asking which superfood would fix everything, so she went to find out properly. Her verdict: vegetables, fibre and actually enjoying your meals. She keeps a carrot behind her ear, has no time for fad diets, and gets visibly prickly whenever anyone says "clean eating".

  • Pregnancy and fertility pages

    Marisol

    Seahorse

    Gentle, unflappable and deeply practical. Has seen every kind of family and loves them all.

    Read her story

    Marisol is a seahorse midwife. In her family it was her dad who carried the eggs, which gave her a lifelong interest in the many ways families begin. She guides people through fertility, pregnancy and the newborn blur with calm, honest advice and zero judgement. She curls her tail around the hard questions and answers them straight.

  • Satire and April Fools pages

    Quill

    Lyrebird

    Mischievous mimic. Can impersonate anyone, especially people who take themselves too seriously.

    Read her story

    Quill is a lyrebird, the world's best mimic, who can copy a chainsaw, a camera shutter and a hospital administrator with equal accuracy. She writes the site's satire because she believes the best way to spot nonsense is to laugh at it. She aims her jokes at hype, jargon and pompous headlines, never at patients, and she always signs her work so nobody mistakes it for real news.

  • Travel health pages

    Yara

    Arctic tern

    Restless, organised and always packed. Has a vaccine record longer than most passports.

    Read her story

    Yara is an Arctic tern, a bird that flies from pole to pole every year, so she has done more long-haul travel than anyone you know. She became the site's travel health guide after one trip without the right vaccines taught her a lesson she won't forget. She carries her passport, insurance and a full travel kit, and she will remind you to book your shots six weeks before you fly.

  • Medicines A–Z host

    Yuki

    Giant panda

    Calm, careful pharmacist. Reads every label twice and never rushes a dose.

    Read her story

    Yuki is a giant panda who spent years in a busy pharmacy, where she learned that most medicine mistakes happen when people are in a hurry. Pandas famously take their time, so the job suits her. She explains what each medicine does, what to watch for and when to ask for help, in plain words. She keeps her own bamboo in a labelled pill bottle, because old habits die hard.

  • Normie mode host

    Norm

    Capybara

    Laid-back, unbothered and friends with everyone. Sunglasses permanently parked on his head.

    Read his story

    Norm is a capybara, the animal every other animal likes to sit on. He hosts Normie mode, where nobody talks about medicine and everybody takes the day off. The game mascots visit him on their days off, and he keeps the snacks stocked and the questions light. He's never once been in a hurry and sees no reason to start.

  • Doctors' hub host

    Inês

    Manatee

    Calm, unhurried and quietly brilliant. Half-moon glasses, white coat, never raises her voice.

    Read her story

    Inês is a manatee who spent thirty years as a country GP before taking over the doctors' hub. Manatees are sometimes called sea cows, and she finds this hilarious. She has seen everything at least twice, reads every guideline update, and believes the best medicine is a good history and a cup of tea. She drifts, but she always gets there.

  • GP School host

    Saga

    Otter

    Bright, playful and a little overcommitted. Always has three tabs and a snack open.

    Read her story

    Saga is an otter GP registrar who keeps her favourite stethoscope in a pouch under her arm, the way otters keep their favourite rock. She learns by doing, asks lots of questions and is never embarrassed to say 'I don't know yet'. She runs GP School because she remembers how scary the first shift was, and she wants yours to feel a little less so.

  • Specialists directory host

    Percy

    Pelican

    Kindly, well connected and knows everyone. Carries far too much in his beak.

    Read his story

    Percy is a pelican and a retired country GP who spent forty years writing referral letters. He knows every specialist by name, which ones answer the phone and which have a two-year wait. He now runs the specialists directory, carrying contact details around in his enormous beak. He has never lost a referral, though he has accidentally swallowed a few.

  • Research hub host

    Mei

    Lab mouse

    Curious, careful and sceptical of anything with a press release. Reads the methods first.

    Read her story

    Mei is a lab mouse who escaped from a research facility after reading one too many badly designed studies about mice. She now reads the studies so you don't have to: every week she sorts the real findings from the hype. She has a soft spot for well-run trials and a deep suspicion of anything that only worked in her cousins.

  • Myth-busting pages

    Ama

    Crow

    Clever, cheeky and impossible to fool. Collects myths the way other crows collect shiny things.

    Read her story

    Ama is a crow, one of the cleverest birds alive, who can solve puzzles and remember faces for years. She spent her youth on the roof of a health-food shop, listening to the claims coming out of the door. Now she collects myths and pecks them apart, one fact at a time. She never mocks the person who believed it, only the person selling it.

  • Neurology pages

    Nalu

    Hammerhead shark

    Precise, wide-angle thinker. Literally sees both sides of every argument.

    Read her story

    Nalu is a hammerhead shark whose eyes sit so far apart she can see almost all the way around herself. That made her a natural neurologist: she notices the sign on the left and the sign on the right at the same time. She loves the brain because it's the most complicated thing in the ocean, and she finds reflexes genuinely thrilling.

  • Allergy pages

    Hana

    Pufferfish

    Gentle, careful and very good in a reaction. Puffs up only when absolutely necessary.

    Read her story

    Hana is a pufferfish and an allergy nurse, so she knows exactly what it feels like when the body overreacts. She's spent years teaching families how to read labels, use an adrenaline autoinjector and stay calm in a reaction. She is very patient, but she will puff up if you call a food intolerance an allergy.

  • Exercise handouts

    Jett

    Kangaroo

    Bouncy, encouraging and relentlessly practical. Thinks every problem improves with a little movement.

    Read her story

    Jett is a kangaroo physio who grew up hopping around the hills of the Hunter. She believes in small, steady movement over heroic bursts, which is a bit rich from a kangaroo. She writes the exercise handouts, demonstrates every move herself and has never once let anyone skip their warm-up.

  • Sleep pages

    Kip

    Koala

    Drowsy, wise and deeply serious about bedtime routines. Up to 20 hours of sleep a day, for research.

    Read her story

    Kip is a koala and a sleep scientist, which she says was always going to happen. Koalas sleep up to 20 hours a day, so she has done more fieldwork than anyone. She knows the difference between tired and sleepy, can talk about circadian rhythms for hours, and thinks scrolling in bed should be a crime.

  • Pacing and fatigue pages

    Layla

    Sloth

    Slow on purpose. Wise about energy, kind about bad days, and very good at saying no.

    Read her story

    Layla is a sloth who learnt the hard way that pushing through is how you crash. She moves slowly on purpose, plans her energy like a budget and rests before she's empty. She hosts the pacing and fatigue pages because she knows how frustrating it is when people say 'just try harder'. Her motto: slow is a strategy, not a failure.

  • My stats host

    Hazel

    Squirrel

    Organised, proud of every streak and a little obsessive about records.

    Read her story

    Hazel is a squirrel who keeps track of everything, because squirrels bury thousands of nuts and have to remember where. She keeps your streaks, scores and records safe, and celebrates every one. She gets very excited about a personal best and a bit anxious when a streak is about to break, so please play today.
