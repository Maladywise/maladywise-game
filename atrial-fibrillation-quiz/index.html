Atrial Fibrillation Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on AF: the irregular heartbeat, why it raises stroke risk, who needs a blood thinner, and rate versus rhythm control.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What is atrial fibrillation?

    1. A slow but regular heartbeat from the pacemaker
    2. A fast, irregular rhythm from chaotic signals in the atria
    3. A heart attack caused by a blocked artery
    4. A heart valve that has narrowed and stiffened
    Show answer

    Answer: A fast, irregular rhythm from chaotic signals in the atria. The atria quiver instead of squeezing, and beats reach the ventricles at random.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    Which pulse finding suggests AF?

    1. Regular but strong
    2. Absent in both arms
    3. Irregularly irregular
    4. Slow and regular
    Show answer

    Answer: Irregularly irregular. No pattern at all. A smartwatch alert or a pulse check can be the first clue.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    What test confirms AF?

    1. A chest X-ray
    2. A urine test
    3. A cholesterol test
    4. An ECG
    Show answer

    Answer: An ECG. Irregular beats with no clear P waves. Some people need a Holter monitor to catch it.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    Why does AF raise stroke risk?

    1. Blood pools in the atria and can form clots
    2. It narrows the arteries in the neck
    3. It raises blood sugar levels
    4. It makes the blood too thin
    Show answer

    Answer: Blood pools in the atria and can form clots. Clots usually form in the left atrial appendage and can travel to the brain.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    Roughly how much does AF raise stroke risk, untreated?

    1. Not at all
    2. By 5%
    3. About 100 times
    4. About 5 times
    Show answer

    Answer: About 5 times. That’s why stroke prevention is the single most important part of AF care.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    Which tool do Australian guidelines use to estimate stroke risk in AF?

    1. CHA₂DS₂-VA
    2. Wells score
    3. APGAR
    4. Glasgow Coma Scale
    Show answer

    Answer: CHA₂DS₂-VA. The 2023 Australian guideline dropped the sex category (the ‘Sc’), giving CHA₂DS₂-VA.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    What is the main medicine type used to prevent stroke in AF?

    1. Aspirin on its own, every day
    2. Anticoagulants, such as apixaban or warfarin
    3. Long-term low-dose antibiotics
    4. Statins, such as atorvastatin
    Show answer

    Answer: Anticoagulants, such as apixaban or warfarin. Aspirin isn’t recommended for stroke prevention in AF: it barely helps and still causes bleeding.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    What does ‘rate control’ mean?

    1. Stopping blood thinners once settled
    2. Making the heart beat faster
    3. Slowing the heart rate while AF continues
    4. Curing AF permanently with surgery
    Show answer

    Answer: Slowing the heart rate while AF continues. Beta blockers, some calcium channel blockers or digoxin. Rhythm control aims to restore a normal rhythm instead.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    Which lifestyle change has the strongest evidence for reducing AF?

    1. Eating more salt to raise blood pressure
    2. Losing weight if overweight, and cutting alcohol
    3. Drinking more coffee to steady the beat
    4. Avoiding all exercise to rest the heart
    Show answer

    Answer: Losing weight if overweight, and cutting alcohol. Australian trials showed weight loss and less alcohol reduce AF episodes. Treating sleep apnoea and blood pressure helps too.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    What is catheter ablation?

    1. Open-heart surgery to replace a leaking valve
    2. A blood transfusion to thin the blood
    3. Changing the battery of a pacemaker
    4. A procedure that scars small spots of heart tissue to block bad signals
    Show answer

    Answer: A procedure that scars small spots of heart tissue to block bad signals. Pulmonary vein isolation is the usual target. It works best early, in paroxysmal AF.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    Which blood test is checked in new AF?

    1. Vitamin B12
    2. PSA
    3. Thyroid function (TSH)
    4. Lipase
    Show answer

    Answer: Thyroid function (TSH). An overactive thyroid is a classic reversible cause. Kidney function guides blood thinner doses.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Who needs an urgent same-day review with AF?

    1. Someone with chest pain, fainting, breathlessness or a very fast rate
    2. Everyone with AF, every week, for monitoring
    3. Anyone who found it on a smartwatch while feeling well
    4. Only people over 90 with other illnesses
    Show answer

    Answer: Someone with chest pain, fainting, breathlessness or a very fast rate. Stable new AF can be assessed by a GP promptly. Signs of instability need emergency care.

Common questions

Can AF go away?

Some people have occasional episodes (paroxysmal AF) that stop on their own; others have it all the time. Ablation can keep many people in normal rhythm.

Is AF dangerous?

It’s usually not immediately dangerous, but it raises stroke and heart failure risk, which treatment reduces a lot.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. See your GP about your own symptoms or results.
