Strabismus (Squint)

When the eyes don’t point the same way. About 1 in 20 children have it. A brief wobble is normal in young babies, but a turned eye after about 4 months needs checking, because the brain can start ignoring that eye.

What it is

  • One eye turns in, out, up or down while the other looks straight. It can be there all the time or come and go, often when tired or unwell.
  • Common types: an eye turning in (esotropia), which can start in babies or in toddlers who are long-sighted, and an eye drifting out (exotropia), often when tired or daydreaming.
  • Many babies look cross-eyed because of a wide, flat nasal bridge and skin folds at the inner corners. The eyes are actually straight (pseudo-squint). A doctor can tell the difference.

What’s normal

  • Short periods of wandering eyes are normal up to about 4 months of age, while focusing develops.
  • After 4 months, or at any age if the turn is constant or getting more obvious, see your GP.

Why early checks matter

  • To avoid double vision, a child’s brain switches off the turned eye. Over time that eye can become lazy (amblyopia) and lose vision.
  • Amblyopia is easiest to treat in early childhood. The younger it’s found, the better the result.
  • Rarely, a turned eye is a sign of another eye problem. A white or abnormal reflection in the pupil in photos needs a doctor promptly.

Treatment

  • Glasses correct focusing problems and can straighten some turns completely, especially an inward turn from long-sightedness.
  • Patching the stronger eye (or eye drops that blur it) makes the brain use the weaker eye.
  • Surgery on the eye muscles straightens the eyes when glasses don’t. It’s usually a day procedure, and some children need more than one.
  • Treatment is planned by an eye specialist (ophthalmologist) and orthoptist, often combining these.

When to see a doctor

  • An eye that turns after about 4 months, or that’s turning more often.
  • Your child tilts or turns their head to look at things, squints in sunlight, or covers one eye.
  • A family history of squint or lazy eye.
  • See a doctor promptly for a new turn with a droopy lid or a different-sized pupil, a white pupil reflection, or a turn after a head injury or with headache or vomiting.

Common questions

Is it normal for a newborn’s eyes to cross?

Yes, for short periods up to about 4 months of age. If an eye turns all the time, or after 4 months, see your GP.

Can a squint fix itself?

A true squint doesn’t usually go away on its own. Some inward turns from long-sightedness fully straighten with glasses. Waiting can risk a lazy eye, so get it checked.

What is the difference between a squint and a lazy eye?

A squint is an eye that points in a different direction. A lazy eye (amblyopia) is reduced vision because the brain hasn’t learned to use that eye. A squint is a common cause of lazy eye.

At what age can a child have squint surgery?

At any age, including babies with a large inward turn from early infancy. The eye specialist decides the timing based on the type of squint and how vision is developing.
