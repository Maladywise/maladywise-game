Lungs and Respiratory System Quiz

    Name the highlighted lobe.

    4. Left upper lobe

    Answer: Right upper lobe. The top lobe of the right lung, above the horizontal fissure.

  2. Question 2 of 13

    Name the highlighted lobe.

    1. Right middle lobe
    2. Right upper lobe
    3. Right lower lobe
    4. Lingula
    Show answer

    Answer: Right middle lobe. Only the right lung has a middle lobe, between the horizontal and oblique fissures.

  3. Question 3 of 13

    Name the highlighted lobe.

    1. Right middle lobe
    2. Right upper lobe
    3. Left lower lobe
    4. Right lower lobe
    Show answer

    Answer: Right lower lobe. The biggest lobe, sitting on the diaphragm. Inhaled material often ends up here.

  4. Question 4 of 13

    Name the highlighted lobe.

    1. Left lower lobe
    2. Left upper lobe
    3. Lingula
    4. Right upper lobe
    Show answer

    Answer: Left upper lobe. The left lung has two lobes; this upper one includes the lingula.

  5. Question 5 of 13

    Name the highlighted tongue-shaped part of the left lung.

    1. Left lower lobe
    2. Left upper lobe
    3. Lingula
    4. Right middle lobe
    Show answer

    Answer: Lingula. Part of the left upper lobe, it’s the left lung’s equivalent of the right middle lobe.

  6. Question 6 of 13

    Name the highlighted lobe.

    1. Left lower lobe
    2. Lingula
    3. Left upper lobe
    4. Right lower lobe
    Show answer

    Answer: Left lower lobe. Below the left oblique fissure, sitting on the diaphragm behind the heart.

  7. Question 7 of 13

    Name the highlighted tube.

    1. Right main bronchus
    2. Trachea
    3. Oesophagus
    4. Larynx
    Show answer

    Answer: Trachea. The windpipe, kept open by C-shaped rings of cartilage.

  8. Question 8 of 13

    Name the highlighted point where the windpipe splits.

    1. Lingula
    2. Larynx
    3. Hilum
    4. Carina
    Show answer

    Answer: Carina. The ridge where the trachea divides into the two main bronchi. It’s very sensitive and triggers coughing.

  9. Question 9 of 13

    Name the highlighted airway, where inhaled objects most often lodge.

    1. Right main bronchus
    2. Trachea
    3. Oesophagus
    4. Left main bronchus
    Show answer

    Answer: Right main bronchus. It’s wider, shorter and more vertical than the left, so peanuts and teeth tend to go right.

  10. Question 10 of 13

    Name the highlighted muscle.

    1. Intercostal muscle
    2. Pericardium
    3. Liver
    4. Diaphragm
    Show answer

    Answer: Diaphragm. The main breathing muscle. It flattens as it contracts, drawing air in.

  11. Question 11 of 13

    How many lobes does the right lung have?

    1. Two
    2. Three
    3. Five
    4. Four
    Show answer

    Answer: Three. Upper, middle and lower. The left has two, leaving room for the heart.

  12. Question 12 of 13

    Where does gas exchange happen?

    1. Bronchi
    2. Bronchioles
    3. Alveoli
    4. Trachea
    Show answer

    Answer: Alveoli. Hundreds of millions of tiny air sacs, each wrapped in capillaries, swap oxygen for carbon dioxide.

  13. Question 13 of 13

    Which nerve powers the diaphragm?

    1. Intercostal nerves
    2. Phrenic nerve
    3. Vagus nerve
    4. Accessory nerve
    Show answer

    Answer: Phrenic nerve. From the C3, C4 and C5 nerve roots: “C3, 4, 5 keeps the diaphragm alive.”

Common questions

How many lobes do the lungs have?

Five in total: three in the right lung (upper, middle, lower) and two in the left (upper and lower). The left upper lobe has a tongue-like part called the lingula.

Why is the left lung smaller?

The heart sits slightly to the left, so the left lung has a notch for it and only two lobes.

What are alveoli?

Tiny air sacs at the ends of the airways where oxygen passes into the blood and carbon dioxide passes out. Adults have hundreds of millions of them.
