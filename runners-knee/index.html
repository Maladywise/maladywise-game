Fact sheet

Runner’s Knee (Kneecap Pain)

An ache around or behind the kneecap that hates stairs, squats, running and long sits. Not damage, not arthritis: an irritated, overloaded joint. Stronger hips and thighs are the best-tested fix.

Typical signs: a dull ache at the front of the knee, worse going down stairs or hills, squatting, kneeling, running, jumping or after sitting with bent knees (the “movie-goer’s sign”). The knee may click or creak; that alone isn’t harmful.

Why it happens: more load on the kneecap joint than it’s used to: a jump in running or training, lots of stairs or squats, weaker hip and thigh muscles. Common in teenagers, runners and active women, but anyone can get it. Also called patellofemoral pain.

Diagnosis: from your story and a knee examination, including watching you squat or step down. X-rays and scans don’t help unless there was an injury, swelling or the knee locks or gives way.

The kneecap pain ladder

Exercise is the main treatment. Give it 6–12 weeks. Longer-standing pain takes longer, so start early.

    Cost

    How long

    Watch for
    Mild pain during the exercises is OK if it settles soon and isn’t worse the next day.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Knee braces and patellar taping on their ownExperts rate the evidence as uncertain. Fine if they make exercise easier, but they’re not a treatment by themselves.
  • Ultrasound, laser, TENS and other machinesNot recommended by the international expert consensus. Warm, buzzy and unhelpful.
  • Custom orthoticsNo proven advantage over off-the-shelf insoles, at several times the price.
  • Scans for typical kneecap painThey don’t change treatment and often show harmless “findings” that cause worry.
  • Glucosamine and joint supplementsThis isn’t arthritis, and there’s no evidence they help kneecap pain.

What you can do

  • Follow our kneecap pain exercise handout: hip and thigh exercises with reps and pain rules.
  • Start early. Pain that has lasted more than 2 months tends to take longer to settle.
  • Return to running gradually: shorter, flatter, softer surfaces first, and add a little each week.
  • Avoid long sits with knees bent: stretch your legs out or stand up every 30–45 minutes.
  • Cycling and swimming usually keep you fit without a flare.
  • If you’re above a healthy weight, losing some eases load on the kneecap.

See your GP if

  • Pain isn’t improving after 6–12 weeks of exercise
  • The knee swells, locks, or gives way
  • Pain started after a twist, fall or blow to the knee
  • A child or teenager has knee pain with a limp, or pain at night

See a doctor the same day for a hot, red, swollen knee, especially with fever; a knee you can’t straighten or put weight on after an injury; or a child with knee pain and a limp (hip problems can show up as knee pain).

Common questions

Is runner’s knee damaging my knee?

No. It’s an irritated, overloaded joint rather than damage, and it doesn’t mean you’ll get arthritis. Clicking or creaking without pain or swelling is common and harmless.

How long does runner’s knee last?

Many people improve over 6 to 12 weeks of exercise, but it can linger: in one study about 4 in 10 weren’t recovered by 12 months. Starting exercises early helps.

Should I stop running?

Usually not completely. Cut back to a level that keeps pain mild and settled by the next day, add strengthening, then build back up gradually.

Should I use a knee brace?

Only if it makes moving easier. The evidence is uncertain, and it doesn’t replace exercise.

What does runner’s knee feel like?

A dull ache around or behind the kneecap, worse with stairs, squatting, kneeling, running downhill or after sitting for a long time with bent knees. The knee rarely swells much.

What exercises help runner’s knee?

Strengthening the hip and thigh muscles works best. Combining hip and knee exercises (such as side-lying leg raises, bridges, step-ups and squats within comfort) improves pain more than knee exercises alone. Most people notice a difference within 6 weeks.

Does taping help runner’s knee?

Kneecap taping can ease pain in the short term, which can make exercise easier. It doesn’t fix the problem on its own, so it works best alongside a strengthening program.
