Fact sheet

Perioral Dermatitis

Clusters of small red bumps and flaky skin around the mouth, and sometimes the nose or eyes, often sparing a thin band next to the lips. Commonly triggered by steroid creams or heavy face products. Not contagious.

First step: "zero therapy". Stop steroid creams and as many face products as you can. Many rashes clear with this alone over a few weeks.

Treatment options

Ratings show how strong the research is.

  • Moderate evidenceAntibiotic tabletsDoxycycline or similar for 6 to 8 weeks, used for their anti-inflammatory effect. Erythromycin for children under 8 and in pregnancy.
  • Moderate evidenceAntibiotic cream or gelMetronidazole or erythromycin, twice daily for up to 8 weeks. For milder rashes, but can sting sensitive skin.
  • Moderate evidencePimecrolimus creamA non-steroid prescription cream. Can ease the flare after stopping a steroid.
  • Limited evidenceAzelaic acidA gentler cream that may help. Suitable in pregnancy.
  • Not recommendedSteroid creams on the faceMay help for a few days, then the rash rebounds worse. Don’t restart them.

What you can do

  • Wash with water or a gentle soap-free cleanser, then rinse well.
  • Pause make-up, thick creams, ointments and fragranced products. Use a light gel sunscreen if needed.
  • Expect a flare for 1 to 2 weeks after stopping a steroid cream. This is normal.
  • Once clear, bring back one product a week and drop any that cause a flare.
  • Rinse your mouth and face after steroid inhalers or nasal sprays, and keep using them.

See your GP if

  • You’ve been using a steroid cream on your face (strong ones may need tapering)
  • No better after 2 to 4 weeks of "zero therapy"
  • The rash is around the eyes or keeps coming back

Don’t stop asthma inhalers or prescribed steroid tablets without talking to your doctor. See a doctor the same day for a painful red eye or blurred vision.

Common questions

What causes perioral dermatitis?

It is commonly triggered by steroid creams or heavy face products. It is not contagious.

How long does perioral dermatitis take to clear?

Many rashes clear over a few weeks after stopping steroid creams and most face products. If needed, antibiotic tablets or creams are used for 6 to 8 weeks.

Why did my rash get worse after stopping steroid cream?

A flare for 1 to 2 weeks after stopping a steroid cream is normal. Don’t restart it, and see your GP if you’ve been using a strong one.
