Fact sheet

Perioral Dermatitis

Small red bumps and flaky skin around the mouth, sometimes the nose or eyes, often sparing a thin rim by the lips. Usually triggered by steroid creams or heavy face products. Not contagious.

The perioral dermatitis ladder

Step 1 clears many rashes on its own. Expect it to look worse before it looks better. Give treatments 6 to 8 weeks.

  1. Step 1 · Self-care“Zero therapy”: stop the steroid cream and most face productsWater or a gentle soap-free cleanser, and not much else for a while.

    Cost
    Free. Actually saves money
    How long
    Weeks to a few months
    Watch for
    A rebound flare for 1–2 weeks after stopping a steroid cream. Normal. Don’t restart it.

  3. Step 3 · PrescriptionAntibiotic gel or cream (metronidazole or erythromycin)Twice a day for milder rashes, used for its anti-inflammatory effect.

  4. Step 4 · PrescriptionPimecrolimus creamA non-steroid prescription cream that can ease the flare after stopping a steroid.
    Works
    Small trials: calms the rash faster in the first weeks, but doesn’t clear it any sooner overall

    How long
    A few weeks
    Watch for

    Cost

    How long
    6–8 weeks
    Watch for

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Hydrocortisone and other steroid creamsCalm it for a few days, then make it worse. Often the cause, never the cure.
  • Tea tree and essential oilsNo good evidence, and a common cause of irritated, allergic skin.
  • Antifungal creamsLittle evidence, unless a doctor thinks yeast is involved.
  • Fluoride-free toothpasteBased on case reports only. Cheap enough to try, but don’t expect miracles.

What you can do

  • Wash with water or a gentle soap-free cleanser, then rinse well.
  • Pause make-up, thick creams, ointments and fragranced products. A light gel sunscreen is fine.
  • Expect a flare for 1 to 2 weeks after stopping a steroid cream. Hold your nerve.
  • Once clear, bring back one product a week and ditch any that cause a flare.
  • Rinse your mouth and face after steroid inhalers or nasal sprays, and keep using them.

See your GP if

  • You’ve been using a steroid cream on your face (strong ones may need tapering)
  • No better after 2 to 4 weeks of “zero therapy”
  • The rash is around the eyes or keeps coming back

Don’t stop asthma inhalers or prescribed steroid tablets without talking to your doctor. See a doctor the same day for a painful red eye or blurred vision.

Common questions

What causes perioral dermatitis?

It is commonly triggered by steroid creams or heavy face products. It is not contagious.

How long does perioral dermatitis take to clear?

Many rashes clear over weeks to a few months after stopping steroid creams and most face products. If needed, antibiotic tablets or creams are used for 6 to 8 weeks.

Why did my rash get worse after stopping steroid cream?

A flare for 1 to 2 weeks after stopping a steroid cream is normal. Don’t restart it, and see your GP if you’ve been using a strong one.
