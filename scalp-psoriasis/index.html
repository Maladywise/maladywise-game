Scalp Psoriasis

Scalp psoriasis causes thick, silvery, well-defined plaques that often spill past the hairline and behind the ears. At least half of people with psoriasis get it, and it’s one of the itchiest, most visible spots. The trick is to lift the scale first, then treat the skin underneath.

Psoriasis or dandruff?

  • Psoriasis: thick, silvery scale on red plaques with sharp edges; often past the hairline, behind the ears and on the neck
  • Seborrhoeic dermatitis (dandruff): thinner, greasier, yellowish flakes, more diffuse, often eyebrows and beside the nose
  • Some people have features of both (“sebopsoriasis”), and treatments overlap
  • Psoriasis elsewhere (elbows, knees, nails) supports the diagnosis

Dandruff flakes. Psoriasis builds plaques with planning permission.

Hair loss

  • Thick plaques and scratching can thin the hair temporarily
  • Hair grows back once the psoriasis is controlled
  • Be gentle: no tight hairstyles, harsh brushing or very hot hair dryers on active plaques

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Apple cider vinegarCan sting and burn broken skin, with no trial evidence.
  • Expensive “natural” psoriasis shampoosOften tar or salicylic acid with a markup.
  • Shaving the headNot needed: lotions, foams and shampoos are made to reach the scalp through hair.

Covering it with a hat treats the embarrassment, not the plaques.

Common questions

How do I get rid of thick scale on my scalp?

Soften it first with a salicylic acid or tar scalp ointment, or oil, left on overnight under a shower cap, then wash and comb gently. Then use a steroid or combination scalp treatment.

Is scalp psoriasis the same as dandruff?

No. Psoriasis has thick, silvery scale on sharply edged plaques, often past the hairline. Dandruff (seborrhoeic dermatitis) is thinner, greasier and more diffuse. They can overlap.

Does scalp psoriasis cause hair loss?

It can thin the hair temporarily, mainly from scratching and scale removal, but hair usually grows back once it’s controlled.
