Sunburn

Sunburn is a radiation burn from ultraviolet (UV) light. It usually settles in a week, but every burn adds to your skin cancer risk, especially in childhood.

Treating sunburn

  • Get out of the sun and cool the skin with a cool shower or damp cloths.
  • Moisturiser or aloe vera gel can soothe. Avoid ‘-caine’ numbing creams, which can irritate.
  • Paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain (follow the packet). Drink extra water.
  • Leave blisters alone; don’t pop them. Loose, soft clothing over burnt skin.
  • Stay out of the sun until it has healed.

See a doctor if

  • Blistering covers a large area, or the face, hands or genitals
  • Fever, chills, headache, vomiting, confusion or fainting (signs of heat illness)
  • A baby under 12 months is sunburnt
  • Signs of infection: spreading redness, pus or worsening pain after a few days

Preventing it

  • Use sun protection whenever the UV index is 3 or above, which in much of Australia is most of the year, even on cloudy days.
  • Slip, slop, slap, seek, slide: clothing that covers, broad-spectrum SPF 50+ sunscreen, a broad-brimmed hat, shade, and sunglasses.
  • Put sunscreen on 20 minutes before going out and reapply every 2 hours, and after swimming or sweating. Most people use too little: about a teaspoon per limb.
  • Keep babies under 12 months out of direct sun.
  • Solariums are banned in Australia for good reason; there’s no safe tan.

Common questions

How do you treat sunburn fast?

Cool the skin with a cool shower or damp cloths, use a moisturiser or aloe vera gel, take paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain, drink water, and stay out of the sun. Don’t pop blisters.

When is sunburn serious?

See a doctor for large areas of blistering, sunburn with fever, chills, vomiting or confusion, sunburn in a baby, or signs of infection.

Does sunburn cause skin cancer?

Yes. UV damage adds up over a lifetime, and sunburn, especially in childhood, raises the risk of melanoma and other skin cancers.

Can you get sunburnt on a cloudy day?

Yes. UV passes through cloud. Check the UV index: protect your skin whenever it’s 3 or above.
