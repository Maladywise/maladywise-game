November 2026

  1. #3 · Sunday 1 November 2026

    1. History of medicine

      Worimi man Kelvin Kong became Australia’s first Aboriginal surgeon. What is his specialty?

      Answer: Ear, nose and throat surgery

      Accept ENT or otolaryngology. He champions ear health for Aboriginal children, who have some of the world’s highest rates of chronic middle-ear infection.

    2. Anatomy

      Which organ contains the islets of Langerhans?

      Answer: The pancreas

      The islets of Langerhans are clusters of hormone-making cells in the pancreas. Their beta cells make insulin.

    3. Pharmacology

      Sildenafil (Viagra) was first trialled for which condition?

      Answer: Angina

      The side effect stole the show.

    4. Eponyms

      Erb’s palsy is an injury to which nerve network?

      Answer: The brachial plexus

      Upper roots, C5–C6. ‘Waiter’s tip’ posture.

    5. Microbiology

      Which virus causes both chickenpox and shingles?

      Answer: Varicella-zoster virus

      It never really leaves.

    6. On screen

      Which city is the emergency department in ‘The Pitt’ set in?

      Answer: Pittsburgh

      The Pitt is set in the emergency department of a Pittsburgh trauma hospital, with each episode covering one hour of a shift.

    7. Etymology

      ‘Quarantine’ comes from the Italian for what?

      Answer: Forty days

      Quaranta giorni. Venice made plague ships wait.

    8. Physiology

      What is the normal pH range of arterial blood?

      Answer: 7.35 to 7.45

      Normal arterial pH is 7.35 to 7.45. Below that is acidaemia, above is alkalaemia. Also accept 7.4.

    9. Famous patients

      Which US president was paralysed by an illness diagnosed as polio in 1921?

      Answer: Franklin D. Roosevelt

      Some now suspect Guillain–Barré syndrome.

    10. Language

      What is the medical term for hiccups?

      Answer: Singultus

      Singultus is the medical term for hiccups, from the Latin for a sob.

    11. Nobel Prizes

      Which chemist, who never finished a PhD, shared the 1988 Nobel Prize for developing drugs including azathioprine and aciclovir?

      Answer: Gertrude Elion

      Accept Trudy Elion. Early on she was turned away from lab jobs for being a woman.

    12. Pathology

      Deficiency of which vitamin causes beriberi?

      Answer: Thiamine (vitamin B1)

      Beriberi is thiamine (vitamin B1) deficiency, historically from diets of polished white rice. Also accept B1.

    13. By the numbers

      Roughly how many protein-coding genes are in the human genome?

      Answer: About 20,000

      Fewer than a grape has.

    14. Toxicology

      Which metal caused the ‘mad hatter’ symptoms in hat makers?

      Answer: Mercury

      Used to treat felt.

    15. History of medicine

      Who was known as ‘the Lady with the Lamp’?

      Answer: Florence Nightingale

      Also a pioneering statistician.

    16. Anatomy

      Which chamber of the heart has the thickest wall?

      Answer: The left ventricle

      The left ventricle pumps blood around the whole body, so its wall is about three times thicker than the right’s.

    17. Pharmacology

      Which chemotherapy drug was first isolated from the Pacific yew tree?

      Answer: Paclitaxel

      Accept Taxol.

    18. On screen

      Which 1966 film shrinks a medical team to travel through a man’s bloodstream?

      Answer: Fantastic Voyage

      In Fantastic Voyage (1966), a shrunken submarine crew travels through a scientist’s bloodstream to remove a brain clot.

    19. Eponyms

      Virchow’s triad describes the causes of what?

      Answer: Thrombosis

      Stasis, endothelial injury and hypercoagulability.

    20. Brain-melter

      Which parts of the body have the highest density of sweat glands?

      Answer: The palms and soles

      Hence sweaty palms in exams.

  1. #2 · Sunday 25 October 2026

    1. History of medicine

      Who gave the first public demonstration of ether anaesthesia, in Boston in 1846?

      Answer: William Morton

      At Massachusetts General. The room is now the Ether Dome.

    2. Anatomy

      What is the smallest skeletal muscle in the body?

      Answer: Stapedius

      It dampens the stapes against loud noise.

    3. Pharmacology

      Drugs ending in ‘-pril’ belong to which class?

      Answer: ACE inhibitors

      Ramipril, perindopril and friends. Hello, dry cough.

    4. Eponyms

      Kernig’s and Brudzinski’s signs are classic signs of what?

      Answer: Meningitis

      Classic, but not very sensitive.

    5. Microbiology

      Which bacterium causes cat-scratch disease?

      Answer: Bartonella henselae

      Bartonella henselae, usually from a kitten scratch or bite, causes swollen lymph nodes near the scratch.

    6. On screen

      Who played paediatrician Doug Ross in ‘ER’?

      Answer: George Clooney

      George Clooney played Dr Doug Ross, the paediatrician, in ER from 1994.

    7. Etymology

      ‘Influenza’ comes from the Italian for what?

      Answer: Influence

      Of the stars, supposedly.

    8. Physiology

      Which hormone does the pineal gland secrete?

      Answer: Melatonin

      The pineal gland releases melatonin as light fades, helping set the sleep-wake cycle.

    9. Famous patients

      Which physicist lived with motor neurone disease for over 50 years?

      Answer: Stephen Hawking

      Diagnosed at 21. Died at 76.

    10. Language

      What is a borborygmus?

      Answer: A rumbling stomach

      Plural: borborygmi.

    11. Nobel Prizes

      Who shared the 1962 Nobel Prize with Watson and Crick for DNA’s structure?

      Answer: Maurice Wilkins

      Rosalind Franklin had died in 1958.

    12. Pathology

      Reed–Sternberg cells are the hallmark of which cancer?

      Answer: Hodgkin lymphoma

      The ‘owl’s eye’ cells.

    13. By the numbers

      How many bones are in one human hand and wrist?

      Answer: 27

      8 carpals, 5 metacarpals, 14 phalanges.

    14. Toxicology

      Which plant poison was used to execute Socrates?

      Answer: Hemlock

      Socrates was sentenced to death in Athens in 399 BC and drank poison hemlock.

    15. History of medicine

      Who pioneered antiseptic surgery using carbolic acid in the 1860s?

      Answer: Joseph Lister

      Yes, Listerine.

    16. Anatomy

      What are you actually hitting when you bang your ‘funny bone’?

      Answer: The ulnar nerve

      Not funny. Not a bone.

    17. Pharmacology

      What is the antidote to paracetamol overdose?

      Answer: N-acetylcysteine

      Accept NAC or acetylcysteine.

    18. On screen

      In ‘Scrubs’, who is JD’s reluctant mentor?

      Answer: Dr Perry Cox

      Dr Perry Cox, played by John C. McGinley, mentors JD while insisting he doesn’t.

    19. Eponyms

      Which spots inside the mouth are an early sign of measles?

      Answer: Koplik spots

      Grains of salt on a red background.

    20. Brain-melter

      What is the medical term for brain freeze?

      Answer: Sphenopalatine ganglioneuralgia

      From eating ice cream too fast.

  2. #1 · Sunday 18 October 2026

    1. History of medicine

      Which Hungarian obstetrician championed handwashing in the 1840s and died in an asylum?

      Answer: Ignaz Semmelweis

      Proven right after his death. Wash your hands.

    2. Anatomy

      Which bone is the only one in the body that doesn’t articulate with another bone?

      Answer: The hyoid

      Held in place by muscles and ligaments alone.

    3. Pharmacology

      William Withering described which heart drug in 1785, from the foxglove plant?

      Answer: Digitalis

      Accept digoxin.

    4. Eponyms

      ‘Saturday night palsy’ is a compression injury of which nerve?

      Answer: The radial nerve

      Arm draped over a chair after a big night. Wrist drop follows.

    5. Microbiology

      Which fungus causes ‘rose gardener’s disease’?

      Answer: Sporothrix schenckii

      Sporotrichosis, from a thorn prick.

    6. On screen

      In the early seasons of ‘Grey’s Anatomy’, what was the hospital called?

      Answer: Seattle Grace Hospital

      Later Seattle Grace Mercy West, then Grey Sloan Memorial.

    7. Etymology

      The word ‘malaria’ comes from Italian for what?

      Answer: Bad air

      Mala aria. Blame the swamps, not the mosquitoes.

    8. Physiology

      How long does a red blood cell live, on average?

      Answer: About 120 days

      Red cells live about 120 days, then the spleen and liver recycle them. That’s why HbA1c reflects roughly the last three months of blood glucose.

    9. Famous patients

      Which US president secretly had Addison’s disease?

      Answer: John F. Kennedy

      JFK had Addison’s disease (adrenal failure), kept quiet during his presidency and managed with steroids.

    10. Language

      What is the photic sneeze reflex better known as, by its acronym?

      Answer: ACHOO syndrome

      Sneezing on stepping into bright light. Autosomal dominant. Bless you.

    11. Nobel Prizes

      Who shared the 1923 Nobel Prize for the discovery of insulin?

      Answer: Frederick Banting and John Macleod

      Banting shared his half with Charles Best.

    12. Pathology

      What is the most common primary malignant bone tumour in adolescents?

      Answer: Osteosarcoma

      Osteosarcoma is the most common primary bone cancer in teenagers, typically around the knee during growth spurts.

    13. By the numbers

      Roughly how many litres of blood does the heart pump per minute at rest?

      Answer: About 5 litres

      Cardiac output: heart rate × stroke volume.

    14. Toxicology

      Which poison is classically said to smell of bitter almonds?

      Answer: Cyanide

      Not everyone can smell it. It’s genetic.

    15. History of medicine

      Which Persian physician wrote ‘The Canon of Medicine’ around 1025?

      Answer: Avicenna

      Ibn Sina. A standard textbook for 600 years.

    16. Anatomy

      What is the longest nerve in the human body?

      Answer: The sciatic nerve

      The sciatic nerve runs from the lower back down the back of the leg, and is about as thick as a thumb at its widest.

    17. Pharmacology

      Aspirin’s ancestor came from the bark of which tree?

      Answer: Willow

      Salicylates, from Salix.

    18. In print

      Which creator of Sherlock Holmes was also a doctor?

      Answer: Arthur Conan Doyle

      Holmes was based on his teacher, Dr Joseph Bell.

    19. Eponyms

      What are the transverse grooves across the nails after a severe illness called?

      Answer: Beau’s lines

      Beau’s lines are dents across the nails where growth paused during a severe illness. They grow out over months.

    20. Brain-melter

      What is the longest word in major English dictionaries, a lung disease from fine silica dust?

      Answer: Pneumonoultramicroscopicsilicovolcanoconiosis

      Invented in 1935 to be the longest word. It worked.
