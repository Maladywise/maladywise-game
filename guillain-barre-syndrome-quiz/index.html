Guillain–Barré Syndrome Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on Guillain–Barré syndrome: how it starts, why breathing is watched closely, and how most people recover.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What is Guillain–Barré syndrome?

    1. A spinal cord tumour
    2. A type of stroke
    3. A muscle infection
    4. An immune attack on the peripheral nerves
    Show answer

    Answer: An immune attack on the peripheral nerves. Antibodies or immune cells damage the nerves’ myelin or axons.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    How does weakness typically spread?

    1. Starting in the legs and rising upwards
    2. Randomly, day to day
    3. Starting in the face only
    4. Only in one hand
    Show answer

    Answer: Starting in the legs and rising upwards. It usually affects both sides and reaches its worst within 4 weeks.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    What often happens to reflexes?

    1. Only the jaw jerk is lost
    2. They stay normal
    3. They’re reduced or absent
    4. They become very brisk
    Show answer

    Answer: They’re reduced or absent. Lost reflexes in weak limbs are a key clue.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    Which infection is a classic trigger?

    1. Tooth decay
    2. Campylobacter gastroenteritis
    3. Head lice
    4. Tinea
    Show answer

    Answer: Campylobacter gastroenteritis. Many cases follow a respiratory or gut infection in the previous weeks. Zika virus was linked to outbreaks.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    What is checked repeatedly in hospital?

    1. Hair growth
    2. Hearing
    3. Eye colour
    4. Breathing strength (vital capacity)
    Show answer

    Answer: Breathing strength (vital capacity). About 1 in 4 people need help breathing on a ventilator, so it’s monitored closely.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    What does a lumbar puncture often show?

    1. Lots of bacteria
    2. Blood in the fluid
    3. High protein with a normal cell count
    4. Low protein and many cells
    Show answer

    Answer: High protein with a normal cell count. Called albuminocytological dissociation. It may be normal in the first week.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    Which test examines nerve function directly?

    1. Nerve conduction studies
    2. An echocardiogram
    3. A colonoscopy
    4. Spirometry alone
    Show answer

    Answer: Nerve conduction studies. They help confirm the diagnosis and the subtype.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    What are the two main treatments?

    1. Surgery on the spine
    2. IVIG (immunoglobulin) or plasma exchange
    3. Steroid tablets alone
    4. Antibiotics or antivirals
    Show answer

    Answer: IVIG (immunoglobulin) or plasma exchange. Both speed recovery when given early. Steroids alone don’t help in GBS.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    Which other system can GBS affect dangerously?

    1. The kidneys, stopping urine altogether
    2. The teeth, loosening them in the gums
    3. The skin, causing widespread rashes
    4. The autonomic system: BP and heart rhythm swings
    Show answer

    Answer: The autonomic system: BP and heart rhythm swings. Heart rate and blood pressure are monitored as well as breathing.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    What is Miller Fisher syndrome?

    1. A heart valve problem in young adults
    2. An autoimmune thyroid disease
    3. A GBS variant: eye weakness, unsteadiness, lost reflexes
    4. A type of migraine with visual aura
    Show answer

    Answer: A GBS variant: eye weakness, unsteadiness, lost reflexes. It’s associated with anti-GQ1b antibodies.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    What is the outlook for most people?

    1. Most recover well over months; some have lasting weakness
    2. Everyone recovers fully within a week
    3. Nobody recovers without a transplant
    4. It always returns every year
    Show answer

    Answer: Most recover well over months; some have lasting weakness. Roughly 80% walk independently by 6 months. Rehabilitation is a big part of recovery.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    How often does GBS come back?

    1. In most people
    2. Rarely, in a few per cent of people
    3. Every winter
    4. After every cold
    Show answer

    Answer: Rarely, in a few per cent of people. Recurrent or slowly progressive cases raise the possibility of CIDP, a related chronic condition.

Common questions

Is Guillain–Barré contagious?

No. The infection before it may be, but GBS itself isn’t.

Can vaccines cause GBS?

A very small increased risk has been reported with a few vaccines, far lower than the risk from the infections they prevent. Your GP can advise on your situation.
