Cutting back on social media

Social media isn’t a diagnosis, but heavy or compulsive use can crowd out sleep, exercise and time with people. The research on mood is mixed: effects on average are small, but some people, especially teens, are affected much more.

What the evidence says

  • Average links between social media time and depression or anxiety are small, and cause and effect is hard to untangle.
  • Some uses seem worse than others: late-night scrolling, comparison, cyberbullying and content about self-harm or eating.
  • Sleep is the clearest casualty: phones in bed delay and disturb sleep.
  • Many people feel better after cutting back, in small trials.

Signs it’s a problem

  • Checking compulsively, or losing hours without meaning to.
  • Feeling worse about yourself or anxious after using it.
  • It’s getting in the way of sleep, study, work or relationships.
  • Feeling irritable or anxious when you can’t check.

Practical ways to cut back

  • Turn off non-essential notifications.
  • Set app limits; move apps off your home screen; log out after use.
  • Keep phones out of the bedroom; charge them elsewhere.
  • Try greyscale mode, which makes feeds less appealing.
  • Unfollow accounts that make you feel worse.
  • Replace the habit: a book, a walk, a call to a friend.

Children and teens

  • Since 10 December 2025, Australian law requires major social media platforms to take reasonable steps to stop under-16s having accounts.
  • Family media plans, devices out of bedrooms at night and modelling good habits help.
  • The eSafety Commissioner has guides for parents and reporting tools for cyberbullying (esafety.gov.au).

Where to get help

  • Your GP, if mood, anxiety or sleep are suffering
  • Kids Helpline: 1800 55 1800 (5 to 25 year olds)
  • Lifeline 13 11 14 if you’re in crisis

Common questions

Is social media bad for mental health?

On average the link is small, but some people, especially teens and those who use it late at night or compare themselves a lot, are affected more. Sleep is the clearest harm.

What is Australia’s social media age limit?

From 10 December 2025, major social media platforms must take reasonable steps to stop under-16s having accounts.

How can I use social media less?

Turn off notifications, set app limits, keep your phone out of the bedroom, try greyscale mode and unfollow accounts that make you feel worse.
