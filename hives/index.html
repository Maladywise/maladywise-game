Fact sheet

Hives and Swelling

Hives are itchy raised welts that move around, each lasting less than a day. Angioedema is deeper swelling, often of the lips, eyelids, hands or feet. Most cases follow a viral infection or have no clear cause and settle within days to weeks. Hives most days for more than 6 weeks is called chronic hives, which is rarely due to allergy.

Treatment options

Ratings show how strong the research is.

  • Strong evidenceNon-drowsy antihistaminesSuch as cetirizine, loratadine or fexofenadine, from a pharmacy. For hives that keep coming back, take them every day, not just when itchy.
  • Strong evidenceSpecialist injectionsFor chronic hives not controlled by antihistamines, an immunologist or dermatologist can offer omalizumab injections.
  • Moderate evidenceHigher antihistamine doseIf a standard dose isn’t enough, your doctor may advise up to 4 times the usual daily dose. Only do this on medical advice.
  • Limited evidenceMontelukastA prescription tablet sometimes added for chronic hives.
  • Limited evidenceShort course of steroid tabletsFor a severe flare, for a few days only. Repeated courses cause side effects.
  • Not recommendedDrowsy antihistaminesOlder types cause next-day drowsiness and aren’t needed.
  • Not recommendedAllergy tests or strict diets for chronic hivesRarely find a cause. Only with medical advice.

What you can do

  • Cool showers or cool packs ease itch. Wear loose clothing.
  • Avoid triggers such as heat, alcohol and spicy food. Use paracetamol instead of aspirin or anti-inflammatories, which can worsen hives.
  • Note what you ate, took or did in the 2 hours before a sudden reaction.

See your GP if

  • Hives last more than 6 weeks, or keep coming back
  • Swelling happens without hives, especially if you take an ACE inhibitor blood pressure tablet (names end in "-pril"). Antihistamines don’t help this type.
  • Individual welts last more than a day, bruise or come with fever or joint pain

Call 000 for swelling of the tongue or throat, trouble breathing or swallowing, a hoarse voice, wheeze, dizziness or collapse. Use an adrenaline autoinjector first if you have one, and lie down rather than stand.

Common questions

What causes hives?

Most cases follow a viral infection or have no clear cause, and settle within days to weeks. Hives most days for more than 6 weeks (chronic hives) is rarely due to allergy.

Which antihistamine is best for hives?

A non-drowsy one such as cetirizine, loratadine or fexofenadine. For hives that keep coming back, take it every day. Only take a higher dose on medical advice.

Do I need allergy tests for hives?

For chronic hives, allergy tests and strict diets rarely find a cause. Only have them with medical advice.

When are hives an emergency?

Call 000 for swelling of the tongue or throat, trouble breathing or swallowing, a hoarse voice, wheeze, dizziness or collapse. Use an adrenaline autoinjector first if you have one.
