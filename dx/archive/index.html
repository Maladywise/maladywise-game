  1. #26 · Wednesday 11 November 2026 Medium

    Four days in wet boots

    The clues:

    • 19-year-old soldier in a flooded front-line dugout in Flanders, winter 1915.
    • He hasn’t had his boots off in four days. The water has been over his ankles the whole time.
    • His feet went numb first, then started burning. They’re swollen, blotchy and blue.
    • It’s just above freezing. Nothing has actually frozen.
    • On rewarming, feet turn red, hot and very painful, with blisters
    • Severe cases can go on to skin breakdown and gangrene. The army’s fix: dry socks and a mate to rub in whale oil

    Diagnosis: Trench foot

    Pearl: Trench foot is a non-freezing cold injury from hours to days of cold, wet feet, and it cost armies huge numbers of soldiers in the First World War. Men were paired up to check and rub each other’s feet with whale oil, and officers were held responsible for foot care. It hasn’t gone away: it still turns up in people sleeping rough and at very wet festivals. Today is Remembrance Day.

  2. #25 · Tuesday 10 November 2026 Easy

    Something in my eye

    The clues:

    • 31-year-old woman. While pruning the roses yesterday, a branch flicked her right eye.
    • Now it’s watering nonstop and feels like there’s grit she can’t blink out.
    • Bright light hurts. She keeps the eye squeezed shut.
    • Visual acuity: 6/9 right, 6/6 left
    • Eversion of the upper lid: no foreign body hiding underneath
    • Fluorescein under blue light: a bright green linear streak across the cornea

    Diagnosis: Corneal abrasion

    Pearl: The corneal surface heals fast, usually within a day or two. Lubricant or antibiotic drops help, but never send anaesthetic drops home: they slow healing and hide new damage. Contact lens wearers need cover against Pseudomonas and a low threshold for review. Today is World Keratoconus Day, another condition of the cornea.

  3. #24 · Monday 9 November 2026 Easy

    Weightless and green

    The clues:

    • 38-year-old woman, six hours into her first trip to the International Space Station.
    • Nausea, then vomiting, then a headache. Turning her head quickly sets it all off again.
    • Her face looks puffy and her legs have gone skinny. Her crewmates call it "puffy face, bird legs".
    • No fever, nobody else on board has gastro and she passed every medical on the ground.
    • Neurological exam normal, apart from feeling awful when she looks around the cabin
    • Her inner ear is reporting zero gravity while her eyes insist the wall is the floor. Better by day 3

    Diagnosis: Space motion sickness

    Pearl: More than half of astronauts get space motion sickness in their first few days, and it usually settles within 2 to 4 days as the brain learns to ignore its confused inner ear. The puffy face comes from fluid shifting headwards without gravity to pull it down. NASA’s Anil Menon, who spacewalked outside the station in late October 2026, is an emergency physician and a former flight surgeon, so he knows the drill.

  4. #23 · Sunday 8 November 2026 Genius

    Mirror image

    The clues:

    • 34-year-old man with a wet cough he says he's had 'since forever'.
    • Blocked nose and sinus infections since childhood. Grommets twice as a kid.
    • He and his partner have been trying for a baby for 3 years.
    • Semen analysis: normal count, but the sperm barely move
    • CT chest: bronchiectasis in both lower lobes
    • Chest X-ray: heart and stomach bubble on the right. The radiographer re-checked the side marker.

    Diagnosis: Kartagener syndrome (primary ciliary dyskinesia)

    Pearl: Primary ciliary dyskinesia: cilia that can't beat properly cause a lifelong wet cough, sinusitis and bronchiectasis, and the same fault slows sperm. About half of people with it have their organs mirror-flipped (situs inversus), which makes it Kartagener syndrome. Always check the side marker before calling dextrocardia. Happy International Day of Radiology.

  5. #22 · Saturday 7 November 2026 Hard

    Straight edges

    The clues:

    • 62-year-old woman with a dry cough and breathlessness for 2 weeks.
    • Low-grade fevers. Two courses of antibiotics haven't helped.
    • She finished treatment for right breast cancer 8 weeks ago.
    • Fine crackles over the right upper chest only. Oxygen saturation 94% on room air.
    • Chest X-ray: hazy right upper zone shadowing with an oddly straight edge
    • CT: ground-glass change matching the shape of her radiotherapy field, ignoring the lobe boundaries

    Diagnosis: Radiation pneumonitis

    Pearl: Radiation pneumonitis usually appears 1 to 6 months after chest radiotherapy. The clue is a shadow that follows the beam, not the anatomy. Medical physicists plan every beam to keep lung dose low, which is why it's uncommon. Mild cases settle on their own; more troublesome ones usually respond to steroids.

  6. #21 · Friday 6 November 2026 Hard

    Family pattern

    The clues:

    • 44-year-old woman with heavy, irregular periods for 6 months.
    • Her father had bowel cancer at 47. His sister had ‘womb cancer’ in her 40s.
    • Pelvic ultrasound: thickened endometrium
    • Endometrial biopsy: endometrioid adenocarcinoma
    • Tumour immunohistochemistry: loss of MSH2 and MSH6 proteins
    • Her 20-year-old son asks whether he should have a colonoscopy.

    Diagnosis: Lynch syndrome

    Pearl: Lynch syndrome is the commonest inherited cause of bowel and endometrial cancer, and in women the womb cancer often comes first. Many countries now test every new bowel and endometrial cancer for mismatch repair loss, which also guides treatment. Mismatch repair-deficient endometrial cancer was a headline topic at the ESMO congress in Madrid in October 2026.

  7. #20 · Thursday 5 November 2026 Medium

    Walking on tiptoes

    The clues:

    • 7-year-old boy who woke up refusing to walk. When he tries, he tiptoes.
    • Three days of fever, cough and a runny nose. Half his class is off sick.
    • Both calves are tender to squeeze. Strength, reflexes and hips are normal.
    • CK 4,200 U/L (normal <200)
    • Urine is a normal colour and kidney function is normal
    • Nose swab: influenza B detected

    Diagnosis: Benign acute childhood myositis

    Pearl: Benign acute childhood myositis follows influenza (usually B, sometimes A), mostly in school-age boys, and settles within about a week. Check urine colour and kidney function, as it rarely tips into rhabdomyolysis. The northern hemisphere flu season started early in 2026, with the UK reporting rising cases from October.

  8. #19 · Wednesday 4 November 2026 Medium

    Wetland weekend

    The clues:

    • 45-year-old woman with painful, stiff hands, wrists and ankles.
    • Exhausted. Struggling to open jars for 3 weeks.
    • A faint red rash on her trunk came and went in the first week.
    • Low-grade fever at the start. No history of joint problems.
    • Went camping near coastal wetlands in late summer and was bitten a lot.

  11. #16 · Sunday 1 November 2026 Genius

    Burning since childhood

    The clues:

    • 32-year-old man with burning pain in his hands and feet since he was a boy.
    • It's worse in heat, with fever, or during exercise. He barely sweats.
    • Clusters of tiny dark red spots around his navel and swimwear area.
    • Urine dipstick: protein 2+
    • His uncle had kidney failure and a stroke in his 40s.
    • Eye exam: whorl-like patterns in both corneas

    Diagnosis: Fabry disease

    Pearl: An X-linked lysosomal storage disease. Women can be affected too, often more mildly. Childhood burning pain plus reduced sweating is a classic early clue. Enzyme replacement and oral therapies exist, so diagnosis matters.

  1. #15 · Saturday 31 October 2026 Hard

    Cold feet

    The clues:

    • 58-year-old woman with burning pain and coldness in both feet for 2 days.
    • Her toes go white, then a dusky blue. Walking a short way makes her calves ache.
    • She started clarithromycin for a chest infection 4 days ago.
    • Foot pulses are barely felt. Heart rhythm is regular.
    • For years she has taken an old migraine tablet a few times a month.
    • Angiogram: smooth, tapered narrowing of arteries in both legs. No clots.

    Diagnosis: Ergotism (ergotamine toxicity)

    Pearl: Clarithromycin blocks CYP3A4, so ergotamine builds up and clamps arteries shut. In the Middle Ages, ergot-infected rye caused ‘St Anthony's fire’. A 1976 paper even blamed the Salem witch trials on it, though historians remain unconvinced. Happy Halloween.

  3. #13 · Thursday 29 October 2026 Medium

    Pain in the neck

    The clues:

    • 42-year-old man with sudden pain on the left side of his neck and a left-sided headache.
    • It began 2 days ago after a vigorous neck stretch at the gym.
    • His left upper eyelid droops slightly and his left pupil is small.
    • This morning his right hand went clumsy and weak for 20 minutes, then recovered.
    • BP 128/80 · ECG: sinus rhythm · Glucose normal
    • CT angiogram: long, tapering narrowing of the left internal carotid artery.

    Diagnosis: Carotid artery dissection

    Pearl: A leading cause of stroke under 50. Neck pain plus a partial Horner's syndrome plus a TIA or stroke is the classic trio. The Horner's is usually partial because sweating fibres to the face travel with the external carotid. Happy World Stroke Day.

    Lifting the baby

    The clues:

    • 31-year-old woman with pain on the thumb side of her wrist for 4 weeks.
    • Her baby is 3 months old. Scooping him out of the cot makes it much worse.
    • Wringing out a cloth sends pain up the forearm.
    • Tender, slightly swollen spot just above the base of the thumb. No numbness or tingling.

    Swelling with no itch

    The clues:

    • 23-year-old woman with recurrent swelling of her hands and lips.
    • Each episode lasts 2–4 days. No hives. No itch.
    • Antihistamines, steroids and even adrenaline don't help.
    • Twice she's had severe abdominal pain and vomiting. Scans showed swollen bowel wall.
    • Swelling followed a dental procedure. It worsened on the combined pill.
    • C4 low · C1-inhibitor level low

    Diagnosis: Hereditary angioedema

  10. #6 · Thursday 22 October 2026 Medium

    Standing ovation

    The clues:

    • 24-year-old woman. Dizzy and foggy since a viral illness 6 months ago.
    • Heart pounds when she stands in the shower queue or at the checkout.
    • Feels better lying down. Worse in heat and after big meals.
    • ECG normal. FBC, TSH, ferritin and cortisol all normal.
    • Lying: HR 68, BP 112/70 · Standing 10 min: HR 112, BP 110/72
    • Symptoms persist over 3 months. No orthostatic hypotension.

    Diagnosis: POTS (postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome)

    Pearl: Sustained HR rise of 30+ bpm (40+ under 20) within 10 minutes of standing, without a BP drop, plus 3+ months of symptoms. A GP-room active stand test does the job before any tilt table.

  15. #1 · Saturday 17 October 2026 Hard

    Toasty shins

    The clues:

    • 71-year-old woman with a painless rash on the fronts of both shins.
    • Net-like, reddish-brown pattern. Not itchy, not raised.
    • The backs of her legs are completely spared.
    • It appeared over winter and has slowly darkened.
    • Lives alone in an old, poorly insulated house.
    • Spends every evening with a bar heater about 30 cm from her legs.

    Diagnosis: Erythema ab igne

    Pearl: Chronic low-level heat causes reticulated pigmentation. Modern versions come from laptops on thighs and heat packs. Remove the heat source; biopsy only if a lesion ulcerates or changes, as SCC can rarely develop.
