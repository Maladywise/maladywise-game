Kawasaki Disease Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on Kawasaki disease: the childhood vasculitis that can harm the heart’s arteries, how it’s recognised and treated.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    Who mostly gets Kawasaki disease?

    1. Teenage athletes
    2. Older adults
    3. Pregnant women
    4. Children under 5
    Show answer

    Answer: Children under 5. It’s the leading cause of acquired heart disease in children in many countries.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    What kind of condition is it?

    1. A food allergy
    2. A vasculitis (inflammation of blood vessels)
    3. An inherited heart valve defect
    4. A bacterial throat infection
    Show answer

    Answer: A vasculitis (inflammation of blood vessels). The cause is still unknown; it’s thought to be an immune reaction to a trigger in susceptible children.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Which symptom is required for the classic diagnosis?

    1. Fever for 5 days or more
    2. Diarrhoea
    3. A cough
    4. A rash only
    Show answer

    Answer: Fever for 5 days or more. Plus at least 4 of 5 other features.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    Which eye finding is typical?

    1. Yellow whites of the eyes
    2. Drooping eyelids
    3. Red eyes without pus or discharge
    4. Sticky eyes with pus
    Show answer

    Answer: Red eyes without pus or discharge. Non-purulent bilateral conjunctival injection.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    Which mouth change is typical?

    1. Red, cracked lips and a strawberry tongue
    2. A swollen gum abscess
    3. White patches that scrape off
    4. Teeth falling out
    Show answer

    Answer: Red, cracked lips and a strawberry tongue. Strawberry tongue also occurs in scarlet fever, a key mimic.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    What happens to the hands and feet?

    1. They turn very cold and white
    2. They go numb and tingle
    3. The nails grow much faster
    4. Redness and swelling, then peeling fingertips
    Show answer

    Answer: Redness and swelling, then peeling fingertips. Peeling usually appears in the second or third week.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    What is the main danger?

    1. Hearing loss
    2. Kidney stones
    3. Coronary artery aneurysms
    4. Blindness
    Show answer

    Answer: Coronary artery aneurysms. Without treatment, about 1 in 4 children develop coronary artery abnormalities.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    Which test checks the coronary arteries?

    1. A skin prick test
    2. An echocardiogram
    3. A chest X-ray
    4. A urine test
    Show answer

    Answer: An echocardiogram. Repeated at diagnosis and in follow-up.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    What is the main treatment?

    1. Antihistamines
    2. Antibiotics alone
    3. Fluids only
    4. IVIG (immunoglobulin) given early
    Show answer

    Answer: IVIG (immunoglobulin) given early. Best within 10 days of fever onset. It cuts aneurysm risk to around 1 in 20.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    Which medicine is used in Kawasaki disease despite usually being avoided in children?

    1. Ibuprofen
    2. Aspirin
    3. Codeine
    4. Tetracycline
    Show answer

    Answer: Aspirin. Used under specialist care. Families get advice about flu and chickenpox, because of Reye syndrome risk.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    What is incomplete Kawasaki disease?

    1. Fever with fewer than 4 classic features; still treated
    2. Kawasaki disease in adults only
    3. A misdiagnosis that needs no treatment
    4. A mild cold that looks like Kawasaki
    Show answer

    Answer: Fever with fewer than 4 classic features; still treated. Common in infants, who are at higher risk of heart complications. Blood tests and echo guide decisions.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Which condition seen after COVID-19 can look like Kawasaki disease?

    1. Bronchiolitis
    2. Long COVID
    3. PIMS-TS / MIS-C
    4. Hay fever
    Show answer

    Answer: PIMS-TS / MIS-C. A multisystem inflammatory syndrome in children that overlaps clinically with Kawasaki disease.

Common questions

Is Kawasaki disease contagious?

No. It isn’t spread from child to child.

Do children recover?

Most recover fully, especially with early IVIG. Children with coronary changes need long-term heart follow-up.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. A child with a fever for 5 days or more needs a doctor’s review.
