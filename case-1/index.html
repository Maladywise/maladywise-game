Case #1 · Fictional teaching case

Rusty joints

Read the clues, make your guess, then reveal more. No peeking.

  1. A 52-year-old man has been tired for a year. “Just getting old, doc.”
  2. His 2nd and 3rd knuckles ache in both hands. A firm handshake hurts.
  3. His partner says he looks tanned all year round. He works in an office.

What would you ask next? What tests would you order?

  1. He was diagnosed with type 2 diabetes last month. His BMI is 23.
  2. Blood tests: ALT 88 U/L, ferritin 1,450 µg/L.

Changed your answer?

  1. Transferrin saturation 78%.

Answer: hereditary haemochromatosis

The body absorbs too much iron from food and stores it in the liver, pancreas, joints and skin. It’s one of the most common genetic conditions in people of Northern European descent.

Teaching points

  • A high ferritin on its own is non-specific. Inflammation, alcohol and fatty liver raise it too.
  • A transferrin saturation over 45% is the clue that points to iron overload and prompts HFE gene testing.
  • Arthritis in the 2nd and 3rd knuckles is a classic early sign.
  • Treatment is regular venesection (removing blood). Australian Red Cross Lifeblood can accept the blood as a donation.
  • First-degree relatives should be offered testing.
