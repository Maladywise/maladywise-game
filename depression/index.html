Fact sheet

Depression

More than a bad week: low mood or loss of interest most of the day, nearly every day, for at least 2 weeks. About 5 in 100 adults worldwide have depression right now. It’s common, it’s not a character flaw, and it’s very treatable.

If you’re thinking about suicide or don’t feel safe, call 000 (Australia) or your local emergency number now. From a mobile, 112 works in most countries. For support any time, call Lifeline on 13 11 14 or Beyond Blue on 1300 22 4636 (both 24/7). Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people can call 13YARN on 13 92 76 (24/7). Young people aged 5–25 can call Kids Helpline on 1800 55 1800 (24/7). Outside Australia, find a free helpline at findahelpline.com. Feelings this heavy can and do ease with support. You don’t have to wait until things are worse to reach out.

Take a free depression self-check (PHQ-9): 9 questions, private, about 2 minutes.

The depression ladder

Milder depression often lifts with self-care and guided help. For moderate or severe depression, talking therapy, medicine or both work best. Needing more than one rung is normal, not failure.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careMove, sleep, connectRegular activity (walking, jogging, yoga or strength training), steady sleep and wake times, and a small enjoyable or useful thing planned each day.
    Works
    Exercise lowered depression scores by a moderate amount in a 2024 review of trials, more with harder effort. Walking, jogging, yoga and strength training all helped
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    Most days; benefits build over a few weeks
    Watch for
    Not a substitute for treatment if you’re very unwell. Alcohol deepens low mood, so go easy.

    Your brain doesn’t need a marathon. A brisk walk counts.

  2. Step 2 · Self-careGP visit and online CBTYour GP checks for physical causes (such as thyroid problems or anaemia), asks about safety, and can write a mental health treatment plan. Free online courses such as the MindSpot Wellbeing Course teach practical CBT skills.
    Works
    Guided self-help is a recommended first choice for milder depression in UK guidelines; it works best with someone checking in on you
    Cost
    GP visit often bulk billed or about $40–80 gap; MindSpot is free; THIS WAY UP courses are free with a clinician’s prescription
    How long
    MindSpot Wellbeing Course: 5 lessons over 8 weeks
    Watch for
    Feeling worse or unsafe? Don’t wait for the course to finish: talk to your GP or a crisis line.

    CBT in your trackies, with a clinician in your corner.

  3. Step 3 · Allied healthTalking therapy (CBT, behavioural activation, interpersonal therapy)With a psychologist or other trained therapist. A first choice for most people, alone or alongside medicine.
    Works
    About 4 in 10 respond within about 2 months, vs about 1 in 6 on a waiting list or usual care
    Cost
    About $200–300 a session; Medicare pays back about $100–150 for up to 10 sessions a year with a GP mental health treatment plan. Some services bulk bill
    How long
    Usually weekly for a few months
    Watch for
    If it isn’t clicking after a few sessions, it’s fine to try a different therapist or approach.

    Not just venting. It’s skills training for your thoughts.

  4. Step 4 · PrescriptionAntidepressant medicine (such as sertraline, escitalopram, fluoxetine or mirtazapine)For moderate to severe depression, or when therapy alone isn’t enough. Often combined with therapy.
    Works
    About 50 in 100 improve within 6–8 weeks, vs about 30 in 100 on a dummy tablet. Staying on it for a year or two after recovery cuts relapse from about 50 in 100 to about 23 in 100
    Cost
    Most generics under the $25 PBS co-payment ($7.70 concession)
    How long
    Often 1–2 weeks to notice a change and several weeks for the full effect. Usually continued for at least 6 months after you feel well
    Watch for
    Nausea, sleep changes, sexual side effects. Under 25s: check in often in the first weeks, as thoughts of self-harm can rise early on. Don’t stop suddenly; taper with your GP.

    Not a personality transplant. More like turning the volume back up.

  5. Step 5 · SpecialistPsychiatristFor severe depression, depression that hasn’t lifted after two treatments, possible bipolar disorder, psychosis, or ongoing risk.
    Works
    Not a trial question: reviews the diagnosis, combines or switches medicines and coordinates more intensive care
    Cost
    Private psychiatrist about $300–600 for a first visit, with partial Medicare rebates; public mental health services are free
    How long
    Waits can be weeks to months
    Watch for
    Keep seeing your GP and therapist while you wait.

    Join the queue early. You can always cancel if you’re better.

  6. Step 6 · SpecialistBrain stimulation (rTMS, ECT) and hospital careSpecialist treatments for severe or hard-to-treat depression, arranged by a psychiatrist.
    Works
    Reserved for depression that hasn’t responded to other treatment; your psychiatrist can explain your likely benefit
    Cost
    Free in public services; private costs vary and some are covered by private hospital cover or Medicare when eligible
    How long
    rTMS: daily sessions over several weeks. ECT: usually 2–3 times a week for a few weeks
    Watch for
    ECT can cause short-term memory problems. Ask what to expect before you start.

    Old reputations, modern methods. Worth a fair hearing if nothing else has worked.

Costs are rough Australian prices in November 2026 and vary by provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Better Access: up to 10 rebated individual sessions a calendar year with a GP mental health treatment plan. Results are averages from trials, and recovery is rarely a straight line.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Fish oil (omega-3) capsulesA Cochrane review found a small effect, probably too small to notice, from low-quality studies.
  • Vitamin D tablets (if your level is normal)In a 5-year trial of over 18,000 adults, vitamin D didn’t prevent depression or lift mood.
  • Medicinal cannabis and CBDA 2026 review of 54 trials found no evidence it helps depression, and the authors warned it may do more harm than good. Pricey, too.
  • St John’s wortSome studies suggest it helps milder depression, but strength varies between products and it interacts with many medicines, including the contraceptive pill, blood thinners and antidepressants. UK guidelines advise against it.

What you can do

  • Plan one small thing each day that’s enjoyable or gives a sense of achievement, even before you feel like it. Motivation often follows action, not the other way round.
  • Keep regular sleep and wake times. If sleep is a battle, see our trouble sleeping page.
  • Tell one person you trust how you’re really going.
  • Cut back on alcohol and other drugs. They borrow tonight’s relief from next week’s mood.
  • Write a simple safety plan with your GP or therapist: warning signs, people to call, and reasons to stay safe.
  • Supporting someone? Ask how they’re really going, listen without fixing, and help them book a GP visit.

See your GP if

  • Low mood or loss of interest most days for 2 weeks or more
  • It’s affecting work, study, relationships or looking after yourself
  • You’ve had depression before and notice the early signs coming back
  • Low mood during pregnancy or after having a baby
  • You’re on an antidepressant and it isn’t helping after 4–6 weeks, or side effects are a problem

Get help the same day if you have thoughts of ending your life, feel unable to keep yourself safe, hear or see things others don’t, or feel unusually high, reckless and sleepless for days. Call your GP, a mental health crisis team, 000 or your local emergency number.

Support lines

If you or someone else is in danger right now, call your emergency number. To talk to someone, any time:

  • Australia: Lifeline 13 11 14 (24/7, or text 0477 13 11 14), Suicide Call Back Service 1300 659 467, Beyond Blue 1300 22 4636, 13YARN 13 92 76 for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people. Emergency: 000.
  • Young people in Australia: Kids Helpline 1800 55 1800 (24/7, ages 5 to 25) or headspace (ages 12 to 25).
  • New Zealand: call or text 1737. Emergency: 111.
  • UK and Ireland: Samaritans 116 123. Emergency: 999 (or 112).
  • US and Canada: call or text 988. Emergency: 911.
  • Anywhere else: findahelpline.com lists free helplines in more than 100 countries.

Common questions

What are the symptoms of depression?

Low or empty mood, or losing interest and pleasure in things, most of the day, nearly every day, for at least 2 weeks. Often with poor sleep or sleeping too much, appetite changes, tiredness, poor concentration, guilt or low self-worth, hopelessness, and sometimes thoughts of death.

What causes depression?

Usually a mix of things rather than one cause: genes and family history, stressful or traumatic events, loneliness, long-term illness or pain, alcohol and drugs, some medicines, and big hormonal changes such as after having a baby. It isn’t weakness or something you can just snap out of.

How is depression diagnosed?

By talking with your GP about your symptoms and how long they’ve lasted, often with a short questionnaire. There’s no blood test for depression, but your GP may check for things that mimic it, such as thyroid problems, anaemia or low vitamin B12.

Are antidepressants addictive?

They don’t cause cravings or a need for higher doses the way addictive drugs do. Stopping suddenly can cause dizziness, sleep problems, nausea and restlessness, so reduce the dose slowly with your GP.

Will I need medicine forever?

Most people don’t. It’s usually continued for at least 6 months after you feel well, then reviewed. People who’ve had several episodes may be advised to stay on it longer to prevent relapse.

What’s the difference between sadness and depression?

Sadness usually eases with time and good things still lift your mood. Depression is low mood or loss of interest most of the day, nearly every day, for at least 2 weeks, along with changes such as poor sleep, tiredness, poor focus or feeling worthless.

Does exercise help depression?

Yes. Large reviews show regular exercise, especially walking, jogging, yoga and strength training, reduces depression symptoms about as much as talking therapy for mild to moderate depression. It works best alongside other treatment, not instead of it when depression is severe.

How can I help someone with depression?

Listen without judging, encourage them to see a GP and offer practical help, such as going with them. Ask directly if they’re thinking of suicide; asking doesn’t put the idea in their head. For crisis support call Lifeline on 13 11 14, or 000 in an emergency.
