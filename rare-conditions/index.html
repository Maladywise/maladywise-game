    How to read it. Most figures are prevalence: how many people have it at any one time. Some are new cases a year, rates at birth, or a count of reported cases where no rate exists. Figures from a single country or group are marked “local figure”, because they can make a condition look commoner than it is worldwide. “Most common first” lists worldwide and Europe-wide figures before local ones. Names use Australian spelling (haemoglobin, oedema); the original Orphanet spelling still finds them. Many figures are bands (for example 1–9 in 100,000), shown as ranges.

    Source. Orphanet, Orphadata rare disease epidemiology and classifications (data dated 23 Jun 2026), CC BY 4.0. Conditions with no usable figure are left out.
