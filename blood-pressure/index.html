Fact sheet

High Blood Pressure

Usually no symptoms, but it quietly raises the risk of stroke, heart attack, kidney disease and dementia. High means 140/90 in clinic or a home average of 135/85. Lowering it helps at any age.

Measuring at home. Use a validated upper-arm monitor. For 7 days, sit quietly for 5 minutes with your back supported, feet flat and arm at heart level, then take 2 readings a minute apart, morning and evening, before food, medicines or caffeine. Write them down for your GP.

The blood pressure ladder

Lifestyle steps stack: do several and the points add up. Many people still need tablets, and that’s not failure.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careLess salt, more plantsUnder 5 g of salt a day (about a teaspoon), plus “DASH”-style eating: vegetables, fruit, wholegrains, legumes, nuts, low-fat dairy.
    Works
    Less salt: about 5 points lower. DASH eating: up to about 10 points, as much as one tablet
    Cost
    Free, or cheaper than takeaway
    How long
    2–4 weeks to show
    Watch for
    Hidden salt in bread, sauces, stock, takeaway and processed meats

    The salt shaker isn’t the main culprit. The sauce bottle and the deli are.

  2. Step 2 · Self-careMove more, lose a little, drink less150 minutes of activity a week plus some strength work; weight loss if needed; no more than 10 standard drinks a week.
    Works
    Exercise: about 4–8 points. About 1 point per kilo lost. Heavy drinkers halving alcohol: about 5 points
    Cost
    Free to cheap
    How long
    Weeks to months
    Watch for
    Energy drinks, decongestants and anti-inflammatories can push it up

    Walking counts. You need shoes, not a gym membership.

  3. Step 3 · PharmacyHome blood pressure monitorA validated upper-arm cuff, so you and your GP see your real numbers.
    Works
    On its own, only about 1–2 points lower; up to about 6 with GP or pharmacist follow-up. Catches “white coat” readings
    Cost
    About $60–150
    How long
    7 days of readings to diagnose, then now and then
    Watch for
    Wrist and finger monitors are unreliable

    Measured straight after a coffee, an argument and the stairs? Doesn’t count.

  4. Step 4 · PrescriptionOne tablet: ACE inhibitor or ARB, calcium channel blocker, or thiazide-type diureticThe usual starting medicines. Taken once a day.
    Works
    Each lowers it by roughly 8–10 points. Every 10 points lower cuts stroke risk by about a quarter
    Cost
    Under the $25 PBS co-payment, about $10–25 a script; many come as 2-month supplies
    How long
    2–4 weeks for full effect; usually long term
    Watch for
    ACE inhibitors: dry cough. Calcium channel blockers: ankle swelling, flushing. Diuretics: more weeing. Dizziness on standing. ACE inhibitors and ARBs: not in pregnancy

    Normal readings on tablets mean they’re working, not that you’re cured.

  5. Step 5 · PrescriptionTwo medicines, often in one tabletLow doses of two medicines instead of a high dose of one.
    Works
    Adding a second medicine lowers it about 5 times more than doubling one dose, with fewer side effects
    Cost
    Under the $25 PBS co-payment, usually about $15–25 a script
    How long
    Long term
    Watch for
    Same as the individual medicines. Blood tests for kidneys and potassium

    Two small doses beat one big one. Teamwork.

  6. Step 6 · SpecialistSpecialist review for resistant blood pressureStill high on three medicines? Time to look for hidden causes such as excess aldosterone, sleep apnoea or kidney disease.
    Works
    Spironolactone lowers it about 9 more points than a dummy treatment. Finds a fixable cause in some people
    Cost
    About $200–400 for a first visit, partly rebated by Medicare with a GP referral
    How long
    Several visits and tests
    Watch for
    Missed doses. Honest pill-taking saves a lot of tests

    Before blaming your glands, check the pill box.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Point drops are averages for the top number. Combine steps and they add up.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Cuffless smartwatches and phone appsNot accurate enough to diagnose or manage blood pressure. Use a validated upper-arm cuff.
  • Sea salt and pink saltSame sodium as table salt, just prettier crystals and a bigger bill.
  • Garlic, coenzyme Q10 and “natural” BP blendsSome trials show small drops, but results are inconsistent and products vary. Not a substitute for proven treatment.
  • Celery juice and apple cider vinegarNo good trials. Wellness influencers aren’t a blood pressure guideline.

What you can do

  • Take tablets every day, even when you feel fine. High blood pressure doesn’t announce itself.
  • Check labels on pharmacy medicines: anti-inflammatories and decongestants can raise blood pressure.
  • Don’t smoke, and go easy on energy drinks.
  • Have a Heart Health Check with your GP from age 45 (30 for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people).

See your GP if

  • Your home average is 135/85 or more
  • You feel dizzy or faint on standing while on blood pressure tablets
  • You snore loudly and are tired during the day (sleep apnoea can raise blood pressure)
  • You’re pregnant or planning to be and your blood pressure is raised, or you take blood pressure tablets

Call 000 for chest pain, sudden weakness, numbness or trouble speaking, or a sudden severe headache. See a doctor the same day for a reading of 180/110 or higher, or if you’re pregnant with a raised reading, headache, vision changes or sudden swelling.

Common questions

What blood pressure is too high?

In Australia, high blood pressure means a clinic reading of 140/90 or more, or a home average of 135/85 or more. See a doctor the same day for a reading of 180/110 or higher.

How do I check my blood pressure at home?

Use a validated upper-arm monitor. For 7 days, sit quietly for 5 minutes, then take 2 readings a minute apart, morning and evening, and write them down for your GP.

Can I lower blood pressure without tablets?

Often by a useful amount. Less salt, healthy eating, regular exercise, weight loss and less alcohol each lower it, and the effects add up. Many people still need tablets as well.

Can I stop my tablets once my readings are normal?

Normal readings usually mean the tablets are working. Talk to your GP before stopping.
