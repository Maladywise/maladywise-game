Fact sheet

High Blood Pressure

Usually causes no symptoms but raises the risk of stroke, heart attack, kidney disease and dementia. In Australia, high blood pressure means a clinic reading of 140/90 or more, or a home average of 135/85 or more. Lowering it reduces these risks at any age.

Measuring at home. Use a validated upper-arm monitor, not a wrist one. For 7 days, sit quietly for 5 minutes with your back supported, feet flat and arm resting at heart level, then take 2 readings a minute apart, morning and evening. Measure before food, medicines or caffeine. Write them down for your GP.

Treatment options

Ratings show how strong the research is.

  • Strong evidenceLess saltAim for less than 5 g of salt (about a teaspoon) a day. Choose low-salt foods and check labels. Lowers blood pressure by about 5 points.
  • Strong evidenceHealthy eatingPlenty of vegetables, fruit, wholegrains, legumes, nuts and low-fat dairy (a "DASH" style diet). Can lower it by up to 10 points, about as much as one tablet.
  • Strong evidenceRegular exerciseAt least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, plus some strength work. Lowers it by about 4 to 8 points.
  • Strong evidenceWeight lossIf you’re above a healthy weight, it drops by about 1 point for each kilo lost.
  • Strong evidenceBlood pressure tabletsUsually an ACE inhibitor or ARB, a calcium channel blocker or a thiazide-type diuretic. Low doses of two together often work better than a high dose of one. Usually long-term.
  • Moderate evidenceLess alcoholNo more than 10 standard drinks a week. Less is better.
  • Not recommendedStopping tablets when readings are normalNormal readings usually mean the tablets are working. Talk to your GP first.

What you can do

  • Check labels on pharmacy medicines: anti-inflammatories and decongestants can raise blood pressure.
  • Don’t smoke, and go easy on energy drinks.
  • Have a Heart Health Check with your GP from age 45 (30 for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people).

See your GP if

  • Your home average is 135/85 or more
  • You feel dizzy or faint on standing while on blood pressure tablets
  • You snore loudly and are tired during the day (sleep apnoea can raise blood pressure)
  • You’re pregnant and your blood pressure is raised

Call 000 for chest pain, sudden weakness, numbness or trouble speaking, or a sudden severe headache. See a doctor the same day if your reading is 180/110 or higher.
