Nail Psoriasis

Up to half of people with psoriasis have nail changes, and most people with psoriatic arthritis do. Nails are slow and stubborn to treat because they grow slowly, but they do improve, especially with modern injections.

What it looks like

  • Pits: small dents in the nail surface, like a thimble
  • Oil-drop or salmon patches: yellow-orange marks under the nail
  • Lifting (onycholysis): the nail separates from the bed, often from the tip
  • Thickening and crumbling, with scale building up under the nail
  • Fingernails are more often affected than toenails

Nail psoriasis is a clue for psoriatic arthritis. Tell your GP about any joint swelling, heel pain or morning stiffness.

Psoriasis or fungus?

  • They look similar, and you can have both
  • A nail clipping sent to the lab confirms fungus before months of antifungal tablets
  • Psoriasis elsewhere and pitting point towards psoriasis
  • Fungus more often starts in one toenail and spreads

Twelve weeks of antifungal tablets for a nail that never had fungus is a lot of liver tests for nothing.

What helps

Expect slow progress: fingernails take about 6 months to grow out, toenails 12–18 months.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Antifungal tablets without a positive testWon’t help psoriasis and carry side effects.
  • Tea tree oil and nail “hardeners”No evidence for psoriasis; can irritate.
  • Biotin for nailsNo benefit in psoriasis and can upset some blood tests, including thyroid and heart tests.

Common questions

What does nail psoriasis look like?

Small pits, yellow-orange oil-drop patches, nails lifting from the bed, thickening and crumbling.

How do I know if it’s nail psoriasis or fungus?

They can look the same. A nail clipping test confirms fungus. Psoriasis elsewhere or pitting points to psoriasis.

How long does nail psoriasis take to improve?

Months: fingernails take about 6 months to grow out and toenails 12 to 18 months, so treatments need time.
