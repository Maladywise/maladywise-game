Fact sheet

Menopause and Perimenopause

Your final period, confirmed after 12 months without one, usually around 51. The years before (perimenopause) can bring hot flushes, poor sleep, mood changes and irregular periods. Very treatable.

The menopause ladder

No one has to just put up with it. Hormone therapy is the most effective treatment for most women; good options exist for those who can’t or don’t want to use it.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careLifestyle and symptom trackingRegular exercise, cutting back alcohol, stopping smoking, layered clothing and a cool bedroom. Track symptoms to see your patterns.
    Works
    Helps general health, sleep and coping; hot flushes improve less than with treatment
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    Ongoing
    Watch for
    Weight-bearing and strength exercise also protects bones and heart, which matter more after menopause.

    A fan, a symptom diary and a strength session: unglamorous, effective.

  2. Step 2 · Allied healthCognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)A structured talking therapy, one-on-one, in a group or online, aimed at hot flushes, sleep and mood.
    Works
    Reduces how often hot flushes happen and how much they bother you, and helps sleep and low mood
    Cost
    Psychologist fees vary; some online and group programs are low cost
    How long
    A short course of sessions
    Watch for
    CBT doesn’t mean it’s ‘all in your head’. It’s a recommended treatment, alongside or instead of hormones.

    Not ‘just think cooler thoughts’. It genuinely helps.

  3. Step 3 · PrescriptionMenopausal hormone therapy (MHT, also called HRT)Oestrogen as a patch, gel, spray or tablet, plus progestogen (if you have a uterus) to protect the womb lining.
    Works
    Cuts hot flushes by about three-quarters more than a dummy treatment; also helps sleep, joint aches and mood, and protects bones
    Cost
    Many options under the $25 PBS co-payment; some cost more
    How long
    As long as benefits outweigh risks for you; review yearly
    Watch for
    Combined MHT adds about 1 extra breast cancer per 1000 women a year in their 50s. Tablets roughly double clot risk (to about 1 extra per 1000); patches and gels carry little or none. Not for people with breast or womb cancer.

    For most healthy women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause, the benefits outweigh the risks.

  4. Step 4 · PrescriptionVaginal oestrogenA low-dose cream, pessary or ring for dryness, painful sex and urinary symptoms.
    Works
    Very effective for vaginal and bladder symptoms, with little hormone absorbed into the body
    Cost
    Under the $25 PBS co-payment for many products
    How long
    Long term, as long as symptoms need it
    Watch for
    Safe for long-term use for most; ask your specialist if you’ve had breast cancer. Moisturisers and lubricants help too.

    The most under-used good treatment in menopause.

  5. Step 5 · PrescriptionNon-hormonal medicinesFezolinetant, certain antidepressants (low-dose venlafaxine, escitalopram), or gabapentin, for people who can’t or don’t want to use hormones.
    Works
    Fezolinetant: about 2–3 fewer moderate to severe hot flushes a day than a dummy pill in trials. Others help less than MHT
    Cost
    Fezolinetant: not PBS-subsidised as of late 2025, so a private script that can be pricey. Others: under the $25 PBS co-payment
    How long
    As long as needed, with review
    Watch for
    Fezolinetant: liver blood tests before starting and monthly for 3 months. Antidepressants: nausea, sleep changes. Gabapentin: drowsiness.

    Good back-up options when hormones aren’t the right fit.

  6. Step 6 · SpecialistMenopause specialist or gynaecologistFor early menopause, symptoms after cancer, complicated health conditions or treatment that isn’t working.
    Works
    No trials of referral itself; helps tailor tricky cases
    Cost
    Specialist fees vary; Medicare rebates with a GP referral
    How long
    As needed
    Watch for
    Menopause before 40 or 45 needs treatment to protect bones and heart, usually until at least 51.

    Early menopause is a medical issue, not just a symptom issue.

Costs are rough Australian prices in November 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials, not promises.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Compounded ‘bioidentical’ hormonesCustom-made creams and lozenges aren’t tested for strength or safety, and have been linked to womb cancer. The Australasian Menopause Society doesn’t recommend them. Approved body-identical hormones exist.
  • Saliva hormone testsNot standardised or reliable. Hormone levels swing wildly in perimenopause, so diagnosis is based on your age and symptoms.
  • Black cohosh, red clover and evening primrose oilEvidence is inconsistent or negative, and black cohosh has possible liver safety concerns.
  • Wild yam or progesterone creamsNo evidence they work for hot flushes, and they don’t protect the womb lining with oestrogen.
  • Acupuncture for hot flushesNo better than fake acupuncture in trials, though some find it helps sleep.

What you can do

  • Fill in a symptom checklist (see resources) and take it to your GP.
  • Keep using contraception until 12 months after your last period, or as your GP advises.
  • Do strength and weight-bearing exercise most days for bones, heart and mood.
  • Check your blood pressure, cholesterol and cervical and breast screening are up to date.
  • Try vaginal moisturisers for dryness, and ask about vaginal oestrogen if they’re not enough.

See your GP if

  • Symptoms are affecting work, sleep, relationships or mood
  • Your periods become very heavy, much more frequent, or you bleed between periods or after sex
  • Your periods stop before 45, or you have menopause symptoms before 40
  • You get unexpected bleeding while on MHT

See your GP promptly for any bleeding after 12 months without a period: it’s usually harmless but must be checked. Get urgent help for chest pain, a painful swollen leg, sudden breathlessness, or stroke signs (face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech): call 000 (Australia) or your local emergency number. From a mobile, 112 works in most countries.

Common questions

What are the symptoms?

Hot flushes, night sweats, poor sleep, irregular periods, low mood, anxiety, irritability, brain fog, joint aches, vaginal dryness, lower libido and urinary symptoms. Symptoms often start before periods stop and can last 5–10 years.

When does it happen?

Most women in Australia reach menopause between 45 and 55, at an average age of 51. Perimenopause often lasts 4–8 years. Menopause before 45 is early; before 40 is called premature ovarian insufficiency. Surgery to remove the ovaries and some cancer treatments cause it suddenly.

Do I need a blood test?

Usually not if you’re over 45 with typical symptoms; hormone levels go up and down too much to be useful. Blood tests help if you’re under 45, have had a hysterectomy, or other causes need ruling out.

Does a hysterectomy cause menopause?

Not on its own. If the ovaries stay, they keep working. Removing both ovaries causes menopause straight away.

How long does menopause last?

Hot flushes and night sweats last about 7 years on average, and for some women more than 10. Symptoms often start in perimenopause, a few years before the last period. Vaginal dryness can continue long term without treatment.

Is menopausal hormone therapy safe?

For most healthy women under 60, or within 10 years of menopause, the benefits of MHT outweigh the risks. Combined MHT slightly raises breast cancer risk over time, and tablets carry a small clot risk, which patches and gels avoid. Your GP can help weigh your personal risks.

Can menopause cause anxiety?

Yes. Anxiety, low mood, irritability and poor sleep are common in perimenopause, sometimes before periods change much. MHT can help with mood linked to hot flushes and sleep, and talking therapy or antidepressants help too.
