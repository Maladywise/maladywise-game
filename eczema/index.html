Fact sheet

Eczema

Dry, itchy, inflamed skin that comes and goes in flares. Common in children and often improves with age. Not contagious.

Treatment options

Ratings show how strong the research is.

  • Strong evidenceMoisturiser every dayThick, fragrance-free cream or ointment at least twice daily, even when skin looks clear. Reduces flares and the need for steroid creams.
  • Strong evidenceSteroid cream for flaresThe main treatment. Safe and effective when used as directed. Apply to all red, itchy skin until settled, usually 1 to 2 weeks.
  • Strong evidenceTwice-weekly "maintenance" steroidFor eczema that keeps returning in the same spots, using steroid cream 2 days a week on those areas prevents flares.
  • Strong evidenceNon-steroid prescription creamsTacrolimus or pimecrolimus. Useful for the face and eyelids or long-term use.
  • Strong evidenceSpecialist treatmentFor severe eczema, a dermatologist can offer light therapy or newer tablets and injections.
  • Moderate evidenceWet wrapsDamp bandages over cream for severe flares, mainly in children. Ask your GP how.
  • Limited evidenceAntihistaminesDon’t treat eczema itself. A drowsy one may help sleep during a bad flare.
  • Not recommendedCutting out foodsRarely helps and can harm growth in children. Only with medical advice if a food allergy is confirmed.

What you can do

  • Soap-free wash and short, lukewarm showers. Pat dry, then moisturise.
  • Cotton clothing and short nails. Stay cool, as overheating and sweat worsen itch.
  • Treat flares early rather than waiting.

See your GP if

  • Skin is weeping, crusted or not improving after a week of treatment
  • Itch is stopping sleep

See a doctor the same day for sudden painful clustered blisters or sores, especially near the eyes, or a child with fever who seems unwell.

Common questions

Are steroid creams safe for eczema?

Yes, when used as directed. They are the main treatment for flares: apply to all red, itchy skin until settled, usually 1 to 2 weeks.

What is the best moisturiser for eczema?

A thick, fragrance-free cream or ointment, used at least twice a day even when skin looks clear. This reduces flares and the need for steroid creams.

Will cutting out foods help eczema?

Rarely, and it can harm growth in children. Only change the diet on medical advice if a food allergy is confirmed.

Is eczema contagious?

No. It is common in children and often improves with age.
