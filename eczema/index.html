Eczema

What it is

Eczema (atopic dermatitis) is a long-term condition that makes skin dry, itchy and inflamed. It often starts in babies and young children, and many grow out of it. It tends to come and go in flares. It isn't contagious.

Common signs

  • Dry, rough or scaly skin
  • Itch, often worse at night
  • Red patches: on the face and outer limbs in babies, and in the elbow and knee creases in older children and adults
  • Thickened skin from long-term scratching

Common triggers

  • Soap, bubble bath and long hot showers
  • Heat, sweat and dry air
  • Wool and rough fabrics
  • Dust mites, pollen and pet hair
  • Skin infections and stress

Treatment

  • Moisturise every day, even when the skin looks good. Use a thick, fragrance-free cream or ointment at least twice a day, and straight after bathing.
  • Wash gently. Use a soap-free wash and lukewarm water, and keep baths and showers short. Pat dry.
  • Treat flares early. Steroid creams are safe and effective when used as directed. Apply to all the red, itchy skin until it settles, then go back to moisturiser alone.
  • Wet dressings can calm severe itch, especially in children.
  • For eczema that keeps coming back, your GP may suggest other creams or a referral to a dermatologist.

What you can do

  • Wear soft cotton and keep fingernails short.
  • Keep bedrooms cool.
  • Write down what seems to set off your flares.
  • Food allergy is rarely the main cause. Don't cut out foods without talking to your GP.

See your GP soon if

  • The skin is weeping, crusting or has yellow crusts
  • Eczema isn't improving after a week of treatment
  • Itch is stopping you or your child from sleeping

See a doctor the same day if eczema suddenly gets worse with painful clustered blisters or sores, especially near the eyes, or if your child has a fever and seems unwell.
