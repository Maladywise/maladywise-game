Dosage Calculation Quiz

Twenty drug calculation questions, from counting tablets to microgram-per-kilo infusions. Have a pen ready. The key formula: what you want ÷ what you’ve got × the volume it comes in. Tap an answer to see if you’re right.

  1. Question 1 of 20

    Ordered: paracetamol 1 g. Stock: 500 mg tablets. How many tablets?

    1. 0.5
    2. 1
    3. 4
    4. 2
    Show answer

    Answer: 2. 1 g = 1,000 mg. 1,000 ÷ 500 = 2 tablets.

  2. Question 2 of 20

    How many milligrams is 0.5 g?

    1. 5,000 mg
    2. 500 mg
    3. 5 mg
    4. 50 mg
    Show answer

    Answer: 500 mg. Multiply grams by 1,000 to get milligrams.

  3. Question 3 of 20

    How many micrograms are in 1 mg?

    1. 100
    2. 10
    3. 1,000
    4. 1,000,000
    Show answer

    Answer: 1,000. g → mg → microgram: each step is × 1,000.

    Answer: 42. 1,000 × 20 = 20,000 drops ÷ 480 minutes ≈ 41.7, so 42 drops a minute.

  19. Question 19 of 20

    If the maximum dose of lidocaine is 3 mg/kg, how many mL of 1% lidocaine is that for a 70 kg adult?

    1. 21 mL
    2. 7 mL
    3. 2.1 mL
    4. 210 mL
    Show answer

    Answer: 21 mL. 1% = 10 mg/mL. 3 × 70 = 210 mg. 210 ÷ 10 = 21 mL.

  20. Question 20 of 20

    Ordered: 5 microgram/kg/min for a 70 kg patient. Stock: 200 mg in 50 mL. What pump rate?

    1. 21 mL/h
    2. 0.875 mL/h
    3. 5.25 mL/h
    4. 52.5 mL/h
    Show answer

    Answer: 5.25 mL/h. 5 × 70 = 350 microgram/min × 60 = 21,000 microgram/h. 200 mg/50 mL = 4,000 microgram/mL. 21,000 ÷ 4,000 = 5.25 mL/h.

Common questions

What is the formula for drug calculations?

Volume = (dose wanted ÷ dose in stock) × stock volume. Put everything in the same units first (g, mg or micrograms).

How do you calculate an IV rate?

Pump rate (mL/h) = volume ÷ time in hours. For a gravity drip: drops per minute = volume (mL) × drop factor (drops/mL) ÷ time in minutes.

How do I avoid calculation errors?

Convert units first, estimate the answer before calculating, write a leading zero (0.5, not .5), never a trailing zero (5, not 5.0), and get an independent double-check for high-risk drugs.
