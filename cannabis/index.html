Cannabis: effects and quitting

Cannabis (marijuana, weed) is the most widely used illicit drug in Australia. Most people who use it don’t become dependent, but regular use, especially of high-THC products or starting young, raises the risk of dependence, psychosis and other harms.

Effects and risks

  • Short term: relaxation, altered perception, slower reactions, poor memory and coordination, anxiety or paranoia in some.
  • Dependence: regular users can become dependent, needing it to get through the day.
  • Mental health: THC raises the risk of psychosis and schizophrenia, more with frequent, high-potency use and starting in the teens.
  • Cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome: repeated bouts of vomiting and tummy pain in heavy long-term users, often eased by hot showers; the only cure is stopping.
  • Smoking it harms the lungs; use in pregnancy is linked to lower birth weight.

Withdrawal

  • Starts within 1 to 2 days of stopping and is mostly gone within about 3 weeks.
  • Irritability, anxiety, low mood, trouble sleeping, vivid dreams, reduced appetite, restlessness and cravings.
  • Usually mild and not dangerous, but enough to make quitting hard.

How to cut down or quit

  • Pick a quit date or a cutting-down plan (fewer days, smaller amounts, lower THC).
  • Remove supplies and equipment; change routines linked to using.
  • Plan for sleep problems: regular wake time, exercise, less caffeine.
  • Counselling (CBT, motivational interviewing) is the most effective treatment; there’s no approved medicine for cannabis dependence.
  • Your GP can help with sleep and mood during withdrawal.

Driving and the law

  • Don’t drive after using cannabis. Roadside drug tests detect THC, and in most states it’s an offence to drive with any THC present, even if prescribed.
  • Medicinal cannabis is legal only on prescription through TGA pathways.

Where to get help

  • National Alcohol and Other Drug Hotline: 1800 250 015 (free, 24/7)
  • Counselling Online: free 24/7 web chat (counsellingonline.org.au)
  • Your GP or a local alcohol and drug service

Common questions

Is cannabis addictive?

It can be. Regular use can lead to dependence, with cravings and withdrawal when stopping. The risk is higher with daily use, high-THC products and starting young.

How long does cannabis withdrawal last?

Symptoms start within 1 to 2 days, peak in the first week and are mostly gone within about 3 weeks.

What is cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome?

Recurring severe vomiting and tummy pain in long-term heavy users. Hot showers ease it temporarily; stopping cannabis cures it.

Can I drive on medicinal cannabis?

In most Australian states it’s an offence to drive with THC in your system, even with a prescription. See medicinal cannabis and driving and the medicinal cannabis guide. Check your state’s rules.
