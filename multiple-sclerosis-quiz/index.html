Multiple Sclerosis Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on MS: how it starts, how it’s diagnosed, and the treatments that now change its course.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What does MS damage?

    1. The skin
    2. The muscles directly
    3. The bones
    4. Myelin, the coating around nerves in the brain and spinal cord
    Show answer

    Answer: Myelin, the coating around nerves in the brain and spinal cord. The immune system attacks myelin, slowing or blocking nerve signals.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    Who most often gets MS?

    1. Men over 80
    2. Only athletes
    3. Women aged 20 to 40
    4. Children under 5
    Show answer

    Answer: Women aged 20 to 40. About three women to every man. Australia has more MS the further south you live.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Which is a common first symptom?

    1. Knee pain
    2. Painful blurred vision in one eye
    3. Chest pain
    4. Hearing loss in both ears
    Show answer

    Answer: Painful blurred vision in one eye. Optic neuritis: pain on moving the eye and washed-out colours, often recovering over weeks.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    Which test is most useful for diagnosis?

    1. MRI of the brain and spine
    2. Blood count
    3. Ultrasound
    4. Chest X-ray
    Show answer

    Answer: MRI of the brain and spine. MRI shows lesions of different ages in different places. Lumbar puncture can add support.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    What is a relapse?

    1. Feeling tired after exercise
    2. A cold
    3. New or worse symptoms lasting at least 24 hours
    4. A brief one-second tingle
    Show answer

    Answer: New or worse symptoms lasting at least 24 hours. Without fever or infection. Many relapses recover fully, especially early on.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    What is Lhermitte’s sign?

    1. A rash on the neck
    2. An electric shock down the spine when bending the neck
    3. A twitching eyelid
    4. Loss of smell
    Show answer

    Answer: An electric shock down the spine when bending the neck. It suggests a lesion in the neck part of the spinal cord.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    What is Uhthoff’s phenomenon?

    1. Symptoms temporarily worsen with heat
    2. Permanent damage from saunas
    3. Symptoms improve in hot baths
    4. Fever caused by MS
    Show answer

    Answer: Symptoms temporarily worsen with heat. A hot shower or exercise can bring old symptoms back briefly. They settle on cooling.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    Which type of MS is most common at diagnosis?

    1. Primary progressive MS
    2. Benign forever MS
    3. Childhood-only MS
    4. Relapsing-remitting MS
    Show answer

    Answer: Relapsing-remitting MS. About 85% start with relapses and recovery.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    What do disease-modifying therapies do?

    1. Reduce relapses and slow disability
    2. Cure MS
    3. Replace myelin overnight
    4. Only treat pain
    Show answer

    Answer: Reduce relapses and slow disability. Options include injections, tablets and infusions such as ocrelizumab and natalizumab. Starting early matters.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    Which factor is linked to a higher MS risk?

    1. Drinking water
    2. Eating chocolate
    3. Low vitamin D and past Epstein–Barr virus infection
    4. Being tall
    Show answer

    Answer: Low vitamin D and past Epstein–Barr virus infection. Smoking also increases risk and speeds progression. Stopping smoking helps.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    Which condition is treated with high-dose steroids in MS?

    1. Every day for life
    2. Fatigue
    3. Constipation
    4. A disabling relapse
    Show answer

    Answer: A disabling relapse. Steroids speed recovery from a relapse but don’t change long-term outcome.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    What does the term ‘clinically isolated syndrome’ mean?

    1. A blood test result
    2. A first MS-like episode that may or may not become MS
    3. Being in hospital alone
    4. MS confirmed for life
    Show answer

    Answer: A first MS-like episode that may or may not become MS. MRI findings help predict who will go on to MS, and some people start treatment early.

Common questions

Is MS fatal?

Most people with MS have a near-normal life expectancy, especially with modern treatment.

Can people with MS have children?

Yes. Plan with the neurologist, as some medicines must be stopped first.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. See your GP or neurologist about your own symptoms.
