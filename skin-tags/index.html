Fact sheet

Skin Tags

Small, soft, skin-coloured or slightly darker flaps of skin that hang off the neck, armpits, eyelids, groin or under the breasts. Very common and harmless. Removal is optional.

Who gets them. Anyone, but they’re more common with age, extra weight and skin folds, type 2 diabetes and pregnancy, and where skin rubs. Lots of new tags can be a nudge to check blood sugar. They don’t turn into cancer.

Diagnosis. Usually obvious by look. A doctor will check anything that’s firm, rough, bleeding or growing, because warts, moles and occasionally skin cancers can look similar. Lumps around the genitals may be genital warts and need a proper check.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Tag-removal bands, freezing pens and creamsNo good evidence. Risk burns, infection and scarring, and could treat something that wasn’t a tag.
  • Tying off with thread or dental flossPainful and risks infection and bleeding. Ask your GP instead.
  • Apple cider vinegar, tea tree oil or toothpasteNo trials. Irritates the skin around the tag.
  • Cutting them off with nail scissorsEven small tags can bleed a surprising amount. Infection and scarring too.

What you can do

  • Wear soft, loose fabrics where tags rub.
  • If you have lots of new tags, ask your GP whether a diabetes check makes sense.
  • Photograph any tag that seems to be changing.

See your GP if

  • A tag is growing, changing colour, bleeding or painful
  • It’s firm, rough or not soft and floppy (might be something else)
  • It catches often and you’d like it removed
  • You have new lumps around the genitals or anus

Don’t remove one yourself if you’re not sure it’s a skin tag. A doctor can check it, and some need testing.

Common questions

Are skin tags cancer?

No. They’re harmless. A growing, bleeding or unusual one should be checked in case it’s something else.

Will they come back after removal?

The removed tag usually doesn’t, but new ones can appear, especially where skin rubs.

Are skin tags linked to diabetes?

They’re more common in people with type 2 diabetes and extra weight. Lots of new tags can be a reason to check your blood sugar.

Can I get them removed for free?

Usually not. Removing them for looks is cosmetic. Medicare may help if there’s a medical reason, such as bleeding or an uncertain diagnosis.

What causes skin tags?

Friction where skin rubs against skin or clothing, such as the neck, armpits and groin. They become more common with age, extra weight, pregnancy and insulin resistance, and tend to run in families.

Can I remove a skin tag at home?

It’s safer not to. Tying off or cutting tags at home can cause bleeding and infection, and home freezing kits can damage nearby skin. Never treat tags near the eyes or on the genitals yourself. A GP can remove them quickly.

Are skin tags contagious?

No. Skin tags can’t spread from person to person. If small growths on the genitals are spreading, see a GP, as they may be genital warts instead.
