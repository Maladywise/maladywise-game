Type 2 diabetes

For health professionals General practice World Diabetes Day, 14 November

Type 2 diabetes is the condition every GP manages and nobody finds glamorous. Yet the last decade rewrote it: drugs that cut deaths and kidney failure, not just glucose; trials showing early disease can go into remission; and a pipeline so crowded the 2015 algorithm looks like a museum piece. World Diabetes Day falls on 14 November, Banting's birthday. Here's the evidence, with the numbers.

At a glance

  • Burden: 589 million adults (20–79) have diabetes, over 90% type 2; 252 million (over 4 in 10) are undiagnosed (IDF Atlas 2025). Australia: 5.3%, 1.3 million (ABS 2022).
  • Mechanism: insulin resistance plus progressive beta-cell failure; ectopic liver and pancreatic fat is the reversible part (Counterpoint, DiRECT).
  • Diagnosis: HbA1c ≥48 mmol/mol (6.5%), fasting glucose ≥7.0 or 2-hour OGTT ≥11.1 mmol/L, confirmed if asymptomatic. Know when HbA1c lies.
  • Organ protection: SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 RAs cut cardiovascular and kidney events (EMPA-REG CV death −38%; CREDENCE renal HR 0.70; FLOW HR 0.76).
  • Remission is real but fragile: 46% at 12 months and 36% at 24 months with intensive weight management; 13% at 5 years (DiRECT).
  • Age at diagnosis matters: diagnosed at 30, about 14 years of life lost; at 50, about 6 (US rates, ERFC 2023).

Biggest take-home: think organs, not just HbA1c. Pick drugs for the heart and kidneys, and treat weight as a disease-modifying target.

For patients: Type 2 diabetes, Prediabetes and the GLP-1 medicines guide.

Definition

  • What it is: hyperglycaemia from a progressive loss of adequate beta-cell insulin secretion, usually on a background of insulin resistance, without autoimmune beta-cell destruction.
  • Diagnostic thresholds (WHO/ADA): HbA1c ≥6.5% (48 mmol/mol), fasting plasma glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L, 2-hour OGTT glucose ≥11.1 mmol/L, or random ≥11.1 mmol/L with symptoms. Without symptoms, confirm with a second test.
  • Remission (ADA-led consensus 2021): HbA1c <6.5% (48 mmol/mol) at least 3 months after stopping glucose-lowering drugs. "Remission", not "cure", because it can return.

History

  • Ancient India, 1st millennium BCE, Sushruta: "madhumeha", honey-like urine that was sweet, sticky and drew ants.
  • 2nd century CE, Aretaeus of Cappadocia: named it diabetes, from the Greek for "siphon" or "pass through".
  • 1674, Thomas Willis: urine "wonderfully sweet like sugar or honey". In the 1770s, Matthew Dobson showed the sweetness was sugar, in urine and blood.
  • 1870, Apollinaire Bouchardat: noticed glycosuria fell in his patients during the siege of Paris and prescribed eating less: an early dietary therapy.
  • 1936, Harold Himsworth: split diabetes into "insulin-sensitive" and "insulin-insensitive" types in the Lancet: the seed of type 1 vs type 2.
  • 1957, Jean Sterne: first reported metformin for diabetes (in a Moroccan journal), derived from goat's rue (Galega officinalis).
  • Late 1950s, Rosalyn Yalow and Solomon Berson: the insulin radioimmunoassay, showing type 2 is inefficient use of insulin, not lack of it. Yalow won the 1977 Nobel; Berson had died in 1972. In 1969 Dorothy Hodgkin solved insulin's 3D structure.
  • 1986, Pan Xiao-Ren and Li Guangwei: the Da Qing study in China, among the first lifestyle-prevention trials. In 2024 Svetlana Mojsov, Joel Habener and Lotte Bjerre Knudsen won the Lasker Award for GLP-1 work.

Pathophysiology

Two defects, one clock: insulin resistance and a beta cell that can't keep up. The fat in the wrong places is the most reversible part.

  • Many organs: DeFronzo's "ominous octet" (2009): muscle, liver and beta cell, plus fat, gut incretins, alpha cell, kidney and brain.
  • Beta-cell decline: function estimated at about 50% of normal at diagnosis (HOMA model), falling progressively on all therapies (UKPDS 16).
  • A late, steep turn: in Whitehall II (6,538 people), fasting glucose rose steeply only in the 3 years before diagnosis, after insulin sensitivity had fallen for 5 (Tabák 2009).
  • Twin cycles of ectopic fat: on 600 kcal/day, liver fat fell 30% in 1 week and fasting glucose normalised (9.2 to 5.9 mmol/L); pancreatic fat fell and first-phase insulin response approached normal by 8 weeks (Counterpoint, n=11; Lim 2011).
  • Genetics: 1,289 independent signals at 611 loci from 2.5 million people (39.7% non-European), falling into 8 mechanistic clusters (Suzuki 2024).
  • Beta cells may go quiet, not die: in mice, beta-cell "loss" was dedifferentiation (Talchai 2012). Human relevance is unproven.

Epidemiology

Global

  • IDF Atlas 11th edition (2025): 589 million adults aged 20–79, rising to 853 million by 2050; over 3.4 million deaths a year; spending over USD 1 trillion.
  • NCD-RisC (2024): 828 million adults (18+) in 2022, up 630 million since 1990; 445 million (59%) of those aged 30+ untreated. WHO: adult prevalence doubled from 7% to 14% (1990–2022).
  • Youth-onset: in the TODAY cohort, 60.1% had at least one complication after 13 years, at a mean age of 26 (diabetic kidney disease 54.8%, retinal disease 51.0%).

Australia

  • Prevalence: 5.3%, 1.3 million people, in 2022; 87.6% type 2 (ABS National Health Survey).
  • First Nations adults: 2.9 times as likely to have diabetes as non-Indigenous adults, age-adjusted (AIHW, 2018–19 data).
  • Deaths and burden: underlying cause of about 6,000 deaths and associated with 11% of all deaths in 2022; type 2 was 2.2% of total disease burden in 2023 (AIHW).
  • Kidneys: diabetic kidney disease caused 36% of new kidney replacement therapy starts in 2023, the leading cause (ANZDATA).

Causes and risk factors

  • Adiposity and inactivity: the main modifiable drivers; weight loss reverses early disease (DiRECT).
  • Genes and ancestry: 611 loci; risk is higher in many non-European populations and in First Nations Australians.
  • Gestational diabetes: nearly 10-fold risk of later type 2 (RR 9.51, 95% CI 7.14–12.67; 1.3 million women; Vounzoulaki 2020).
  • Air pollution: HR 1.15 per 10 μg/m³ PM2.5; about 3.2 million incident cases in 2016 (Bowe 2018). GBD 2019: about one-fifth of type 2 burden.
  • Shift work: OR 1.09 (1.05–1.12), highest with rotating shifts (Gan 2015, 12 studies).
  • Prenatal famine: late-gestation exposure in the Dutch Hunger Winter: 2-hour glucose +0.5 mmol/L in adulthood (Ravelli 1998).

Preventable factors

  • Lifestyle in impaired glucose tolerance: −58% incidence in both the Finnish DPS (n=522) and US DPP (n=3,234; NNT 6.9 over 3 years). Metformin −31% in DPP.
  • Durable: DPPOS at 15 years, lifestyle −27%, metformin −18%.
  • Hard outcomes: Da Qing, 30 years: diabetes delayed a median 3.96 years, CVD events −26%, CV death −33%, all-cause death −26%, life expectancy +1.44 years.
  • Drugs (trial data): tirzepatide in obesity with prediabetes: type 2 in 1.3% vs 13.3% over 176 weeks (HR 0.07); HR 0.12 after 17 weeks off drug (SURMOUNT-1).
  • After gestational diabetes: the RR of 9.51 makes this one of the best-defined groups for recall and testing.

Diagnosis essentials

  • Australian screening (RACGP Red Book): AUSDRISK every 3 years from 40; score ≥12 means high risk: test with fasting glucose or HbA1c, then every 3 years (yearly with IFG or IGT). Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people: yearly blood test from 18.
  • Medicare: HbA1c for diagnosis in high-risk asymptomatic people (item 66841), once per 12 months.
  • When HbA1c misleads (ADS 2015): haemoglobinopathies, raised HbF, haemolysis, splenomegaly, increased red cell turnover (iron, B12 or folate therapy, EPO, chronic liver disease), dapsone, ribavirin, antiretrovirals, high-dose vitamins C and E. Use glucose-based tests instead.
  • Is it really type 2? LADA is 2–12% of adult-onset diabetes. In UKPDS 25, GAD antibodies were found in 34% aged 25–34 vs 7% aged 55–65, and 84% of antibody-positive under-35s needed insulin within 6 years. Test antibodies if young, lean, or failing fast.
  • At diagnosis: baseline kidney (eGFR, urine ACR), eyes, feet, lipids, BP and cardiovascular risk.

Current treatment

Targets

  • RACGP: HbA1c generally ≤7% (53 mmol/mol), individualised.
  • Too low can harm: targeting <6.0% raised mortality (HR 1.22, 1.01–1.46; ACCORD, n=10,251).
  • Treat everything: intensive multifactorial care in Steno-2 cut deaths from 50% to 30% at 13 years (HR 0.54) and added a median 7.9 years of life at 21 years.

Medicines: effect sizes

  • Metformin: all-cause mortality −36% in overweight patients (UKPDS 34, n=753); MI −33% and death −27% persisted 10 years after the trial (UKPDS 80). Caveat: early metformin added to a sulfonylurea raised diabetes-related death in UKPDS 34.
  • SGLT2 inhibitors: empagliflozin MACE HR 0.86, CV death −38%, HF hospitalisation −35%, all-cause death −32% (EMPA-REG, n=7,020). Canagliflozin renal composite HR 0.70 (CREDENCE). KDIGO 2022: start if eGFR ≥20.
  • GLP-1 RAs: liraglutide MACE HR 0.87 (LEADER); semaglutide HR 0.74 (SUSTAIN-6; retinopathy complications HR 1.76); semaglutide kidney events HR 0.76, CV death HR 0.71 (FLOW).
  • Tirzepatide: HbA1c −2.30 vs −1.86 points and weight −11.2 vs −5.7 kg vs semaglutide 1 mg (SURPASS-2). Noninferior, not superior, to dulaglutide for MACE (HR 0.92, 0.83–1.01; SURPASS-CVOT).
  • Durability: over 5 years, glargine and liraglutide held targets best, sitagliptin worst (HbA1c ≥7% at 26.5 and 26.1 vs 38.1 per 100 person-years; GRADE).
  • Early combination: metformin plus vildagliptin halved early treatment failure vs stepwise metformin (HR 0.51; VERIFY).

Weight, remission and surgery

  • DiRECT (n=298, primary care, under 6 years since diagnosis): remission 46% vs 4% at 12 months; 86% in those losing ≥15 kg; 36% at 24 months; 13% at 5 years.
  • Metabolic surgery: HbA1c ≤6.0% at 5 years in 29% after gastric bypass and 23% after sleeve vs 5% with medical therapy (STAMPEDE, n=150).

Emerging treatments and trials

  • Orforglipron (oral non-peptide GLP-1 RA): HbA1c −1.24 to −1.48 points at 40 weeks; weight −4.5% to −7.6% (ACHIEVE-1). FDA-approved for obesity in April 2026.
  • Retatrutide (GIP/GLP-1/glucagon): HbA1c −2.02 at 24 weeks in phase 2. Phase 3 topline (not yet peer-reviewed): TRANSCEND-T2D-2, HbA1c −2.2 vs −1.8 and weight −20.9% vs −9.4% vs semaglutide 2 mg at 80 weeks.
  • CagriSema: HbA1c −1.91 vs −1.76 and weight −14.2% vs −10.2% vs semaglutide 2.4 mg at 68 weeks (REIMAGINE 2).
  • Weekly basal insulin: icodec FDA-approved for type 2 in March 2026 and on the ARTG; efsitora FDA-approved in September 2026 (QWINT-2: HbA1c −1.26 vs −1.17 with degludec).
  • Stem-cell islets: one 59-year-old man received autologous stem-cell-derived islet tissue in Shanghai in 2021 and was insulin-free for 33 months (Wu 2024). One case.

Theoretical frontiers

Hypotheses, not recommendations.

Causes: theories

  • Hypothesis: ectopic fat causes type 2 (twin cycle). For: liver fat −30% in 1 week with normal fasting glucose (Lim 2011); remission tracks weight loss (DiRECT). Against: Counterpoint had 11 people; not everyone with type 2 has a high BMI. Test: weight-loss trials in normal-BMI type 2 with MR liver and pancreatic fat.
  • Hypothesis: hyperinsulinaemia comes first. For: insulin excess can drive insulin resistance (Shanik 2008; Corkey 2012). Against: Whitehall II shows a compensatory rise, then fall, in beta-cell function. Test: trials that lower insulin secretion in early dysglycaemia.
  • Hypothesis: beta cells dedifferentiate rather than die. For: FoxO1 mouse model (Talchai 2012); early function returns with weight loss (Lim 2011). Against: mostly mouse data. Test: human pancreas and biomarker studies before and after remission.
  • Hypothesis: the gut microbiome is causal. For: metformin-altered microbiota improved glucose tolerance in germ-free mice (Wu 2017); lean-donor FMT improved insulin sensitivity at 6 weeks (Vrieze 2012). Against: no effect by 18 weeks (Kootte 2017). Test: larger, longer FMT or defined-consortium RCTs.
  • Hypothesis: air pollution is a major cause. For: HR 1.15 per 10 μg/m³ (Bowe 2018); one-fifth of burden in GBD 2019. Against: observational, residual confounding. Test: natural experiments around clean-air policies.
  • Hypothesis: diabetes is programmed in utero. For: thrifty phenotype (Hales and Barker 1992); Dutch famine (Ravelli 1998). Against: small effect (+0.5 mmol/L). Test: maternal nutrition RCTs with offspring follow-up.
  • Hypothesis: endocrine-disrupting chemicals contribute. For: dioxins RR 1.91, PCBs 2.39, BPA 1.45 (Song 2015). Against: mostly cross-sectional (41 of 49 studies). Test: prospective biomonitoring cohorts.
  • Hypothesis: circadian disruption contributes. For: shift work OR 1.09, rotating shifts worst (Gan 2015). Against: small effect, confounding. Test: RCTs of shift-schedule redesign with glycaemic outcomes.

Prevention: theoretical options

  • Hypothesis: incretin drugs can prevent type 2 at scale. For: HR 0.07 on tirzepatide (SURMOUNT-1). Against: cost, and higher risk after stopping (HR 0.12 vs 0.07). Test: long pragmatic trials with hard outcomes and cost-effectiveness.
  • Hypothesis: lifestyle prevention extends life. For: Da Qing all-cause death −26%, +1.44 years. Against: one cluster-randomised trial (n=577). Test: long-term outcome follow-up of DPP and DPS.
  • Hypothesis: post-GDM programs prevent much type 2. For: RR 9.51 (Vounzoulaki 2020). Test: postpartum prevention RCTs with registry linkage.
  • Hypothesis: cleaner air prevents diabetes. For: 3.2 million incident cases attributed to PM2.5 (Bowe 2018). Test: stepped policy evaluations.
  • Hypothesis: time-restricted eating is as good as calorie counting. For: HbA1c −0.91 vs control, no different from calorie restriction (Pavlou 2023, n=75). Test: larger prevention trials in prediabetes.

Treatment: theoretical options

  • Hypothesis: remission should be the default goal early. For: DiRECT 46% at 1 year. Against: 13% at 5 years. Test: trials of maintenance strategies, including incretins, after remission.
  • Hypothesis: early combination preserves beta cells. For: VERIFY HR 0.51; GRADE durability with liraglutide. Against: VERIFY measured treatment failure, not beta-cell mass. Test: trials with clamp-measured beta-cell function.
  • Hypothesis: anti-inflammatory therapy treats type 2. For: salsalate HbA1c −0.37% (TINSAL-T2D, n=286). Against: weight, LDL and urine albumin rose. Test: targeted anti-inflammatory trials with outcomes.
  • Hypothesis: microbiome modulation can treat it. For: Vrieze 2012. Against: effect gone by 18 weeks (Kootte 2017). Test: repeated-dose RCTs.
  • Hypothesis: cell replacement can cure insulin-requiring type 2. For: 33 insulin-free months in one man (Wu 2024). Against: n=1. Test: phase 1/2 trials.
  • Hypothesis: triple agonists make weight-based remission routine. For: weight −20.9% (TRANSCEND-T2D-2 topline). Against: unpublished, no outcome data. Test: remission-designed trials.

Gaps and open questions

  • The undiagnosed 252 million: over 4 in 10 adults with diabetes don't know they have it (IDF).
  • Untreated majority: 59% of people aged 30+ with diabetes get no treatment (NCD-RisC).
  • Remission maintenance: most remissions lapse by 5 years.
  • Hard outcomes for new agents: tirzepatide wasn't superior to dulaglutide; retatrutide and CagriSema have no CV outcome results yet.
  • Youth-onset disease: 60% have a complication by their mid-20s (TODAY). How best to treat them is unclear.

Prognosis and quality of life

  • Life expectancy: diagnosis at 30, 14 years shorter; at 40, 10; at 50, 6 (US rates; 13, 9, 5 with EU rates). About 3–4 years lost per decade of earlier diagnosis (ERFC 2023, 1.5 million people).
  • Multifactorial care pays: +7.9 years median life (Steno-2).
  • Kidneys: the leading cause of new kidney replacement therapy in Australia (36%).
  • Legacy effect: early good control still showed MI and mortality benefit 10 years after trial differences vanished (UKPDS 80).

Weird trivia

  • World Diabetes Day is Banting's birthday, 14 November; IDF and WHO created it in 1991 and the UN made it official in 2006.
  • Banting, Best and Collip sold their insulin patents to the University of Toronto for $1 each.
  • Leonard Thompson, 14, got the first insulin injection on 11 January 1922.
  • Glucophage means "glucose eater". Its plant ancestor, goat's rue, is now a noxious weed in many US states.
  • Exenatide comes from Gila monster venom: John Eng found exendin-4 in 1992 at the Bronx VA, where Rosalyn Yalow worked.
  • Bouchardat's prescription was "mangez le moins possible": eat as little as possible.
  • Hodgkin took 34 years from her first insulin X-ray photo (1935) to the structure (1969).
