COPD Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on chronic obstructive pulmonary disease: causes, the breathing test, inhalers, flare-ups and what actually helps.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What does COPD stand for?

    1. Chronic oxygen pressure disorder
    2. Congestive organ pulmonary disease
    3. Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease
    4. Chest obstruction pain disease
    Show answer

    Answer: Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. An umbrella term including emphysema and chronic bronchitis.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    What is the leading cause of COPD in Australia?

    1. Eating dairy
    2. Asthma in childhood
    3. Too much exercise
    4. Smoking
    Show answer

    Answer: Smoking. Other causes include workplace dusts and fumes, indoor wood smoke, and rarely alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Which test confirms COPD?

    1. A blood oxygen check alone
    2. Spirometry showing airflow that doesn’t fully reverse
    3. A chest X-ray alone
    4. A peak flow meter alone
    Show answer

    Answer: Spirometry showing airflow that doesn’t fully reverse. A post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC below 0.7 confirms persistent airflow limitation.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    What is the single most effective treatment to slow COPD?

    1. Stopping smoking
    2. Cough medicine
    3. Daily antibiotics
    4. Steroid tablets
    Show answer

    Answer: Stopping smoking. It’s never too late: quitting slows the decline in lung function.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    What is pulmonary rehabilitation?

    1. A lung transplant
    2. A type of inhaler
    3. A breathing machine at night
    4. A supervised exercise and education program
    Show answer

    Answer: A supervised exercise and education program. It improves breathlessness, exercise ability and quality of life, and cuts hospital admissions.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    Which inhaler type is usually started first for symptoms?

    1. Oral steroids
    2. Nebulised antibiotics
    3. A long-acting bronchodilator (LAMA or LABA)
    4. Inhaled steroid alone
    Show answer

    Answer: A long-acting bronchodilator (LAMA or LABA). Long-acting bronchodilators relax the airway muscle all day. Inhaled steroids are added for people with frequent flare-ups.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    What is a COPD flare-up (exacerbation)?

    1. A sustained worsening of breathlessness, cough or sputum
    2. Feeling tired after a walk
    3. A normal winter cold
    4. A brief cough after laughing
    Show answer

    Answer: A sustained worsening of breathlessness, cough or sputum. Often triggered by infections. Each flare-up can leave lung function a bit worse.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    Which vaccines are recommended for people with COPD?

    1. Only tetanus
    2. Flu, COVID-19, pneumococcal and RSV (in eligible people)
    3. None
    4. Only travel vaccines
    Show answer

    Answer: Flu, COVID-19, pneumococcal and RSV (in eligible people). Vaccines reduce flare-ups and hospital admissions.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    Why are inhaler technique checks important?

    1. Technique doesn’t matter
    2. Most people make at least one error that reduces the dose reaching the lungs
    3. Checks are only for children
    4. Inhalers expire weekly
    Show answer

    Answer: Most people make at least one error that reduces the dose reaching the lungs. Ask your pharmacist or GP to watch you use it.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    When does long-term home oxygen help?

    1. For anyone who feels breathless
    2. For everyone with COPD
    3. Only during exercise in all cases
    4. When blood oxygen is low at rest
    Show answer

    Answer: When blood oxygen is low at rest. Oxygen improves survival in people with severe resting hypoxaemia. It doesn’t help breathlessness when oxygen levels are normal.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    What is a COPD action plan?

    1. A gym timetable
    2. A diet plan
    3. A written plan for recognising and treating a flare-up early
    4. A hospital discharge letter
    Show answer

    Answer: A written plan for recognising and treating a flare-up early. It may include when to use extra puffers, start a standby course of medicines and call for help.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Which rare genetic cause should be tested in younger people with emphysema?

    1. Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency
    2. Cystic fibrosis in all adults
    3. Huntington’s disease
    4. Haemophilia
    Show answer

    Answer: Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency. Especially in people under 45, non-smokers or with a family history.

Common questions

Is COPD the same as asthma?

No. Both narrow the airways, but COPD is usually smoking-related, mostly irreversible and progressive. Some people have features of both.

Can COPD be reversed?

Lung damage can’t be undone, but quitting, rehab, the right inhalers and vaccines can make a big difference to breathing and life.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. See your GP about your own symptoms or results.
