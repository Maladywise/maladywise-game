Cushing’s Syndrome Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on Cushing’s syndrome: too much cortisol, the clues to look for and how it’s tested and treated.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What causes Cushing’s syndrome?

    1. Too little sodium
    2. Too little insulin
    3. Too much cortisol
    4. Too much thyroid hormone
    Show answer

    Answer: Too much cortisol. Cortisol is the body’s main stress hormone.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    What is the most common cause overall?

    1. A lung tumour
    2. Stress at work
    3. Too much coffee
    4. Taking steroid medicines such as prednisolone
    Show answer

    Answer: Taking steroid medicines such as prednisolone. Long-term high-dose steroids cause the same features.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Which feature fits Cushing’s syndrome?

    1. A round ‘moon’ face and weight gain around the middle
    2. Tanned skin
    3. Very thin face and weight loss
    4. Low blood pressure
    Show answer

    Answer: A round ‘moon’ face and weight gain around the middle. Also a fatty hump at the back of the neck, thin arms and legs and easy bruising.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    Which skin sign is more specific?

    1. Freckles
    2. Wide purple stretch marks
    3. Pale silver stretch marks only
    4. Acne alone
    Show answer

    Answer: Wide purple stretch marks. Purple striae over 1 cm wide on the abdomen are a useful clue.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    Which muscle problem is typical?

    1. Stiff neck
    2. Weakness standing up from a chair
    3. Muscle cramps in the jaw
    4. Twitching eyelids
    Show answer

    Answer: Weakness standing up from a chair. Proximal myopathy: the big hip and shoulder muscles weaken.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    Which screening test can be done at home?

    1. Peak flow
    2. Urine pregnancy test
    3. Blood sugar strip
    4. Late-night saliva cortisol
    Show answer

    Answer: Late-night saliva cortisol. Cortisol should be lowest at midnight. Other first tests: 24-hour urine cortisol or an overnight dexamethasone test.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    What should happen to cortisol after an overnight 1 mg dexamethasone test?

    1. It should be suppressed to a low level
    2. It should stay the same
    3. It should rise steeply
    4. It should double
    Show answer

    Answer: It should be suppressed to a low level. Failure to suppress suggests Cushing’s syndrome.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    What is Cushing’s disease specifically?

    1. An adrenal infection
    2. A skin condition
    3. Cushing’s caused by a pituitary tumour
    4. Any steroid use
    Show answer

    Answer: Cushing’s caused by a pituitary tumour. The tumour makes ACTH, which drives the adrenals. It’s the most common non-medicine cause.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    Which conditions often come with it?

    1. Gout always
    2. Low cholesterol
    3. High blood pressure, diabetes and osteoporosis
    4. High platelets only
    Show answer

    Answer: High blood pressure, diabetes and osteoporosis. These often improve when cortisol is controlled.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    Why must long-term steroids never be stopped suddenly?

    1. They become addictive
    2. The adrenals may not make enough cortisol, risking adrenal crisis
    3. It causes asthma
    4. Weight gain doubles
    Show answer

    Answer: The adrenals may not make enough cortisol, risking adrenal crisis. Doses are tapered so the adrenals can wake up.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    What is the usual treatment for Cushing’s disease?

    1. Surgery to remove the pituitary tumour through the nose
    2. Thyroxine
    3. Antibiotics
    4. Physio alone
    Show answer

    Answer: Surgery to remove the pituitary tumour through the nose. Transsphenoidal surgery. Medicines or radiotherapy may follow if needed.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Which rarer source can make ACTH and cause Cushing’s?

    1. The appendix
    2. The knee
    3. The skin
    4. A tumour outside the pituitary, often in the lung
    Show answer

    Answer: A tumour outside the pituitary, often in the lung. Called ectopic ACTH. It often causes rapid, severe disease with low potassium.

Common questions

Is Cushing’s syndrome rare?

Apart from steroid medicines, yes: only a few people per million each year.

Does taking prednisolone always cause it?

No. It depends on dose and duration. Short courses rarely do.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. Don’t stop steroids without talking to your doctor.
