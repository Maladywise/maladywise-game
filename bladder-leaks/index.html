Fact sheet

Bladder Leaks (Incontinence)

Leaks when you cough, laugh or jump, or a sudden urge that won’t wait. Very common, rarely talked about, and very treatable. The first fix is free and lives in your pelvis.

Types: stress leaks happen with coughing, sneezing, laughing or exercise. Urge leaks come with a sudden, strong need to go, often with frequent and night-time trips (overactive bladder). Mixed is both. Overflow is leaking from a bladder that doesn’t empty properly, more common in men with prostate problems.

Risk factors: pregnancy and childbirth, extra weight, constipation, urine infections, chronic cough, diabetes, some medicines, mobility problems, and in men an enlarged prostate or prostate surgery.

Diagnosis: your story, an examination, a urine test and a bladder diary for a few days. Sometimes a bladder ultrasound.

The bladder ladder

Pelvic floor training is the main event for stress leaks; bladder training for urge leaks. Give them a fair go: 3 months and 6 weeks respectively.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careTweak drinks and bowelsCut back caffeine, fizzy drinks and alcohol, drink evenly through the day, treat constipation, and lose weight if needed.
    Works
    Recommended first; helps some people and costs nothing
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    2–4 weeks to notice
    Watch for
    Drinking too little can make things worse, not better.

    Dehydration is not a continence strategy.

  2. Step 2 · Self-carePelvic floor muscle trainingAt least 8 squeezes, 3 times a day, for at least 3 months. See our pelvic floor exercise plan.
    Works
    Stress leaks: about 56 in 100 cured vs 6 in 100 untreated; 74 in 100 cured or improved vs 11 in 100
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    3 months to judge, then keep going
    Watch for
    Many people squeeze the wrong muscles at first; a check helps.

    The only workout you can do in a meeting.

  3. Step 3 · Allied healthContinence physio or nurseChecks your technique and tailors the program. Electrical stimulation if you can’t contract the muscles. Also helps men after prostate surgery.
    Works
    Most trials tested supervised programs; a check makes sure you’re training the right muscles
    Cost
    About $90–130 a session; Medicare rebates with a GP care plan
    How long
    3–6 visits over 3 months
    Watch for
    Ask for a physio with pelvic health training.

    Like a personal trainer, for muscles no one sees.

  4. Step 4 · Self-careBladder training (urge leaks)Gradually stretch the time between the urge and going to the toilet. Combine with pelvic floor training for mixed leaks.
    Works
    The standard first treatment for urge leaks
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    At least 6 weeks
    Watch for
    Don’t “just in case” wee; it trains the bladder to hold less.

    You’re the boss of your bladder. It just hasn’t been told yet.

  5. Step 5 · PrescriptionBladder medicines (urge leaks)Tablets or patches that calm an overactive bladder, such as oxybutynin or mirabegron.
    Works
    Reduce urgency for some people; reviewed at 4 weeks
    Cost
    Prescription; many under or around the $25 PBS co-payment
    How long
    Ongoing, reviewed every 6–12 months
    Watch for
    Dry mouth, constipation, blurred vision, tiredness.

    Helpful backup, not a substitute for the training.

  6. Step 6 · SpecialistSurgery for stress leaksIf stress leaks don’t improve with training. A gynaecologist or urologist explains the options.
    Works
    Usually the next step when training hasn’t worked
    Cost
    Specialist fees vary; Medicare rebates with a GP referral
    How long
    Weeks to recover
    Watch for
    Ask about the risks and long-term results of each option.

    Worth it for some, after the free fix has had its turn.

Costs are rough Australian prices in November 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials, not promises.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Biofeedback gadgets and apps on their ownLittle good evidence they add much, though they can motivate some people.
  • Cutting right back on fluidsConcentrated urine can irritate the bladder and worsen urgency.
  • Pads as the whole planPads manage leaks but don’t treat them. Useful while treatment kicks in.

What you can do

  • Start our pelvic floor exercise plan today.
  • Keep a 3-day bladder diary: drinks, trips and leaks.
  • Treat constipation; straining weakens the pelvic floor.
  • Australia: call the National Continence Helpline on 1800 33 00 66 for free advice.

See your GP if

  • Leaks bother you or limit what you do
  • Burning, stinging or blood in your urine (see urinary tract infection)
  • Constant dribbling, or feeling you can’t empty your bladder
  • Leaks started after surgery or with a new medicine

Get emergency help (call 000 (Australia) or your local emergency number. From a mobile, 112 works in most countries.) for sudden loss of bladder or bowel control with back pain, numbness between the legs or leg weakness. This can mean nerve compression.

Common questions

Is bladder leakage just part of having children or getting older?

It is common, but it is not something you have to accept. Most people improve with pelvic floor or bladder training.

Do pelvic floor exercises really work?

Yes, especially for stress leaks. In trials, about 56 in 100 women were cured with training, compared with 6 in 100 without it, and about 3 in 4 were cured or improved.

Can men get bladder leaks?

Yes, especially with an enlarged prostate or after prostate surgery. Pelvic floor training and continence physiotherapy help men too.

Should I drink less to stop leaking?

No. Drinking too little can concentrate your urine and make urgency worse. Drink evenly through the day and cut back on caffeine and fizzy drinks.
