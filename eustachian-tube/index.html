Fact sheet

Blocked Ears

The tube between your middle ear and the back of your nose isn’t opening properly. Ears feel full, muffled or crackly. Usually follows a cold or hay fever and settles in a few weeks.

The blocked ears ladder

Most people never get past step 2. Fix the nose and the ears usually follow.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careSwallow, yawn and gently pop your earsChew gum, yawn, or pinch your nose, close your mouth and blow gently until you feel a pop.
    Works
    Not tested in trials; most cases after a cold settle within a few weeks
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    Several times a day until it settles
    Watch for
    Blow gently. Stop if it hurts.

    You’re popping an ear, not inflating a jumping castle.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacySaline spray or rinseSalt water to clear a blocked, snotty nose.
    Works
    Evidence weak but low risk: clears the nose; little direct evidence for ears
    Cost
    About $8–20
    How long
    1–3 times a day while the nose is blocked
    Watch for
    Use cooled boiled or sterile water for rinses, never straight from the tap.

    Cheap, safe and underrated.

  3. Step 3 · PharmacyNasal balloon (autoinflation)Inflate a balloon with your nose to push air up the tube.
    Works
    Kids with glue ear: about 5 in 10 cleared at 3 months vs 4 in 10 without; less evidence in adults
    Cost
    About $20–30 for a kit
    How long
    2–3 times a day for up to 1–3 months
    Watch for
    Not during an ear infection. Kids need to be old enough to do it.

    Possibly the only time blowing up a balloon with your nose is medical advice.

  4. Step 4 · PharmacySteroid nasal sprayCalms a swollen nose lining.
    Works
    Helps if hay fever or sinus problems are the cause; no better than a dummy treatment for other blocked ears in trials
    Cost
    About $15–25 a bottle
    How long
    Daily for at least 2–4 weeks
    Watch for
    Nosebleeds and dryness. Aim away from the middle of the nose.

    Not a quick fix. Daily or don’t bother.

  5. Step 5 · PharmacyDecongestant nasal spray, short termShrinks a blocked nose fast, e.g. before a flight.
    Works
    Quick, short-term relief for a blocked nose; little trial evidence for ears
    Cost
    About $8–15
    How long
    No more than 3–5 days
    Watch for
    Longer use makes the nose more blocked. Makes the “stuck open” kind of tube worse.

    Day 6 is where the rebound starts. Your nose keeps count.

  6. Step 6 · SpecialistENT treatment: grommet or balloon dilationA tiny tube in the eardrum, or widening the Eustachian tube with a balloon.
    Works
    Balloon: normal ear pressure in about 1 in 2 at 6 weeks, vs about 1 in 7 on medical treatment alone
    Cost
    Free as a public patient (waits vary); private costs vary, so get a quote
    How long
    Day procedure; grommets usually fall out by themselves in 6–18 months
    Watch for
    Grommets: ear discharge, keep water out if advised. Balloon: small risk of bleeding or surgical emphysema.

    For months-long problems, not a week of muffled ears after a cold.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from studies, not promises.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Ear candlingDoesn’t create suction or pull anything out, and can burn your ear or drip wax into it. A fire hazard with a spa playlist.
  • AntibioticsBlocked ears after a cold usually aren’t an infection, so antibiotics don’t help, but the side effects still turn up.
  • Antihistamine tabletsDon’t help blocked ears unless hay fever is behind it, and even then a steroid nasal spray works better.
  • Decongestant tabletsLittle evidence they help blocked ears, and they can raise blood pressure, cause jitters and wreck sleep.

What you can do

  • Swallow, yawn or chew gum often.
  • Flying: swallow, chew or suck a lolly during descent and stay awake for it. Feed babies or give a dummy for take-off and landing.
  • Blocked nose before a flight? A decongestant spray about 30 minutes before descent may help.
  • Don’t smoke or vape: smoke irritates the tube.
  • Hearing your own voice or breathing loudly, better lying down? The tube may be stuck open instead. Tell your GP; decongestants make that worse.

See your GP if

  • Symptoms last more than 6 weeks, or keep coming back
  • Ear pain, discharge or fever
  • Only one ear is affected for a long time

See a doctor the same day for sudden hearing loss, severe dizziness or spinning, or severe ear pain after flying or diving.

Common questions

How do I unblock my ears?

Swallow, yawn or chew gum often. You can also gently pinch your nose, close your mouth and blow until you feel a pop, or use a nasal balloon from the pharmacy. Treating a blocked nose helps too.

How long do blocked ears last after a cold?

Usually a few weeks, settling on their own. See your GP if symptoms last more than 6 weeks or keep coming back.

Do nasal sprays help blocked ears?

A steroid nasal spray helps if hay fever or sinus problems are the cause, but needs daily use for several weeks. Decongestant sprays help short term, for no more than 3 to 5 days.

How do I stop my ears blocking on a plane?

Swallow, yawn, chew or suck a lolly during descent and stay awake for it. If your nose is blocked, a decongestant spray about 30 minutes before descent may help. Avoid flying with a bad cold if you can.
