Huntington’s Disease Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on Huntington’s disease: the genetics, the three groups of symptoms, predictive testing and support.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What kind of condition is Huntington’s disease?

    1. A vitamin deficiency
    2. A bacterial infection
    3. A stroke
    4. An inherited brain disease that worsens over time
    Show answer

    Answer: An inherited brain disease that worsens over time. It affects movement, thinking and mood.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    How is it inherited?

    1. It skips every generation
    2. Autosomal dominant: each child of an affected parent has a 50% chance
    3. It’s never inherited
    4. Only through mothers
    Show answer

    Answer: Autosomal dominant: each child of an affected parent has a 50% chance. Men and women are affected equally.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Which gene is involved?

    1. BRCA2
    2. SMN1
    3. HTT (huntingtin)
    4. FBN1
    Show answer

    Answer: HTT (huntingtin). The faulty gene has too many CAG repeats.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    Roughly what CAG repeat count causes Huntington’s with full penetrance?

    1. 40 or more
    2. 5
    3. Under 20
    4. 10
    Show answer

    Answer: 40 or more. 36–39 repeats is reduced penetrance; 27–35 is an intermediate range that can expand in children.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    What is chorea?

    1. Ringing in the ears
    2. Involuntary, irregular, dance-like movements
    3. A heart rhythm
    4. A type of rash
    Show answer

    Answer: Involuntary, irregular, dance-like movements. The name comes from the Greek for dance.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    At what age do symptoms usually start?

    1. In infancy
    2. Only after 80
    3. At puberty in all cases
    4. Between 30 and 50
    Show answer

    Answer: Between 30 and 50. Juvenile onset (before 20) happens with very long repeats.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    Which symptoms can appear before movement problems?

    1. Changes in mood, irritability and thinking
    2. Hair loss
    3. Hearing loss
    4. Itching
    Show answer

    Answer: Changes in mood, irritability and thinking. Depression, apathy and trouble planning or multitasking are common early features.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    What is anticipation?

    1. Waiting for test results
    2. A side effect of treatment
    3. Symptoms starting earlier in the next generation as repeats expand
    4. Predicting relapses
    Show answer

    Answer: Symptoms starting earlier in the next generation as repeats expand. Expansion is more likely when the gene is passed down from the father.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    What is predictive testing?

    1. A brain scan
    2. Genetic testing in someone without symptoms who is at risk
    3. A test for current infection
    4. A memory quiz
    Show answer

    Answer: Genetic testing in someone without symptoms who is at risk. Done with genetic counselling before and after, because results are life-changing. Many at-risk people choose not to test.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    Which medicine can reduce chorea?

    1. Penicillin
    2. Insulin
    3. Levothyroxine
    4. Tetrabenazine
    Show answer

    Answer: Tetrabenazine. It can worsen depression, so mood is monitored. Some antipsychotics are also used.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    Is there a cure?

    1. Yes, a special diet
    2. Yes, a one-off injection
    3. Not yet; treatment manages symptoms, and gene-lowering therapies are in trials
    4. Yes, surgery
    Show answer

    Answer: Not yet; treatment manages symptoms, and gene-lowering therapies are in trials. Huntingtin-lowering approaches, including AMT-130 gene therapy, are under study.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Who described the condition in 1872?

    1. George Huntington
    2. James Parkinson
    3. Alois Alzheimer
    4. Jean-Martin Charcot
    Show answer

    Answer: George Huntington. He described a family in Long Island, New York, at age 22.

Common questions

If my parent has Huntington’s, will I get it?

Each child has a 50% chance of inheriting the gene. Genetic counselling helps people decide whether and when to test.

Where can families get support?

Huntington’s Australia and state-based associations offer information, counselling and support services.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. See your GP about your own symptoms or results.
