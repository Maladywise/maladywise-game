Febrile Seizures

A fit (seizure) triggered by a fever in a young child. It’s terrifying to watch, but most last only a minute or two and leave no lasting harm. Knowing what to do makes a big difference.

What it is

  • A seizure caused by a quick rise in temperature, usually with a fever of 38°C or more. Sometimes the seizure is the first sign of the fever.
  • Most common between about 6 months and 6 years.
  • The child may go stiff, jerk, roll their eyes or go floppy, and won’t respond. Most seizures last 1 to 2 minutes.
  • Afterwards the child is usually sleepy or grumpy, and back to normal within 30 to 60 minutes.

What to do during a seizure

  • Stay calm and note the time.
  • Lay your child on their side on a soft, low surface, away from anything hard or sharp.
  • Don’t hold them down, don’t put anything in their mouth, and don’t put them in a bath.
  • Stay with them until they wake up.

Call 000 if

  • It’s your child’s first seizure.
  • The seizure lasts more than 5 minutes.
  • Your child doesn’t wake up after it stops, or is still very drowsy 30 minutes later.
  • They have trouble breathing, look blue or very pale, or seem very sick.
  • Also see a doctor urgently if your baby is under 1 month old, or has a rash that doesn’t fade when pressed.

Reassuring facts

  • Febrile seizures don’t cause brain damage, even long ones.
  • They don’t mean epilepsy, even if a child has more than one. Epilepsy is repeated seizures without fever.
  • Many children only ever have one. Some have another with a later fever; most grow out of it by about age 6.
  • Paracetamol and ibuprofen make a child more comfortable, but they don’t prevent febrile seizures.

Common questions

Do febrile seizures cause brain damage?

No. Febrile seizures don’t cause brain damage or learning problems, even when they last a long time.

Will my child get epilepsy?

A febrile seizure doesn’t mean a child has epilepsy, even if they have several. Epilepsy is repeated seizures without a fever.

Can I prevent febrile seizures with paracetamol?

No. Fever medicines can make a child more comfortable, but they don’t stop febrile seizures.

When should I call an ambulance for a febrile seizure?

If it’s the first seizure, it lasts more than 5 minutes, your child doesn’t wake up afterwards, has trouble breathing, or looks very unwell.
