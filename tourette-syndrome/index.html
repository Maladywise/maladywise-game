Tourette Syndrome

Tourette syndrome is a brain difference that causes tics: sudden, repeated movements or sounds that are hard to stop. It starts in childhood, is more common than people think, and usually gets better by adulthood.

What tics are

  • Motor tics: blinking, grimacing, shoulder shrugging, head jerking.
  • Vocal tics: sniffing, throat clearing, grunting, words or phrases.
  • Most people feel an urge or itch before a tic, and the tic relieves it.
  • Tics can be held back for a while, but that takes effort. They often get worse with stress, excitement or tiredness, and ease with focused activities.

Tourette syndrome

  • Diagnosed when someone has both motor and vocal tics for more than a year, starting before 18.
  • Usually starts around age 5–7.
  • Affects up to 1 in 100 children, and boys 3–4 times more often than girls.
  • Many children with milder tics are never diagnosed.

Myths

  • “It’s the swearing condition.” Only about 1 in 10 people with Tourette syndrome have swearing tics.
  • “They could stop if they tried.” Holding tics back is exhausting and they come back.
  • “It’s for life.” Tics usually peak in the early teens. By adulthood about a third have no tics and another third only mild ones.

Often comes with

  • ADHD (about half), OCD or obsessive thoughts, anxiety and learning difficulties.
  • These often cause more trouble than the tics, so they’re worth treating first.

What helps

  • Understanding: explaining tics to the child, family and school is the first step. Many people need nothing more.
  • Behaviour therapy: CBIT (habit reversal) teaches a competing movement to use when the urge comes. It’s the first-line treatment when tics are a problem.
  • Medicines: clonidine or guanfacine first for many people, especially with ADHD; aripiprazole or risperidone for more severe tics. A specialist usually starts these.
  • Don’t tell children to stop or draw attention to tics. It usually makes them worse.

See your GP if

  • Tics are causing pain, distress, bullying or trouble at school or work
  • Tics start suddenly in an adult, or come with other new symptoms

Common questions

What causes Tourette syndrome?

It’s a difference in how the brain develops, with a strong genetic link. It isn’t caused by parenting or by anything the person did.

Do all people with Tourette syndrome swear?

No. Only about 1 in 10 have swearing or rude tics (coprolalia).

Does Tourette syndrome go away?

Tics usually peak in the early teenage years. By adulthood about a third of people have no tics and another third have mild ones.

What is the best treatment for tics?

Understanding and support first. If tics are a problem, behaviour therapy (CBIT) is first-line, with medicines for some people.
