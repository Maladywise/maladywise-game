Fact sheet

Acne

What it is

Acne happens when hair follicles get blocked with oil and dead skin, then inflamed. It causes blackheads, whiteheads, pimples and sometimes deeper lumps. It's very common in teenagers but can carry on into adulthood. It isn't caused by being dirty.

Skin care basics

  • Wash your face no more than twice a day with a gentle, soap-free cleanser.
  • Remove make-up before bed.
  • Use oil-free, non-comedogenic (won't block pores) moisturiser, make-up and sunscreen.
  • Don't pick or squeeze spots. It makes scarring more likely.
  • A diet lower in sugar and processed foods may help some people.

Treatment, step by step

  • Step 1: benzoyl peroxide wash, gel or cream from the pharmacy. Start with a lower strength if your skin is sensitive. It can bleach towels and pillowcases.
  • Step 2: a retinoid cream or gel (adapalene or tretinoin) if there are lots of blackheads and whiteheads. Apply a pea-sized amount to the whole area at night. Start every second night, as dryness and redness are common at first.
  • Step 3: combination creams with both ingredients work well for mixed acne.
  • If creams aren't enough, your GP may suggest tablets, such as an antibiotic or, for some women, the contraceptive pill.
  • For severe or scarring acne, a dermatologist can prescribe stronger treatment.

Give it time

Acne treatments take 6 to 12 weeks to work, and acne can look a little worse in the first few weeks. Keep going unless the irritation is severe. Use sunscreen, as some treatments make skin burn more easily.

See your GP if

  • Over-the-counter treatment hasn't helped after 3 months
  • You have painful deep lumps or cysts
  • Acne is leaving scars or dark marks
  • It's affecting your mood or confidence

Some acne treatments aren't safe in pregnancy. Tell your doctor if you're pregnant or planning a pregnancy.
