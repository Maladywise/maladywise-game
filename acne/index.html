Fact sheet

Acne

Blocked, inflamed hair follicles causing blackheads, whiteheads and pimples. Very treatable. Not caused by poor hygiene.

Treatment options

Ratings show how strong the research is.

  • Strong evidenceBenzoyl peroxidePharmacy wash or gel. Reduces bacteria and inflammation. Can dry skin and bleach fabric.
  • Strong evidenceRetinoid cream or gelAdapalene or tretinoin, at night on the whole area. Best for blackheads and whiteheads. Start every second night.
  • Strong evidenceCombination gelAdapalene with benzoyl peroxide. The most effective cream for most people.
  • Strong evidenceAntibiotic tabletsSuch as doxycycline, for moderate acne. Used for about 3 months alongside a cream, not on their own.
  • Strong evidenceContraceptive pillSome types help acne in women.
  • Strong evidenceIsotretinoinFor severe or scarring acne, prescribed by a dermatologist. Very effective. Must not be used in pregnancy.
  • Moderate evidenceAzelaic acidA gentler option, also helps dark marks. Suitable in pregnancy.
  • Limited evidenceDiet changesA lower-sugar diet may help some people. Evidence is limited.

What you can do

  • Gentle cleanser up to twice a day.
  • Oil-free, non-comedogenic products and sunscreen.
  • Don’t pick or squeeze.
  • Allow 8 to 12 weeks for any treatment to work.

See your GP if

  • No better after 3 months of pharmacy treatment
  • Deep painful lumps, scarring or dark marks
  • It’s affecting your mood

Some acne treatments are unsafe in pregnancy. Tell your doctor if you’re pregnant or planning to be.
