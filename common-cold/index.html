Fact sheet

Common Cold

A viral infection of the nose and throat. Adults catch 2–4 colds a year and young children up to 8. Miserable but usually harmless, most people feel better in 7–10 days, whatever they take.

Symptoms

  • Blocked or runny nose, sneezing, sore throat, cough, headache and tiredness.
  • Sometimes a mild fever (more common in children), aches, or a hoarse voice.
  • Symptoms build over 2–3 days, peak around day 3 or 4, then ease. A cough can hang on for a couple of weeks.
  • Young children may be grumpy, feed or sleep poorly, or vomit after coughing.

Causes and risk factors

  • Hundreds of different viruses, rhinoviruses most often. That’s why you can catch colds again and again.
  • Spread by droplets from coughs and sneezes, and by hands touching surfaces then your eyes, nose or mouth.
  • Children in childcare and school, and the adults who live with them, catch the most.

How it’s diagnosed

  • No test is usually needed: the pattern of symptoms is enough.
  • See a doctor if symptoms are severe, unusual, or not settling, to check for something else such as flu, COVID-19, an ear or chest infection, or sinusitis.

The cold ladder

Nothing cures a cold. Everything here is about feeling better while your immune system does the work. Most people never need to climb past step 2.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careRest, fluids, saline and honeyRest, plenty of fluids, saline nose drops or spray, and warm honey and lemon for a sore throat or cough.
    Works
    Honey probably eases cough and helps children sleep better than no treatment (only for children over 12 months)
    Cost
    A few dollars
    How long
    Until you feel better, usually 7–10 days
    Watch for
    No honey for babies under 12 months: risk of infant botulism.

    Grandma’s honey and lemon has better evidence than most of the cold aisle.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacyParacetamol or ibuprofenFor headache, sore throat, aches and fever.
    Works
    Eases aches and fever; doesn’t shorten the cold
    Cost
    About $3–10
    How long
    A few days, as needed
    Watch for
    Many cold-and-flu tablets already contain paracetamol, so check labels to avoid doubling up. No aspirin for children.

    The boring basics, still the best value in the aisle.

  3. Step 3 · PharmacyDecongestant nasal spraySprays such as oxymetazoline for a very blocked nose in adults, for a few days only.
    Works
    Clears a blocked nose for a few hours. Decongestant tablets containing phenylephrine don’t work: a US expert panel found them no better than a dummy pill
    Cost
    About $8–15
    How long
    3–5 days at most
    Watch for
    Longer use causes rebound blockage. Not for children under 6.

    Day 6 is when the spray starts causing the stuffiness it was treating.

  4. Step 4 · GPA check for something elseIf symptoms are severe, getting worse, or not improving after about 10 days, a doctor looks for ear, sinus or chest infections, or flu or COVID-19.
    Works
    Most people don’t need this step. Antibiotics don’t help colds because they don’t work on viruses
    Cost
    GP fees vary; bulk billing at some clinics
    How long
    One visit
    Watch for
    Also see a GP sooner if you have a long-term heart or lung condition or a weak immune system.

    A cold that’s still there in 3 weeks is either very loyal or not a cold.

Costs are rough Australian prices in November 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials, not promises.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • AntibioticsColds are viral; antibiotics only kill bacteria. They add side effects and resistance, not a faster recovery.
  • Vitamin C once a cold startsHigh doses after symptoms begin showed no consistent benefit. Taking it every day all year shortens colds by only about 8% in adults, roughly half a day.
  • Echinacea and garlicLittle evidence that they prevent or shorten colds.
  • Cough and cold medicines for under-6sAustralia’s medicines regulator says they shouldn’t be given to children under 6: little evidence they work and a risk of serious side effects. Ask a pharmacist before using them in 6- to 11-year-olds.

What you can do

  • Wash your hands often and cough or sneeze into a tissue or your elbow.
  • Stay home if you have a fever or feel too unwell for normal activities.
  • Use saline spray for a blocked nose, especially in children.
  • Don’t smoke, and keep children away from smoke.
  • Have asthma? Follow your asthma action plan early: colds are a common trigger.

See your GP if

  • Symptoms aren’t improving after 10 days, or are getting worse after starting to improve
  • A high fever lasts more than 3 days
  • A cough lasts more than 3 weeks
  • Ear pain, or face pain that’s severe or on one side
  • You have a long-term heart or lung condition or a weakened immune system

Call 000 (Australia) or your local emergency number for anyone who is struggling to breathe. For children: get urgent help if a baby under 3 months has a fever of 38°C or more, or a child is hard to wake, can’t be settled, has blue or grey skin, a rash that doesn’t fade when pressed, is vomiting and can’t keep fluids down, or has had no wet nappy for several hours. From a mobile, 112 works in most countries.

Common questions

How long am I contagious?

You can spread a cold for as long as you have symptoms. Babies and young children can stay infectious for longer.

Is it a cold or the flu?

Flu tends to hit suddenly and harder, with fever, aches and exhaustion that put you in bed. A cold is milder and mostly in the nose and throat. If unsure, or you’re at higher risk, ask about testing.

Should I go to work or school?

Stay home if you have a fever or feel too unwell to do your normal activities. If you go out, wash hands often and cover coughs.

Why is my cough still here after the cold has gone?

Airways stay irritated for a while after the virus clears, so a cough can linger for a couple of weeks. See a GP if it lasts more than 3 weeks.

How long does a cold last?

Most colds last 7 to 10 days, but a cough can hang around for up to 3 weeks. Adults get 2 to 3 colds a year and young children often 6 to 8, more in their first year of childcare.

Does vitamin C help a cold?

Taking vitamin C after a cold starts doesn’t reliably help. Taking it every day only shortens colds slightly, by about 8% in adults (less than a day), and doesn’t prevent them for most people.

Can I exercise with a cold?

Gentle exercise is usually fine if your symptoms are only above the neck, such as a runny nose or mild sore throat. Rest if you have a fever, body aches, chest symptoms or feel very tired.
