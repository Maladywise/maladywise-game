Migraine Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on migraine: what an attack looks like, the treatments that work, preventers, and when a headache needs urgent care.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    Which feature is typical of migraine?

    1. A throbbing one-sided headache with nausea or light sensitivity
    2. Pain only when pressing the scalp
    3. A brief stab lasting one second
    4. A tight band around the head with no other symptoms
    Show answer

    Answer: A throbbing one-sided headache with nausea or light sensitivity. Attacks last 4 to 72 hours and are made worse by moving around.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    What is a migraine aura?

    1. A ringing in the ears forever
    2. A smell of burning that lasts all day
    3. Feeling hungry before the headache
    4. Temporary nerve symptoms, often zigzag lights, before the headache
    Show answer

    Answer: Temporary nerve symptoms, often zigzag lights, before the headache. Aura usually builds over 5 minutes and lasts under an hour. About 1 in 3 people with migraine get it.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Who gets migraine more often?

    1. Only children
    2. Women, about three times as often as men
    3. Only people over 70
    4. Men only
    Show answer

    Answer: Women, about three times as often as men. Hormone changes play a role, and many women notice attacks around their period.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    What is a sensible first step for a mild attack?

    1. Waiting until it’s severe before taking anything
    2. Antibiotics
    3. Early aspirin, ibuprofen or paracetamol with an anti-nausea medicine
    4. Strong opioids
    Show answer

    Answer: Early aspirin, ibuprofen or paracetamol with an anti-nausea medicine. Treating early works best. Opioids are not recommended for migraine.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    What are triptans, such as sumatriptan?

    1. Daily preventers
    2. Sleeping tablets
    3. Antibiotics
    4. Migraine-specific medicines taken at the start of the headache
    Show answer

    Answer: Migraine-specific medicines taken at the start of the headache. Best taken early in the headache phase. Avoid them in people with heart disease or past stroke.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    What causes medication overuse headache?

    1. Drinking water
    2. Taking pain relief on too many days a month
    3. Too much sleep only once
    4. Taking a preventer daily
    Show answer

    Answer: Taking pain relief on too many days a month. Roughly 10 or more days a month of triptans or combination painkillers, or 15 or more of simple painkillers. Cutting back usually helps.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    When is a preventer usually considered?

    1. When attacks are frequent or disabling, often 4 or more days a month
    2. Only for children
    3. Never
    4. After the first ever migraine
    Show answer

    Answer: When attacks are frequent or disabling, often 4 or more days a month. Options include propranolol, amitriptyline, candesartan, topiramate and others. They take weeks to work.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    Which headache needs urgent care?

    1. A headache after a long day
    2. Your usual migraine pattern
    3. A sudden, severe ‘thunderclap’ headache
    4. A mild headache with a cold
    Show answer

    Answer: A sudden, severe ‘thunderclap’ headache. Peak pain within a minute can mean a bleed. Also urgent: fever with a stiff neck, new weakness, or a new headache over 50.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    What do the newest preventers (e.g. fremanezumab, galcanezumab) block?

    1. CGRP, a protein that drives migraine pain
    2. Histamine
    3. Serotonin completely
    4. Oestrogen
    Show answer

    Answer: CGRP, a protein that drives migraine pain. Monthly injections, PBS-listed for chronic migraine after other preventers have failed.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    Which combined pill caution applies to migraine with aura?

    1. All contraception is banned
    2. There is no caution
    3. The pill cures migraine
    4. Oestrogen-containing pills raise stroke risk and are usually avoided
    Show answer

    Answer: Oestrogen-containing pills raise stroke risk and are usually avoided. Progestogen-only options are generally fine. Discuss with your GP.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    Which injection is PBS-listed for chronic migraine prevention?

    1. Adrenaline
    2. Botulinum toxin (Botox) every 12 weeks
    3. Vitamin B12
    4. Insulin
    Show answer

    Answer: Botulinum toxin (Botox) every 12 weeks. For people with 15 or more headache days a month who haven’t responded to oral preventers.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    What is a vestibular migraine?

    1. A migraine that never causes headache in anyone
    2. Migraine only in the eyes
    3. Migraine where vertigo is a main symptom
    4. A headache from ear wax
    Show answer

    Answer: Migraine where vertigo is a main symptom. Dizziness episodes can occur with or without headache. It’s one of the most common causes of recurrent vertigo.

Common questions

Are migraines just bad headaches?

No. Migraine is a brain disorder with attacks that can include aura, nausea and sensitivity to light and sound.

Does a headache diary help?

Yes. It shows patterns, triggers and how many days you use pain relief.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. See your GP about a plan for your own headaches.
