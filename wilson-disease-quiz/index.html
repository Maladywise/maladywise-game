Wilson Disease Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on Wilson disease: a rare, treatable genetic condition where copper builds up in the liver and brain.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What builds up in Wilson disease?

    1. Copper
    2. Lead
    3. Iron
    4. Calcium
    Show answer

    Answer: Copper. The liver can’t get rid of copper into bile, so it builds up in the liver, brain and eyes.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    How is it inherited?

    1. Autosomal dominant
    2. Autosomal recessive: a faulty copy from each parent
    3. From the father only
    4. It’s caught from food
    Show answer

    Answer: Autosomal recessive: a faulty copy from each parent. The gene is ATP7B. Brothers and sisters of someone with Wilson disease have a 1 in 4 chance.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Which eye sign is classic?

    1. A cherry-red spot
    2. Blue sclera
    3. Red eyes
    4. Kayser–Fleischer rings: a golden-brown ring around the iris
    Show answer

    Answer: Kayser–Fleischer rings: a golden-brown ring around the iris. Copper deposits in the cornea. Often needs a slit lamp to see.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    Which blood protein is usually low?

    1. Ferritin
    2. Albumin
    3. Ceruloplasmin
    4. Troponin
    Show answer

    Answer: Ceruloplasmin. Ceruloplasmin carries copper. Low levels plus high urine copper point to Wilson disease.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    Which urine test helps diagnose it?

    1. Urine culture
    2. Urine pregnancy test
    3. 24-hour urine copper
    4. Urine drug screen
    Show answer

    Answer: 24-hour urine copper. High copper loss in the urine is typical.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    When does it usually show up?

    1. Only after age 70
    2. Only in pregnancy
    3. At birth
    4. Liver problems in children, or brain and psychiatric symptoms in young adults
    Show answer

    Answer: Liver problems in children, or brain and psychiatric symptoms in young adults. Think of it in anyone under 40 with unexplained liver disease or a new movement disorder.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    Which movement problems can it cause?

    1. Sneezing fits
    2. Tremor, stiffness and slurred speech
    3. Night blindness
    4. Hiccups only
    Show answer

    Answer: Tremor, stiffness and slurred speech. A ‘wing-beating’ tremor of outstretched arms is the classic description.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    Which psychiatric changes can be the first sign?

    1. Personality change, depression or psychosis
    2. Only memory loss in old age
    3. None ever
    4. Only phobias
    Show answer

    Answer: Personality change, depression or psychosis. Young people with new psychiatric symptoms and liver or movement problems should be tested.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    What is the main treatment?

    1. Venesection
    2. Antibiotics
    3. Chelating drugs (penicillamine or trientine) and zinc
    4. Iron tablets
    Show answer

    Answer: Chelating drugs (penicillamine or trientine) and zinc. Chelators pull copper out. Zinc blocks copper absorption from food.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    Which foods are high in copper?

    1. Shellfish, liver, nuts, chocolate and mushrooms
    2. Apples
    3. Rice and pasta
    4. Milk
    Show answer

    Answer: Shellfish, liver, nuts, chocolate and mushrooms. Diet helps, but medicine does most of the work.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    What happens if treatment is stopped?

    1. Copper levels drop
    2. Copper builds up again and can cause liver failure
    3. Only the eyes are affected
    4. Nothing, it’s cured
    Show answer

    Answer: Copper builds up again and can cause liver failure. Treatment is lifelong. Stopping can be fatal.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    What is the treatment for acute liver failure from Wilson disease?

    1. Rest and fluids
    2. Steroids
    3. High-dose iron
    4. Liver transplant
    Show answer

    Answer: Liver transplant. A transplant cures the copper problem, because the new liver handles copper normally.

Common questions

Is Wilson disease curable?

It can’t be cured without a transplant, but lifelong treatment controls it and most people live normally.

Should siblings be tested?

Yes. Brothers and sisters have a 1 in 4 chance and should be screened even without symptoms.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. Talk to your doctor or a liver specialist.
