Teen Health

The teenage years bring new health questions, many of them awkward to ask. Here are the common ones, what helps, and where to get confidential help. From age 15, teens can have their own Medicare card and see a GP on their own.

Skin and body

  • Acne: very common and very treatable. Don’t wait it out if it’s scarring or getting you down.
  • Testicular self-checks: a monthly check. Sudden testicle pain is an emergency.
  • Varicocele: a ‘bag of worms’ above the testicle, usually harmless.

Periods

  • Period pain: some is normal; pain that stops school or sport isn’t, and can be endometriosis.
  • Heavy periods: changing pads or tampons every 1 to 2 hours, or flooding, needs checking for low iron or a bleeding problem.
  • Irregular periods are common in the first couple of years. No periods by 15, or long gaps later, are worth a GP visit (PCOS is one cause).

Infections and injuries

  • Glandular fever: sore throat, fever and big glands, with tiredness that can last weeks. No contact sport until a doctor says the spleen is safe.
  • Concussion: if in doubt, sit them out. Under-19s need 14 symptom-free days before contact training and at least 21 days before contact games.

Mind, sleep and habits

  • Teens need about 8 to 10 hours of sleep. Screens off an hour before bed helps.
  • Low mood, anxiety, self-harm thoughts or eating worries: talk to a GP, school counsellor or headspace. You don’t need a parent’s permission.
  • Vaping is addictive, and selling vapes to under-18s is illegal in Australia. Quitline (13 78 48) helps teens too.

Get help

  • Emergency: call 000.
  • Kids Helpline: 1800 55 1800, free and confidential, any time, for ages 5 to 25.
  • headspace: free or low-cost mental health and GP services for ages 12 to 25.
  • Your GP: from 14 or 15, you can usually see your GP on your own, and what you say is kept confidential unless someone’s safety is at risk.

Common questions

Can a teenager see a GP without their parents?

Yes. From around 14 or 15, young people can usually see a GP on their own, and from 15 they can have their own Medicare card. What you discuss is confidential unless there’s a serious risk to safety.

When do teens need to see a doctor about periods?

If periods stop you going to school or sport, you’re changing products every 1 to 2 hours, you have no periods by 15, or you feel faint or very tired.

How long should a teen sit out after a concussion?

Under Australian guidelines, under-19s need 14 symptom-free days before contact training and at least 21 days before returning to contact games.

Where can teenagers get free mental health help?

Kids Helpline (1800 55 1800) any time, headspace centres for ages 12 to 25, school counsellors, or a GP.
