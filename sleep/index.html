Fact sheet

Trouble Sleeping

Trouble getting to sleep, staying asleep or waking too early. Short spells after stress usually pass. Insomnia lasting 3 months or more is best fixed with CBT-I, not tablets.

The insomnia ladder

Start at the bottom. CBT-I is the real treatment: it works for most people and keeps working after you stop. Tablets are a short-term crutch.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careSleep habitsSame wake-up time every day, morning light, no caffeine after midday, screens down an hour before bed.
    Works
    Small benefit in trials; on its own rarely fixes long-term insomnia
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    Daily, for good
    Watch for
    Lying in bed for hours trying harder. Sleep doesn’t respond to effort

    Sleep is the only thing that gets worse the harder you try.

  2. Step 2 · Self-careOnline CBT-IA structured self-help version of CBT for insomnia, such as the free This Way Up Insomnia Program (4 online modules).
    Works
    In a large trial, about 1 in 2 recovered at a year vs 1 in 4 given sleep advice; a bit below therapist-led CBT-I
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    About 6–10 weeks
    Watch for
    A rough first couple of weeks if you try sleep restriction. It gets better

    The best free thing on this page, and nobody advertises it.

  3. Step 3 · PharmacySlow-release melatonin (55 and over)A pharmacist-only short course for people 55 and over. Other forms need a prescription.
    Works
    A small benefit: asleep about 7–9 minutes sooner than a dummy treatment
    Cost
    About $35 for 30 tablets
    How long
    Up to 13 weeks
    Watch for
    Headache, next-day drowsiness. Gummies from overseas often don’t contain what the label says

    A nudge, not a knockout.

  4. Step 4 · Allied healthCBT-I with a psychologist4 to 8 sessions covering sleep scheduling, rethinking sleep worries and winding down.
    Works
    About 7 in 10 people sleep noticeably better and around 4 in 10 recover fully; the benefits last
    Cost
    About $150–250 a session; Medicare rebates with a GP mental health treatment plan
    How long
    4–8 sessions over 6–10 weeks
    Watch for
    Sleep restriction makes you more tired for a week or two before it helps

    Homework for your bedroom. Annoying, effective.

  5. Step 5 · PrescriptionShort-term sleeping tabletsSuch as temazepam or zolpidem, for a few nights in a crisis.
    Works
    In over-60s, about 1 in 13 sleep better but 1 in 6 get side effects; stop working quickly
    Cost
    Under the $25 PBS co-payment, about $10–20
    How long
    A few nights only
    Watch for
    Next-day drowsiness, falls, memory problems, dependence. Sleepwalking or sleep-driving with zolpidem

    A short loan, with interest.

  6. Step 6 · PrescriptionOrexin-blocking tabletsNewer medicines (daridorexant, lemborexant or suvorexant) when CBT-I isn’t enough.
    Works
    Vs dummy treatment: about 20 minutes less awake at night and asleep about 10 minutes sooner. Small to moderate benefit
    Cost
    Private script, roughly $60–100 a month
    How long
    Can be used for months, with review
    Watch for
    Next-day drowsiness, headache, vivid dreams. Not with narcolepsy

    Better than the old tablets, still not better than CBT-I.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials: your 3 am brain didn’t read them.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Sedating antihistamines (such as doxylamine)Next-day grogginess, little benefit, and not suitable for older people. A hangover without the party.
  • ValerianMost trials find little or no benefit over a dummy treatment. Smells like old socks, works like a sugar pill.
  • MagnesiumSmall, low-quality studies. Unlikely to fix insomnia unless you’re actually low.
  • CBD oilLittle good evidence for insomnia, and it isn’t cheap. Expensive vibes.
  • Sleep trackersNot a treatment, and chasing a perfect score can make sleep worse. Your watch doesn’t know how you feel.

What you can do

  • Get up at the same time every day, even after a bad night and on weekends.
  • Go to bed only when sleepy. If you’re awake after about 20 minutes, get up, do something calm in dim light, and return when sleepy.
  • Alcohol gets you to sleep and then wrecks the second half of the night.
  • Keep naps under 30 minutes and before 3 pm.

See your GP if

  • Trouble sleeping most nights for 3 months or more
  • Loud snoring, gasping or pauses in breathing at night, or dozing while driving
  • Low mood, anxiety, pain or restless legs keep you awake
  • You rely on sleeping tablets or alcohol to sleep

Don’t drive if you feel drowsy. Don’t stop long-term sleeping tablets suddenly: your GP can help you reduce them slowly.

Common questions

What is the best treatment for insomnia?

CBT for insomnia (CBT-I), usually 4 to 8 sessions with a psychologist or a free online program such as This Way Up. It helps most people, and the benefits last.

Does melatonin help you sleep?

Slow-release melatonin gives a small benefit for people over 55, as a short course from a pharmacy. Other forms need a prescription.

Are sleeping tablets safe long term?

No. Long-term use risks dependence, falls, memory problems and next-day drowsiness, especially in older people. Don’t stop them suddenly: your GP can help you reduce them slowly.

What should I do if I can’t fall asleep?

If you’re still awake after about 20 minutes, get up, do something calm in dim light, and return when sleepy. Get up at the same time every day, even after a bad night.
