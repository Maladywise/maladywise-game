Fact sheet

Neck Pain

Very common, rarely serious, usually better within a few weeks. A stiff, sore neck is mostly muscles and joints grumbling. Keep it moving, then make it stronger.

The neck pain ladder

Most new neck pain settles with steps 1–2. Strengthening (step 3) has the best evidence for pain that hangs around or keeps coming back.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careKeep moving, heat and reassuranceGentle neck movement through the day, a heat pack or wrapped cold pack, a low firm pillow, and regular breaks from screens and driving.
    Works
    Most neck pain improves within a few weeks
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    Days to weeks
    Watch for
    Skip activities that need a full head turn, like driving, until you can check over your shoulder safely.

    Your neck isn’t made of glass. Moving it gently is how it gets better.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacyPain relief (ibuprofen, paracetamol, anti-inflammatory gel)Short-term, to help you move and sleep.
    Works
    Short-term relief for some; won’t speed up healing on its own
    Cost
    About $5–15 a pack
    How long
    Days, not months
    Watch for
    Anti-inflammatories: stomach, kidney and blood pressure risks. Check with a pharmacist first if you have other conditions.

    A crutch for a week, not a lifestyle.

  3. Step 3 · Allied healthNeck and shoulder strengtheningShrugs, rows and shoulder raises with weights, 3 times a week, plus gentle neck movement through the day. A physio can tailor it.
    Works
    For neck pain lasting more than 3 months, strength training reduced pain by a moderate amount in trials
    Cost
    Free at home with our handout; physio about $80–120 a session (Medicare rebates with a GP care plan)
    How long
    At least 10 weeks, then keep it going
    Watch for
    Effort and mild ache are fine; pain or tingling spreading down the arm means stop and get checked.

    Ten weeks of boring rows beats ten years of heat packs.

  4. Step 4 · SpecialistSpecialist reviewFor arm pain from a pinched nerve that isn’t settling, arm weakness, or warning signs.
    Works
    No trials of referral itself; helps when nerve pain doesn’t settle or there’s weakness
    Cost
    Specialist fees vary; Medicare rebates with a GP referral
    How long
    As needed
    Watch for
    Scans are useful here, not for everyday neck pain.

    The specialist route is for nerve trouble, not for the garden-variety stiff neck.

Costs are rough Australian prices in November 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials, not promises.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Neck collarsThey keep the neck still, which slows recovery. Keep moving unless a doctor says otherwise, for example after a fracture.
  • Opioid painkillersIn a 2023 Australian trial, opioids worked no better than a dummy pill for new neck and back pain, and raised the risk of misuse a year later.
  • Routine X-rays and MRIWear-and-tear changes are common in people with no pain at all, so scans often find things that aren’t the cause. Save them for warning signs or nerve problems.
  • Pricey ‘orthopaedic’ pillowsNot needed. The standard advice is simply a low, firm pillow that keeps your head level with your body.

What you can do

  • Follow our neck pain exercise handout: one printable page of strength exercises with simple pain rules.
  • Set a reminder to move your neck and shoulders every 30–60 minutes at a desk.
  • Bring your screen up to eye level and your phone up to your face, not your face down to it.
  • Sleep on your back or side, not your front, with one low, firm pillow.
  • Stay generally active: walking, swimming and lifting all count.

See your GP if

  • The pain hasn’t eased after a few weeks, or painkillers aren’t helping
  • Pain, pins and needles or numbness spreading down an arm
  • You’re worried, or it’s affecting sleep or work
  • Neck pain after a car accident or fall, even if it seemed minor

Get urgent help for neck pain after a significant injury or fall; with fever, a severe headache and a stiff neck, or dislike of bright light (possible meningitis); sudden severe pain on one side with headache, dizziness, slurred speech or vision changes; new weakness or numbness in the arms or legs, clumsy hands, trouble walking or loss of bladder or bowel control; or with chest pain or breathlessness: call 000 (Australia) or your local emergency number. From a mobile, 112 works in most countries.

Common questions

What causes neck pain?

Usually strained muscles or stiff joints: sleeping awkwardly, long hours at a desk or screen, stress, a sudden movement or a minor injury. Wear-and-tear changes and pinched nerves are less common causes. Serious causes are rare.

What are the symptoms?

Ache and stiffness in the neck, sometimes spreading to the shoulders, upper back or head. A pinched nerve causes sharp pain, pins and needles or numbness down one arm.

How is it diagnosed?

By talking and examining your neck, arms and nerves. Most people don’t need scans or blood tests. Your GP checks for warning signs that need more tests.

Should I rest it?

No. Gentle movement helps more than rest, and collars slow recovery. Ease off what really hurts, keep the rest of your day going, and build up strength exercises as the pain settles.
