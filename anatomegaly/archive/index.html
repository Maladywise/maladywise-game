November 2026

  1. #12 · Friday 13 November 2026 Einstein

    1. Name the structure · Ear

      Name the highlighted structure.

      Answer: Round window

      The pressure-release valve. When the stapes pushes the oval window in, the round window bulges out; without it, cochlear fluid couldn’t move at all.

    2. Which nerve?

      Jacobson’s nerve, crossing the promontory of the middle ear, is a branch of which nerve?

      Answer: Glossopharyngeal nerve (IX)

      The tympanic branch of IX. It forms the tympanic plexus and carries the parotid’s secretomotor fibres onwards as the lesser petrosal nerve.

    3. Which nerve?

      Taste from the epiglottis travels in which nerve?

      Answer: Internal laryngeal nerve (vagus)

      Back third of the tongue is IX, front two-thirds is the chorda tympani, and the epiglottis region is the vagus.

    4. Which nerve?

      Which nerve supplies inferior gemellus?

      Answer: Nerve to quadratus femoris

      Gemelli split their loyalties: the superior goes with obturator internus, the inferior with quadratus femoris.

    5. Bone trivia

      Which carpal bone is the last to appear on X-ray in childhood?

      Answer: Pisiform

      The capitate shows up first, in early infancy. The pisiform, a pea-sized sesamoid in the flexor carpi ulnaris tendon, waits until around 9 to 12 years.

  2. #11 · Thursday 12 November 2026 Medium

    1. Name the structure · Brain

      Name the highlighted lobe.

      Answer: Temporal lobe

      Hearing, memory and understanding language. ‘Temporal’ may come from tempus, time, because grey hairs often show first at the temples.

    2. Name the structure · Eye

      Name the highlighted structure.

      Answer: Optic nerve

      Over a million fibres leave the eye here, creating a blind spot with no photoreceptors. Your brain quietly fills in the gap.

    3. What’s this bone? · Skeleton

      Name the highlighted bone.

      Answer: Sternum

      The breastbone, where you push for CPR: lower half, 100 to 120 times a minute. Its tip, the xiphoid, may not fully turn to bone until middle age.

    4. Which nerve? · Hand, palm side

      Which nerve gives feeling to the highlighted palm-side area?

      Answer: Median nerve

      Thumb, index, middle and half the ring finger on the palm side. Squash it in the carpal tunnel and this zone tingles, often at night.

    5. Which nerve?

      Which cranial nerve moves the eye outwards, via lateral rectus?

      Answer: Abducens nerve (VI)

      Abducens abducts. Its long path along the base of the skull means raised brain pressure can stretch it and cause double vision.

  3. #10 · Wednesday 11 November 2026 Medium

    1. What’s this bone? · Hand bones

      Name the highlighted bone.

      Answer: First metacarpal

      The thumb’s metacarpal is short, chunky and very mobile on the trapezium. That saddle joint gives you an opposable thumb.

    2. Name the structure · Knee

      Name the highlighted structure.

      Answer: Medial meniscus

      A C-shaped cartilage cushion on the inner side. It’s anchored to the joint capsule and MCL, so it moves less and tears more often than the lateral one.

    3. Name the structure · Heart

      Name the highlighted vessel.

      Answer: Pulmonary trunk

      Carries blood from the right ventricle to the lungs. An artery carrying low-oxygen blood: the classic exam trap.

    4. Name the structure · Spine

      Name the highlighted region.

      Answer: Lumbar spine

      Five chunky vertebrae carrying most of your upper body weight. No ribs attach here, which is how you tell them from thoracic ones.

    5. Which nerve?

      Which nerve supplies the quadriceps?

      Answer: Femoral nerve

      Roots L2 to L4. Tap the patellar tendon and you’re testing it: the knee jerk is mostly L3 and L4.

  4. #9 · Tuesday 10 November 2026 Easy

    1. What’s this bone? · Skeleton

      Name the highlighted bone.

      Answer: Tibia

      The shin bone and main weight-bearer of the lower leg. Its front edge sits right under the skin, which is why coffee tables hurt so much.

    2. Name the structure · Eye

      Name the highlighted layer.

      Answer: Retina

      The light-sensing film at the back. It’s wired backwards: light passes through layers of nerve cells before it reaches the photoreceptors.

    3. Name the structure · Lungs

      Name the highlighted muscle.

      Answer: Diaphragm

      Your main breathing muscle. Hiccups are it spasming, followed by the vocal cords snapping shut. That snap is the ‘hic’.

    4. Name the structure · Abdomen

      Name the highlighted organ.

      Answer: Gallbladder

      Stores and concentrates bile from the liver, then squeezes it out after a fatty meal. You can live perfectly well without one.

    5. Name the structure · Ear

      Name the highlighted structure.

      Answer: Cochlea

      Snail-shaped (that’s literally the Greek). Unrolled it’s about 3 cm long. High pitches register at the base, low pitches at the tip.

  5. #8 · Monday 9 November 2026 Easy

    1. What’s this bone? · Skeleton

      Name the highlighted bone.

      Answer: Humerus

      The upper arm bone. It sounds like ‘humorous’, which is one theory for the name ‘funny bone’, though the zing is really the ulnar nerve.

    2. Name the structure · Abdomen

      Name the highlighted organ.

      Answer: Stomach

      A J-shaped muscle bag full of acid around pH 1 to 3. Its surface lining renews every few days so it doesn’t digest itself.

    3. Name the structure · Heart

      Name the highlighted chamber.

      Answer: Right atrium

      The receiving bay for blood coming back from the body. It houses the heart’s own pacemaker, the sinoatrial node.

    4. Name the structure · Brain

      Name the highlighted lobe.

      Answer: Occipital lobe

      Vision lives at the back of your head, about as far from your eyes as it gets. A hard knock there can make you literally ‘see stars’.

    5. Which nerve?

      Which cranial nerve lets you smell?

      Answer: Olfactory nerve (I)

      Cranial nerve number one. Smell skips the thalamus relay that other senses use on the way in, one reason a smell can drag up a memory so fast.

  6. #7 · Sunday 8 November 2026 Genius

    1. What’s this bone? · Hand bones

      Name the highlighted wrist bone.

      Answer: Trapezoid

      The trapezium sits by the thumb; the trapezoid is its little neighbour. Memory trick: trapeziUM by the thUMb. The trapezoid is one of the least often broken carpals.

    2. Name the structure · Brain

      Name the highlighted area of the left hemisphere.

      Answer: Broca’s area

      In the left frontal lobe, for producing speech. Paul Broca’s famous patient was nicknamed ‘Tan’ because it was almost the only word he could say.

    3. Name the structure · Lungs

      Name the highlighted part of the left lung.

      Answer: Lingula

      The left lung has no middle lobe; this tongue-shaped part of the upper lobe does the job. ‘Lingula’ is Latin for little tongue.

    4. Which nerve?

      Numb skin over the angle of the jaw. Which nerve normally supplies it?

      Answer: Great auricular nerve

      The trigeminal skips the angle of the jaw; it belongs to the great auricular (C2–C3). Numbness that includes the jaw angle points away from the trigeminal.

    5. Which nerve?

      Tensor veli palatini, the palate muscle that helps pop your ears, is supplied by which nerve?

      Answer: Mandibular nerve (V3)

      The rebel of the palate: every other palate muscle is vagus. It helps open the Eustachian tube when you yawn or swallow on a plane.

  7. #6 · Saturday 7 November 2026 Hard

    1. What’s this bone? · Hand bones

      Name the highlighted wrist bone.

      Answer: Lunate

      Moon-shaped, in the middle of the first row. It’s the carpal bone that dislocates most often, and it can quietly lose its blood supply in Kienböck disease.

    2. Name the structure · Eye

      Name the highlighted structure.

      Answer: Ciliary body

      A ring of muscle that changes the lens shape so you can focus up close. It also makes aqueous humour, the fluid that keeps the front of your eye inflated.

    3. What’s this bone? · Skeleton

      Name the highlighted bone.

      Answer: Fibula

      The skinny outer leg bone. It carries little weight, so surgeons can borrow a length of it, with its blood supply, to rebuild a jaw.

    4. Name the structure · Abdomen

      Name the highlighted organ, mostly hidden behind the stomach.

      Answer: Pancreas

      Its head nestles in the C-curve of the duodenum. It makes insulin plus over a litre of digestive juice a day, which is why pancreatitis is so nasty.

    5. Which nerve?

      Foot drop after a tight plaster cast just below the knee. Which nerve?

      Answer: Common fibular (peroneal) nerve

      It wraps around the neck of the fibula just under the skin, so casts, crossed legs and long squats can squash it.

  8. #5 · Friday 6 November 2026 Hard

    1. What’s this bone? · Hand bones

      Name the highlighted wrist bone.

      Answer: Hamate

      ‘Hamate’ means hooked. Its hook can snap in golfers, cricketers and batters when the grip handle jams into the palm on a mistimed swing.

    2. Name the structure · Ear

      Name the highlighted ossicle.

      Answer: Incus

      The anvil, middle of the three ear bones. The ossicles are already close to adult size at birth, so they barely grow while the rest of you does.

    3. Name the structure · Knee

      Name the highlighted ligament.

      Answer: Anterior cruciate ligament

      Front of the cross, hence ‘anterior’. Pop, swelling within hours, wobbly knee. Women footballers tear theirs two to eight times more often than men.

    4. Name the structure · Heart

      Name the highlighted valve.

      Answer: Mitral valve

      Two leaflets between the left atrium and ventricle. It’s named because someone thought it looked like a bishop’s mitre. Yes, really.

    5. Which nerve?

      Winged shoulder blade when pushing against a wall. Which nerve?

      Answer: Long thoracic nerve

      It supplies serratus anterior, which pins the shoulder blade to the ribs. It runs long and exposed down the chest wall, so heavy backpacks and surgery can injure it.

  9. #4 · Thursday 5 November 2026 Medium

    1. What’s this bone? · Hand bones

      Name the highlighted wrist bone.

      Answer: Scaphoid

      Fall on an outstretched hand, tender snuffbox: think scaphoid. Its blood supply enters from the far end, so a break across the middle can starve the near piece.

    2. Name the structure · Brain

      Name the highlighted structure.

      Answer: Cerebellum

      Latin for ‘little brain’: about a tenth of brain volume, yet roughly four in five of its neurons. Neuroscientist Suzana Herculano-Houzel counted them by turning brains into soup.

    3. Name the structure · Abdomen

      Name the highlighted structure.

      Answer: Appendix

      Maybe not useless after all: it may act as a safe house for good gut bacteria. Its tip can point in lots of directions, which is why appendicitis pain can wander.

    4. Name the structure · Eye

      Name the highlighted layer.

      Answer: Cornea

      The clear front window, doing about two-thirds of the eye’s focusing. It has no blood vessels and takes much of its oxygen straight from the air.

    5. Which nerve?

      Which nerve supplies biceps?

      Answer: Musculocutaneous nerve

      It punches through coracobrachialis, powers elbow flexion, then ends as a skin nerve on the outer forearm. Hence ‘musculo’ plus ‘cutaneous’.

  10. #3 · Wednesday 4 November 2026 Medium

    1. What’s this bone? · Skeleton

      Name the highlighted forearm bone.

      Answer: Radius

      The thumb-side forearm bone. It rolls around the ulna when you turn a doorknob, and it’s the usual wrist break after a fall on an outstretched hand.

    2. Name the structure · Heart

      Name the highlighted vessel.

      Answer: Aorta

      The body’s main pipe, about as wide as your thumb. It rises out of the left ventricle, arches over the top and dives down behind the heart.

    3. Which nerve? · Hand, back

      Which nerve gives feeling to the highlighted patch on the back of the hand?

      Answer: Radial nerve

      The superficial branch of the radial nerve owns the back of the first web space. It’s the go-to spot for testing radial sensation after an upper arm fracture.

    4. Name the structure · Ear

      Name the highlighted structure.

      Answer: Tympanic membrane

      The eardrum: about a tenth of a millimetre thick, thinner than paper. Small holes often heal on their own within a few weeks.

    5. Which nerve?

      After a shoulder dislocation: weak deltoid and a numb patch on the outer shoulder. Which nerve?

      Answer: Axillary nerve

      It wraps around the neck of the humerus, so dislocations stretch it. The numb ‘regimental badge’ patch, where a sleeve badge would sit, gives it away.

  11. #2 · Tuesday 3 November 2026 Easy

    1. Name the structure · Abdomen

      Name the highlighted organ.

      Answer: Liver

      Your biggest internal organ and a full-time chemistry lab. It can regrow from about a quarter of its tissue, which is why living donors can give a slice.

    2. Name the structure · Eye

      Name the highlighted structure.

      Answer: Lens

      Clear and flexible, it fine-tunes focus. It keeps adding layers all your life, like an onion, and slowly stiffens. Hello, reading glasses at 45.

    3. What’s this bone? · Skeleton

      Name the highlighted bone.

      Answer: Patella

      The kneecap: the body’s biggest sesamoid bone, meaning it grows inside a tendon. Babies have a cartilage version that only turns to bone at around three to six.

    4. Name the structure · Lungs

      Name the highlighted tube.

      Answer: Trachea

      The windpipe, held open by C-shaped rings of cartilage. The open back of each ‘C’ lets the oesophagus bulge forward when you swallow a big mouthful.

    5. Which nerve?

      You knock your ‘funny bone’. Which nerve just zapped you?

      Answer: Ulnar nerve

      Not funny, not a bone. The ulnar nerve sits just under the skin behind the elbow, so a knock fires off tingles into your ring and little fingers.

  12. #1 · Monday 2 November 2026 Easy

    1. What’s this bone? · Skeleton

      Name the highlighted bone.

      Answer: Femur

      The thigh bone: your longest and strongest. It’s roughly a quarter of your height, which is how forensic teams estimate stature from a single bone.

    2. Name the structure · Heart

      Name the highlighted chamber.

      Answer: Left ventricle

      The muscly one that pumps blood to the whole body. Diagrams face you, so the patient’s left is on your right. Everyone falls for that once.

    3. Name the structure · Brain

      Name the highlighted lobe.

      Answer: Frontal lobe

      Planning, personality and impulse control. In 1848 an iron rod shot through Phineas Gage’s frontal lobe; he survived, but friends said he was ‘no longer Gage’.

    4. Which nerve?

      Which nerve supplies the diaphragm, your main breathing muscle?

      Answer: Phrenic nerve

      ‘C3, 4, 5 keeps the diaphragm alive.’ It starts in the neck because the diaphragm began life up there in the embryo, then migrated down, dragging its nerve along.

    5. What’s this bone? · Skeleton

      Name the highlighted bone.

      Answer: Clavicle

      The collarbone. It’s the first bone in the body to start hardening, around week five or six of pregnancy, and one of the most commonly broken bones in kids.
