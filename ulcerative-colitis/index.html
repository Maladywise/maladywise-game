Ulcerative Colitis

Ulcerative colitis is an inflammatory bowel disease of the large bowel lining, starting at the rectum and spreading upwards. It causes bloody diarrhoea and urgency in flares. Most people do well with medicines that keep it quiet, and surgery can cure it if needed.

Symptoms

  • Diarrhoea with blood or mucus, urgency, and needing to go at night
  • Cramping tummy pain, tiredness, low iron
  • Joint pain, red eyes, skin rashes or liver problems (PSC) in some

Go to hospital the same day with 6 or more bloody stools a day plus fever, a racing heart, feeling very unwell, or a swollen tender tummy. This can be acute severe colitis, which needs urgent treatment.

Diagnosis

  • Bloods, faecal calprotectin and stool tests for infection
  • Colonoscopy with biopsies confirms it and shows how much bowel is involved

Treatment

Usually led by a gastroenterologist, with your GP.

Long term

  • Bowel cancer risk rises after about 8 years of widespread colitis: regular surveillance colonoscopies
  • Vaccines and bone health checks on immune-lowering treatment
  • Smoking seems to protect against UC, oddly, but the harms far outweigh it

Common questions

What is ulcerative colitis?

An inflammatory bowel disease affecting the lining of the large bowel, continuously from the rectum upwards. It causes bloody diarrhoea, urgency and cramps in flares.

What is the first treatment for ulcerative colitis?

For mild to moderate disease, mesalazine tablets plus suppositories or enemas. Steroids are used short term for flares, and other medicines for more severe disease.

Can ulcerative colitis be cured?

Surgery to remove the large bowel cures the colitis. Most people instead manage well long term with medicines.
