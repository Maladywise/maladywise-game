Ear Physiology Quiz

10 questions on how the ear works: hearing, the cochlea and balance. Easy to hard. Tap an answer to see if you’re right.

  1. Question 1 of 10

    Which tiny bone sits against the oval window of the cochlea?

    1. Hyoid
    2. Incus
    3. Malleus
    4. Stapes
    Show answer

    Answer: Stapes. The stapes (stirrup) is the smallest bone in the body, about 3 mm long.

  2. Question 2 of 10

    What turns sound vibrations into nerve signals?

    1. The ossicles
    2. Hair cells in the cochlea
    3. The Eustachian tube
    4. The eardrum
    Show answer

    Answer: Hair cells in the cochlea. Inner hair cells in the organ of Corti bend with the fluid waves and release neurotransmitter.

  3. Question 3 of 10

    What does the Eustachian tube do?

    1. Equalises pressure in the middle ear
    2. Carries sound to the brain
    3. Drains wax from the ear canal
    4. Senses head rotation
    Show answer

    Answer: Equalises pressure in the middle ear. It links the middle ear to the back of the nose. Swallowing or yawning opens it on a plane.

  4. Question 4 of 10

    High-pitched sounds are detected near the…

    1. Apex of the cochlea
    2. Semicircular canals
    3. Base of the cochlea
    4. Eardrum
    Show answer

    Answer: Base of the cochlea. The stiff, narrow base resonates with high pitches; the floppy apex with low. Noise and age damage the base first.

  5. Question 5 of 10

    The semicircular canals sense…

    1. Head rotation
    2. Pressure in the middle ear
    3. Gravity and straight-line movement
    4. Sound loudness
    Show answer

    Answer: Head rotation. Three canals at right angles. The utricle and saccule sense gravity and linear acceleration.

  6. Question 6 of 10

    Loose crystals in the semicircular canals cause…

    1. Ménière’s disease
    2. Tinnitus only
    3. Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo
    4. Otosclerosis
    Show answer

    Answer: Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo. Otoconia break free from the utricle and drift into a canal. Brief spins with head turns; the Epley manoeuvre often fixes it.

  7. Question 7 of 10

    Sound is measured in decibels. A 10 dB increase means the sound intensity is…

    1. Twice as loud to the ear
    2. 10 times greater
    3. 2 times greater
    4. 100 times greater
    Show answer

    Answer: 10 times greater. Decibels are logarithmic. A 10 dB rise is ten times the intensity and sounds roughly twice as loud.

  8. Question 8 of 10

    What is the normal range of human hearing?

    1. About 2 to 2,000 Hz
    2. About 50 to 5,000 Hz
    3. About 200 to 200,000 Hz
    4. About 20 to 20,000 Hz
    Show answer

    Answer: About 20 to 20,000 Hz. The top end falls with age. Many adults over 50 can’t hear above about 12,000 Hz.

  9. Question 9 of 10

    The small muscle that dampens loud sounds by pulling on the stapes is the…

    1. Stapedius
    2. Tensor tympani
    3. Temporalis
    4. Masseter
    Show answer

    Answer: Stapedius. Supplied by the facial nerve. Facial palsy can make sounds seem too loud (hyperacusis).

  10. Question 10 of 10

    Earwax is mainly made by glands in the…

    1. Eardrum
    2. Inner ear
    3. Middle ear
    4. Outer third of the ear canal
    Show answer

    Answer: Outer third of the ear canal. Ceruminous and sebaceous glands. Wax naturally migrates outwards, so cotton buds mostly push it back in.

Common questions

How does the ear turn sound into hearing?

The eardrum and three tiny bones amplify sound into the fluid of the cochlea, where hair cells convert it to nerve signals sent to the brain.

Why do I get dizzy when I spin?

Fluid in the semicircular canals keeps moving after you stop, so your brain thinks you’re still turning.
