Teeth Physiology Quiz

10 questions on how teeth work: enamel, decay, fluoride and baby teeth. Easy to hard. Tap an answer to see if you’re right.

  1. Question 1 of 10

    What is the hardest substance in the human body?

    1. Bone
    2. Dentine
    3. Cementum
    4. Enamel
    Show answer

    Answer: Enamel. About 96% mineral (hydroxyapatite). It can’t regrow because the cells that made it are gone once the tooth erupts.

  2. Question 2 of 10

    How many baby (primary) teeth does a child usually have?

    1. 24
    2. 32
    3. 20
    4. 28
    Show answer

    Answer: 20. Usually all in by about age 3. Adults have 32 including wisdom teeth.

  3. Question 3 of 10

    Tooth decay happens when bacteria ferment sugar into…

    1. Fluoride
    2. Acid
    3. Calcium
    4. Alcohol
    Show answer

    Answer: Acid. Streptococcus mutans and friends drop the pH below about 5.5, dissolving enamel.

  4. Question 4 of 10

    How does fluoride protect teeth?

    1. It helps rebuild enamel into a more acid-resistant form
    2. It whitens teeth
    3. It kills all mouth bacteria
    4. It numbs nerves
    Show answer

    Answer: It helps rebuild enamel into a more acid-resistant form. Fluorapatite resists acid better. Community water fluoridation reduces decay at low cost.

  5. Question 5 of 10

    Which layer of the tooth contains nerves and blood vessels?

    1. Cementum
    2. Enamel
    3. Dentine
    4. Pulp
    Show answer

    Answer: Pulp. Decay reaching the pulp causes toothache and may need root canal treatment.

  6. Question 6 of 10

    Sensitive teeth to cold are usually due to exposed…

    1. Enamel rods
    2. Dentine tubules
    3. Wisdom teeth
    4. Gums
    Show answer

    Answer: Dentine tubules. Dentine has tiny fluid-filled tubes; cold moves the fluid and stimulates nerves.

  7. Question 7 of 10

    Gum disease (periodontitis) is linked with which condition?

    1. Diabetes
    2. Asthma
    3. Short-sightedness
    4. Gout
    Show answer

    Answer: Diabetes. The link runs both ways: diabetes worsens gum disease and gum disease makes sugars harder to control.

  8. Question 8 of 10

    When do the first adult molars usually come through?

    1. About age 12
    2. About age 18
    3. About age 6
    4. About age 2
    Show answer

    Answer: About age 6. The ‘6-year molars’ often erupt behind the baby teeth without anyone noticing.

  9. Question 9 of 10

    Which tooth is attached to the jaw by a ligament, not fused to bone?

    1. All teeth
    2. Only molars
    3. Only incisors
    4. Only wisdom teeth
    Show answer

    Answer: All teeth. The periodontal ligament lets teeth move slightly. That’s how braces work.

  10. Question 10 of 10

    Which antibiotic, given in childhood, can permanently stain teeth?

    1. Cefalexin
    2. Amoxicillin
    3. Tetracycline (e.g. doxycycline at high doses)
    4. Penicillin
    Show answer

    Answer: Tetracycline (e.g. doxycycline at high doses). Tetracyclines bind calcium in developing teeth. Short courses of doxycycline are now thought to be low risk.

Common questions

Can enamel grow back?

No. Enamel can remineralise small weak spots with fluoride and saliva, but lost enamel doesn’t regrow.

Why do baby teeth matter if they fall out?

They hold space for adult teeth, help speech and chewing, and decay in them can spread and cause pain or infection.
