Nose Physiology Quiz

10 questions on how the nose works: smell, airflow, sinuses and mucus. Easy to hard. Tap an answer to see if you’re right.

  1. Question 1 of 10

    Smell receptors sit in the…

    1. Nasal septum
    2. Olfactory epithelium in the roof of the nose
    3. Back of the tongue
    4. Maxillary sinus
    Show answer

    Answer: Olfactory epithelium in the roof of the nose. A small patch high in the nose. Its nerve fibres pass through the cribriform plate to the olfactory bulb.

  2. Question 2 of 10

    Which cranial nerve carries smell?

    1. Olfactory nerve (CN I)
    2. Vagus nerve (CN X)
    3. Trigeminal nerve (CN V)
    4. Facial nerve (CN VII)
    Show answer

    Answer: Olfactory nerve (CN I). The only sense that doesn’t relay through the thalamus first.

  3. Question 3 of 10

    Most of what we call ‘flavour’ is actually…

    1. Temperature
    2. Touch
    3. Smell
    4. Taste
    Show answer

    Answer: Smell. Retronasal smell from the back of the mouth. That’s why food tastes bland with a blocked nose.

  4. Question 4 of 10

    What do the turbinates (conchae) mainly do?

    1. Drain the sinuses
    2. Detect smell
    3. Make antibodies
    4. Warm and humidify air
    Show answer

    Answer: Warm and humidify air. Bony scrolls covered in blood-rich lining. They swell and shrink in turns: the nasal cycle.

  5. Question 5 of 10

    Mucus is moved towards the throat by…

    1. Muscle in the nose
    2. Sneezing only
    3. Cilia
    4. Gravity alone
    Show answer

    Answer: Cilia. Tiny hairs beat about 1,000 times a minute, clearing mucus every 10 to 20 minutes.

  6. Question 6 of 10

    Which sinus is present at birth and is the most common site of sinusitis?

    1. Frontal
    2. Sphenoid
    3. Mastoid
    4. Maxillary
    Show answer

    Answer: Maxillary. The maxillary sinuses drain upwards, which is poor plumbing. The frontal sinuses only develop in childhood.

  7. Question 7 of 10

    A common source of nosebleeds is…

    1. The olfactory bulb
    2. Little’s area (Kiesselbach’s plexus) on the septum
    3. The maxillary sinus
    4. The adenoids
    Show answer

    Answer: Little’s area (Kiesselbach’s plexus) on the septum. Several arteries join at the front of the septum. Pinch the soft part of the nose and lean forward.

  8. Question 8 of 10

    Loss of smell can be an early sign of…

    1. Parkinson’s disease
    2. Diabetes insipidus
    3. Asthma
    4. Gout
    Show answer

    Answer: Parkinson’s disease. Hyposmia can precede the movement symptoms of Parkinson’s by years. COVID-19 is the most famous recent cause.

  9. Question 9 of 10

    About how many types of smell receptor do humans have?

    1. About 40
    2. About 4
    3. About 40,000
    4. About 400
    Show answer

    Answer: About 400. Each odour activates a pattern of receptors, letting us tell apart a huge number of smells.

  10. Question 10 of 10

    Sneezing is triggered mainly through the…

    1. Hypoglossal nerve
    2. Trigeminal nerve
    3. Olfactory nerve
    4. Optic nerve
    Show answer

    Answer: Trigeminal nerve. Irritants stimulate trigeminal endings. Bright light can trigger it too: the photic sneeze reflex.

Common questions

Why can’t I taste food when I have a cold?

Most flavour is smell. A blocked nose stops odours reaching the smell receptors, so food tastes flat.

What do the sinuses do?

They lighten the skull, add resonance to the voice and help humidify air. Nobody is completely sure of their main purpose.
