Lung Physiology Quiz

10 questions on how the lung works: breathing, gas exchange and oxygen carriage. Easy to hard. Tap an answer to see if you’re right.

  1. Question 1 of 10

    The main muscle of breathing is the…

    1. Pectoralis major
    2. Abdominals
    3. Intercostals only
    4. Diaphragm
    Show answer

    Answer: Diaphragm. It does most of the work at rest. Supplied by the phrenic nerve (C3, 4, 5 keeps the diaphragm alive).

  2. Question 2 of 10

    What normally drives the urge to breathe?

    1. Falling oxygen
    2. Rising carbon dioxide
    3. Falling carbon dioxide
    4. Rising oxygen
    Show answer

    Answer: Rising carbon dioxide. Chemoreceptors sense CO₂ via blood pH. Low oxygen becomes the main driver only when it’s quite low.

  3. Question 3 of 10

    Surfactant in the lungs…

    1. Lowers surface tension so alveoli don’t collapse
    2. Kills bacteria
    3. Carries oxygen
    4. Makes mucus
    Show answer

    Answer: Lowers surface tension so alveoli don’t collapse. Premature babies can lack it; steroids before birth help their lungs mature.

  4. Question 4 of 10

    Most oxygen in the blood is carried…

    1. As bicarbonate
    2. In white cells
    3. Bound to haemoglobin
    4. Dissolved in plasma
    Show answer

    Answer: Bound to haemoglobin. About 98%. Each haemoglobin carries up to four oxygen molecules.

  5. Question 5 of 10

    Most carbon dioxide in the blood is carried as…

    1. Bound to haemoglobin
    2. Dissolved gas
    3. Carbon monoxide
    4. Bicarbonate
    Show answer

    Answer: Bicarbonate. Carbonic anhydrase in red cells turns CO₂ and water into carbonic acid, then bicarbonate.

  6. Question 6 of 10

    Carbon monoxide is dangerous because it…

    1. Blocks the airways
    2. Binds haemoglobin over 200 times more tightly than oxygen
    3. Destroys surfactant
    4. Stops the diaphragm
    Show answer

    Answer: Binds haemoglobin over 200 times more tightly than oxygen. A pulse oximeter can read falsely normal. High-flow oxygen speeds its removal.

  7. Question 7 of 10

    Tidal volume in a resting adult is about…

    1. 50 mL
    2. 5 litres
    3. 500 mL
    4. 2 litres
    Show answer

    Answer: 500 mL. Total lung capacity is about 6 litres.

  8. Question 8 of 10

    The air left in the lungs after breathing out as hard as possible is the…

    1. Residual volume
    2. Dead space
    3. Tidal volume
    4. Vital capacity
    Show answer

    Answer: Residual volume. About 1.2 litres. It keeps the alveoli from collapsing.

  9. Question 9 of 10

    A shift of the oxygen dissociation curve to the right means haemoglobin…

    1. Holds oxygen more tightly
    2. Carries more carbon monoxide
    3. Can’t carry oxygen
    4. Gives up oxygen more easily
    Show answer

    Answer: Gives up oxygen more easily. Caused by more CO₂, acid, heat and 2,3-DPG: just what working muscles need.

  10. Question 10 of 10

    Breathing faster and deeper (hyperventilating) makes blood…

    1. Alkaline, because CO₂ is blown off
    2. Acidic
    3. Unchanged
    4. Low in oxygen
    Show answer

    Answer: Alkaline, because CO₂ is blown off. Low CO₂ causes tingling fingers and lips. It’s respiratory alkalosis.

Common questions

How does oxygen get into the blood?

It diffuses across the thin walls of the alveoli into the capillaries, where it binds haemoglobin in red blood cells.

What controls breathing?

The brainstem sets the rhythm, and it mainly responds to carbon dioxide levels in the blood.
