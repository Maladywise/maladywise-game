Mouth Physiology Quiz

10 questions on how the mouth works: taste, saliva, swallowing and the tongue. Easy to hard. Tap an answer to see if you’re right.

  1. Question 1 of 10

    Which is one of the five basic tastes?

    1. Cool
    2. Spicy
    3. Metallic
    4. Umami
    Show answer

    Answer: Umami. Sweet, salty, sour, bitter and umami (savoury, from glutamate). Chilli heat is pain, not taste.

  2. Question 2 of 10

    About how much saliva does an adult make a day?

    1. 0.5 to 1.5 litres
    2. 3 to 4 litres
    3. 50 to 100 mL
    4. 10 litres
    Show answer

    Answer: 0.5 to 1.5 litres. Mostly from the parotid, submandibular and sublingual glands.

  3. Question 3 of 10

    Which enzyme in saliva starts digesting starch?

    1. Lipase
    2. Amylase
    3. Pepsin
    4. Trypsin
    Show answer

    Answer: Amylase. Salivary amylase breaks starch into sugars. Chew bread long enough and it tastes sweet.

  4. Question 4 of 10

    Which nerve moves the tongue?

    1. Trigeminal nerve (CN V)
    2. Glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX)
    3. Hypoglossal nerve (CN XII)
    4. Facial nerve (CN VII)
    Show answer

    Answer: Hypoglossal nerve (CN XII). A damaged hypoglossal nerve makes the tongue point towards the weak side.

  5. Question 5 of 10

    Taste from the front two-thirds of the tongue is carried by the…

    1. Glossopharyngeal nerve
    2. Vagus nerve
    3. Hypoglossal nerve
    4. Facial nerve (chorda tympani)
    Show answer

    Answer: Facial nerve (chorda tympani). The back third uses the glossopharyngeal nerve. Bell’s palsy can dull taste on one side.

  6. Question 6 of 10

    What stops food going into the airway when you swallow?

    1. The tongue blocks the nose
    2. The larynx lifts and the epiglottis folds over it
    3. The uvula closes the larynx
    4. Breathing speeds up
    Show answer

    Answer: The larynx lifts and the epiglottis folds over it. Swallowing briefly stops breathing too. Strokes can disrupt this, causing aspiration.

  7. Question 7 of 10

    Which salivary gland is the largest?

    1. Parotid
    2. Submandibular
    3. Sublingual
    4. Thyroid
    Show answer

    Answer: Parotid. Sits in front of the ear. Mumps famously swells it.

  8. Question 8 of 10

    Dry mouth (xerostomia) is most often caused by…

    1. Too much water
    2. Brushing too often
    3. Medicines
    4. Chewing gum
    Show answer

    Answer: Medicines. Antidepressants, antihistamines, blood pressure tablets and many more. Radiotherapy and Sjögren’s syndrome are other causes.

  9. Question 9 of 10

    The ‘tongue map’ (sweet at the tip, bitter at the back) is…

    1. A myth: all tastes are sensed across the tongue
    2. True for salt only
    3. Only true in children
    4. Correct
    Show answer

    Answer: A myth: all tastes are sensed across the tongue. It came from a mistranslated 1901 German paper. Taste buds everywhere detect all five tastes.

  10. Question 10 of 10

    How often are taste receptor cells replaced?

    1. Once a year
    2. Every hour
    3. About every 10 days to 2 weeks
    4. Never
    Show answer

    Answer: About every 10 days to 2 weeks. Fast turnover is why burned tongues heal quickly, and why chemotherapy can change taste.

Common questions

What are the five basic tastes?

Sweet, salty, sour, bitter and umami. Fat taste is being studied as a possible sixth.

What does saliva do?

Saliva moistens food, starts starch digestion, protects teeth with calcium, phosphate and bicarbonate, and washes away bacteria.
