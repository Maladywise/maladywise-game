Eye Physiology Quiz

10 questions on how the eye works: focusing, rods and cones, pupils and tears. Easy to hard. Tap an answer to see if you’re right.

  1. Question 1 of 10

    Which part of the eye does most of the focusing (bending light)?

    1. Vitreous
    2. Cornea
    3. Lens
    4. Iris
    Show answer

    Answer: Cornea. The curved cornea does about two-thirds of the focusing; the lens fine-tunes it for near and far.

  2. Question 2 of 10

    Which cells in the retina work best in dim light?

    1. Rods
    2. Cones
    3. Müller cells
    4. Ganglion cells
    Show answer

    Answer: Rods. About 120 million rods handle low light and movement; they don’t see colour.

  3. Question 3 of 10

    How many types of cone does a typical human retina have?

    1. Two
    2. Five
    3. Four
    4. Three
    Show answer

    Answer: Three. Short, medium and long wavelength cones: roughly blue, green and red.

  4. Question 4 of 10

    Focusing on something close (accommodation) needs the lens to…

    1. Flatten as the ciliary muscle contracts
    2. Move forwards on the zonules
    3. Become rounder as the ciliary muscle contracts
    4. Become harder
    Show answer

    Answer: Become rounder as the ciliary muscle contracts. Ciliary muscle contraction slackens the zonules so the elastic lens rounds up. The lens stiffens with age: presbyopia.

  5. Question 5 of 10

    Which nerve constricts the pupil?

    1. Oculomotor nerve (parasympathetic)
    2. Trigeminal nerve
    3. Sympathetic chain
    4. Optic nerve
    Show answer

    Answer: Oculomotor nerve (parasympathetic). Parasympathetic fibres in cranial nerve III supply the sphincter pupillae. Sympathetic nerves dilate it.

  6. Question 6 of 10

    Where is vision sharpest?

    1. Peripheral retina
    2. Fovea
    3. Optic disc
    4. Ora serrata
    Show answer

    Answer: Fovea. The fovea is packed with cones and has no blood vessels in front of it.

  7. Question 7 of 10

    Why do we have a blind spot?

    1. Eyelashes shadow the retina
    2. The lens blocks light there
    3. The fovea has no rods
    4. The optic disc has no photoreceptors
    Show answer

    Answer: The optic disc has no photoreceptors. Where the optic nerve leaves the eye there are no rods or cones. The brain fills in the gap.

  8. Question 8 of 10

    Aqueous humour drains mainly through the…

    1. Tear duct
    2. Vitreous into the retina
    3. Trabecular meshwork into Schlemm’s canal
    4. Lens capsule
    Show answer

    Answer: Trabecular meshwork into Schlemm’s canal. Blocked drainage raises eye pressure, the main risk factor for glaucoma.

  9. Question 9 of 10

    Which vitamin is needed to make rhodopsin, the light pigment in rods?

    1. Vitamin A
    2. Vitamin K
    3. Vitamin C
    4. Vitamin D
    Show answer

    Answer: Vitamin A. Vitamin A deficiency causes night blindness and is a leading cause of preventable childhood blindness worldwide.

  10. Question 10 of 10

    Red-green colour blindness is most common in…

    1. Both equally
    2. Males, because the genes are on the X chromosome
    3. People with blue eyes
    4. Females, because of X inactivation
    Show answer

    Answer: Males, because the genes are on the X chromosome. About 8% of men of northern European descent and 0.5% of women have it.

Common questions

What is the difference between rods and cones?

Rods work in dim light and see shades of grey; cones work in bright light and see colour and fine detail.

Why do older people need reading glasses?

The lens stiffens with age, so it can’t round up to focus up close. This is presbyopia, and it starts in the 40s.
