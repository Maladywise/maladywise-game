Brain Physiology Quiz

10 questions on how the brain works: neurons, signals, sleep and the blood–brain barrier. Easy to hard. Tap an answer to see if you’re right.

  1. Question 1 of 10

    About how much of the body’s energy does the brain use at rest?

    1. About 80%
    2. About 50%
    3. About 20%
    4. About 2%
    Show answer

    Answer: About 20%. Despite being about 2% of body weight. It runs mainly on glucose.

  2. Question 2 of 10

    The main excitatory neurotransmitter in the brain is…

    1. GABA
    2. Glutamate
    3. Serotonin
    4. Glycine
    Show answer

    Answer: Glutamate. GABA is the main inhibitory one. Many epilepsy and sleep medicines boost GABA.

  3. Question 3 of 10

    A nerve signal (action potential) starts when sodium…

    1. Leaves the cell
    2. Binds to myelin
    3. Is pumped out by the kidney
    4. Rushes into the cell
    Show answer

    Answer: Rushes into the cell. Voltage-gated sodium channels open, the inside becomes positive, then potassium leaving resets it.

  4. Question 4 of 10

    What does myelin do?

    1. Speeds up nerve signals
    2. Stops pain
    3. Feeds neurons glucose
    4. Makes neurotransmitters
    Show answer

    Answer: Speeds up nerve signals. Signals jump between gaps (nodes of Ranvier). Multiple sclerosis strips myelin in the brain and spinal cord.

  5. Question 5 of 10

    Which part of the brain is key to forming new memories?

    1. Occipital lobe
    2. Hippocampus
    3. Medulla
    4. Cerebellum
    Show answer

    Answer: Hippocampus. Patient H.M. couldn’t form new memories after both hippocampi were removed in 1953.

  6. Question 6 of 10

    Cerebrospinal fluid is mostly made by the…

    1. Spinal cord
    2. Pituitary gland
    3. Choroid plexus
    4. Dura mater
    Show answer

    Answer: Choroid plexus. About 500 mL a day, though only about 150 mL is in the system at once.

  7. Question 7 of 10

    Which brain area controls breathing and heart rate?

    1. Thalamus
    2. Cerebellum
    3. Frontal lobe
    4. Brainstem (medulla)
    Show answer

    Answer: Brainstem (medulla). That’s why brainstem injuries are so dangerous.

  8. Question 8 of 10

    Dreaming mostly happens in which sleep stage?

    1. REM sleep
    2. Deep (N3) sleep
    3. Stage N1
    4. Wakefulness
    Show answer

    Answer: REM sleep. Muscles are mostly paralysed in REM so we don’t act out dreams.

  9. Question 9 of 10

    The blood–brain barrier is formed mainly by…

    1. The dura mater
    2. Tight junctions between capillary cells
    3. The skull
    4. Cerebrospinal fluid
    Show answer

    Answer: Tight junctions between capillary cells. It keeps many drugs and toxins out, which makes treating brain infections and tumours hard.

  10. Question 10 of 10

    The ‘sleep pressure’ chemical that builds up while awake, and that caffeine blocks, is…

    1. Dopamine
    2. Cortisol
    3. Adenosine
    4. Melatonin
    Show answer

    Answer: Adenosine. Caffeine sits on adenosine receptors so you don’t feel the tiredness. Sleep clears the adenosine.

Common questions

How do neurons send signals?

An electrical signal travels along the nerve, then chemicals called neurotransmitters cross a tiny gap (the synapse) to the next cell.

Why do we need sleep?

Sleep consolidates memory, clears waste products from the brain and resets body systems. The exact reasons are still being researched.
