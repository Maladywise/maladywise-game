Kidney Physiology Quiz

10 questions on how the kidney works: filtering, urine, salt, water and hormones. Easy to hard. Tap an answer to see if you’re right.

  1. Question 1 of 10

    The working unit of the kidney is the…

    1. Islet
    2. Alveolus
    3. Nephron
    4. Neuron
    Show answer

    Answer: Nephron. Each kidney has about a million.

  2. Question 2 of 10

    About how much fluid do the kidneys filter each day?

    1. About 1,800 litres
    2. About 180 litres
    3. About 1.5 litres
    4. About 18 litres
    Show answer

    Answer: About 180 litres. Over 99% is reabsorbed, leaving 1 to 2 litres of urine.

  3. Question 3 of 10

    ADH (vasopressin) makes the kidneys…

    1. Keep more water
    2. Lose more salt
    3. Lose more water
    4. Make more urine
    Show answer

    Answer: Keep more water. It adds water channels (aquaporins) to the collecting ducts.

  4. Question 4 of 10

    Aldosterone causes the kidneys to keep…

    1. Glucose
    2. Potassium (and lose sodium)
    3. Calcium only
    4. Sodium (and lose potassium)
    Show answer

    Answer: Sodium (and lose potassium). Part of the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system that holds blood pressure up.

  5. Question 5 of 10

    Which hormone made by the kidneys tells the bone marrow to make red cells?

    1. Erythropoietin (EPO)
    2. Insulin
    3. Calcitriol
    4. Renin
    Show answer

    Answer: Erythropoietin (EPO). Kidney disease causes anaemia partly through low EPO.

  6. Question 6 of 10

    The kidneys activate vitamin D into…

    1. Calcitonin
    2. Calcitriol
    3. Retinol
    4. Cholecalciferol
    Show answer

    Answer: Calcitriol. That’s why kidney failure affects calcium and bones.

  7. Question 7 of 10

    eGFR measures…

    1. Kidney size
    2. Protein in the urine
    3. How well the kidneys filter
    4. How much urine you make
    Show answer

    Answer: How well the kidneys filter. Estimated from creatinine, age and sex. Under 60 for 3 months is chronic kidney disease.

  8. Question 8 of 10

    Glucose appears in urine when blood glucose rises above about…

    1. 20 mmol/L
    2. 50 mmol/L
    3. 4 mmol/L
    4. 10 mmol/L
    Show answer

    Answer: 10 mmol/L. Above the renal threshold the transporters are full. SGLT2 inhibitor medicines cause glucose in urine on purpose.

  9. Question 9 of 10

    Where in the nephron is most sodium and water reabsorbed?

    1. Distal tubule
    2. Proximal tubule
    3. Collecting duct
    4. Loop of Henle
    Show answer

    Answer: Proximal tubule. About two-thirds of the filtered load, plus nearly all glucose and amino acids.

  10. Question 10 of 10

    Which part of the nephron creates the concentration gradient that allows concentrated urine?

    1. Loop of Henle
    2. Glomerulus
    3. Proximal tubule
    4. Bladder
    Show answer

    Answer: Loop of Henle. The countercurrent multiplier. Desert animals have extra-long loops.

Common questions

What do the kidneys do?

Filter blood, remove waste, balance salt, water and acid, control blood pressure, make red-cell hormone (EPO) and activate vitamin D.

What is eGFR?

A blood-test estimate of how well the kidneys filter. Normal is over 90; under 60 for 3 months means chronic kidney disease.
