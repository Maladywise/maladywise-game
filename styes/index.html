Fact sheet

Styes

A sore, red lump on the edge of your eyelid that appeared overnight. Styes are common, rarely serious, and usually clear by themselves within a week or two. Warm compresses and patience are the mainstays.

What it is: a painful lump on or inside the eyelid, usually from a blocked oil gland or eyelash root that has become infected. It may have a yellow head of pus. The eye can be red and watery, but your vision should be normal.

Stye or chalazion? a chalazion is a firm lump in the eyelid that isn’t very painful. It can follow a stye and may take longer to settle.

Who gets them: anyone, but long-term blepharitis (inflamed eyelids) and rosacea make them more likely. They often come back.

The stye ladder

Most styes heal on their own. Warm compresses are standard advice, though there are no good trials of any treatment. A GP can help if one is very painful or won’t settle.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Squeezing or popping itThis can spread the infection. Let it drain on its own.
  • Antibiotic drops or ointment as routineA Cochrane review found no good trials showing they help a typical stye, which usually heals by itself.
  • Pulling out the eyelash yourselfLeave that to a doctor; doing it yourself can spread infection.

What you can do

  • Use a warm compress 3 or 4 times a day.
  • Wash your hands before touching your eyes or contact lenses.
  • Don’t share towels or flannels.
  • Keep eyelids clean if you get blepharitis.
  • If your eyes are often dry and gritty, see our dry eye fact sheet.
  • If your eye is red and sticky without a lump, read about conjunctivitis.

See your GP if

  • The stye is very painful or swollen
  • It hasn’t improved after 1 to 2 weeks
  • It affects your vision
  • Styes keep coming back
  • A firm, painless lump remains for weeks

See a doctor the same day if redness and swelling spread across the eyelid or around the eye, the eye is very painful, you feel unwell or feverish, or your vision changes.

Common questions

How long does a stye last?

Usually 1 to 2 weeks. Most drain and heal on their own.

Should I pop a stye?

No. Squeezing it can spread the infection. Use a warm compress and let it drain naturally, or see a GP if it is very painful.

Do I need antibiotics for a stye?

Usually not. A Cochrane review found no good trials showing antibiotics help a typical stye.

What is the difference between a stye and a chalazion?

A stye is a painful, red lump, often with pus, from an infected gland or lash root. A chalazion is a firmer lump that isn’t very painful and can last longer.

How do I get rid of a stye fast?

Hold a clean, warm (not hot) compress on the closed eyelid for 5 to 10 minutes, 3 to 4 times a day, then gently massage towards the lashes. This helps it drain and usually speeds healing. Don’t squeeze it.

What causes a stye?

A blocked oil gland or eyelash follicle on the eyelid edge that gets infected, usually with staph bacteria from the skin. Blepharitis, rubbing your eyes and old eye makeup make styes more likely.

Are styes contagious?

Not really. Styes don’t usually spread to other people, but the bacteria can spread on hands and towels, so wash your hands after touching the eye and don’t share face cloths.

Can I wear makeup or contact lenses with a stye?

Best to avoid eye makeup and contact lenses until the stye has healed. Throw away mascara or eyeliner you used just before it started, as it can carry bacteria.
