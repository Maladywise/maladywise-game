Heavy Periods

Changing pads every hour or two, doubling up, or planning life around flooding? That’s heavy. About 1 in 4 people with periods have it, and good treatments exist.

Signs: changing a pad or tampon every 1–2 hours, needing two products at once, periods lasting more than 7–8 days, clots bigger than a 50 cent coin, bleeding through to clothes or bedding, taking time off, or feeling tired and breathless.

Why it happens: often no cause is found. Causes include fibroids, polyps, adenomyosis, endometriosis, PCOS, an underactive thyroid, perimenopause or the teen years, bleeding disorders such as von Willebrand disease, and blood thinners. Rarely, womb cancer.

Tests: a period diary, a blood count and iron levels, and depending on your story, thyroid tests, an STI test and a pelvic ultrasound.

The heavy period ladder

Of the first-line options, the hormonal IUD cuts bleeding the most; tranexamic acid is the strongest non-hormonal tablet. Check your iron either way.

  1. Step 1 · Self-carePeriod diary and iron checkTrack days, products used and flooding. Ask for a blood count and iron levels.
    Works
    Low iron is common with heavy periods and is fixable with tablets or an infusion
    Cost
    Free diary; blood tests usually Medicare-covered
    How long
    A few cycles
    Watch for
    Tiredness, dizziness or breathlessness can mean anaemia. See our iron deficiency sheet.

    Your period app finally earns its keep.

  2. Step 2 · PrescriptionTranexamic acidNon-hormonal tablets taken only on heavy days.
    Works
    Cuts blood loss by about 40–50%
    Cost
    Prescription; usually $25 or less on the PBS
    How long
    Only during heavy days
    Watch for
    Not if you’ve had a blood clot. Trials were too small to rule out a small clot risk.

    Works on the bleeding, leaves your hormones alone.

  3. Step 3 · PharmacyAnti-inflammatories (naproxen, mefenamic acid)Taken during the period; also ease period pain.
    Works
    Smaller effect: less than tranexamic acid or the hormonal IUD, but a bonus for pain
    Cost
    About $5–15
    How long
    During the period
    Watch for
    Stomach, kidney and asthma cautions.

    A two-for-one: less pain, slightly less blood.

  4. Step 4 · PrescriptionHormonal IUD (levonorgestrel)A small device in the womb that releases a progestogen. Also highly effective contraception.
    Works
    The biggest drop in bleeding of the first-line treatments (about 100 mL less a cycle)
    Cost
    PBS-listed device ($25 or less) plus an insertion fee, which varies
    How long
    Lasts years
    Watch for
    Irregular spotting for the first few months is common.

    Fit once, forget it for years.

  5. Step 5 · PrescriptionCombined pill or progestogen tabletsHormone tablets that lighten periods and make them predictable.
    Works
    Reduce bleeding; long-cycle progestogens cut about 77 mL a cycle in trials (low certainty)
    Cost
    Many under the $25 PBS co-payment; some brands are private
    How long
    Ongoing
    Watch for
    The combined pill isn’t safe for everyone, such as some people with clot or migraine risks.

    A familiar option that does double duty as contraception.

  6. Step 6 · SpecialistGynaecologist: ablation, fibroid surgery or hysterectomyIf tablets and the IUD fail, or fibroids or polyps are the cause.
    Works
    Ablation and hysterectomy reduce bleeding the most; hysterectomy is permanent
    Cost
    Public waiting lists; private fees vary, with Medicare rebates
    How long
    Weeks to recover
    Watch for
    Ablation isn’t suitable if you may want a pregnancy.

    Big guns, for when the small ones miss.

Costs are rough Australian prices in November 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials, not promises.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • “Hormone balancing” supplementsNot properly tested for heavy bleeding. Expensive, and they delay treatments that work.
  • Diet alone to fix low ironWhen heavy periods have drained your iron, supplements or an infusion are usually needed. Spinach can’t keep up.
  • Just putting up with itHeavy periods are common, not compulsory. Effective treatments exist.

What you can do

  • Keep a period diary for 2–3 cycles and bring it to your GP.
  • Ask for an iron check if you’re tired or breathless.
  • Use period-proof underwear or layered products while sorting treatment.
  • Read Healthdirect’s heavy periods guide for Australian services.

See your GP if

  • Heavy periods are affecting your life
  • Bleeding between periods, after sex or after menopause
  • Heavy periods with pain when peeing, pooing or having sex
  • You feel tired, dizzy or breathless
  • Periods are suddenly much heavier than usual

Get urgent care (an urgent care clinic or emergency department, or call 000 (Australia) or your local emergency number. From a mobile, 112 works in most countries.) for heavy bleeding with dizziness, fainting or weakness, or bleeding much heavier than ever before. If you could be pregnant, say so.

Common questions

How do I know if my periods are too heavy?

Signs include changing a pad or tampon every 1 to 2 hours, needing two products together, periods longer than 7 to 8 days, clots bigger than a 50 cent coin, or bleeding through to clothes or bedding.

Which treatment works best for heavy periods?

Of the first-line options, the hormonal IUD reduces bleeding the most. Tranexamic acid cuts blood loss by about 40 to 50%. Anti-inflammatories help less but ease pain.

Does tranexamic acid cause blood clots?

Trials found very few clots, but they were too small to rule out a small increase in risk. It is not recommended for people who have had a blood clot.

Could heavy periods be cancer?

Rarely. Most heavy periods have a harmless cause or none is found. Bleeding between periods, after sex or after menopause needs checking.
