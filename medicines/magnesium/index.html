Drug info

Magnesium supplements

Mineral supplement Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

Hugely popular for cramps and sleep, where the evidence is weak. It does help constipation, migraine prevention and genuine deficiency.

Quick answer: Magnesium treats low magnesium (common with long-term PPIs, diuretics and alcohol), works as a laxative, and may help prevent migraine. Trials don’t show it helps ordinary leg cramps. Check the label: many magnesium products also contain vitamin B6, which can cause nerve damage at high intakes.

How it works

Magnesium is needed for nerve, muscle and heart function. In the gut, unabsorbed magnesium draws water in, which is why it loosens stools.

Used for

  • Low magnesium
  • Constipation
  • Migraine prevention
  • Sleep or anxiety

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • ✅ Low magnesiumReplaces a measured deficiency.
  • ✅ ConstipationMagnesium oxide or citrate works as an osmotic laxative.
  • 🟡 Migraine prevention400 to 600 mg a day reduced attacks in some trials.
  • 🟡 Sleep or anxietySmall, low-quality studies only.
  • ❌ Leg crampsCochrane review: unlikely to help typical night cramps in older adults.
  • ❌ Energy and fatigueNo benefit unless you’re deficient.

Avoid or check first if

  • You have kidney disease
  • You take antibiotics (tetracyclines, quinolones), levothyroxine or bisphosphonates: separate doses by 2 to 4 hours
  • You already take another product containing vitamin B6

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Diarrhoea (especially magnesium oxide)
  • Nausea, stomach cramps

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • High magnesium levels in people with kidney disease (weakness, slow heart)
  • Nerve damage from vitamin B6 in combination products

Get help urgently if

  • Signs of an allergic reaction: swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing (call 000)

Good to know

  • Long-term proton pump inhibitors (like omeprazole) can lower magnesium: ask about a blood test.
  • Check the label for vitamin B6 (pyridoxine); keep total B6 low.
  • If it gives you diarrhoea, try glycinate or a lower dose.

Common questions

Does magnesium help leg cramps?

Probably not for typical night cramps. A Cochrane review found it unlikely to help. Stretching before bed may.

Which form of magnesium is best?

Glycinate and citrate are absorbed better and gentler on the gut. Oxide is cheap and works well as a laxative.
