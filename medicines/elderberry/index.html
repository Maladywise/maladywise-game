Drug info

Elderberry

Herbal medicine Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

Small trials suggest it may shorten flu symptoms, but the evidence is low quality. Raw berries are toxic.

Quick answer: A few small trials suggest elderberry syrup may shorten cold or flu symptoms by a couple of days; larger, better trials are lacking. It doesn’t replace the flu vaccine or antivirals for high-risk people. Raw or unripe berries, leaves and stems can cause vomiting and diarrhoea.

How it works

Elderberry compounds may block flu viruses from entering cells in the lab.

Used for

  • Flu and cold symptoms

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • 🟡 Flu and cold symptomsSmall, low-quality trials suggest shorter illness.
  • ❌ Preventing fluNo good evidence; the vaccine works.

Avoid or check first if

  • You have an autoimmune condition
  • You’re pregnant or breastfeeding (no safety data)

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Common questions

Does elderberry help the flu?

Small trials suggest it may shorten symptoms, but the evidence is weak.

Are elderberries poisonous?

Raw or unripe berries, leaves and stems can make you sick. Cooked and commercial products are fine.
