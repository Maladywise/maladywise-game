Drug info

Diuretics (water tablets)

Water tablet Prescription only (S4)

Medicines that make the kidneys pass more salt and water. Used for blood pressure, heart failure and fluid build-up, and (spironolactone) acne and excess hair.

Quick answer: Diuretics help the kidneys get rid of salt and water. Thiazide-type diuretics (indapamide, hydrochlorothiazide, chlortalidone) are long-acting blood pressure medicines. Loop diuretics (furosemide) are powerful and used for fluid build-up in heart failure. Spironolactone and eplerenone protect the heart in heart failure and help resistant blood pressure.

At a glance

For
High blood pressure, heart failure, fluid swelling, liver ascites; spironolactone for acne and hirsutism
Works
Thiazides lower pressure about 8 to 10 mmHg; spironolactone saves lives in heart failure
Cost
PBS: $25 a script ($7.70 concession), often less
Kicks in
Furosemide within an hour; thiazides over weeks for blood pressure
Dose
Usually in the morning to avoid night-time toilet trips
Watch for
Dehydration, low or high potassium, low sodium, gout

Cheap, old and still among the best-proven medicines in cardiology.

Brand names

  • Furosemide: Lasix, Urex. Hydrochlorothiazide (in many combinations). Indapamide: Natrilix. Chlortalidone: Hygroton. Spironolactone: Aldactone. Eplerenone: Inspra

How it works

Each type blocks salt reabsorption in a different part of the kidney’s filtering tubes. Water follows the salt out. Spironolactone and eplerenone block aldosterone, a hormone that holds on to salt and scars the heart.

Used for

  • High blood pressure (thiazide-type)
  • Heart failure: furosemide for swelling and breathlessness; spironolactone or eplerenone to protect the heart
  • Fluid in the abdomen from liver disease (spironolactone with furosemide)
  • Resistant high blood pressure (spironolactone)
  • Off label: spironolactone for acne and excess hair in women

Side effects

Side effects mostly come from shifts in salts and fluid. Regular blood tests catch most problems.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Very common more than 1 in 10

  • Passing more urine

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Dizziness, especially on standing
  • Low potassium (furosemide, thiazides)
  • High potassium (spironolactone, eplerenone)
  • Low sodium (thiazides, especially in older people)
  • Gout attacks
  • Higher blood sugar
  • Breast tenderness or growth (spironolactone)

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Dehydration and kidney strain
  • Erection problems
  • Sun sensitivity

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Hearing loss with high-dose IV furosemide
  • Pancreatitis
  • Severe skin reactions

Get help urgently if

  • Confusion, severe weakness or muscle cramps (salt imbalance)
  • Fainting or very low blood pressure
  • Passing very little urine

Good to know

  • Take in the morning (furosemide twice a day: morning and early afternoon).
  • Blood tests for sodium, potassium and kidney function after starting and regularly.
  • ‘Sick day rule’: pause if you have vomiting, diarrhoea or can’t drink.
  • Avoid regular anti-inflammatories (NSAIDs), especially with an ACE inhibitor or sartan.
  • Hydrochlorothiazide used long term is linked to a slightly higher risk of non-melanoma skin cancer: use sun protection and check your skin.

Common questions

When is the best time to take a water tablet?

In the morning, so you’re not up at night to pass urine. With twice-daily furosemide, take the second dose by mid-afternoon.

Do diuretics cause gout?

Thiazide and loop diuretics raise uric acid and can trigger gout. Your doctor may change medicine or add gout prevention.

Why is spironolactone used for acne?

It blocks androgen hormones that drive oil production. It’s used off label in women with hormonal acne or excess hair.
