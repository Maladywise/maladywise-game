Drug info

Vaginal oestrogen

Local oestrogen Prescription only (S4); some pharmacist-supplied

Tiny doses of oestrogen applied to the vagina. Treats dryness and painful sex after menopause and cuts recurrent UTIs, with very little reaching the bloodstream.

Quick answer: Low oestrogen after menopause thins vaginal and urethral tissue (genitourinary syndrome of menopause). Vaginal oestrogen restores it. A classic trial (Raz and Stamm, NEJM 1993) found estriol cream cut UTIs from about 5.9 to 0.5 episodes per woman-year. Large observational studies haven’t found higher rates of breast cancer, endometrial cancer or heart disease. No progestogen is needed.

Brand names

  • Vagifem (estradiol pessary), Vagifem Low, Ovestin (estriol cream and pessaries)

How it works

Oestrogen restores the thickness, moisture, acidity and healthy lactobacilli of vaginal and urethral tissue, which protects against infection.

Used for

  • Genitourinary syndrome of menopause: dryness, burning, painful sex
  • Preventing recurrent UTIs after menopause
  • Overactive bladder symptoms after menopause
  • Before vaginal surgery and with pessaries
  • Women with early menopause or on some cancer treatments (with specialist advice)

Side effects

Very well tolerated.

Good to know

  • It’s fine long term; review yearly.
  • No progestogen is needed with standard low doses.
  • Breast cancer survivors: discuss with your oncologist; studies, including a 2024 JAMA Oncology study, haven’t shown higher breast cancer death rates, but those on aromatase inhibitors need specialist advice.
  • Lubricants and moisturisers can be used too.

Common questions

Is vaginal oestrogen safe?

Yes for most women. Very little is absorbed, and large studies haven’t shown higher risks of breast cancer, endometrial cancer, stroke or heart disease.

Does vaginal oestrogen prevent UTIs?

Yes. In a randomised trial, vaginal estriol cut recurrent UTIs in postmenopausal women by about 90%.

Do I need a progestogen with vaginal oestrogen?

Not at standard low doses.
