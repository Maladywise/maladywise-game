Drug info

SGLT2 inhibitors

Diabetes, heart and kidney medicine Prescription only

Tablets that make the kidneys flush extra sugar out in the urine. They started as diabetes medicines, then turned out to protect the heart and kidneys, even in people without diabetes. The rare medicine that over-delivered.

At a glance

For
Type 2 diabetes; heart failure; chronic kidney disease (with or without diabetes)
Works
Heart failure: about a fifth to a quarter fewer hospital stays for heart failure or heart deaths. Kidney disease: risk of kidney decline or death cut by about 30–40% in big trials. Diabetes: a modest drop in blood sugar, often with a little weight loss
Cost
Dapagliflozin and empagliflozin: on the PBS for type 2 diabetes, heart failure and chronic kidney disease (criteria apply). Ertugliflozin: PBS for type 2 diabetes
Kicks in
Blood sugar falls within days; heart and kidney protection builds over months to years
Taken for
Long term
Watch for
Genital thrush, passing more urine, dizziness on standing, urine infections; rarely ketoacidosis

Weeing out sugar sounds like a cheat code. The fine print: keep things clean down there, and pause it on sick days.

Brand names

  • Empagliflozin: Jardiance; with metformin (Jardiamet) or linagliptin
  • Dapagliflozin: Forxiga; with metformin (Xigduo XR) or saxagliptin
  • Ertugliflozin: Steglatro; also in combinations
  • Canagliflozin: Invokana (used overseas; removed from the PBS in 2015)

How it works

SGLT2 is a pump in the kidney that pulls sugar back into the blood. Blocking it lets extra sugar, plus some salt and water, leave in the urine. That lowers blood sugar, eases the fluid load on the heart and lowers the pressure inside the kidney’s filters.

Used for

  • Type 2 diabetes, alongside diet and exercise (all four)
  • Heart failure, whether the heart pump is weak or stiff, with or without diabetes (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin)
  • Chronic kidney disease, with or without diabetes (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin)
  • Not approved for type 1 diabetes in Australia

Compared

Same family, one tablet a day each. The difference is which big trials they have behind them, and so what they’re approved for. Trial results are “bad outcome” rates on the medicine versus a dummy treatment.

Side effects

Most people have no trouble. The usual ones: genital thrush (vaginal thrush or an itchy, sore penis tip), passing more urine, thirst and feeling light-headed on standing, especially in older people or with fluid tablets.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the empagliflozin product information and its big trials. Others in the family are similar. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Genital thrush (about 4 in 100; up to about 1 in 10 women in the long heart trial)
  • Urine infections, mainly in women
  • Passing more urine, including at night
  • Low blood sugar, mainly with insulin or a sulfonylurea
  • Dehydration and dizziness on standing in people 75 and over

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Dehydration or low blood pressure (younger people)
  • Kidney infection or urine infection spreading to the blood
  • Ketoacidosis, which can happen with a normal blood sugar

Frequency not known reported, rate unclear

  • Fournier’s gangrene: a very rare, serious infection of the skin around the genitals and back passage

Get help urgently if

  • Nausea, vomiting, tummy pain, unusual tiredness or fast, deep breathing, even if your blood sugar is normal (possible ketoacidosis)
  • Pain, tenderness, redness or swelling around the genitals or back passage, with fever or feeling unwell
  • Fever, back pain or feeling very unwell with a urine infection
  • Fainting or severe dizziness from dehydration

Good to know

  • Sick day rules. Pause it if you’re vomiting, have diarrhoea or a fever, or can’t eat and drink normally. Restart once you’ve been eating and drinking normally for 24 to 48 hours.
  • Surgery and procedures. Usually stopped for at least 3 days (2 days before, plus the day) for surgery needing a hospital stay or a colonoscopy with bowel prep; just on the day for a short day procedure like a gastroscopy. Your team will confirm.
  • Fasting, crash diets and being dehydrated raise the ketoacidosis risk.
  • Keep the genital area clean and dry. Tell your doctor about itching or discharge.
  • Not used in pregnancy or breastfeeding.

Common questions

Why take a diabetes tablet if I don’t have diabetes?

Dapagliflozin and empagliflozin protect the heart and kidneys whether or not you have diabetes. In big trials of heart failure and kidney disease, people without diabetes benefited too.

What are SGLT2 sick day rules?

Pause the tablet if you’re vomiting, have diarrhoea or a fever, or can’t eat and drink normally. Restart once you’ve been eating and drinking normally for 24 to 48 hours. This lowers the risk of dehydration and ketoacidosis.

Why do SGLT2 inhibitors cause thrush?

They put extra sugar in the urine, which feeds yeast in the genital area. Washing daily and keeping the area dry helps. See your doctor about itching or discharge.

Do I need to stop my SGLT2 inhibitor before surgery?

Usually yes. For surgery needing a hospital stay or a colonoscopy, it’s usually stopped 2 days before and on the day. For a short day procedure, often just on the day. Your surgical team will confirm.
