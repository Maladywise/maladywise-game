Drug info

Amoxicillin and Augmentin

Penicillin antibiotic Prescription only (S4)

The most prescribed antibiotic in Australia, from the penicillin family. Augmentin adds clavulanic acid to beat bacteria that would otherwise destroy amoxicillin.

Quick answer: Amoxicillin treats common bacterial infections such as strep throat, ear and sinus infections and chest infections. Augmentin (amoxicillin with clavulanic acid) covers more bacteria and is used for bites, some skin and more complicated infections, at the cost of more diarrhoea. Neither helps colds, flu or most sore throats, which are viral.

At a glance

Cost

Kicks in

Dose
Commonly 500 mg three times a day or 1 g twice a day; children by weight

Diarrhoea, thrush, rash; allergy

A brilliant antibiotic that’s wasted on colds.

Brand names

  • Amoxicillin: Amoxil, Alphamox, Moxacin. Amoxicillin with clavulanic acid: Augmentin, Augmentin Duo Forte, Clamoxyl, Curam

How it works

Amoxicillin stops bacteria building their cell walls, so they burst. Some bacteria make an enzyme (beta-lactamase) that breaks amoxicillin down; clavulanic acid blocks that enzyme.

Used for

  • Ear infections (when antibiotics are needed), sinusitis and strep throat
  • Community-acquired pneumonia
  • Augmentin: animal and human bites, some skin infections, dental infections, some urine infections
  • Part of H. pylori eradication treatment
  • Not for colds, flu, most coughs or viral sore throats

Side effects

Usually well tolerated. Gut upset is the most common side effect, more so with Augmentin.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Diarrhoea (Augmentin more than amoxicillin)
  • Nausea
  • Thrush (mouth or vaginal)
  • Rash

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Hives and itch

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Anaphylaxis
  • C. difficile diarrhoea
  • Liver problems (Augmentin, usually in older people)
  • Severe skin reactions

Get help urgently if

  • Swelling of face, lips or throat, or trouble breathing: call 000
  • Severe or bloody diarrhoea, even weeks later
  • Yellow skin or eyes
  • Blistering or peeling rash

Good to know

  • Tell your doctor about any penicillin allergy. Many people labelled allergic aren’t: a GP or allergy service can test and remove a wrong label.
  • A flat, non-itchy rash with amoxicillin during glandular fever is common and isn’t a true allergy.
  • Take Augmentin with food to reduce nausea.
  • Finish the course your doctor recommends; shorter courses are now common for many infections.

Common questions

Is amoxicillin a penicillin?

Yes. Avoid it if you’ve had a true penicillin allergy, and tell your doctor or pharmacist.

What’s the difference between amoxicillin and Augmentin?

Augmentin adds clavulanic acid, which protects amoxicillin from bacteria that would break it down. It covers more infections but causes more diarrhoea.

Does amoxicillin help a cold or flu?

No. Colds and flu are viruses, and antibiotics don’t work on them. Unneeded antibiotics cause side effects and resistance.

Can I drink alcohol on amoxicillin?

Moderate drinking doesn’t interact with amoxicillin, though rest and fluids help you recover.
