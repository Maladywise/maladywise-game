Drug info

Clonidine

Alpha-2 agonist Prescription only

An old blood pressure tablet that turns down the body’s “fight or flight” signals. Now used more for ADHD, getting to sleep and tics than for blood pressure.

At a glance

For
ADHD (often for sleep onset or added to a stimulant), tics, hot flushes, withdrawal, blood pressure
Works
ADHD: a modest benefit, smaller than stimulants; may help some children fall asleep faster (evidence limited). Blood pressure: effective but rarely first choice
Cost
$25 a script on the PBS ($7.70 concession)
Kicks in
Drowsiness within an hour or two; ADHD benefit builds over 2–4 weeks
Taken for
Months to years, reviewed regularly
Watch for
Drowsiness, dizziness on standing, dry mouth, constipation, low blood pressure

Stop it suddenly and blood pressure bounces back with interest. Always taper.

Brand names

  • Catapres
  • Several generic brands sold as clonidine

How it works

Clonidine switches on alpha-2 receptors in the brainstem, turning down adrenaline-type signals. That lowers blood pressure and heart rate and has a calming, sedating effect.

Used for

  • High blood pressure (now rarely a first choice)
  • ADHD, and trouble getting to sleep with ADHD, usually in children and started by a specialist (off-label in Australia)
  • Tics and Tourette syndrome (off-label)
  • Menopausal hot flushes
  • Easing withdrawal from opioids or alcohol, under supervision (off-label)

Side effects

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Very common more than 1 in 10

  • Dizziness
  • Drowsiness or sedation
  • Light-headedness on standing (low blood pressure)
  • Dry mouth

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Low mood
  • Sleep problems
  • Headache
  • Constipation
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Pain in the salivary glands
  • Erection problems
  • Tiredness

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Delusions or seeing things that aren’t there
  • Nightmares
  • Pins and needles
  • Slow heart rate
  • Cold, pale fingers (Raynaud’s)
  • Itch, rash or hives
  • Feeling generally unwell

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Breast swelling in men
  • Heart block (an electrical problem causing a very slow pulse)
  • Dry eyes or a dry nose
  • Severe bowel slow-down (colonic pseudo-obstruction)
  • Hair loss
  • Raised blood sugar

Frequency not known reported, rate unclear

  • Confusion
  • Lower sex drive
  • Blurred near vision
  • Slow or irregular heart rhythm

Get help urgently if

  • Fainting, a very slow pulse or feeling very faint
  • Extreme sleepiness or slowed breathing
  • A child swallows any tablets not meant for them: even one can be dangerous. Call 000 or the Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26

Good to know

  • Don’t stop suddenly. Blood pressure can rebound and cause agitation, headache and a racing heart. Your doctor will reduce it slowly.
  • Alcohol, sleeping tablets and other sedating medicines add to the drowsiness.
  • Take care driving until you know how it affects you.
  • Store locked away from children. Even one tablet can be dangerous to a child.

Common questions

What is clonidine used for?

It’s an older blood pressure tablet, now used more for ADHD and trouble getting to sleep with ADHD (off-label, usually started by a specialist), tics, and menopausal hot flushes. Its ADHD effect is modest, smaller than stimulants.

What are the common side effects of clonidine?

Dizziness, drowsiness, light-headedness on standing and a dry mouth are very common. Take care driving until you know how it affects you.

Can I stop clonidine suddenly?

No. Blood pressure can rebound and cause agitation, headache and a racing heart. Your doctor will reduce it slowly.
