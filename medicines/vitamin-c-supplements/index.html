Vitamin C supplements

Vitamin supplement Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

Taking vitamin C every day shortens colds by about a day a year for most adults. Starting it when you’re already sick doesn’t reliably help.

Quick answer: Regular vitamin C slightly shortens colds (about 8% in adults) but doesn’t prevent them, except in people under extreme physical stress such as marathon runners. High doses can cause diarrhoea and kidney stones. Most people get enough from fruit and vegetables.

Brand names

  • Blackmores, Cenovis, Nature’s Way, Berocca (with B vitamins), effervescent tablets

How it works

Vitamin C is an antioxidant needed for collagen, wound healing and immune cells. The body can’t store much, so extra is passed in the urine.

Used for

  • Vitamin C deficiency (scurvy)
  • Colds in people under extreme physical stress
  • Shorter colds

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • ✅ Vitamin C deficiency (scurvy)Rare, but seen with very restricted diets and alcohol use.
  • ✅ Colds in people under extreme physical stressHalved the risk of colds in marathon runners and soldiers in subarctic training.
  • 🟡 Shorter coldsDaily use shortens colds by about 8% in adults; starting once sick hasn’t shown a consistent benefit.
  • ❌ Preventing colds in most peopleNo reduction in how often colds happen.
  • ❌ Cancer treatmentNot supported; high-dose IV vitamin C hasn’t shown benefit in good trials.

Avoid or check first if

  • You’ve had kidney stones
  • You have haemochromatosis (vitamin C increases iron absorption)

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Diarrhoea and stomach cramps at high doses

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Kidney stones (oxalate) with long-term high doses, especially in men

Get help urgently if

  • Signs of an allergic reaction: swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing (call 000)

Good to know

  • Two serves of fruit a day gives most people enough.
  • Vitamin C with iron tablets helps iron absorption.
  • Effervescent tablets can contain a lot of sodium.

Common questions

Should I take vitamin C when I get a cold?

Starting it after symptoms begin hasn’t shown a consistent benefit. Regular daily use shortens colds slightly.

Can vitamin C cause kidney stones?

High doses (1 g or more a day) long term increase the risk of oxalate stones, especially in men.
