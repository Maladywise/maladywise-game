Drug info

Beta blockers

Heart and blood pressure medicine Prescription only

A family of medicines that slow the heart and blunt the effects of adrenaline. Used for heart conditions, blood pressure, migraine and tremor.

Brand names

  • Metoprolol (Betaloc, Lopresor, Minax), atenolol (Noten, Tenormin)
  • Bisoprolol (Bicor), carvedilol (Dilatrend), nebivolol (Nebilet)
  • Propranolol (Deralin, Inderal), sotalol (Sotacor), timolol eye drops, many generics

How it works

Beta blockers block beta receptors, which adrenaline acts on. This slows the heart, reduces how hard it pumps and lowers blood pressure, so the heart needs less oxygen. Some, like propranolol, also act elsewhere in the body.

Used for

  • Angina, and after a heart attack
  • Heart failure (bisoprolol, carvedilol, metoprolol slow-release, nebivolol)
  • Heart rate control in atrial fibrillation and other fast rhythms
  • High blood pressure (now rarely first choice)
  • Preventing migraine; tremor (propranolol)
  • Performance anxiety (propranolol; off-label); glaucoma (eye drops)

Side effects

How often, from the official product information (e.g. metoprolol). Bold = serious.

Very common more than 1 in 10

  • Tiredness
  • Drop in blood pressure, incl. on standing, sometimes with fainting

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Dizziness or headache
  • Slow heart rate
  • Palpitations
  • Cold hands and feet
  • Short of breath on exertion
  • Nausea, tummy pain, diarrhoea or constipation

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Low mood, poor concentration, nightmares
  • Sleepiness or poor sleep, weight gain, pins and needles
  • Worsening heart failure, heart block
  • Chest pain
  • Wheeze or asthma attack (bronchospasm)
  • Vomiting, rash, sweating, cramps or swelling

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Nervousness, anxiety
  • Vision changes, dry or sore eyes
  • Heart rhythm disturbances
  • Runny nose, dry mouth, hair loss
  • Abnormal liver tests
  • Unmasking of diabetes, sexual problems

Very rare fewer than 1 in 10,000

  • Low platelets or white cells
  • Memory problems, confusion or hallucinations
  • Ringing in the ears, hearing problems
  • Worse leg pain on walking or Raynaud’s
  • Taste change, hepatitis, psoriasis flare
  • Sun sensitivity, joint pain, weakness

Get help urgently if

  • Wheeze or trouble breathing
  • Fainting, a very slow pulse or chest pain
  • Worsening breathlessness, swollen ankles or rapid weight gain

Good to know

  • Don’t stop suddenly. This can trigger angina or a heart attack. Doses are reduced over 1 to 2 weeks.
  • Usually avoided in asthma; used with care in COPD.
  • Can hide signs of low blood sugar in diabetes. Verapamil and diltiazem add to the slowing.
  • Some are used in pregnancy (e.g. labetalol). Discuss with your doctor.
  • Take care driving until you know if it makes you tired or dizzy.

Common questions

Are beta blockers used for anxiety?

Propranolol is sometimes used for performance anxiety, which is an off-label use. Beta blockers are mainly used for heart conditions, blood pressure, migraine prevention and tremor.

Can I stop beta blockers suddenly?

No. Stopping suddenly can trigger angina or a heart attack. Doses are reduced over 1 to 2 weeks.

Can I take beta blockers if I have asthma?

They are usually avoided in asthma and used with care in COPD. Get help urgently for wheeze or trouble breathing.
