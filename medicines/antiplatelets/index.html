Drug info

Antiplatelets

Antiplatelet Aspirin OTC; others prescription

Medicines that stop platelets sticking together, to prevent heart attacks and strokes in people who’ve already had one or have a stent. No longer recommended for most healthy people.

Quick answer: Antiplatelets make platelets less sticky, reducing clots in arteries. After a heart attack, stent or stroke they clearly prevent repeat events. For healthy people without heart disease, daily aspirin’s small benefit is cancelled out by bleeding risk, so it’s no longer routinely recommended for prevention.

Brand names

  • Aspirin: Cartia, Astrix, Cardiprin. Clopidogrel: Plavix, Iscover. Ticagrelor: Brilinta. Prasugrel: Effient. Aspirin with dipyridamole: Asasantin

How it works

Platelets plug damaged blood vessels. In narrowed arteries, they also trigger dangerous clots. Aspirin blocks platelets’ ability to make thromboxane; clopidogrel, ticagrelor and prasugrel block their P2Y12 receptor.

Used for

  • After a heart attack or stent: usually two antiplatelets (dual therapy) for 6 to 12 months, then one
  • After an ischaemic stroke or TIA
  • Stable angina and peripheral artery disease
  • Not recommended for most healthy people to prevent a first heart attack
  • Low-dose aspirin is used in pregnancy to prevent pre-eclampsia in high-risk women

Side effects

Bleeding is the main risk, higher with two antiplatelets or with blood thinners.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Bruising
  • Nosebleeds
  • Indigestion
  • Breathlessness (ticagrelor)

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Stomach or bowel bleeding
  • Stomach ulcer (aspirin)

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Bleeding in the brain
  • Asthma attacks triggered by aspirin in some people
  • Low platelets (TTP) with clopidogrel

Get help urgently if

  • Black or bloody stools or vomiting blood
  • Sudden severe headache, weakness or trouble speaking: call 000
  • Bleeding that won’t stop

Good to know

  • Never stop antiplatelets after a stent without talking to your cardiologist: early stopping can cause the stent to block.
  • Tell your surgeon and dentist; most dental work can go ahead.
  • Some people taking aspirin with another blood thinner need a stomach-protecting medicine (PPI).
  • Omeprazole and esomeprazole may weaken clopidogrel slightly; pantoprazole is often preferred.

Common questions

Should I take aspirin every day?

If you’ve had a heart attack, stent or stroke, usually yes. If you’re healthy, guidelines no longer recommend it for most people because bleeding risk outweighs the small benefit.

Why two blood thinners after a stent?

Dual antiplatelet therapy stops clots forming on the new stent while the artery heals, usually for 6 to 12 months.

Do I stop aspirin before surgery?

Ask your surgeon. Aspirin is often continued, especially after a stent. Clopidogrel, ticagrelor and prasugrel are usually stopped 5 to 7 days before major surgery, but only on specialist advice.
