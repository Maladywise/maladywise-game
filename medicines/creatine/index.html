Drug info

Creatine

Sports supplement Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

One of the best-studied supplements. With resistance training it adds strength and muscle, and it’s safe in healthy adults.

Quick answer: Creatine monohydrate (3 to 5 g a day) increases strength, power and lean mass when combined with resistance training, in young and older adults. It causes a 1 to 2 kg gain from water in muscle. It raises blood creatinine, which can look like kidney damage on tests even when kidneys are healthy. Avoid if you have kidney disease.

How it works

Creatine tops up phosphocreatine stores in muscle, which rapidly regenerate energy (ATP) for short, intense effort.

Used for

  • Strength and muscle with resistance training
  • Brain function and memory
  • Preventing muscle loss with ageing

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • ✅ Strength and muscle with resistance trainingConsistent benefit across many trials, including older adults.
  • 🟡 Brain function and memorySmall studies suggest benefit, especially with sleep loss or older age.
  • 🟡 Preventing muscle loss with ageingPromising with exercise.
  • ❌ Endurance sport performanceLittle benefit for long, steady efforts.

Avoid or check first if

  • You have kidney disease
  • You’re having kidney blood tests (tell your doctor you take creatine)

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Good to know

  • Choose a product tested for banned substances if you play competitive sport.
  • Timing doesn’t matter much; consistency does.

Common questions

Is creatine bad for your kidneys?

Not in healthy people at normal doses. It raises blood creatinine, which can look like kidney damage on tests, so tell your doctor.

Is creatine a steroid?

No. It’s a natural compound found in meat and fish, and made by the body.
