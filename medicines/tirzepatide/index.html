Drug info

Tirzepatide

GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist Prescription only

A weekly injection that copies two gut hormones controlling blood sugar and appetite. Used for type 2 diabetes, weight management and sleep apnoea with obesity. Powerful, and fully private.

At a glance

For
Type 2 diabetes; weight management; moderate to severe sleep apnoea with obesity
Works
Weight: about 15–21% of body weight lost on average over 72 weeks, depending on dose (about 3% on a dummy treatment). Diabetes: HbA1c falls by about 2%. Sleep apnoea: breathing pauses roughly halved
Cost
Not on the PBS for any use (October 2026). Private, about $280–690 a month, rising with dose
Kicks in
Appetite drops within weeks; dose stepped up every 4 weeks; weight loss builds over about 18 months
Taken for
Long term: weight usually returns after stopping
Watch for
Nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting, constipation, tummy pain, less appetite

On the pill? For 4 weeks after starting and after every dose step, add condoms or switch to a non-pill method. Mounjaro can make the pill less reliable, and surprise pregnancies are a real plot twist, not an internet myth.

Brand names

  • Mounjaro (weekly injection pen)

How it works

Tirzepatide acts on two hormone receptors, GIP and GLP-1, boosting insulin when blood sugar is high, slowing the stomach and reducing appetite. Hitting both is thought to explain its bigger effect on weight.

Used for

  • Type 2 diabetes, alongside diet and exercise
  • Weight management in people with obesity, or overweight with a weight-related condition, alongside diet and activity
  • Moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnoea in adults with obesity

Side effects

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Very common more than 1 in 10

  • Nausea
  • Diarrhoea
  • Vomiting, constipation and tummy pain, especially at weight-loss doses
  • Less appetite
  • Low blood sugar, if also on insulin or a sulfonylurea

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Allergic reactions such as rash or itch
  • Dizziness
  • Low blood pressure
  • Indigestion, reflux, burping, wind or bloating
  • Gallstones or gallbladder inflammation
  • Hair thinning
  • Injection-site reactions
  • Tiredness
  • Faster heart rate
  • Raised pancreatic enzymes on blood tests

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Pancreatitis (inflamed pancreas)
  • Slower stomach emptying
  • Altered taste or unusual skin sensations
  • Injection-site pain

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Anaphylaxis (severe allergic reaction)
  • Angioedema (swelling of the face, lips or throat)

Get help urgently if

  • Severe tummy pain that won’t settle, sometimes going through to the back (possible pancreatitis)
  • Pain under the right ribs, fever or yellow skin (possible gallstones)
  • Vomiting or diarrhoea so bad you can’t keep fluids down (risk of dehydration and kidney problems)
  • Swelling of the face or throat, or trouble breathing
  • Shakiness, sweating or confusion if you also take insulin or a sulfonylurea (low blood sugar)

Good to know

  • Can make the contraceptive pill less effective. Use a non-pill method or condoms for 4 weeks after starting and after each dose increase.
  • Not for use in pregnancy. Stop at least 1 month before trying to get pregnant.
  • Not suitable with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2.
  • Tell your doctor before any surgery or anaesthetic. Use only TGA-approved products from a pharmacy.

Common questions

What is Mounjaro used for?

Type 2 diabetes, weight management, and moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnoea in adults with obesity.

Is Mounjaro on the PBS?

No, not for any use as of October 2026. The PBAC recommended it for type 2 diabetes in March 2026, but the company didn’t proceed with listing. Private scripts cost roughly $280 to $690 a month, depending on dose.

Does Mounjaro affect the contraceptive pill?

It can make the pill less effective. Use a non-pill method or condoms for 4 weeks after starting and after each dose increase.

What are the common side effects of tirzepatide?

Nausea, diarrhoea and less appetite are very common, and vomiting, constipation and tummy pain especially at weight-loss doses.
