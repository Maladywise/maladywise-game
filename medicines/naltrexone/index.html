Drug info

Naltrexone (for alcohol)

Opioid antagonist Prescription only (S4)

A daily tablet that takes the reward out of drinking, so it’s easier to stop at one or not start. Underused and effective.

Quick answer: Naltrexone blocks the opioid ‘reward’ system that alcohol activates. It reduces cravings, heavy drinking days and relapse. It also blocks opioid painkillers, so anyone who may need opioids must plan ahead. It works best with support or counselling.

Takes the buzz out of the booze.

Brand names

  • Revia, Naltrexone generics

How it works

Naltrexone blocks opioid receptors, reducing the dopamine release and pleasure alcohol triggers.

Used for

  • Alcohol use disorder
  • Maintaining abstinence after opioid detox
  • Low-dose naltrexone (a different, off-label use) is covered on its own page

Side effects

Usually well tolerated; nausea is the commonest early issue.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Nausea
  • Headache
  • Dizziness
  • Tiredness
  • Trouble sleeping

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Raised liver enzymes
  • Low mood

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Sudden opioid withdrawal if taken while using opioids
  • Overdose risk if using opioids after stopping (lost tolerance)

Get help urgently if

  • Severe tummy pain, dark urine or yellow skin
  • Needing pain relief: tell staff you take naltrexone

Good to know

  • Don’t take if you’ve used opioids in the last 7 to 10 days.
  • Carry a card or tell all doctors and pharmacists.
  • Liver tests before and during treatment.
  • Acamprosate is an alternative, especially with liver disease or opioid use.

Common questions

Can I drink while on naltrexone?

It’s designed to work if you do drink, by reducing the reward and urge to continue. Many people use it to cut down.

How long do I take naltrexone for alcohol?

Usually at least 3 to 6 months, sometimes longer, with counselling or support.

Does naltrexone block painkillers?

Yes. It blocks opioids such as oxycodone and codeine. Stop it 72 hours before planned surgery, as advised by your doctor.
