Drug info

Gabapentin

Nerve pain and epilepsy medicine Prescription only (S4)

A medicine for nerve pain and seizures, closely related to pregabalin. Helps some people with nerve pain, but not most other pain, and it can cause sleepiness, dizziness and dependence.

Quick answer: Gabapentin is approved for epilepsy and nerve pain, such as after shingles. It helps roughly 1 in 3 to 1 in 6 people with nerve pain meaningfully. It’s widely used off label, but the evidence for most other pains is poor. Dizziness and sleepiness are common, and combining it with opioids raises the risk of overdose.

Sometimes a lifesaver for nerve pain. Often a dud for everything else.

Brand names

  • Neurontin, Gantin, and generic gabapentin

How it works

Gabapentin binds to a part of calcium channels on nerves (the alpha-2-delta subunit), calming overexcited nerve signalling. It doesn’t act on GABA receptors despite its name.

Used for

  • Nerve pain after shingles (postherpetic neuralgia) and diabetic nerve pain
  • Epilepsy (as an add-on)
  • Off label: restless legs, hot flushes, alcohol use disorder, itch and some anxiety, with variable evidence
  • A 2017 trial found no benefit over placebo for sciatica (pregabalin); evidence for back pain is poor

Side effects

Side effects are common, mainly when starting or increasing the dose.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Very common more than 1 in 10

  • Dizziness
  • Sleepiness

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Unsteadiness
  • Tiredness
  • Weight gain and swelling
  • Blurred vision
  • Dry mouth
  • Memory and concentration problems
  • Mood changes

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Breathing problems (especially with opioids or lung disease)
  • Misuse and dependence
  • Falls

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Suicidal thoughts
  • Severe allergic reactions

Get help urgently if

  • Very drowsy or slow breathing, especially with opioids: call 000
  • Thoughts of self-harm
  • Swelling of the face or tongue

Good to know

  • Lower doses are needed in kidney disease.
  • Don’t combine with opioids or alcohol without your doctor’s advice.
  • Taper slowly to stop; stopping suddenly can cause withdrawal and, in epilepsy, seizures.
  • Don’t drive until you know how it affects you.

Common questions

Is gabapentin a painkiller?

Not a general one. It can help nerve pain, but usually doesn’t help other types of pain such as back pain or arthritis.

Is gabapentin addictive?

It can be misused and cause dependence, especially in people who use opioids. Stopping should be gradual.

What’s the difference between gabapentin and pregabalin?

They work the same way. Pregabalin is absorbed more predictably and is on the PBS for nerve pain; gabapentin needs slower dose increases.
