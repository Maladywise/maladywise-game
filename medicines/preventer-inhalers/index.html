Drug info

Preventer inhalers (inhaled corticosteroids)

Inhaled corticosteroid Prescription only (S4)

The puffers that calm airway inflammation, so attacks happen less. Low doses, tiny amounts reaching the body, and the backbone of asthma care.

Quick answer: Preventers contain a low dose of inhaled corticosteroid (steroid), sometimes combined with a long-acting airway opener. Used regularly, they cut asthma attacks, hospital visits and deaths. For mild asthma, Australian guidelines also support budesonide-formoterol used only as needed, as an ‘anti-inflammatory reliever’.

Boring, daily and quietly life-saving.

Brand names

  • Steroid only: Flixotide (fluticasone), Pulmicort (budesonide), QVAR (beclometasone), Alvesco (ciclesonide). Combination: Symbicort and Rilast (budesonide-formoterol), Seretide (fluticasone-salmeterol), Breo (fluticasone-vilanterol), Flutiform

How it works

Inhaled steroids switch off the inflammation that makes airways swollen and twitchy. Combination inhalers add a long-acting bronchodilator that keeps airways open for 12 to 24 hours.

Used for

  • Asthma prevention (daily, or as-needed budesonide-formoterol for mild asthma)
  • Maintenance-and-reliever therapy (MART): one budesonide-formoterol inhaler used daily and as the reliever
  • COPD with frequent flare-ups, usually combined with other inhalers

Side effects

Inhaled doses are small; most side effects are in the mouth and throat.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Hoarse voice
  • Mouth or throat thrush
  • Sore throat

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Easy bruising (higher doses)
  • Pneumonia risk in COPD (higher doses)

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Slowed growth in children on higher doses (small)
  • Cataracts or glaucoma with long-term high doses
  • Allergic reaction

Get help urgently if

  • Asthma getting worse despite the preventer
  • White patches in the mouth that don’t clear
  • Night-time waking with asthma

Good to know

  • Rinse and spit after each dose to prevent thrush.
  • Ask your pharmacist or doctor to check your technique: most people make errors.
  • Don’t stop when you feel well: that’s the preventer working.
  • Have a written asthma action plan.

Common questions

Are steroid puffers safe long term?

Yes, at the usual doses. The amount reaching the body is small, and the benefit of preventing severe attacks far outweighs the risks. Your doctor aims for the lowest dose that works.

Do I need a preventer if I only have mild asthma?

Australian guidelines now recommend that almost all adults and adolescents with asthma have an inhaled steroid, either daily or as an as-needed budesonide-formoterol reliever.

Why rinse my mouth after a preventer?

Some medicine lands in the mouth and throat, where it can cause thrush and a hoarse voice. Rinsing and spitting, and using a spacer, reduce this.
