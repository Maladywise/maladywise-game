Drug info

Prazosin

Alpha-1 blocker Prescription only (S4)

An older alpha blocker, now mostly used at night for prostate symptoms, Raynaud’s or trauma nightmares. The first dose can make you faint, so start low at bedtime.

Quick answer: Prazosin relaxes blood vessels and the muscle around the prostate. It treats high blood pressure (less often now), urinary symptoms from an enlarged prostate, Raynaud’s phenomenon and, off-label, PTSD-related nightmares, where trial results are mixed.

Brand names

  • Minipress, Pratsiol

How it works

It blocks alpha-1 receptors on blood vessel and prostate smooth muscle, letting them relax.

Used for

  • High blood pressure
  • Urinary symptoms from an enlarged prostate
  • Raynaud’s phenomenon
  • Nightmares in PTSD (off-label; a large 2018 trial in veterans found no benefit, smaller trials did)

Side effects

Dizziness is the main issue, especially early.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Dizziness, light-headedness
  • Headache
  • Tiredness
  • Stuffy nose

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Fainting with the first dose
  • Palpitations
  • Swollen ankles

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Prolonged erection
  • Floppy iris syndrome during cataract surgery

Get help urgently if

  • Fainting or falls
  • Erection lasting over 4 hours

Good to know

  • Take the first dose at bedtime, and get up slowly at night.
  • Increase the dose gradually.
  • Extra care with other blood pressure medicines, PDE5 inhibitors (e.g. sildenafil) or alcohol.
  • Tell your eye surgeon before cataract surgery.

Common questions

Why take prazosin at night?

The first doses can drop blood pressure and cause fainting. Taking it at bedtime reduces the risk.

Does prazosin help PTSD nightmares?

Some trials found benefit, but a large 2018 trial in veterans didn’t. It’s still used for some people under medical advice.

Is prazosin used for prostate problems?

Yes, it can improve flow, though once-daily tamsulosin is more commonly used.
