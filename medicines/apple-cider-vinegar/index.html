Drug info

Apple cider vinegar

Food supplement Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

A social media favourite. Weight loss evidence is weak; it can erode teeth and burn the throat.

Quick answer: Small studies suggest vinegar with meals can slightly blunt blood sugar rises. Weight loss trials are few and small, and one widely shared trial was retracted. Undiluted vinegar can erode tooth enamel and burn the throat, and large amounts have caused low potassium.

Evidence first, marketing second.

Brand names

  • Bragg, Goli and Swisse gummies, supermarket brands

How it works

Acetic acid may slow stomach emptying and slightly improve insulin sensitivity.

Used for

  • Blood sugar after meals

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • 🟡 Blood sugar after mealsSmall effect in small studies.
  • ❌ Weight lossWeak evidence; one prominent trial retracted.
  • ❌ ‘Detox’ and alkalisingNo basis.

Avoid or check first if

  • You take insulin, diuretics or digoxin (low potassium risk)
  • You have reflux or slow stomach emptying (gastroparesis)

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Tooth enamel erosion
  • Throat irritation, reflux

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Low potassium with large amounts

Get help urgently if

  • Signs of an allergic reaction: swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing (call 000)

Good to know

  • Always dilute and drink through a straw; rinse your mouth after.
  • Gummies contain far less acetic acid and added sugar.

Common questions

Does apple cider vinegar help weight loss?

There’s no good evidence. One widely publicised trial was retracted.

Is apple cider vinegar safe?

Diluted, in small amounts, usually. Undiluted, it can damage teeth and the throat.
