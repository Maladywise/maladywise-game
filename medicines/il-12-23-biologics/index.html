Drug info

Ustekinumab and IL-23 biologics

Biologic (interleukin blocker) Prescription only (S4), specialist

Biologics that block interleukin-23, a key inflammation signal in Crohn’s and colitis. An IV start, then injections every 4 to 8 weeks, with a good safety record.

Quick answer: Ustekinumab blocks IL-12 and IL-23; risankizumab, mirikizumab and guselkumab block IL-23 only. They work in Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis, including after anti-TNF failure. In the SEQUENCE trial, risankizumab outperformed ustekinumab for endoscopic remission in Crohn’s after anti-TNF failure.

Brand names

  • Ustekinumab: Stelara and biosimilars. Risankizumab: Skyrizi. Mirikizumab: Omvoh. Guselkumab: Tremfya

How it works

IL-23 drives Th17 cells that fuel gut inflammation; blocking it calms the process.

Used for

  • Crohn’s disease
  • Ulcerative colitis
  • Also psoriasis and psoriatic arthritis

Side effects

Generally well tolerated.

Good to know

  • Before starting: screening for tuberculosis, hepatitis B and C and HIV, and catch-up vaccines.
  • Avoid live vaccines (e.g. yellow fever, some shingles and travel vaccines) unless your specialist says otherwise.
  • Tell your doctors before surgery, and if you get a fever or infection.
  • Most biologics can be continued in pregnancy after discussion with your gastroenterologist.

Common questions

What is the difference between ustekinumab and risankizumab?

Ustekinumab blocks IL-12 and IL-23; risankizumab blocks IL-23 only. In Crohn’s after anti-TNF failure, risankizumab gave higher endoscopic remission in a head-to-head trial.

How often are the injections?

Every 4 to 8 weeks after induction, depending on the drug.

Are IL-23 biologics safe?

They have a good safety record, with low rates of serious infection, but still need screening and monitoring.
