Drug info

Levothyroxine (thyroxine)

Thyroid hormone Prescription only (S4)

Replaces the hormone an underactive thyroid can’t make. One small tablet a day, for life, with a blood test to get the dose right.

Quick answer: Levothyroxine (T4) is identical to the thyroid hormone your body makes. It treats hypothyroidism and is used after thyroid surgery or radioactive iodine. The dose is adjusted using TSH blood tests about 6 weeks after each change. Taken correctly, side effects only happen if the dose is too high.

The easiest hormone to replace, if you take it right.

Brand names

  • Eltroxin, Oroxine, Eutroxsig

How it works

Your body converts levothyroxine (T4) into the active hormone T3, which sets the speed of metabolism in almost every cell.

Used for

  • Hypothyroidism
  • After thyroid removal or radioactive iodine
  • Suppressing TSH in some thyroid cancers
  • Sometimes in pregnancy with mild thyroid problems

Side effects

At the right dose there are no side effects; too much causes symptoms of an overactive thyroid.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Over-replacement: palpitations, tremor, heat intolerance, weight loss, poor sleep

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Atrial fibrillation and bone loss with long-term over-replacement
  • Angina in people with heart disease if started too fast
  • Allergy to tablet ingredients

Get help urgently if

  • Chest pain or a racing irregular heartbeat
  • You become pregnant: increase the dose and test soon

Good to know

  • Take on an empty stomach with water, 30 to 60 minutes before breakfast (or at bedtime, consistently).
  • Keep iron, calcium, antacids and some soy products 4 hours away.
  • Stick to one brand; recheck TSH about 6 weeks after any brand or dose change.
  • Pregnancy: tell your doctor straight away; many women need about 25 to 30% more.

Common questions

What time should I take levothyroxine?

On an empty stomach, 30 to 60 minutes before breakfast, at the same time each day. Bedtime (3 or more hours after food) also works if consistent.

Can I switch levothyroxine brands?

Brands may not be exactly interchangeable. Stay on one brand, and if it changes, have a TSH test about 6 weeks later.

Does levothyroxine cause weight gain?

No. At the right dose it restores normal metabolism; untreated hypothyroidism is what causes weight gain.
