Drug info

Garlic supplements

Herbal medicine Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

Garlic modestly lowers blood pressure in people with high blood pressure. Effects on cholesterol are small.

Quick answer: Meta-analyses suggest garlic supplements (especially aged garlic extract) lower systolic blood pressure by about 8 mmHg in people with high blood pressure, similar to a low-dose blood pressure tablet. Cholesterol falls only slightly. Evidence for preventing colds is weak.

Brand names

  • Blackmores Odourless Garlic, Kyolic aged garlic extract, Nature’s Way, Herbs of Gold Garlic + Horseradish

How it works

Allicin and related sulfur compounds relax blood vessels and affect platelets.

Used for

  • High blood pressure
  • Cholesterol

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • 🟡 High blood pressureAbout 8 mmHg lower systolic in meta-analyses.
  • 🟡 CholesterolSmall reduction.
  • ❌ Preventing coldsWeak evidence (one small trial).

Avoid or check first if

  • You take warfarin or other blood thinners
  • You take HIV medicines (saquinavir)
  • You have surgery coming up (stop 1 to 2 weeks before)

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Garlic breath and body odour
  • Heartburn, wind

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Bleeding, especially with blood thinners or before surgery

Get help urgently if

  • Signs of an allergic reaction: swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing (call 000)

Good to know

  • Don’t swap prescribed blood pressure tablets for garlic.
  • Cooking with garlic is great for flavour, less so for blood pressure.

Common questions

Does garlic lower blood pressure?

Yes, modestly, in people with high blood pressure. It doesn’t replace prescribed treatment.

Is garlic safe with blood thinners?

Supplements may increase bleeding risk. Check with your doctor.
