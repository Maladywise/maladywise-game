Drug info

Amitriptyline

Tricyclic antidepressant Prescription only

An old antidepressant reborn as a low-dose night-time tablet for nerve pain, migraine prevention and IBS. Helpful for many, sedating for most, dangerous in overdose.

At a glance

For
Nerve pain, migraine and tension headache prevention, IBS (mostly off-label, low dose); depression (rarely now)
Works
Helps some people: for nerve pain, about 1 extra person in 5 gets real relief compared with a dummy treatment (evidence modest). Low doses clearly helped IBS in a large UK trial
Cost
Under the $25 PBS co-payment, about $22 for 50 tablets; $7.70 concession
Kicks in
Sleepiness from the first night; pain benefit in 2–6 weeks
Taken for
Months, then reviewed (often at 6–12 months)
Watch for
Drowsiness, dry mouth, constipation, weight gain, dizziness on standing

Take it 1–2 hours before bed, not at midnight, unless you enjoy mornings that feel like wading through custard.

Brand names

  • Endep, plus generic brands sold as amitriptyline

How it works

It raises serotonin and noradrenaline in the brain and spinal cord, turning down pain signals. Pain doses (often 5 to 50 mg at night) are much lower than depression doses.

Used for

  • Depression (TGA-approved, but now rarely used for this because of side effects and overdose risk)
  • Bed-wetting in children, after other causes are excluded (TGA-approved)
  • Nerve pain, e.g. shingles pain or diabetic nerve pain (off-label)
  • Migraine and tension headache prevention (off-label)
  • IBS: low doses improved symptoms in the 2023 ATLANTIS trial in UK general practice (off-label)

Side effects

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Very common more than 1 in 10

  • Drowsiness, dizziness, headache, tremor
  • Dry mouth, constipation, nausea
  • Blurred near vision, blocked nose
  • Fast heartbeat, dizzy on standing
  • Sweating, weight gain
  • Slurred speech, aggression

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Confusion, agitation, low sodium
  • Poor focus, odd taste, pins and needles
  • Unsteadiness, large pupils
  • Heart block, long QT on ECG
  • Trouble passing urine
  • Low sex drive, erection problems
  • Tiredness, thirst

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Anxiety, poor sleep, nightmares, mania
  • Seizures, collapse, worse heart failure
  • High blood pressure, ringing ears
  • Diarrhoea, vomiting, liver problems
  • Rash, hives, flushing, face swelling
  • Unable to pass urine, raised eye pressure

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Low white cells or platelets
  • Delirium (older people), hallucinations
  • Suicidal thoughts, irregular heart rhythm
  • Bowel paralysis, jaundice
  • Hair loss, sun sensitivity, fever
  • Breast swelling in men

Very rare fewer than 1 in 10,000

  • Restlessness (akathisia), nerve damage
  • Acute glaucoma (painful red eye)
  • Heart muscle disease, torsades
  • Lung inflammation

Frequency not known reported, rate unclear

  • Anaphylaxis or angioedema
  • Severe skin reaction (DRESS)
  • Paranoia, movement problems, dry eyes
  • Hepatitis, overheating

Get help urgently if

  • Overdose is life-threatening, even a few days’ supply. Call 000, or Poisons Information 13 11 26
  • Fainting, chest pain or a racing, irregular heartbeat
  • A painful red eye with blurred vision
  • Unable to pass urine, or severe constipation with tummy swelling
  • Thoughts of self-harm: Lifeline 13 11 14

Good to know

  • Older people: drowsiness, dizziness on standing and confusion raise the risk of falls. Low doses are used.
  • Avoid alcohol and other sedating medicines. Don’t drive until you know how it affects you.
  • Many medicines interact, incl. other antidepressants and tramadol. Check with your pharmacist.
  • Don’t stop suddenly after long use. Pregnancy or breastfeeding: talk to your doctor. Store out of reach of children.

Common questions

Why is amitriptyline used for pain?

In low doses it turns down pain signals in the brain and spinal cord. Pain doses (often 5 to 50 mg at night) are much lower than depression doses. It helps some people, not everyone.

How long does amitriptyline take to work for pain?

The pain benefit can take 2 to 6 weeks. It is taken 1 to 2 hours before bed, as drowsiness is common.

Is amitriptyline dangerous in overdose?

Yes. An overdose, even of a few days’ supply, is life-threatening. Store it out of reach of children, and call 000 or Poisons Information on 13 11 26 if too much is taken.
