Drug info

Weight-loss medicines

Weight management medicine Mostly prescription only

The approved weight-loss medicines side by side: how much weight people actually lost in the trials, what they cost your stomach, and what happens when you stop. Spoiler: the newer injections lead, and none of them is a course you finish.

At a glance

For
Adults with obesity (BMI 30 or more), or BMI 27 or more with a weight-related condition, alongside diet and activity
Works
Average loss in the main trials: tirzepatide 15–21%, semaglutide about 15%, phentermine/topiramate about 9% more than dummy treatment, liraglutide 8%, naltrexone/bupropion about 6%, orlistat a few kilograms. Dummy treatment: about 1–3%
Cost
None are on the PBS for weight loss (October 2026). Wegovy is being reconsidered for some people with heart disease and obesity
Kicks in
Appetite changes within weeks; most weight loss over 6–18 months
Taken for
Long term for most. Phentermine is approved only for short courses (up to 3 months)
Watch for
Nausea and gut upsets (injections), racing heart and poor sleep (phentermine), oily leakage (orlistat)

If it’s sold with a before-and-after photo and a countdown timer, it isn’t on this list for a reason.

Brand names

  • Tirzepatide: Mounjaro
  • Semaglutide 2.4 mg: Wegovy
  • Liraglutide 3 mg: generic liraglutide (the Saxenda brand left Australia in December 2025)
  • Phentermine: Duromine
  • Naltrexone with bupropion: Contrave
  • Orlistat: Xenical (pharmacist only, no script needed)
  • Phentermine with topiramate: Qsymia (US); not approved as a combination in Australia

How it works

  • Gut-hormone copies (tirzepatide, semaglutide, liraglutide): slow the stomach and turn up fullness signals in the brain. See GLP-1 medicines.
  • Phentermine: a stimulant that suppresses appetite.
  • Naltrexone/bupropion: acts on the brain’s appetite and reward pathways.
  • Orlistat: blocks about a third of the fat you eat from being absorbed.

Used for

  • Weight management alongside a lower-calorie diet and more activity
  • Extra approved uses for some: type 2 diabetes, lowering heart risk (Wegovy), sleep apnoea with obesity (Mounjaro)

Compared

Weight figures are average loss in the main trial, on the medicine versus a dummy treatment. Both groups had diet and activity advice, so the medicine’s effect is on top of lifestyle changes.

    Acts on the brain’s appetite and reward pathways
    How taken
    Tablets, built up over 4 weeks to 2 tablets twice a day
    Weight loss

    Common side effects

    How taken

Save your money

  • Weight-loss supplements (garcinia, green tea extract, raspberry ketone, chitosan, glucomannan): little or no effect in good-quality trials. Garcinia and green tea extract have been linked to liver damage.
  • HCG injections or drops: no evidence they work and not approved for weight loss. Any loss comes from the 500-calorie diet sold with them.
  • Compounded or online “Ozempic”: banned from compounding in Australia since October 2024, and often fake or the wrong dose.
  • Lifestyle still matters. Every trial above added medicine on top of diet and activity changes. Protein, strength exercise and sleep help keep muscle and keep weight off.

Side effects

  • Injections (tirzepatide, semaglutide, liraglutide): nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation, reflux, gallstones; rarely pancreatitis.
  • Phentermine: racing heart, raised blood pressure, poor sleep, restlessness, dry mouth; rarely heart valve or lung blood pressure problems.
  • Naltrexone/bupropion: nausea, constipation, headache, dizziness; rarely seizures or mood changes.
  • Topiramate: tingling, poor concentration, mood changes, kidney stones.
  • Orlistat: oily leakage and urgency; take a multivitamin at bedtime.

Get help urgently if

  • Severe tummy pain that won’t settle, sometimes going through to the back (possible pancreatitis)
  • Pain under the right ribs, fever or yellow skin (possible gallstones)
  • Chest pain, a racing or irregular heartbeat, fainting or new breathlessness (especially on phentermine)
  • A seizure, or new low mood or thoughts of self-harm. In Australia, Lifeline is on 13 11 14
  • Swelling of the face or throat, or trouble breathing

Good to know

  • Pregnancy: none are used in pregnancy. Topiramate causes birth defects; tirzepatide can make the pill less reliable.
  • Muscle: a good share of weight lost is lean tissue. Enough protein and strength exercise help protect it.
  • Surgery: tell your team you take a GLP-1 medicine (slower stomach emptying) or naltrexone (blocks opioid pain relief).
  • Use only TGA-approved products from a pharmacy.

Common questions

Which weight-loss medicine works best?

On average, tirzepatide, then semaglutide. In a head-to-head trial, people lost 20.2% of their weight on tirzepatide versus 13.7% on semaglutide over 72 weeks. Results vary a lot from person to person.

Do I have to take weight-loss medicine forever?

For most people, the weight comes back after stopping. In trials, most of the lost weight returned within a year of stopping semaglutide or tirzepatide. They work more like blood pressure medicine than a course of antibiotics.

Are weight-loss injections on the PBS?

Not for weight loss, as of October 2026. Ozempic is PBS-subsidised for type 2 diabetes only. Wegovy is being reconsidered for some people with heart disease and obesity.

Do weight-loss supplements work?

Not in good-quality trials. Garcinia, green tea extract, raspberry ketone and similar products show little or no effect, and some have been linked to liver damage.
