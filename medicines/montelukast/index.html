Drug info

Montelukast

Leukotriene receptor antagonist Prescription only (S4)

A daily tablet that blocks leukotrienes, another inflammation chemical in asthma. Handy for some children and exercise asthma, but watch for mood and sleep changes.

Quick answer: Montelukast is a once-daily tablet or chewable that helps prevent asthma symptoms, especially exercise-triggered asthma, and can help hay fever. It’s generally less effective than an inhaled steroid. It carries a warning about neuropsychiatric effects: nightmares, agitation, mood changes and, rarely, suicidal thoughts.

At a glance

Easy tablet, keep an eye on the mood.

Brand names

  • Singulair, Lukair, Montelukast generics

How it works

Montelukast blocks leukotriene receptors in the airways, reducing swelling, mucus and airway tightening.

Used for

  • Asthma prevention in children (PBS for ages 2 to 14 in some situations)
  • Exercise-induced bronchoconstriction
  • Add-on in adult asthma
  • Hay fever alongside asthma

Side effects

Most people have no problems; behaviour and mood effects are the main concern.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Headache
  • Tummy pain
  • Upper respiratory infections

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Nightmares, sleep disturbance
  • Irritability, agitation, anxiety
  • Low mood

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Suicidal thoughts or behaviour
  • Churg–Strauss-type vasculitis (very rare)

Get help urgently if

  • Any thoughts of self-harm or suicide: stop and get help today
  • New aggression, depression or sleepwalking
  • Asthma getting worse

Good to know

  • Tell your family to watch for mood or sleep changes, especially in children.
  • Stop and see your doctor if behaviour changes; effects usually resolve after stopping.
  • It isn’t a reliever: keep your blue puffer.

Common questions

Does montelukast cause mood changes?

It can. Regulators, including the TGA, warn about nightmares, agitation, depression and, rarely, suicidal thoughts. Stop it and see your doctor if this happens.

Is montelukast as good as a steroid puffer?

Usually not. Inhaled steroids are more effective for most people; montelukast suits some children and exercise-induced asthma.

When should I take montelukast?

Once a day, usually in the evening.
