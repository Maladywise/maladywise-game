Drug info

Kava

Herbal medicine Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

May ease anxiety short term, but the best recent trial found no benefit. It can damage the liver; Australia limits kava products.

Quick answer: Older trials suggested kava eases anxiety short term; a well-designed Australian trial in generalised anxiety disorder found no benefit over placebo. Kava has been linked to liver injury, so Australian medicines are restricted to water-based root extracts with a capped daily dose. Avoid alcohol and driving.

How it works

Kavalactones act on GABA, the brain’s calming messenger.

Used for

  • Short-term anxiety

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • 🟡 Short-term anxietyMixed: older trials positive, the best recent trial negative.
  • ❌ Generalised anxiety disorderNo benefit in a large Australian trial.

Avoid or check first if

  • You have liver disease or drink alcohol
  • You take sedatives, benzodiazepines or antidepressants
  • You drive or operate machinery
  • You’re pregnant or breastfeeding

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Drowsiness, upset stomach
  • Dry scaly skin with heavy long-term use

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Liver injury (rare): yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, severe tiredness

Get help urgently if

  • Signs of an allergic reaction: swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing (call 000)
  • Yellow skin or eyes, dark urine or severe tiredness (possible liver injury)

Good to know

  • Psychological therapy and proven medicines work better for anxiety.
  • Stop and see a doctor if you develop yellow eyes or dark urine.

Common questions

Does kava work for anxiety?

Evidence is mixed. A large Australian trial in generalised anxiety disorder found no benefit.

Is kava legal in Australia?

Yes, as TGA-listed medicines made from water extracts of the root, with dose limits. Importing kava for drinking has separate rules.
