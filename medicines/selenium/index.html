Drug info

Selenium

Mineral supplement Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

Helps mild thyroid eye disease. Doesn’t prevent cancer, and too much is toxic.

Quick answer: Selenium 100 mcg twice daily for 6 months is recommended for mild Graves’ eye disease. The large SELECT trial found selenium didn’t prevent prostate cancer and may raise type 2 diabetes risk. Brazil nuts are extremely high in selenium (one or two a day is plenty). Over 400 mcg a day long term risks toxicity.

How it works

Selenium is part of antioxidant enzymes and is needed for thyroid hormone metabolism.

Used for

  • Mild Graves’ eye disease
  • Hashimoto’s thyroiditis

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • ✅ Mild Graves’ eye disease6 months of selenium improved symptoms and slowed progression in a European trial.
  • 🟡 Hashimoto’s thyroiditisLowers antibodies in some studies, without clear symptom benefit.
  • ❌ Preventing cancerNo benefit in the SELECT trial; possible increase in diabetes risk.

Avoid or check first if

  • You already eat Brazil nuts daily or take a multivitamin with selenium

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Selenium toxicity: garlic breath, hair and nail loss, skin rash, nerve symptoms

Get help urgently if

  • Signs of an allergic reaction: swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing (call 000)

Good to know

  • One to two Brazil nuts can provide a day’s selenium.
  • Australian soils vary; most people on a mixed diet get enough.

Common questions

Does selenium help the thyroid?

It helps mild Graves’ eye disease. For Hashimoto’s, it can lower antibodies, but symptom benefit isn’t clear.

Can you take too much selenium?

Yes. Over 400 mcg a day long term can cause hair and nail loss, garlic breath and nerve problems.
