Drug info

Echinacea

Herbal medicine Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

May slightly reduce the chance of catching a cold. Doesn’t reliably shorten one you already have.

Quick answer: A Cochrane review found echinacea products may slightly lower the risk of catching a cold, but don’t clearly shorten or ease colds once started. Products vary hugely in species, plant part and extraction, so results don’t carry from one brand to another.

How it works

Thought to stimulate parts of the immune system.

Used for

  • Preventing colds

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • 🟡 Preventing coldsPossibly a small reduction in risk.
  • ❌ Treating colds once startedNo clear benefit.

Avoid or check first if

  • You have an autoimmune condition
  • You’re allergic to daisy-family plants
  • You have asthma or hay fever (higher allergy risk)

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Upset stomach
  • Rash

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Severe allergic reactions, especially if allergic to daisies, ragweed or chrysanthemums

Get help urgently if

  • Signs of an allergic reaction: swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing (call 000)

Good to know

  • Hand washing, sleep and staying home when sick work better.
  • ‘Immune’ combos often add zinc: count the total.

Common questions

Does echinacea stop colds?

It may slightly reduce your chance of catching one. It doesn’t reliably shorten a cold.

Is echinacea safe for children?

Rashes were more common in children in one trial. Check with a pharmacist first.
