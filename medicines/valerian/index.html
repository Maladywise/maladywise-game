Drug info

Valerian

Herbal medicine Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

A popular sleep herb with inconsistent evidence. Better-designed trials mostly show little or no benefit.

Quick answer: Trials of valerian for insomnia are mixed: some show a small benefit on how people rate their sleep, but better-designed trials show little difference from placebo. It’s generally well tolerated. Don’t combine with alcohol or other sedatives.

Brand names

How it works

Valerian may act on GABA, the brain’s calming messenger.

Used for

  • Insomnia

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • 🟡 InsomniaSmall, inconsistent benefit in some trials.
  • ❌ AnxietyNo clear evidence.

Avoid or check first if

  • You take sleeping tablets, benzodiazepines, opioids or drink alcohol
  • You’re pregnant or breastfeeding

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Morning grogginess, headache, vivid dreams

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Liver injury (rare): yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, severe tiredness

Get help urgently if

  • Signs of an allergic reaction: swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing (call 000)
  • Yellow skin or eyes, dark urine or severe tiredness (possible liver injury)

Good to know

  • Sleep habits and CBT for insomnia work better than any pill long term.
  • Don’t drive if you feel drowsy.

Common questions

Does valerian help sleep?

Evidence is inconsistent. Better-quality trials show little or no benefit over placebo.

Is valerian safe?

Usually, short term. Rare liver injury has been reported. Avoid alcohol and other sedatives.
