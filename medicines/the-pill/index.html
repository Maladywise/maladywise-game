Drug info

The combined pill

Combined hormonal contraceptive Prescription only (S4)

Oestrogen plus progestogen, taken daily. Over 99% effective if perfect, about 93% in real life. Also helps heavy, painful periods and acne.

Quick answer: The combined pill stops ovulation. Most people tolerate it well; the main serious risk is a small increase in blood clots, which is still lower than the risk in pregnancy. It isn’t suitable with migraine with aura, some heart and clotting conditions, or for smokers over 35.

Small pill, big job, small risks.

Brand names

  • Levlen, Microgynon, Yasmin, Yaz, Diane-35 (acne), Qlaira, Zoely, Brenda-35, Femme-Tab and others

How it works

Oestrogen and progestogen stop ovulation, thicken cervical mucus and thin the womb lining.

Used for

  • Contraception
  • Heavy or painful periods
  • Acne and excess hair (some types)
  • Cycle control, PCOS, period-related migraine without aura (some cases)

Side effects

Many side effects settle in the first 3 months.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Breakthrough bleeding or spotting
  • Nausea
  • Breast tenderness
  • Headache
  • Mood changes

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Raised blood pressure
  • Reduced libido

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Blood clots (DVT, PE): about 5 to 12 per 10,000 users a year v about 2 in non-users, and more in pregnancy
  • Stroke (higher with migraine with aura)

Get help urgently if

  • Calf pain or swelling, chest pain, sudden breathlessness: urgent care
  • New migraine with aura, sudden weakness or speech trouble: call 000
  • Yellow skin

Good to know

  • Missed one active pill (under 24 hours late): take it as soon as you remember, carry on.
  • Missed by 24 hours or more: take the latest missed pill, use condoms or abstain for 7 days; if this falls in the last 7 active pills, skip the inactive pills; consider emergency contraception if you had sex in the previous days.
  • Vomiting within 3 hours counts as a missed pill.
  • Some epilepsy and HIV medicines and St John’s wort make it less effective.

Common questions

How effective is the pill?

Over 99% with perfect use and about 93% with typical use, because pills get missed.

Does the pill cause blood clots?

It slightly raises the risk, to roughly 5 to 12 per 10,000 users a year, compared with about 2 in non-users. Pregnancy and the weeks after birth carry a higher risk than the pill.

Can I skip the sugar pills?

Yes, with most monophasic pills you can run active pills together to skip periods. Breakthrough bleeding is common at first.
