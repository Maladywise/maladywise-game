Drug info

Melatonin

Sleep hormone Pharmacist (S3, 55+) or prescription (S4)

The body’s ‘darkness’ hormone. Mild help for falling asleep, more useful for body-clock problems like jet lag than for general insomnia.

Quick answer: Melatonin shifts the body clock and slightly shortens the time to fall asleep. In Australia, 2 mg prolonged-release melatonin is available from pharmacists for people 55 and over; otherwise it’s prescription only, including for children. Imported gummies vary widely in actual dose.

At a glance

For
Insomnia in people 55+, sleep onset problems in children with autism or ADHD, jet lag, delayed sleep phase
Works
Modest: falls asleep about 7 to 12 minutes faster on average
Cost
About $30 to $40 a month; PBS for some children with autism
Kicks in
1 to 2 hours; body-clock shifts over days
Dose
Adults 55+: 2 mg prolonged-release 1 to 2 hours before bed
Watch for
Morning grogginess, headache, vivid dreams

A clock-setter, not a knockout pill.

Brand names

  • Circadin (2 mg prolonged-release, adults 55+), Slenyto (children with autism), compounded or imported products

How it works

Melatonin signals ‘night’ to the brain’s master clock, shifting sleep timing and promoting sleepiness.

Used for

  • Primary insomnia in adults 55 and over (short courses up to 13 weeks)
  • Sleep onset problems in children with autism, ADHD or Smith-Magenis syndrome (prescription)
  • Jet lag (taken at the destination bedtime)
  • Delayed sleep phase in teenagers (low dose, timed early evening)

Side effects

Generally well tolerated in the short term.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Morning drowsiness
  • Headache
  • Dizziness
  • Vivid dreams

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Nausea
  • Irritability

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Interactions with fluvoxamine (raises levels a lot) and warfarin

Get help urgently if

  • Child accidentally swallows lots of gummies: Poisons Information 13 11 26
  • Ongoing insomnia: see your doctor

Good to know

  • Avoid driving if drowsy.
  • Use the lowest dose; children’s doses should be guided by a doctor.
  • Imported gummies often contain more or less than the label says.
  • Pair with good sleep habits: consistent wake time, light in the morning.

Common questions

Is melatonin safe for children?

Short-term, prescribed melatonin is generally safe for children with specific sleep problems, but it should be guided by a doctor. In Australia it’s prescription-only under 55.

How much melatonin should I take?

In Australia, adults 55+ can use 2 mg prolonged-release 1 to 2 hours before bed. Higher isn’t necessarily better.

Does melatonin help jet lag?

Yes, especially flying east: take it at the destination’s bedtime for a few nights.
