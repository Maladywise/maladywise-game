Drug info

Ashwagandha (withania)

Herbal medicine Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

Hugely popular for stress. Small trials look positive, but they’re short and low quality, and liver injury has been reported.

Quick answer: Small, short trials (mostly 8 weeks) suggest ashwagandha may lower stress and anxiety scores and modestly improve sleep. Long-term safety isn’t known. Cases of liver injury have been reported, and it can raise thyroid hormone levels. Avoid in pregnancy.

How it works

An Ayurvedic root, thought to dampen the stress hormone response.

Used for

  • Stress and anxiety
  • Sleep

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • 🟡 Stress and anxietySmall benefit in short trials.
  • 🟡 SleepSmall benefit in small trials.
  • ❌ Testosterone and muscleWeak, low-quality evidence.

Avoid or check first if

  • You’re pregnant (linked to miscarriage in traditional use)
  • You have thyroid disease or take levothyroxine
  • You have an autoimmune condition or take sedatives

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Drowsiness, upset stomach, diarrhoea

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Liver injury (rare): yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, severe tiredness
  • Overactive thyroid

Get help urgently if

  • Signs of an allergic reaction: swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing (call 000)
  • Yellow skin or eyes, dark urine or severe tiredness (possible liver injury)

Good to know

  • Stop if you notice yellow eyes, dark urine or itch.
  • Limit use to a few months until long-term safety is known.

Common questions

Does ashwagandha reduce stress?

Small, short trials suggest a modest effect. Larger, longer studies are needed.

Is ashwagandha safe?

Short term, usually. Liver injury and thyroid effects have been reported. Avoid in pregnancy.
