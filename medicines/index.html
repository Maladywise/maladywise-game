Medicines

Plain-English medicine guides: brand names, how they work, what they’re for and side effects.

  • Blood pressure and heart medicineACE inhibitorsA widely used family of tablets that relax blood vessels. Used for blood pressure, heart failure, after a heart attack and to protect the kidneys.
  • Medicine familyAlpha-2 agonistsClonidine, guanfacine and relatives compared. Calm the body’s “fight or flight” signals; used for ADHD, tics, blood pressure and hot flushes.
  • Tricyclic antidepressantAmitriptylineAn older antidepressant now mostly used in low doses for nerve pain, migraine prevention and irritable bowel syndrome (IBS). Dangerous in overdose.
  • Heart and blood pressure medicineBeta blockersA family of medicines that slow the heart and blunt the effects of adrenaline. Used for heart conditions, blood pressure, migraine and tremor.
  • Alpha-2 agonistClonidineAn older blood pressure tablet that calms the body’s “fight or flight” signals. Now used more for ADHD, sleep and tics than for blood pressure.
  • Stimulant (amfetamine)DexamfetamineA stimulant tablet for ADHD and narcolepsy. A controlled (Schedule 8) medicine with strict prescribing rules.
  • SSRI antidepressantEscitalopramA widely used antidepressant for depression and anxiety disorders. Taken once a day. Usually well tolerated.
  • SSRI antidepressantFluoxetineA long-established antidepressant. Its very long half-life means fewer withdrawal symptoms but slow wash-out. Sometimes used in young people, with specialist guidance.
  • Medicine familyGLP-1 medicinesSemaglutide, tirzepatide, liraglutide and dulaglutide compared. Gut-hormone copies for type 2 diabetes and weight.
  • Alpha-2A agonist (non-stimulant)GuanfacineA non-stimulant ADHD medicine for children and teenagers. Not addictive. Can be used alone or added to a stimulant.
  • Biguanide (diabetes tablet)MetforminThe usual first tablet for type 2 diabetes. Cheap, well proven, and doesn’t cause hypos or weight gain on its own.
  • Stimulant (methylphenidate)MethylphenidateThe most widely used ADHD medicine, in short- and long-acting forms. A controlled (Schedule 8) medicine with PBS age-at-diagnosis rules.
  • Antidepressant (NaSSA)MirtazapineAn antidepressant taken at night. Often causes drowsiness and a bigger appetite, which can help some people and be a problem for others.
  • Wakefulness-promoting agentModafinilA tablet that helps people stay awake. Used for excessive daytime sleepiness from narcolepsy, sleep apnoea or shift work.
  • Acid-reducing medicineProton pump inhibitors (PPIs)Tablets that turn down stomach acid. Used for reflux, ulcers and H. pylori. Best at the lowest dose for the shortest time.
  • Atypical antipsychoticQuetiapineAn antipsychotic for schizophrenia and bipolar disorder. Low doses for sleep or anxiety are not approved and not recommended.
  • GLP-1 receptor agonistSemaglutideCopies a natural gut hormone that controls blood sugar and appetite. Used for type 2 diabetes and weight management.
  • SSRI antidepressantSertralineA widely used antidepressant for depression, OCD, panic and other anxiety conditions. One of the best-studied in pregnancy and breastfeeding.
  • Medicine familySGLT2 inhibitorsEmpagliflozin, dapagliflozin and relatives compared. Diabetes tablets that also protect the heart and kidneys.
  • Cholesterol-lowering medicineStatinsA family of tablets that lower LDL cholesterol and cut heart attacks and strokes. Muscle aches are usually not the statin.
  • GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonistTirzepatideCopies two natural gut hormones that control blood sugar and appetite. Used for type 2 diabetes, weight management and sleep apnoea with obesity.
