Drug info

Colchicine

Anti-inflammatory (gout) Prescription only (S4)

An old plant-derived medicine for gout attacks. Low doses work as well as high doses, with far fewer side effects. Overdose is dangerous.

Quick answer: Colchicine eases gout attacks if started early, and prevents flares when starting urate-lowering treatment. The modern low-dose regimen is as effective as the old ‘until diarrhoea’ approach. It has a narrow safety margin and serious interactions.

Brand names

  • Colgout, Lengout

How it works

Colchicine disrupts microtubules in white blood cells, stopping them swarming into an inflamed joint.

Used for

  • Gout attacks (early)
  • Flare prevention for 3 to 6 months when starting allopurinol
  • Pericarditis (with anti-inflammatories)
  • Familial Mediterranean fever
  • Some cardiology uses for heart attack prevention

Side effects

Gut side effects are common; serious toxicity mostly happens with high doses or interactions.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Diarrhoea
  • Nausea, vomiting
  • Tummy cramps

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Muscle damage (especially with statins or kidney disease)
  • Low blood counts
  • Toxicity from overdose: can be fatal

Get help urgently if

  • Severe diarrhoea or vomiting
  • Muscle weakness or pain
  • Any overdose: call Poisons Information 13 11 26 or 000

Good to know

  • Don’t take more than prescribed: more doesn’t work better.
  • Check interactions: clarithromycin, erythromycin, some antifungals (ketoconazole, itraconazole), some HIV medicines, verapamil, diltiazem, ciclosporin and grapefruit juice can raise levels dangerously.
  • Lower doses in kidney or liver disease.
  • Store well away from children.

Common questions

How much colchicine should I take for gout?

Usually 1 mg at the first sign of an attack, then 0.5 mg an hour later, no more than 1.5 mg on the first day. Follow your doctor’s plan.

Why is colchicine dangerous in overdose?

The gap between an effective and a toxic dose is small. Overdose can cause organ failure. Keep it locked away from children.

Can I take colchicine with a statin?

Often yes, with care, but report any muscle pain or weakness.
