Drug info

Calcium supplements

Mineral supplement Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

Food first. Supplements help when your diet falls short, especially with osteoporosis treatment, but more isn’t better.

Quick answer: Adults need about 1,000 to 1,300 mg of calcium a day, mostly from food (dairy, calcium-set tofu, fortified milks, canned fish with bones). Supplements top up a low intake, often with vitamin D, alongside osteoporosis medicines. They slightly raise the risk of kidney stones and constipation; whether they affect heart risk is debated.

At a glance

For
Low dietary calcium, Osteoporosis treatment, Preventing fractures on its own
Evidence
Good for: Low dietary calcium, Osteoporosis treatment
Cost
About $10 to $20 a month
Dose
Usually 500 to 600 mg a day, as a top-up. Carbonate with food; citrate any time. Don’t take more than 500 mg at once.
Watch for
Constipation, bloating

Evidence first, marketing second.

Brand names

  • Caltrate, Ostelin Calcium and Vitamin D, Blackmores Bio Calcium, Citracal

How it works

Calcium builds bone. Vitamin D helps the gut absorb it.

Used for

  • Low dietary calcium
  • Osteoporosis treatment
  • Preventing fractures on its own

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • ✅ Low dietary calciumTops up intake to recommended levels.
  • ✅ Osteoporosis treatmentUsed with vitamin D alongside bone medicines when intake is low.
  • 🟡 Preventing fractures on its ownSmall or no effect in people with an adequate diet.
  • ❌ Calcium for strong bones when diet is already adequateExtra beyond needs doesn’t add benefit.

Avoid or check first if

  • You’ve had kidney stones
  • You take levothyroxine, iron, bisphosphonates or some antibiotics: separate doses by at least 2 to 4 hours

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Constipation, bloating

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Kidney stones
  • High calcium levels with very high doses (thirst, confusion)

Get help urgently if

  • Signs of an allergic reaction: swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing (call 000)

Good to know

  • A glass of milk, a tub of yoghurt and a slice of cheese each give about 300 mg.
  • Calcium citrate is gentler if you take acid-suppressing medicines.

Common questions

Should I take calcium for my bones?

Only if your diet is low in calcium. Food sources are preferred. With osteoporosis, your doctor may suggest a top-up.

Do calcium supplements cause heart attacks?

Some studies raised concern, others didn’t. Getting calcium from food avoids the question.
