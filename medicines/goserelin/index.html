Drug info

Zoladex (Goserelin)

GnRH agonist (hormone blocker) Prescription only (S4)

A tiny implant injected under the skin of the tummy that switches off the ovaries or testes for 1 or 3 months at a time. Powerful, reversible, and comes with a menopause-like package.

Quick answer: Zoladex (goserelin) is an implant given into the skin of the tummy every 4 weeks (3.6 mg) or 12 weeks (10.8 mg). After an initial short surge, it shuts down oestrogen or testosterone. It treats prostate and breast cancer, endometriosis, and shrinks fibroids before surgery. Side effects come from low hormones: hot flushes, sweats, low libido, mood changes, vaginal dryness and bone thinning with longer use.

Brand names

  • Zoladex 3.6 mg (monthly) and Zoladex LA 10.8 mg (3-monthly) implants

How it works

Goserelin is a copy of GnRH, the brain hormone that tells the pituitary to switch on the ovaries or testes. Given continuously instead of in pulses, it first overstimulates, then switches the system off, so oestrogen or testosterone drops to menopausal or castrate levels.

Used for

  • Prostate cancer: advanced or high-risk, often with radiotherapy (TGA-approved)
  • Breast cancer: hormone-sensitive, in women before menopause (TGA-approved)
  • Endometriosis: pain relief, usually for up to 6 months, often with add-back hormone therapy (TGA-approved)
  • Fibroids: shrinking them and correcting anaemia before surgery (TGA-approved)
  • IVF: suppressing natural cycles before stimulation

Side effects

Nearly everyone gets low-hormone effects: hot flushes and sweats are very common, plus lower libido, mood changes and headaches. Women get vaginal dryness and periods stop; men may get erection problems and breast swelling. Longer courses thin the bones. In prostate cancer, symptoms can briefly flare in the first weeks.

Get help urgently if

  • Sudden severe headache with vomiting or vision loss
  • New back pain with leg weakness, numbness or bladder problems (prostate cancer). Call 000
  • Heavy bleeding or a growing lump at the injection site
  • Low mood with thoughts of self-harm. Call Lifeline 13 11 14

Good to know

  • Bones: for courses longer than a few months, ask about calcium, vitamin D, exercise and a bone density scan.
  • Add-back HRT (a little oestrogen and progestogen) for endometriosis reduces flushes and bone loss without undoing the benefit.
  • Contraception: periods usually stop, but use a non-hormonal method; it’s not a contraceptive and harms pregnancy.
  • Prostate cancer: an anti-androgen tablet is often given for the first weeks to block the flare. Men on long-term treatment need heart, sugar and bone checks.
  • Effects wear off over a few months after the last implant.

Common questions

What is Zoladex used for?

Prostate cancer, hormone-sensitive breast cancer before menopause, endometriosis, shrinking fibroids before surgery, and IVF. It switches off the body’s sex hormones.

What are the side effects of Zoladex?

Mostly menopause-like effects from low hormones: hot flushes, sweats, low libido, mood changes, headaches, vaginal dryness and, over longer courses, bone thinning. Men may have erection problems and breast swelling.

Is Zoladex a contraceptive?

No. Periods usually stop, but you should still use a non-hormonal contraceptive, because pregnancy is possible early on and the medicine can harm a pregnancy.
