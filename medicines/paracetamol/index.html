Drug info

Paracetamol

Pain and fever reliever Over the counter

Australia’s most-used pain and fever medicine. Gentle on the stomach and safe at the right dose, but one of the commonest causes of serious overdose.

Quick answer: Paracetamol eases mild to moderate pain and brings down fever. At the right dose it has few side effects and suits most people, including in pregnancy. The danger is taking too much: more than the daily maximum, or doubling up with combination products, can seriously damage the liver.

The quiet workhorse of the medicine cabinet. Respect the maximum dose.

Brand names

  • Panadol, Panamax, Herron, Panadol Osteo (slow release), and many generics; also in many combination products

How it works

Exactly how paracetamol works is still debated. It acts mainly in the brain and spinal cord, reducing pain signals and resetting the temperature control centre. Unlike anti-inflammatories it does little to inflammation and is easy on the stomach.

Used for

  • Mild to moderate pain: headache, toothache, period pain, muscle aches
  • Fever in adults and children
  • Often combined with an anti-inflammatory for stronger relief
  • Large reviews found it adds little on its own for low back pain and only a small benefit in osteoarthritis

Side effects

At normal doses side effects are rare. The big risk is liver damage from overdose, which can happen with as little as a day or two of taking too much.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Skin rash
  • Low blood counts

Very rare fewer than 1 in 10,000

  • Severe skin reactions
  • Liver damage (mainly with overdose or heavy alcohol use)

Get help urgently if

  • Any overdose, even if you feel well: call the Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26 or go to hospital. Treatment works best within 8 hours
  • Yellow skin or eyes, or severe tummy pain
  • A rash with blistering or peeling skin

Good to know

  • Count every product: many cold and flu tablets contain paracetamol.
  • People who weigh under 50 kg, drink heavily, are frail or have liver disease may need lower doses.
  • Children’s doses go by weight, not age: use the right strength liquid and an oral syringe.
  • Since 2025, pack sizes sold outside pharmacies are smaller to reduce overdoses.

Common questions

What is the maximum dose of paracetamol?

For most adults, 1 g (two 500 mg tablets) every 4 to 6 hours, with no more than 4 g in 24 hours. Lower doses may be needed if you’re small, frail, drink heavily or have liver disease.

Is paracetamol safe in pregnancy?

Paracetamol is considered the safest pain and fever medicine in pregnancy when used at the lowest effective dose for the shortest time.

Can I take paracetamol and ibuprofen together?

Yes, for most adults. They work differently and together give better pain relief. Stick to each one’s dose limits.

What happens if you take too much paracetamol?

It can cause serious liver damage, often with no symptoms for the first day. Get help straight away: Poisons Information Centre 13 11 26.
