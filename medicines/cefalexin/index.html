Drug info

Cefalexin

Cephalosporin antibiotic Prescription only (S4)

A first-generation cephalosporin antibiotic, commonly used for skin infections like cellulitis, and some urine infections, including in pregnancy.

Quick answer: Cefalexin treats skin and soft tissue infections (cellulitis, infected wounds), some urine infections and some other infections. It’s related to penicillin, but most people with a mild penicillin allergy can take it safely; people with a severe penicillin reaction should check first.

The skin infection workhorse.

Brand names

  • Keflex, Ibilex, Cefalexin Sandoz and other generics

How it works

Like penicillins, cefalexin blocks bacteria from building their cell walls.

Used for

  • Cellulitis and other skin and soft tissue infections
  • Urine infections, including in pregnancy
  • Some bone and joint infections
  • Prevention before some dental or surgical procedures in specific cases

Side effects

Generally well tolerated.

Get help urgently if

  • Swelling of the face or throat, or trouble breathing: call 000
  • Severe or bloody diarrhoea
  • Spreading redness with fever despite treatment

Good to know

  • Tell your doctor about any penicillin or cephalosporin allergy and what happened.
  • With cellulitis, mark the edge of the redness with a pen so you can see if it’s spreading or shrinking.
  • Lower doses may be needed in kidney disease.

Common questions

Can I take cefalexin if I’m allergic to penicillin?

Most people with a mild or uncertain penicillin allergy can. If you’ve had anaphylaxis or a severe skin reaction to penicillin, check with your doctor first.

How quickly does cefalexin work for cellulitis?

You should see improvement within 2 to 3 days. The redness can spread a little in the first day before it improves; see your doctor if it keeps spreading or you feel worse.

Is cefalexin safe in pregnancy?

Yes. It’s commonly used for urine and skin infections in pregnancy.
