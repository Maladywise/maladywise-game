Antihistamines

H1 antihistamines Pharmacy medicines (S2/S3)

Block histamine, the chemical behind itchy eyes, sneezing and hives. The modern ones barely cause drowsiness; the old ones knock you out.

Quick answer: Non-sedating antihistamines treat hay fever, hives and itchy allergic reactions. Older sedating ones (like promethazine) are rarely needed and shouldn’t be used in young children. Antihistamines don’t treat anaphylaxis: adrenaline does.

Great for sneezes, useless for anaphylaxis.

Brand names

  • Non-sedating: Zyrtec (cetirizine), Claratyne (loratadine), Telfast (fexofenadine), Aerius (desloratadine), Xyzal (levocetirizine). Sedating: Phenergan (promethazine), Polaramine (dexchlorpheniramine)

How it works

Histamine is released from mast cells in allergic reactions and causes itch, swelling, sneezing and redness. Antihistamines block its H1 receptor.

Used for

  • Hay fever (allergic rhinitis), often with a steroid nasal spray
  • Hives: doctors may increase the dose up to 4 times the usual for chronic hives
  • Itchy eczema flares (limited benefit)
  • Mild allergic reactions without breathing problems

Side effects

Non-sedating antihistamines are very well tolerated.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Drowsiness (more with cetirizine; strong with sedating types)
  • Headache
  • Dry mouth

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Tummy upset

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Paradoxical agitation in children (sedating types)
  • Breathing suppression in under-2s (promethazine: don’t use)

Get help urgently if

  • Swelling of the tongue or throat, wheeze or collapse: use adrenaline and call 000
  • Hives lasting more than 6 weeks: see your doctor

Good to know

  • Sedating antihistamines impair driving like alcohol; avoid driving.
  • Don’t use sedating antihistamines in children under 2.
  • For hay fever, a steroid nasal spray is often more effective than tablets.
  • Loratadine and cetirizine are generally considered fine in pregnancy and breastfeeding: check with your pharmacist.

Common questions

Which antihistamine is least drowsy?

Fexofenadine and loratadine cause the least drowsiness; cetirizine slightly more. Older types like promethazine are very sedating.

Can antihistamines treat anaphylaxis?

No. Anaphylaxis needs adrenaline (EpiPen or Anapen) straight away and a call to 000. Antihistamines only help itch and hives.

Can I take antihistamines every day?

Yes, non-sedating antihistamines are safe to take daily through the hay fever season or for chronic hives.
