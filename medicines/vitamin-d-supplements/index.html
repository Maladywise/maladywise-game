Drug info

Vitamin D supplements

Cholecalciferol (vitamin D3) Pharmacy and supermarket

Treatment doses for a low level are often higher than people expect: Australian guidance suggests 3,000 to 5,000 IU a day for 6 to 12 weeks for moderate deficiency.

Quick answer: Vitamin D supports bone and muscle health. Australian and New Zealand guidance sets adequacy at 50 nmol/L or more (end of winter). Mild deficiency is 30 to 49 nmol/L, moderate 12.5 to 29, and severe below 12.5. Most people then need about 1,000 to 2,000 IU a day to maintain a normal level.

More than you think to fix it, less to keep it.

Brand names

  • Ostelin, Blackmores, Nature’s Way and many others; some combined with calcium

How it works

Vitamin D3 is converted in the liver and kidneys to the active hormone, which increases calcium absorption and supports bone mineralisation and muscle function.

Used for

  • Treating vitamin D deficiency
  • Preventing deficiency in people at risk
  • Alongside osteoporosis treatment
  • Pregnancy and breastfeeding when levels are low

Side effects

At recommended doses it’s very safe.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • High calcium from long-term very high doses (nausea, thirst, confusion, kidney stones)

Get help urgently if

  • Excessive thirst, confusion or vomiting on high doses
  • Low levels that don’t rise with treatment: ask about coeliac disease or other malabsorption

Good to know

  • Example: a level of 28 nmol/L is moderate deficiency, so guidance suggests 3,000 to 5,000 IU daily for 6 to 12 weeks, then maintenance.
  • Take with a meal containing fat.
  • Sensible sun exposure helps, balanced against skin cancer risk (Australia has very high UV).
  • Routine testing isn’t needed for most healthy people; testing is for those at risk.

Common questions

How much vitamin D should I take for a low level?

Australian and New Zealand guidance suggests 3,000 to 5,000 IU daily for at least 6 to 12 weeks for moderate to severe deficiency (below 30 nmol/L), then around 1,000 to 2,000 IU daily. Check your plan with your doctor.

What is a normal vitamin D level?

50 nmol/L or above at the end of winter is considered adequate in Australia.

Can you take too much vitamin D?

Yes, but toxicity usually needs very high doses for a long time. Stick to recommended doses.
