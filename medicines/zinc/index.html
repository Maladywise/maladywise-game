Drug info

Zinc supplements

Mineral supplement Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

Zinc lozenges started early may shorten a cold a little. Long-term high doses can cause copper deficiency and nerve problems.

Quick answer: Zinc treats deficiency. Lozenges started within a day of cold symptoms may shorten colds slightly, though recent reviews rate the evidence as uncertain. More than 40 mg a day long term can block copper absorption, causing anaemia and nerve damage.

Brand names

How it works

Zinc is needed for immune cells, wound healing, taste and smell. It may interfere with cold viruses in the throat.

Used for

  • Zinc deficiency
  • Colds
  • Acne

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • ✅ Zinc deficiencyReplaces low zinc (more common with alcohol, gut conditions, vegan diets).
  • 🟡 ColdsLozenges started within 24 hours may shorten colds slightly; evidence is uncertain.
  • 🟡 AcneSmall benefit in some trials, less than standard treatments.
  • ❌ Preventing COVID-19No good evidence.
  • ❌ Nasal zinc spraysLinked to permanent loss of smell: avoid.

Avoid or check first if

  • You take antibiotics (tetracyclines, quinolones): separate by at least 2 hours
  • You’ve been taking more than 40 mg a day for months

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Nausea, especially on an empty stomach
  • Metallic taste (lozenges)

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Copper deficiency with long-term high doses (anaemia, numbness, walking problems)

Get help urgently if

  • Signs of an allergic reaction: swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing (call 000)

Good to know

  • Take with food if it makes you nauseous.
  • Many ‘immune’ products combine zinc with vitamin C; add up the total zinc you take.
  • Never use zinc nasal sprays.

Common questions

Does zinc stop colds?

It may shorten a cold slightly if lozenges are started within a day of symptoms. It doesn’t reliably prevent them.

Can you take too much zinc?

Yes. Over 40 mg a day long term can cause copper deficiency, anaemia and nerve damage.
