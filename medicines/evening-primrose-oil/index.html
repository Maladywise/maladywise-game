Drug info

Evening primrose oil

Herbal medicine Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

Long popular for eczema, breast pain and hot flushes. Good-quality trials show it doesn’t work for any of them.

Quick answer: A Cochrane review of eczema trials found evening primrose oil no better than placebo. Trials for breast pain, PMS and menopausal hot flushes also show no clear benefit. It’s mostly safe, but may raise bleeding risk with blood thinners.

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • ❌ EczemaNo benefit (Cochrane).
  • ❌ Breast painNo better than placebo.
  • ❌ Hot flushesNo clear benefit.
  • ❌ PMSNo clear benefit.

Avoid or check first if

  • You take blood thinners
  • You take phenothiazine antipsychotics (seizure reports)

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Good to know

  • For eczema, moisturisers and steroid creams are proven.
  • For hot flushes, menopause hormone therapy is the most effective option.

Common questions

Does evening primrose oil help eczema?

No. A Cochrane review found it no better than placebo.

Does it help hot flushes?

Trials show no clear benefit.
