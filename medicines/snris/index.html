Drug info

Venlafaxine and desvenlafaxine (SNRIs)

SNRI antidepressant Prescription only (S4)

Antidepressants that boost serotonin and noradrenaline. Effective for depression and anxiety, but stopping suddenly causes notorious withdrawal symptoms.

Quick answer: Venlafaxine and desvenlafaxine treat depression and anxiety disorders and can reduce menopausal hot flushes. They take 2 to 6 weeks to work. Missing doses or stopping quickly often causes dizziness, ‘brain zaps’ and irritability, so doses are reduced slowly.

At a glance

For
Depression, generalised anxiety, social anxiety, panic disorder; hot flushes (off-label)
Works
Similar to other antidepressants

Kicks in
2 to 6 weeks

Effective, with a sting in the tail if you stop fast.

Brand names

  • Venlafaxine: Efexor-XR, Enlafax XR, Elaxine SR. Desvenlafaxine: Pristiq, Desfax, Desven

How it works

SNRIs block reuptake of serotonin and noradrenaline, increasing their signalling between nerve cells.

Used for

  • Major depression
  • Generalised anxiety, social anxiety and panic disorder
  • Hot flushes when hormone therapy isn’t suitable (off-label)
  • Some nerve pain (duloxetine is the SNRI more often used)

Side effects

Many early side effects settle in 1 to 2 weeks.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Very common more than 1 in 10

  • Nausea
  • Sweating

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Insomnia or drowsiness
  • Dry mouth, constipation
  • Headache, dizziness
  • Sexual side effects
  • Raised blood pressure (higher doses)

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Low sodium in older people

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Serotonin syndrome with other serotonergic drugs
  • Suicidal thoughts in under-25s when starting

Get help urgently if

  • Thoughts of suicide or self-harm: call Lifeline 13 11 14 or 000
  • Agitation, fever, shaking and muscle jerks (serotonin syndrome)
  • Severe headache with very high blood pressure

Good to know

  • Take at the same time daily, with food.
  • Don’t stop suddenly: taper with your doctor, sometimes over weeks to months.
  • Check blood pressure.
  • Avoid St John’s wort, tramadol and other serotonergic medicines unless checked.

Common questions

What is venlafaxine withdrawal like?

Dizziness, ‘brain zaps’, nausea, irritability and vivid dreams, sometimes within a day of a missed dose. Slow tapering reduces this.

Is desvenlafaxine the same as venlafaxine?

Desvenlafaxine is the active substance venlafaxine turns into. They work similarly.

Does venlafaxine help hot flushes?

Yes, it reduces flushes, though less than hormone therapy.
