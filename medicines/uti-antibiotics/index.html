Antibiotics for urine infections (UTI)

Antibiotics Prescription only (S4); some pharmacist-supplied

Short courses of antibiotic clear most uncomplicated bladder infections in women within a few days. Pharmacists in several states can now supply some of them.

Quick answer: Uncomplicated bladder infections (cystitis) in women are usually treated with trimethoprim for 3 days, nitrofurantoin for 5 days, or cefalexin for 5 days. Men, pregnant women, kidney infections and recurrent infections need longer or different treatment. A urine culture helps when infections keep coming back or don’t respond.

Brand names

  • Trimethoprim: Alprim, Triprim. Nitrofurantoin: Macrodantin, Furadantin. Cefalexin: Keflex. Fosfomycin: Monurol. Amoxicillin-clavulanate: Augmentin

How it works

Each works in a different way, but all reach high levels in urine. Nitrofurantoin barely enters the bloodstream, so it’s only for bladder infections, not kidney infections.

Used for

  • Uncomplicated bladder infections in women: trimethoprim (3 days), nitrofurantoin (5 days) or cefalexin (5 days)
  • Pregnancy: cefalexin, nitrofurantoin (not near term) or amoxicillin-clavulanate, guided by culture
  • Men: usually 7 days, and a urine culture
  • Kidney infection: different antibiotics and longer courses, sometimes hospital
  • Fosfomycin: a single dose, mostly for resistant bacteria
  • Low-dose prevention for recurrent UTIs in some people

Side effects

Usually mild.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Nausea
  • Rash and itch
  • Headache
  • Thrush

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • High potassium (trimethoprim, especially with some blood pressure medicines)
  • Dark yellow or brown urine (nitrofurantoin, harmless)

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Lung or liver reactions with long-term nitrofurantoin
  • Low blood counts (trimethoprim)
  • Severe skin reactions

Get help urgently if

  • Fever, shivers, back or side pain, or vomiting: possible kidney infection, see a doctor today
  • No improvement after 48 hours
  • Blood in the urine that persists after treatment

Good to know

  • Drink normally; there’s no need to force fluids.
  • See a doctor if you’re pregnant, male, have symptoms coming back, or have kidney disease.
  • Nitrofurantoin isn’t suitable with poor kidney function.
  • For recurrent UTIs, ask about non-antibiotic options too (vaginal oestrogen after menopause, methenamine).

Common questions

How long do UTI antibiotics take to work?

Most people feel better within 1 to 2 days. If you’re not improving after 48 hours, or develop fever or back pain, see a doctor.

Can I get UTI antibiotics from a pharmacist?

In several Australian states, pharmacists can now supply antibiotics for uncomplicated UTIs in women aged about 18 to 65 under specific rules.

Why is my urine brown on nitrofurantoin?

Nitrofurantoin can turn urine dark yellow or brown. It’s harmless.
