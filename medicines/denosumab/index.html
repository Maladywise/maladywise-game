Drug info

Denosumab (Prolia)

RANKL antibody Prescription only (S4)

A twice-yearly injection that strongly protects bones, but its effect wears off fast. Never just stop it: you need a plan.

Quick answer: Denosumab blocks a signal that switches on bone-resorbing cells. It reduces spine fractures by about two-thirds. Unlike bisphosphonates, it doesn’t stay in bone: if a dose is late or stopped, bone loss rebounds and multiple spine fractures can happen, so a bisphosphonate is usually given afterwards.

Brand names

  • Prolia (osteoporosis). Xgeva is a different, higher-dose product for cancer in bone

How it works

Denosumab is an antibody against RANKL, the signal osteoclasts need to form and work.

Used for

  • Postmenopausal osteoporosis
  • Osteoporosis in men
  • Bone loss from hormone treatments for breast or prostate cancer
  • When bisphosphonates aren’t suitable (e.g. poor kidney function)

Side effects

Usually well tolerated.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Joint or muscle aches
  • Back pain

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Low calcium
  • Skin infections (cellulitis), eczema

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Osteonecrosis of the jaw
  • Atypical thigh-bone fracture
  • Multiple spine fractures after stopping without follow-on treatment

Get help urgently if

  • Muscle cramps, tingling around the mouth (low calcium)
  • Sudden back pain after a missed or stopped dose
  • Thigh pain, jaw pain

Good to know

  • Book every dose on time: no more than about 4 weeks late.
  • Never stop without a plan; your doctor usually follows it with zoledronic acid or alendronate.
  • Keep calcium and vitamin D adequate.
  • Have a dental check before starting.

Common questions

What happens if I stop Prolia?

Bone loss rebounds within months, and some people get several spine fractures. If stopping, a bisphosphonate is usually given to lock in the benefit.

How often is denosumab given?

Every 6 months, as an injection under the skin.

Is denosumab better than alendronate?

Both reduce fractures. Denosumab increases density more and suits poor kidney function, but must be continued or followed by another treatment.
