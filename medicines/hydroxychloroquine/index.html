Drug info

Hydroxychloroquine

Antimalarial DMARD Prescription only (S4)

A gentle, long-term immune modulator for lupus and arthritis. Safe for most, with yearly eye checks after 5 years.

Quick answer: Hydroxychloroquine reduces flares and organ damage in lupus and helps rheumatoid arthritis, usually alongside other medicines. It works slowly. The main long-term risk is damage to the retina, which is rare if the dose stays at or below 5 mg/kg of actual body weight a day and screening is done.

Brand names

  • Plaquenil, Hydroxychloroquine generics

How it works

It accumulates in immune cells and dampens signals (such as toll-like receptors) that drive autoimmune inflammation.

Used for

  • Systemic lupus erythematosus (recommended for nearly all)
  • Rheumatoid arthritis (often combined)
  • Sjögren’s syndrome
  • Cutaneous lupus and some other skin conditions

Side effects

Generally well tolerated.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Nausea, diarrhoea
  • Headache
  • Rash

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Skin darkening
  • Low blood sugar

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Retinal damage (risk rises after 5 years and above recommended doses)
  • Heart rhythm problems (QT prolongation)
  • Severe skin reactions

Get help urgently if

  • Any change in vision
  • Fainting or palpitations
  • Blistering rash

Good to know

  • Have a baseline eye check, then yearly screening after 5 years (earlier with risk factors).
  • Take with food.
  • Generally continued in pregnancy for lupus: it lowers flare risk.
  • Keep out of reach of children: overdose is very dangerous.

Common questions

Why do I need eye tests on hydroxychloroquine?

Long-term use can damage the retina. Screening finds it early, before vision is affected.

Is hydroxychloroquine safe in pregnancy?

Yes, for lupus it’s usually continued, as stopping raises the risk of flares.

How long does hydroxychloroquine take to work?

Usually 2 to 3 months, sometimes up to 6.
