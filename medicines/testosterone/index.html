Drug info

Testosterone therapy

Androgen Prescription only (S4); Schedule-controlled

Replaces testosterone when the testes or pituitary truly can’t make enough. Not a fix for ageing or low energy with normal levels.

Quick answer: In men, testosterone is prescribed when symptoms match AND repeated morning blood tests confirm a low level with an identifiable cause (hypogonadism). It shuts down sperm production, so it’s not for men wanting children. In Australia, a low-dose cream is also approved for low libido in postmenopausal women.

Brand names

  • Men: Testogel, Androforte, Reandron (injection), Primoteston. Women: AndroFeme 1 (cream)

How it works

Testosterone acts on androgen receptors throughout the body: muscle, bone, skin, brain and the blood-forming marrow.

Used for

  • Men with hypogonadism from testicular, pituitary or genetic causes (e.g. Klinefelter syndrome)
  • Gender-affirming care
  • Postmenopausal women with distressing low desire (after other causes are considered)

Side effects

Monitoring blood counts and levels keeps it safe.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • High red blood cell count (polycythaemia)
  • Acne, oily skin
  • Skin irritation from gel
  • Breast tenderness or growth

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Fluid retention
  • Worsening sleep apnoea

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Blood clots
  • Prostate problems (PSA monitoring)

Get help urgently if

  • Headache, flushing or redness with a high blood count
  • Calf swelling or breathlessness
  • Children or partners exposed to gel developing body hair or acne

Good to know

  • Testosterone stops sperm production: discuss fertility first.
  • Expect blood tests (testosterone, full blood count, PSA in men over 40) at about 3 and 12 months, then yearly.
  • Wash hands after gel and cover the area to avoid transfer to others.

Common questions

How do I know if I have low testosterone?

It needs symptoms plus two low early-morning blood tests and investigation of the cause. Levels fall with obesity, illness and poor sleep, which often improve without testosterone.

Does testosterone affect fertility?

Yes. It suppresses sperm production, sometimes to zero. Men wanting children should discuss other options.

Can women use testosterone?

Yes, a low-dose cream is approved in Australia for postmenopausal women with low sexual desire that causes distress.
