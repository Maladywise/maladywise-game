Drug info

Pregabalin

Gabapentinoid (nerve pain medicine) Prescription only (S4)

A nerve-pain capsule that helps a minority of people a lot and many people not at all. Worth a careful trial; not worth staying on if it isn’t clearly helping.

Quick answer: Pregabalin (Lyrica) treats nerve pain, such as diabetic nerve pain, shingles pain and spinal cord injury pain, and is an add-on for epilepsy. Only about 1 in 4 to 1 in 3 people get a meaningful benefit. It doesn’t help sciatica or most back pain. Dizziness and sleepiness are very common, and weight gain, swelling and blurred vision are common. It can be misused and causes withdrawal if stopped suddenly, and it’s dangerous with opioids.

The “try it, then judge it honestly” medicine. If you can’t tell it’s helping at 4 weeks, it probably isn’t.

Brand names

  • Lyrica, and generic pregabalin brands

How it works

Pregabalin attaches to calcium channels on overactive nerve endings and reduces the release of pain and excitement signals. It doesn’t touch ordinary inflammation pain.

Used for

  • Neuropathic pain, e.g. diabetic nerve pain, after shingles, spinal cord injury (TGA-approved)
  • Add-on treatment for focal seizures (TGA-approved)
  • Off label: fibromyalgia (modest benefit for some), generalised anxiety (approved overseas)
  • Doesn’t help: sciatica (no better than placebo in a large Australian trial) or most low back pain

Side effects

Dizziness and sleepiness affect more than 1 in 10. Weight gain, increased appetite, swelling of the legs, blurred vision, poor concentration, dry mouth and constipation are common. Some people feel euphoric, which is part of why it can be misused.

Very common more than 1 in 10

  • Dizziness
  • Sleepiness

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Weight gain and more appetite
  • Swelling of hands, legs or feet
  • Blurred or double vision
  • Unsteadiness, falls, tremor

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Low mood or agitation
  • Fainting
  • Breathing problems (especially with opioids)
  • Muscle pain
  • Low blood sugar

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Severe allergic reactions or angioedema
  • Heart failure
  • Suicidal thoughts
  • Kidney problems

Get help urgently if

  • Slow or shallow breathing or extreme sleepiness, especially with opioids, alcohol or sedatives. Call 000
  • Swelling of the face, lips or throat
  • Thoughts of self-harm. Call Lifeline 13 11 14
  • New breathlessness or major leg swelling

Good to know

  • Opioids, alcohol, sleeping tablets: combining raises the risk of overdose and breathing problems.
  • Driving: don’t drive until you know how it affects you.
  • Stopping: taper over at least a week; stopping suddenly can cause anxiety, sweats, insomnia, nausea and, rarely, seizures.
  • Misuse and dependence happen, especially with a history of substance use.
  • Lower doses in kidney disease. Ask before pregnancy.

Common questions

Does pregabalin help sciatica?

No. A large Australian randomised trial found pregabalin was no better than placebo for sciatica, and it caused more side effects.

How long does pregabalin take to work?

Some benefit can start within 1–2 weeks. If you can’t clearly tell it’s helping after about 4 weeks at a steady dose, talk to your doctor about tapering off.

Can I stop pregabalin suddenly?

It’s best not to. Taper over at least a week to avoid withdrawal symptoms such as anxiety, insomnia, sweats and nausea.
