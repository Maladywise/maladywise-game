Drug info

Sartans (ARBs)

Blood pressure medicine Prescription only (S4)

Blood pressure medicines that block the hormone angiotensin II. They protect the kidneys and heart, and unlike ACE inhibitors rarely cause a cough.

Quick answer: Sartans (angiotensin II receptor blockers, ARBs) lower blood pressure and protect the kidneys in diabetes and kidney disease. They work like ACE inhibitors but further along the same pathway, so they almost never cause the dry cough. They need blood tests for kidney function and potassium.

Once a day, e.g. candesartan 8 to 32 mg, irbesartan 150 to 300 mg

The ACE inhibitor without the cough.

Brand names

  • Candesartan: Atacand. Irbesartan: Avapro, Karvea. Telmisartan: Micardis. Losartan: Cozaar. Olmesartan: Olmetec. Valsartan: Diovan. Many combinations with a diuretic or amlodipine

How it works

Angiotensin II tightens blood vessels and makes the body keep salt and water. Sartans block its receptor, so vessels relax, the kidneys release salt and pressure falls. They also reduce pressure inside the kidney’s filters, which slows kidney damage.

Used for

  • High blood pressure (first-line)
  • Kidney protection when there’s protein in the urine, especially with diabetes
  • Heart failure (candesartan, valsartan; valsartan also in sacubitril/valsartan, Entresto)
  • An option when an ACE inhibitor causes a cough

Side effects

Usually very well tolerated.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Dizziness, especially at first
  • High potassium
  • Changes in kidney function on blood tests

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Tiredness
  • Low blood pressure

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Angioedema (swelling of the face, lips or tongue), much rarer than with ACE inhibitors
  • Severe diarrhoea and weight loss with olmesartan (sprue-like enteropathy)

Get help urgently if

  • Swelling of the lips, tongue or throat: call 000
  • Fainting or very low blood pressure
  • Weakness or an irregular heartbeat (possible high potassium)

Good to know

  • Not in pregnancy: stop and see your doctor if you’re planning pregnancy or become pregnant.
  • Have kidney and potassium blood tests after starting and after dose changes.
  • Avoid potassium supplements and salt substitutes containing potassium unless advised.
  • ‘Sick day rule’: pause it if you have vomiting, diarrhoea or can’t drink, then restart when well.
  • Avoid regular anti-inflammatories (NSAIDs), especially with a diuretic too.

Common questions

What is the difference between a sartan and an ACE inhibitor?

Both block the same hormone pathway and lower blood pressure similarly. Sartans almost never cause the dry cough that ACE inhibitors can.

Do sartans protect the kidneys?

Yes. In people with protein in the urine, especially with diabetes, they slow the decline in kidney function.

Can I take a sartan when pregnant?

No. They can harm the baby’s kidneys. Talk to your doctor before pregnancy about switching.
