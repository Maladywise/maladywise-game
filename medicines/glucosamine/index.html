Drug info

Glucosamine and chondroitin

Joint supplement Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

One of Australia’s best-selling supplements. Large trials show little or no benefit over placebo for most people.

Quick answer: The large GAIT trial found glucosamine and chondroitin no better than placebo for knee osteoarthritis pain overall. Some trials of glucosamine sulfate showed small benefits, mostly industry-funded. If you try it, give it 3 months and stop if there’s no change. Australian guidelines (RACGP) recommend against it.

How it works

Glucosamine and chondroitin are building blocks of cartilage. Swallowed, they don’t clearly reach or rebuild joints.

Used for

  • Knee osteoarthritis (glucosamine sulfate)

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • 🟡 Knee osteoarthritis (glucosamine sulfate)Small benefit in some trials; others show none.
  • ❌ Rebuilding cartilageNo good evidence.
  • ❌ Glucosamine hydrochloride for knee painNo benefit in GAIT.

Avoid or check first if

  • You take warfarin (can raise the INR)
  • You have a shellfish allergy (most glucosamine comes from shellfish)
  • You have diabetes (monitor sugars)

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Good to know

  • Exercise and weight loss help knee osteoarthritis far more.
  • Stop after 3 months if you notice no difference.

Common questions

Does glucosamine work?

For most people, large trials found it no better than placebo. Australian guidelines recommend against it.

Is glucosamine safe?

Usually. It can raise the INR with warfarin, and most products are made from shellfish.
