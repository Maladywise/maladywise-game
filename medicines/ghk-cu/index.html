Drug info

GHK-Cu (copper peptide)

Cosmetic peptide Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

In skin creams, GHK-Cu is a cosmetic ingredient with small studies behind it. Injectable GHK-Cu is unapproved and the TGA warns against it.

Quick answer: GHK-Cu is a small copper-binding peptide found naturally in blood. Small, mostly older and industry-linked studies suggest creams may modestly improve fine lines and skin firmness. There are no good human trials of injected GHK-Cu, which is sold online as a ‘research peptide’. In 2026 the TGA and Chief Medical Officer warned about unapproved peptides including GHK-Cu, with reports of severe allergic reactions.

How it works

GHK-Cu delivers copper to skin cells and may stimulate collagen and wound repair in lab studies.

Used for

  • Fine lines and skin firmness (creams)

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • 🟡 Fine lines and skin firmness (creams)Small, mostly industry-linked studies show modest improvement.
  • ❌ Injected for anti-ageing, hair or healingNo good human trials; unapproved in Australia.

Avoid or check first if

  • You’re thinking of injecting it: unapproved products may be mislabelled or contaminated
  • You have Wilson disease or copper sensitivity

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Common questions

Does copper peptide cream work?

Small studies suggest a modest effect on fine lines. It’s a cosmetic, not a medicine.

Is injectable GHK-Cu legal in Australia?

It isn’t approved by the TGA. The TGA warns unapproved peptides may be unsafe and unlawful to buy or supply.
