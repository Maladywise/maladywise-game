Drug info

Cranberry for UTIs

Herbal and food supplement Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

Cranberry reduces repeat UTIs in women who get them often. It doesn’t treat an infection you already have.

Quick answer: A 2023 Cochrane review found cranberry products reduce the risk of repeat symptomatic UTIs in women with recurrent UTIs, in children, and after bladder procedures. It doesn’t help older people in care homes much, and it doesn’t treat an active infection: that needs antibiotics.

Brand names

  • Ethical Nutrients Urinary Tract Support, Blackmores Cranberry, Ocean Spray juice

How it works

Proanthocyanidins (PACs) stop E. coli sticking to the bladder wall.

Used for

  • Preventing recurrent UTIs in women
  • Preventing UTIs in children

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • ✅ Preventing recurrent UTIs in womenFewer repeat infections (Cochrane 2023).
  • ✅ Preventing UTIs in childrenFewer infections.
  • ❌ Treating an active UTIDoesn’t work: see a doctor.
  • ❌ UTI prevention in care homesLittle or no benefit.

Avoid or check first if

  • You take warfarin (large amounts may raise the INR)
  • You’ve had kidney stones (oxalate)

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Common questions

Does cranberry treat a UTI?

No. It can help prevent repeat UTIs, but an active infection needs antibiotics.

Who benefits from cranberry?

Women with recurrent UTIs, children prone to UTIs, and people after some bladder procedures.
