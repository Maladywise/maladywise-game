Drug info

Multivitamins

Vitamin supplement Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

For most well-fed adults, a multivitamin is expensive urine. It makes sense for pregnancy, restricted diets, and some older adults.

Quick answer: Large trials in healthy adults found multivitamins don’t prevent heart disease, cancer or early death. They’re reasonable when diet is restricted (vegan, very low intake, after bariatric surgery) and in pregnancy (as a pregnancy-specific product). Watch the vitamin B6 content: many Australians develop nerve damage from B6 across several products.

Brand names

  • Centrum, Swisse Ultivite, Blackmores Multivitamin, Cenovis, Nature’s Way; pregnancy: Elevit, Blackmores Pregnancy

How it works

A mixture of vitamins and minerals at or above recommended daily amounts.

Used for

  • Restricted diets
  • Pregnancy
  • Memory in older adults

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • ✅ Restricted dietsVegan (especially B12), very low intake, after weight-loss surgery.
  • ✅ PregnancyPregnancy-specific multivitamins provide folic acid and iodine.
  • 🟡 Memory in older adultsOne large trial (COSMOS) suggested a small benefit; needs confirming.
  • ❌ Preventing heart disease or cancerNo benefit in large trials and the US Preventive Services Task Force review.
  • ❌ More energyNo effect unless you’re deficient.

Avoid or check first if

  • You take several supplements: add up vitamin B6, vitamin A, zinc and iron
  • You smoke (avoid high-dose beta-carotene)
  • You’re pregnant (use a pregnancy formula, not a standard one)

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Upset stomach
  • Bright yellow urine (riboflavin, harmless)

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Nerve damage from vitamin B6 across combined products
  • Vitamin A excess (liver, bone, pregnancy harm) with high-dose products

Get help urgently if

  • Signs of an allergic reaction: swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing (call 000)
  • Yellow skin or eyes, dark urine or severe tiredness (possible liver injury)

Good to know

  • Food first: a varied diet beats a pill.
  • Vegans need reliable vitamin B12.
  • Keep iron-containing vitamins away from children: iron overdose is dangerous.

Common questions

Is it worth taking a multivitamin?

For most healthy adults eating a varied diet, probably not. Trials show no protection against heart disease or cancer.

Can multivitamins be harmful?

Usually not at standard doses, but vitamin B6 adds up across products and can cause nerve damage. Check every label.
