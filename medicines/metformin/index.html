Drug info

Metformin

Biguanide (diabetes tablet) Prescription only

The first-choice tablet for type 2 diabetes for decades. Cheap, well proven, and doesn’t cause hypos or weight gain on its own. Your gut may need a few weeks to warm to it.

At a glance

For
Type 2 diabetes. Also used for prediabetes, polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) and diabetes in pregnancy
Works
Lowers HbA1c by about 1% on its own. In the UKPDS trial in overweight people, it cut deaths by about a third over 10 years. In prediabetes, it cuts the chance of diabetes by about a third (lifestyle change does even better)
Cost
Under the $25 PBS co-payment, about $22–23 a script for generics (brand premium extra); $7.70 concession. 60-day scripts available
Kicks in
Sugars start falling within days; near full effect in about 2 weeks; HbA1c shows it by 3 months
Taken for
Usually long term
Watch for
Nausea, diarrhoea, tummy pain, less appetite, metallic taste

Take it with meals, start low and build up. Slow-release (XR) is kinder to the gut. Swallowing a full dose on an empty stomach is a bold move your bowels will remember.

Brand names

  • Diabex, Diaformin, plus generic brands sold as metformin
  • Slow-release: Diabex XR, Diaformin XR, Metex XR and generic XR brands
  • In combination tablets: Janumet, Jardiance Duo, Xigduo XR, Galvumet and others

How it works

Mainly makes the liver release less glucose, and helps the body respond better to its own insulin. It doesn’t make the pancreas pump out more insulin, so on its own it rarely causes hypos.

Used for

  • Type 2 diabetes, alone or with other diabetes medicines, including insulin (TGA-approved)
  • Prediabetes and polycystic ovary syndrome (off-label)
  • Diabetes in pregnancy, under specialist care

Side effects

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Very common more than 1 in 10

  • Nausea, vomiting
  • Diarrhoea
  • Tummy pain
  • Less appetite

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Taste changes (often metallic)
  • Low vitamin B12 with long-term use

Very rare fewer than 1 in 10,000

  • Lactic acidosis
  • Abnormal liver tests or hepatitis
  • Skin reactions: redness, itch, hives

Get help urgently if

  • Feeling very unwell with fast breathing, muscle pain or cramps, tummy pain, vomiting, drowsiness or feeling cold (possible lactic acidosis, rare)
  • Vomiting or diarrhoea so bad you can’t keep fluids down: pause metformin and see a doctor the same day

Good to know

  • Sick days: if you’re vomiting, have diarrhoea or can’t drink properly, pause metformin until you’re eating and drinking normally again.
  • Kidneys: the dose is lowered if kidney function drops, and it’s stopped if kidney function is very low. Kidney blood tests at least once a year.
  • Scans and surgery: tell the team you take it. It may be paused around operations and X-ray dye (contrast) if your kidneys aren’t at full strength. Heavy drinking raises the risk of lactic acidosis.
  • Vitamin B12 can drop over years. Tingling or numb feet aren’t always the diabetes, so B12 is worth checking. See B12 deficiency.

Common questions

Does metformin cause weight loss?

It’s weight-neutral or leads to a small loss of a couple of kilos. Helpful, but it isn’t a weight-loss medicine.

How do I stop metformin upsetting my stomach?

Take it with meals, start on a low dose and increase slowly, or ask about the slow-release (XR) version. Gut side effects usually settle within a few weeks.

Does metformin cause vitamin B12 deficiency?

It can, with long-term use. Low B12 is listed as common (between 1 in 10 and 1 in 100 people), so periodic B12 checks make sense, especially if you get tingling or numb feet.

Should I stop metformin when I’m sick?

If you’re vomiting, have diarrhoea or are dehydrated, pause it until you’re eating and drinking normally, and follow your sick-day plan. Restart once you’re well.

What are the side effects of metformin?

Nausea, diarrhoea, tummy pain and a metallic taste are the most common, usually early on. Slow-release tablets and taking it with food help. Long-term use can lower B12. Lactic acidosis is very rare, mainly with severe kidney problems or serious illness.

Can metformin cause low blood sugar?

Not on its own. Metformin doesn’t usually cause hypos, but the risk rises if it’s taken with insulin or a sulfonylurea (such as gliclazide), or with heavy drinking or missed meals.

Can I drink alcohol on metformin?

Moderate drinking is usually fine. Heavy or binge drinking raises the risk of low blood sugar and, rarely, lactic acidosis, so keep within the guideline of no more than 10 standard drinks a week.
