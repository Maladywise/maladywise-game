Drug info

Berberine

Herbal medicine Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

Called ‘nature’s Ozempic’ online. It modestly lowers blood sugar and cholesterol, but weight loss is small, and it interacts with many medicines.

Quick answer: Trials (mostly from China, varying quality) show berberine lowers blood sugar and HbA1c roughly like metformin, and modestly lowers LDL cholesterol. Weight loss is about 2 kg on average, nothing like GLP-1 medicines. It interacts with many medicines and isn’t safe in pregnancy, breastfeeding or newborns.

How it works

Berberine activates AMPK (like metformin) and lowers liver cholesterol production.

Used for

  • Blood sugar in type 2 diabetes
  • Cholesterol

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • 🟡 Blood sugar in type 2 diabetesLowers glucose and HbA1c in trials of varying quality.
  • 🟡 CholesterolModest LDL reduction.
  • ❌ Weight loss like GLP-1 medicinesAverage loss about 2 kg: not comparable.

Avoid or check first if

  • You take diabetes medicines (risk of low sugars)
  • You take ciclosporin, some statins or other medicines broken down by CYP3A4
  • You’re pregnant or breastfeeding (risk of jaundice in babies)

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Common questions

Is berberine like Ozempic?

No. It lowers blood sugar modestly, but average weight loss is about 2 kg, far less than GLP-1 medicines.

Is berberine safe?

Usually short term, but it causes gut upset, interacts with many medicines and should be avoided in pregnancy.
