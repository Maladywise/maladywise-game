Drug info

Green tea extract

Herbal medicine Complementary medicine (AUST L) or food

Sold for weight loss, with tiny effects at best. Concentrated extracts can cause liver injury: Australian labels carry a warning.

Quick answer: A Cochrane review found green tea preparations cause very small, not meaningful, weight loss. Concentrated green tea extract (high EGCG) has caused rare serious liver injury, so the TGA requires a liver warning on labels. Drinking green tea is safe.

Evidence first, marketing second.

Brand names

  • Found in many ‘fat burner’ and weight loss products; Blackmores, Nature’s Way, Swisse Green Tea

How it works

EGCG and caffeine slightly increase energy use. High concentrated doses can damage liver cells.

Used for

  • See the evidence section

What the evidence says

From systematic reviews and large trials where they exist. ✅ good evidence · 🟡 some or mixed evidence · ❌ doesn’t work.

  • ❌ Weight lossVery small, not meaningful (Cochrane).

Avoid or check first if

  • You have liver disease
  • You take medicines that affect the liver
  • You’re sensitive to caffeine or pregnant

Side effects

Most people have no side effects at usual doses. Supplements can still interact with medicines: tell your doctor and pharmacist what you take.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Upset stomach, jitteriness (caffeine)

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Liver injury (rare): yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, severe tiredness

Get help urgently if

  • Signs of an allergic reaction: swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing (call 000)
  • Yellow skin or eyes, dark urine or severe tiredness (possible liver injury)

Good to know

  • Take with food: fasting raises liver risk.
  • Stop and see a doctor if you develop yellow eyes, dark urine or tummy pain.
  • Check ‘fat burners’ for hidden green tea extract.

Common questions

Does green tea extract help weight loss?

Effects are so small they don’t matter (Cochrane review).

Is green tea extract safe?

Concentrated extracts have caused rare liver injury. Australian labels must warn about it. Drinking green tea is safe.
