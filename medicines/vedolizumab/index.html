Drug info

Vedolizumab

Biologic (anti-integrin) Prescription only (S4), specialist

A biologic that stops inflammatory cells entering the gut, so it works mainly in the bowel with little effect on the rest of the immune system.

Quick answer: Vedolizumab blocks the α4β7 integrin that guides white cells into the bowel wall. It’s effective for ulcerative colitis and Crohn’s disease, particularly in older people or those worried about infections. It can take longer to work than anti-TNF drugs.

At a glance

Gut-only bouncer.

Brand names

  • Entyvio

How it works

It blocks α4β7 integrin on white cells, stopping them homing to the gut.

Used for

  • Ulcerative colitis
  • Crohn’s disease
  • Pouchitis (specialist use)

Side effects

Well tolerated; serious infections are uncommon.

Good to know

  • Before starting: screening for tuberculosis, hepatitis B and C and HIV, and catch-up vaccines.
  • Avoid live vaccines (e.g. yellow fever, some shingles and travel vaccines) unless your specialist says otherwise.
  • Tell your doctors before surgery, and if you get a fever or infection.
  • Most biologics can be continued in pregnancy after discussion with your gastroenterologist.

Common questions

Is vedolizumab safer than other biologics?

It acts mainly in the gut, so its infection risk is generally lower, but all biologics need screening and care.

How long does vedolizumab take to work?

Often 6 to 14 weeks, sometimes longer in Crohn’s disease.

Can vedolizumab be given as an injection?

Yes, after initial infusions an under-the-skin form can be used.
