Drug info

Menopause hormone therapy (MHT)

Oestrogen ± progestogen Prescription only (S4)

The most effective treatment for hot flushes and night sweats. For most healthy women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause, benefits outweigh risks.

Quick answer: MHT replaces oestrogen, plus a progestogen if you have a uterus (to protect the womb lining). It treats flushes, sweats, sleep disruption and vaginal dryness, and prevents bone loss. Patches and gels avoid the small clot risk of tablets. Risks depend on age, type and duration.

Back in favour, for the right person.

Brand names

  • Patches: Estradot, Climara, Estalis (combined). Gels: Estrogel, Sandrena. Tablets: Progynova, Femoston, Kliovance. Progesterone: Prometrium. Tibolone: Livial. Vaginal oestrogen: Vagifem, Ovestin

How it works

Oestrogen falls at menopause, which disrupts temperature control and thins vaginal and bone tissue. MHT restores it.

Used for

  • Moderate to severe flushes and sweats
  • Premature or early menopause (until at least the average age of menopause)
  • Vaginal dryness and recurrent UTIs after menopause (vaginal oestrogen, very low absorption)
  • Preventing osteoporosis in younger postmenopausal women

Side effects

Side effects often settle after a few months or a dose change.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Breast tenderness
  • Irregular bleeding in the first months
  • Bloating
  • Headache

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Mood changes with some progestogens

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Breast cancer: a small increase with combined MHT, rising with duration (oestrogen-only has little or no increase)
  • Blood clots and stroke with oral oestrogen (much less with patches and gels)

Get help urgently if

  • Bleeding after 6 months on continuous MHT or after stopping
  • Calf pain, chest pain or breathlessness
  • A breast lump

Good to know

  • If you have a uterus, you need a progestogen with oestrogen.
  • Transdermal (patch or gel) oestrogen is preferred with migraine, clot risk or raised BMI.
  • There’s no fixed time limit: review benefits and risks yearly.
  • Keep up breast screening.

Common questions

Is HRT safe?

For most healthy women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause, the benefits outweigh the risks. Risks rise with age, starting later, and longer use of combined MHT.

Do patches have fewer risks than tablets?

Yes. Oestrogen through the skin doesn’t raise clot risk the way tablets do.

How long can I stay on MHT?

There’s no set limit. Many women use it for 5 years or more; review it yearly with your doctor.
