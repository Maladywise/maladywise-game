Drug info

Blood thinners (anticoagulants)

Anticoagulant Prescription only (S4)

Medicines that slow blood clotting, to prevent strokes in atrial fibrillation and to treat or prevent clots in the legs and lungs. They save lives, at the cost of a higher bleeding risk.

Quick answer: Anticoagulants reduce the blood’s ability to clot. The newer DOACs (apixaban, rivaroxaban, dabigatran) need no routine blood tests and have fewer interactions, so they’ve mostly replaced warfarin. Warfarin is still used for mechanical heart valves and some other conditions. In atrial fibrillation they cut stroke risk by about two-thirds.

A small bleeding risk traded for a big stroke reduction. Usually a good deal.

Brand names

  • Apixaban: Eliquis. Rivaroxaban: Xarelto. Dabigatran: Pradaxa. Warfarin: Coumadin, Marevan. Enoxaparin injections: Clexane

How it works

DOACs directly block one clotting factor (factor Xa for apixaban and rivaroxaban, thrombin for dabigatran). Warfarin blocks vitamin K recycling, lowering several clotting factors, which is why diet and many medicines change its effect.

Used for

  • Atrial fibrillation: stroke prevention, based on stroke risk (CHA2DS2-VA score)
  • Treating deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE)
  • Preventing clots after hip and knee replacement
  • Mechanical heart valves and some other conditions: warfarin only (DOACs don’t work well enough)

Side effects

Bleeding is the main side effect. Most is minor (nosebleeds, bruising), but serious bleeding can happen.

Full side-effect list (from the product information)

How often, from the official product information. Bold = serious.

Common 1 in 10 to 1 in 100

  • Bruising
  • Nosebleeds and gum bleeding
  • Heavier periods
  • Indigestion (dabigatran)

Uncommon 1 in 100 to 1 in 1,000

  • Stomach or bowel bleeding
  • Blood in the urine
  • Anaemia

Rare 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000

  • Bleeding in the brain
  • Skin necrosis or purple toes (warfarin)

Get help urgently if

  • Any head injury, even minor: get checked the same day
  • Black or bloody stools, vomiting blood, or coughing blood
  • Sudden severe headache, weakness or trouble speaking: call 000
  • Bleeding that won’t stop

Good to know

  • Take it at the same time every day; missing doses raises clot risk.
  • Tell every doctor, dentist and pharmacist. Most dental work can go ahead without stopping.
  • Avoid anti-inflammatories (NSAIDs) and aspirin unless your doctor says otherwise.
  • Warfarin: keep green-vegetable intake steady, have regular INR tests, and check every new medicine or supplement.
  • DOACs need lower doses in kidney disease; have kidney blood tests at least yearly.
  • Reversal agents exist for dabigatran (idarucizumab) and warfarin (vitamin K).

Common questions

Which is better, apixaban or warfarin?

For most people with atrial fibrillation, DOACs like apixaban are at least as good at preventing stroke with less brain bleeding, and need no INR tests. Warfarin is still needed for mechanical valves.

What if I miss a dose of apixaban?

Take it as soon as you remember the same day, then carry on as normal. Don’t double up. Check the leaflet or your pharmacist for your specific medicine.

Can I drink alcohol on blood thinners?

Small amounts are usually fine. Heavy drinking raises bleeding risk and makes warfarin unpredictable.
