Fact sheet

Skin Cysts

A round, smooth lump under the skin, filled with a cheesy keratin paste, often with a tiny dark pore on top. Usually harmless and slow-growing. Often called “sebaceous cysts”, though they mostly aren’t.

Common types. Epidermoid cysts grow on the face, neck, chest and back. Pilar cysts grow on the scalp, have no pore and often run in families. They can be pea-sized to several centimetres, and can become red, swollen and sore if they leak or get infected. Other lumps, such as lipomas (soft fatty lumps), ganglions near joints, and boils, can look similar.

Diagnosis is usually by feel and look. An ultrasound helps if the lump is unusual. On brown and black skin, removal scars are more likely to become raised and thick (keloids), worth weighing up before cosmetic removal.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Squeezing or poppingSpreads the contents under the skin, causing inflammation, infection and a bigger scar.
  • DIY lancing with a needleInfection risk, and the cyst comes back because the sac is still there.
  • Drawing ointments and “cyst dissolving” creamsNo good trials. A cyst’s lining doesn’t dissolve.
  • Antibiotics for every red cystMany are inflamed, not infected, and settle without them.

What you can do

  • Leave it alone unless it bothers you.
  • For a sore one, use a warm compress and simple pain relief.
  • If you want it removed, wait until it’s calm: surgery on an inflamed cyst is messier and it’s more likely to return.

See your GP if

  • You’re not sure what the lump is
  • It’s red, sore, swollen or leaking
  • It grows quickly, feels hard or fixed, or is over about 5 cm
  • It keeps flaring and you’d like it removed
  • You find a new lump in the breast, testicle, neck or groin (these always need a check)

See a doctor the same day for a red, hot, very painful lump with fever, spreading redness, or feeling unwell.

Common questions

Is a skin cyst cancer?

Almost never. Cysts are harmless. A lump that grows fast, feels hard or is stuck down should be checked to make sure it’s a cyst.

Why does it smell?

The cheesy contents are keratin and skin oils. The smell is normal and isn’t a sign of infection on its own.

Will it go away on its own?

Some shrink after leaking, but most stay and can flare again. Removing the whole sac is the only permanent fix.

Are they contagious?

No. You can’t catch or spread skin cysts.

Can I squeeze a skin cyst?

Best not. Squeezing can push the contents into the skin, causing painful inflammation or infection, and the cyst usually refills. If it’s red and painful, see a GP.

Will a cyst come back after removal?

If the whole cyst wall is removed, it rarely comes back. If it’s only drained, it often refills, which is why removal is usually done when the cyst is calm, not inflamed.

Is it a cyst or a lipoma?

A cyst is a firm, round lump in the skin, sometimes with a small dark pore on top, and may leak cheesy material. A lipoma is a soft, rubbery fatty lump under the skin that moves easily. Both are usually harmless, and a GP can usually tell them apart.
