Fact sheet

Male Infertility

About 1 in 6 couples have trouble conceiving, and a male factor is involved in about half. A semen test is cheap and quick, so it should happen early, not after months of tests on the woman.

When to get checked

  • No pregnancy after 12 months of regular unprotected sex, or 6 months if your partner is 35 or older
  • Sooner with a known issue: undescended testes, testicular surgery, cancer treatment, low libido or erection problems, or steroid use

About half of fertility problems involve the male side. It's a two-person test.

Common causes

  • Unknown in many men
  • Varicocele: enlarged veins around the testicle, found in about 1 in 7 men
  • Testosterone or anabolic steroids: they switch off sperm production, sometimes for months to years
  • Past infections, injury, undescended testes, chemotherapy or radiotherapy
  • Genetic causes: Klinefelter syndrome, Y chromosome changes, cystic fibrosis gene changes (missing tubes)
  • Lifestyle: smoking, heavy drinking, cannabis, obesity, and heat (saunas, laptops on laps)

Tests

  • Semen analysis after 2–7 days without ejaculating. If abnormal, it’s repeated, because results vary a lot.
  • Hormone tests (testosterone, FSH, LH), and genetic tests if sperm counts are very low
  • Scrotal ultrasound if a lump or varicocele is suspected

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Fertility supplements and antioxidantsA large US trial (FAZST) found folic acid and zinc didn’t improve live births. Evidence for most antioxidants is weak.
  • Repeated home sperm tests without a lab testThey miss shape and movement. Get a proper lab analysis.

Common questions

How common is male infertility?

About 1 in 6 couples have trouble conceiving, and a male factor is involved in about half.

Does testosterone make you infertile?

Yes. Testosterone and anabolic steroids switch off sperm production. Recovery after stopping can take months to years.

How long does it take to improve sperm?

Sperm take about 3 months to develop, so lifestyle changes take about 3 months to show.
