Funny Medical Scales

🤕 The pain scale, honest edition

  • 0What pain? Living your best life.
  • 1Paper cut. Mild outrage.
  • 2Stubbed toe. Quietly forgiven.
  • 3Stubbed toe on the bed leg. Loudly not forgiven.
  • 4Stood on a toy brick at 2am.
  • 5Can still scroll your phone, but resentfully.
  • 6Cancelled plans and sent the ‘so sorry’ text.
  • 7Searching symptoms at 3am.
  • 8No comfortable position exists in any dimension.
  • 9Kidney stone territory.
  • 10Can’t speak. (If you’re texting us a 10, it’s probably a 7.)

The real oneDoctors use a 0 to 10 number scale because it’s quick, not because it’s perfect. What matters most is the change over time and what the pain stops you doing. Children often use picture-based faces scales instead.

💩 The Bristol stool chart, after hours

  • Type 1Gravel. Drink a glass of water. Then another.
  • Type 2Lumpy log. A personal struggle.
  • Type 3Sausage with cracks. Respectable.
  • Type 4Smooth, soft sausage. The gold standard. Frame it. (Please don’t.)
  • Type 5Soft blobs with clear edges. A bit hasty.
  • Type 6Porridge. Stay within sprinting distance of a toilet.
  • Type 7Gravy. Sip fluids and read the real version below.

The real oneThe real Bristol stool form scale (Lewis and Heaton, 1997) sorts poo into 7 types: 1–2 suggest constipation, 3–4 are ideal, 5–7 lean towards diarrhoea. See a doctor for blood or black poo, a lasting change in habit, weight loss, or diarrhoea with dehydration.

💻 The ADHD browser-tab scale

  • 1 tabMonk-like focus. Possibly asleep.
  • 5 tabsA normal Tuesday.
  • 12 tabsStarted a recipe, now reading about medieval cheese.
  • 25 tabsThree of them are playing music. You can’t find which.
  • 50 tabsOne is a form you started in March.
  • 99+ tabsThe browser has given up and so has the laptop fan.
  • New windowFresh start. Same 99 tabs, just hiding.

The real oneADHD isn’t about tabs, but the feeling is familiar to many: attention that’s hard to steer, not absent. Diagnosis needs symptoms from childhood, in more than one setting, that get in the way of life.

🤸 The bendiness scale

  • 0Can’t touch your knees. Respect your hamstrings.
  • 2Thumb gets close to the wrist if you stare at it.
  • 4Elbows bend slightly the wrong way. Mildly unsettling.
  • 6Party trick level: everyone says ‘eww, do it again’.
  • 8Physios ask to take photos.
  • 9Joints mostly for decoration.

The real oneThe real Beighton score counts 9 points across fingers, thumbs, elbows, knees and spine. 5 or more in adults suggests generalised hypermobility. Being bendy is common and often harmless; it matters when it comes with pain, dislocations or other features.

🥱 The tiredness scale

  • 1A small yawn. Coffee is optional.
  • 3Read the same email four times.
  • 5Put the milk in the cupboard and the cereal in the fridge.
  • 7Fell asleep in the car park before going into the shops.
  • 9Too tired to sleep, which feels illegal.
  • 10Fell asleep reading this list.

The real oneTiredness has hundreds of causes, from sleep apnoea and low iron to thyroid problems, depression and ME/CFS. If it lasts weeks, crashes you after effort, or comes with weight loss, fevers or breathlessness, see your GP.

🤧 The common cold severity scale

  • 1A tickle. Denial phase.
  • 2Tissues in every pocket.
  • 3Voice like a jazz singer.
  • 4Can taste nothing. Ate a lemon to check.
  • 5Wrote a will. Left everything to the cat.
  • 6Back at work, coughing near the printer.

The real oneColds usually last 7 to 10 days, with a cough that can hang on for 3 weeks. Antibiotics don’t help. See a doctor for trouble breathing, a high fever lasting more than 3 days, chest pain, or if a baby is unwell.
