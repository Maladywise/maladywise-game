Fact sheet

Seborrhoeic Keratoses

Harmless, warty or waxy spots that look stuck on to the skin, from pale to almost black. Extremely common after 50, often many at once. They can’t turn into cancer and aren’t contagious, so treatment is optional.

What they look like. Flat to raised, a few millimetres to a few centimetres across, with a rough, crumbly or waxy surface, on the chest, back, face, scalp or under the breasts. They can itch, catch on clothing or bleed when knocked. The cause isn’t known; they’re not viral warts and nothing to do with hygiene or diet. Many people inherit a tendency to get lots.

Diagnosis. Usually obvious to a doctor with a dermatoscope. Because a dark one can look like a melanoma, any spot that’s different from your others, changing fast or uncertain gets checked or removed for testing. On brown and black skin, many small dark bumps on the cheeks and around the eyes are often a related harmless condition called dermatosis papulosa nigra, which tends to run in families.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Over-the-counter freeze kits, pens and removal creamsNo good evidence, and DIY risks burns, scars and missing a melanoma that looked like a seb K.
  • Apple cider vinegar, tea tree oil or garlicNo trials. Can burn the skin around the spot.
  • Diet or supplements to stop new onesNothing in diet or lifestyle has been shown to prevent them.

What you can do

  • Get to know your spots so you notice one that’s different.
  • Moisturise itchy ones and wear soft fabrics where they rub.
  • Use sun protection anyway: the same skin can still get skin cancer.

See your GP if

  • One spot looks different from the others, or is changing quickly
  • A spot bleeds without being knocked, or doesn’t heal
  • A spot is sore, inflamed or keeps catching
  • Lots of new spots appear suddenly over a few months (rare, but worth a check)

Not sure if it’s a seb K or a mole? Don’t treat it yourself. Melanoma can look similar, and DIY removal destroys the tissue a lab would need.

Common questions

Can seborrhoeic keratoses turn into cancer?

No. They’re harmless. The only worry is mistaking a melanoma for one, so changing or unusual spots should be checked.

Are they contagious?

No. They’re not viral warts and don’t spread to other people.

Why do I keep getting more?

They become more common with age and often run in families. New ones are normal.

Does Medicare cover removal?

Usually only when there’s a medical reason, such as an uncertain diagnosis, bleeding or inflammation. Cosmetic removal is usually private.
