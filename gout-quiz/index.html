Gout Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on gout: why crystals form, treating a flare, and long-term urate lowering that actually stops attacks.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    What causes gout?

    1. Uric acid crystals forming in a joint
    2. Too much calcium in the blood
    3. A bacterial infection in the joint
    4. Wear and tear of the cartilage
    Show answer

    Answer: Uric acid crystals forming in a joint. High urate levels let needle-shaped crystals form, triggering intense inflammation.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    Which joint is classically affected first?

    1. The lower spine
    2. The base of the big toe
    3. The jaw joint
    4. The hip joint
    Show answer

    Answer: The base of the big toe. Ankles, knees and fingers can also be affected.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Which drink is linked to gout flares?

    1. Water
    2. Milk
    3. Black coffee
    4. Beer and sugary soft drinks
    Show answer

    Answer: Beer and sugary soft drinks. Beer and fructose raise urate. Coffee and low-fat dairy may slightly lower it.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    What is a common flare treatment?

    1. Antibiotics
    2. Heat packs alone
    3. An anti-inflammatory, low-dose colchicine or prednisolone
    4. Allopurinol started at a high dose
    Show answer

    Answer: An anti-inflammatory, low-dose colchicine or prednisolone. Treat early. Rest, elevate and ice the joint too.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    What is the low-dose colchicine flare regimen?

    1. 10 tablets at once, then stop
    2. One every hour until diarrhoea starts
    3. 1 mg, then 0.5 mg an hour later
    4. One tablet a week for a month
    Show answer

    Answer: 1 mg, then 0.5 mg an hour later. Higher doses add side effects without extra benefit. Colchicine overdose is dangerous.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    Which medicine lowers uric acid long term?

    1. Allopurinol
    2. Prednisolone
    3. Paracetamol
    4. Ibuprofen
    Show answer

    Answer: Allopurinol. Febuxostat is an alternative. These prevent flares; they don’t treat a flare that has started.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    What is the usual uric acid target on treatment?

    1. Below 1.0 mmol/L
    2. Below 0.36 mmol/L
    3. Above 0.6 mmol/L
    4. There is no target
    Show answer

    Answer: Below 0.36 mmol/L. Lower (below 0.30) if there are tophi. The dose is increased step by step until the target is reached.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    How should allopurinol be started?

    1. Only during a painful flare
    2. Once a month, as an injection
    3. At the maximum dose straight away
    4. At a low dose, increased slowly every few weeks
    Show answer

    Answer: At a low dose, increased slowly every few weeks. Starting low reduces the risk of a serious rash and of triggering flares.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    Why is flare prevention often given when allopurinol starts?

    1. To prevent a joint infection
    2. Lowering urate can trigger flares at first
    3. It makes urate rise at first
    4. Allopurinol can be addictive
    Show answer

    Answer: Lowering urate can trigger flares at first. Low-dose colchicine or an anti-inflammatory for the first few months reduces these early flares.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    Which test confirms gout?

    1. Urate crystals seen in joint fluid
    2. An ECG during the flare
    3. A chest X-ray
    4. A normal uric acid blood level
    Show answer

    Answer: Urate crystals seen in joint fluid. Uric acid can be normal during a flare, so check it again a few weeks later.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    What are tophi?

    1. Air-filled cysts under the skin
    2. Bone spurs from arthritis
    3. Lumps of urate crystals under the skin
    4. Small blood clots in the toes
    Show answer

    Answer: Lumps of urate crystals under the skin. Common on ears, elbows and fingers. They shrink with good urate lowering.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Which medicines can raise uric acid?

    1. Metformin for diabetes
    2. Vitamin C supplements
    3. Losartan, a blood pressure tablet
    4. Diuretics such as hydrochlorothiazide
    Show answer

    Answer: Diuretics such as hydrochlorothiazide. Losartan slightly lowers urate. Low-dose aspirin can raise it too.

Common questions

Is gout caused by diet alone?

No. Genes and kidney handling of urate matter most. Diet changes help a little; medicine does most of the work.

Do I stop allopurinol during a flare?

No. Keep taking it and treat the flare separately.

Is this quiz medical advice?

No. It’s general education. See your GP about your own treatment.
