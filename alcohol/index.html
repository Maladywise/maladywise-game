Fact sheet

Cutting down or quitting alcohol

Australian guidelines say healthy adults should drink no more than 10 standard drinks a week and no more than 4 on any one day. Less is lower risk. If you drink heavily every day, don’t stop suddenly without medical advice: withdrawal can be dangerous.

The guidelines

  • Healthy adults: no more than 10 standard drinks a week and no more than 4 on any one day.
  • Under 18s: no alcohol. Pregnant, planning pregnancy or breastfeeding: no alcohol is safest.
  • A standard drink is 10 g of alcohol: roughly a middy of full-strength beer, a 100 mL glass of wine or a 30 mL nip of spirits.
  • Alcohol causes at least 7 cancers (including breast and bowel), liver disease, high blood pressure, atrial fibrillation and injuries.

Signs it’s becoming a problem

  • Drinking more or longer than you meant to; trying and failing to cut down.
  • Cravings, needing more for the same effect, or drinking to stop shakes or anxiety in the morning.
  • Missing work or family commitments, or arguments about drinking.
  • Drinking in risky situations, like before driving.

Cutting down: what works

  • Count your drinks: use an app or a note on your phone.
  • Have alcohol-free days each week, and start later in the day.
  • Alternate with water; choose low-alcohol options; use smaller glasses.
  • Plan for triggers (stress, certain people, after work) and have something else ready.
  • Tell people you’re cutting down. Support doubles your chances.

Stopping safely

  • If you drink heavily every day, stopping suddenly can cause shakes, sweating, anxiety, seizures or delirium tremens, which can be life-threatening.
  • See your GP first: supervised withdrawal at home or in a detox unit, often with diazepam for a few days, and thiamine (vitamin B1) to protect the brain.
  • Withdrawal usually starts 6 to 24 hours after the last drink and peaks at 1 to 3 days.

Medicines that help

  • Naltrexone: reduces cravings and heavy drinking days. See the naltrexone guide.
  • Acamprosate: helps people stay alcohol-free after stopping; safe with liver disease.
  • Disulfiram: makes you sick if you drink; only for some people, with supervision.
  • These work best alongside counselling, support groups or a GP plan.

Where to get help

  • National Alcohol and Other Drug Hotline: 1800 250 015 (free, 24/7)
  • Counselling Online: free 24/7 web chat about alcohol and drugs (counsellingonline.org.au)
  • Your GP: a check-up, blood tests and a plan
  • Support groups such as SMART Recovery and Alcoholics Anonymous

Common questions

How many drinks a week is safe in Australia?

The guideline is no more than 10 standard drinks a week and no more than 4 on any one day for healthy adults. Fewer is lower risk.

Is it dangerous to stop drinking suddenly?

If you drink heavily every day, yes. Withdrawal can cause seizures and delirium tremens. See a doctor for a supervised plan.

What medicines help you stop drinking?

Naltrexone and acamprosate both reduce relapse. Disulfiram is used for some people. Your GP can prescribe them.

How long does alcohol withdrawal last?

It usually starts 6 to 24 hours after the last drink, peaks at 1 to 3 days and settles within about a week.
