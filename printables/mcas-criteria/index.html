Printable MCAS criteria

A checklist version of the consensus criteria for mast cell activation syndrome (MCAS). All three criteria are needed.

MCAS criteria

NameDate

1. Typical episodes: severe, recurring, in 2 or more body systems at once

  • Skin: flushing, itch, hives or swelling
  • Gut: cramps, diarrhoea, nausea or vomiting
  • Heart and circulation: fast heart rate, low blood pressure or fainting
  • Breathing: wheeze, throat tightness or a blocked nose
  • Eyes: red, itchy eyes

2. Proof that mast cells are involved

  • Best test: blood tryptase taken within 1 to 4 hours of an episode is raised by more than 20% plus 2 ng/mL above your own baseline (taken when well)
  • Other urine or blood markers (e.g. histamine metabolites, prostaglandin D2 metabolites) can support it

3. Response to treatment

  • Symptoms clearly improve with medicines that block mast cell chemicals (e.g. antihistamines for H1 and H2, or mast cell stabilisers)

Also checked

  • Other causes, such as allergy, other skin, gut or heart conditions, and mastocytosis (a raised baseline tryptase over 20 ng/mL needs specialist review)

Based on the international consensus criteria for mast cell activation syndrome (Valent et al. 2012, updated 2019). Summarised in our own words. Printed from maladywise.com/printables/mcas-criteria/. For discussion with a clinician; it can’t diagnose on its own.
