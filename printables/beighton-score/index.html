About EDS and hypermobility

Beighton score: joint hypermobility

NameDate

One point for each side that can do it. A clinician should check the angles.

TestLeft (1)Right (1)
1Bend the little finger back beyond 90°
2Bring the thumb to touch the forearm
3Elbow straightens past 10°
4Knee straightens past 10°
5Palms flat on the floor with knees straight–

Total (out of 9): ______

  • Generalised hypermobility: 5 or more in adults (6 or more before puberty; 4 or more over age 50).
  • Hypermobility on its own is common and often harmless. It matters when it comes with pain, injuries or other features: see the hEDS checklist.

Beighton P et al., Ann Rheum Dis 1973; age cut-offs from the 2017 hEDS criteria (Malfait et al.). Printed from maladywise.com/printables/beighton-score/.
