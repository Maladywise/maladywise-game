Printable hEDS criteria

A checklist version of the 2017 criteria for hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos syndrome. All three criteria must be met.

hEDS criteria

NameDate

Criterion 1: generalised joint hypermobility (Beighton score)

  • Score 5 or more out of 9 for adults (6 or more before puberty; 4 or more over age 50)
  • If the score is one point short, a ‘yes’ to 2 or more of the 5-point hypermobility questionnaire can count

Criterion 2, Feature A: tick each (5 or more needed for Feature A)

  • Unusually soft or velvety skin
  • Mildly stretchy skin
  • Unexplained stretch marks (back, groin, thighs, breasts, abdomen) without big weight changes
  • Small fatty bumps on both heels when standing (piezogenic papules)
  • Recurrent or multiple abdominal hernias
  • Thin, wide scars at 2 or more sites (not the typical scars of classical EDS)
  • Pelvic floor, rectal or uterine prolapse in a child, man or woman who hasn’t given birth, without another cause
  • Crowded teeth and a high or narrow palate
  • Long, slender fingers on both hands (positive thumb or wrist sign)
  • Arm span at least 1.05 times height
  • Mitral valve prolapse on echo (strict criteria)
  • Widened aortic root (Z-score above +2)

Criterion 2, Features B and C (need 2 of A, B and C)

  • Feature B: a first-degree relative who independently meets these criteria
  • Feature C: limb pain in 2 or more limbs, daily for 3 months or more
  • Feature C: widespread pain for 3 months or more
  • Feature C: repeated dislocations or clear joint instability without injury

Criterion 3: all of these

  • No unusual skin fragility (which points to another EDS type)
  • Other inherited and acquired connective tissue conditions, including autoimmune rheumatic disease, ruled out
  • Other causes of joint hypermobility (e.g. low muscle tone, other connective tissue conditions) ruled out

Based on the 2017 International Classification of the Ehlers-Danlos Syndromes (Malfait et al. 2017). Summarised in our own words. Printed from maladywise.com/printables/heds-criteria/. For discussion with a clinician; it can’t diagnose on its own.
