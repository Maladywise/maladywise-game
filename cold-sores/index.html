Fact sheet

Cold Sores

Tingly, blistering sores on the lip from the herpes simplex virus. Very common, harmless for most adults, gone in about 10 days. The trick is speed: treat at the first tingle.

The cold sore ladder

Most cold sores heal on their own. Treatment shaves days off, and only if you start early. The tingle is your starting gun.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careHands off, ice on, SPF onCool packs for pain, SPF lip balm to block sun triggers, and no picking.
    Works
    Not tested for pain; SPF blocked UV-lamp-triggered sores in small trials, but real-sunlight results are mixed
    Cost
    About $5–10 for an SPF lip balm
    How long
    Until healed, usually 7–10 days
    Watch for
    Picking spreads virus and invites infection. Don’t share cups, towels or lip balm.

    Squeezing a cold sore is not a treatment. It’s a distribution plan.

  2. Step 2 · PharmacyAntiviral cream (aciclovir)Dab on 5 times a day from the first tingle.
    Works
    Modest: heals about half a day faster than a dummy treatment (roughly 4.5 vs 5 days)
    Cost
    About $10–20 a tube
    How long
    4–5 days
    Watch for
    Little point once it’s crusted. Dab, don’t rub, and wash hands after.

    Works best on a sore that hasn’t fully arrived yet. Punctuality matters.

  3. Step 3 · PharmacySingle-dose antiviral tablets (famciclovir)Pharmacist-only: 3 tablets taken together, once, for adults who get repeat cold sores.
    Works
    Heals about 1.5–2 days faster than a dummy treatment (4.4 vs 6.2 days) when taken within an hour of the tingle
    Cost
    About $10–20 for the 3-tablet pack
    How long
    One dose. That’s it.
    Watch for
    Headache, nausea. Not for first-ever sores, children, pregnancy or weak immune systems without advice.

    Keep a pack at home. Dawdling to the chemist tomorrow is how the sore wins.

  4. Step 4 · PrescriptionAntiviral tablets (valaciclovir), short courseFor bigger, frequent or nasty outbreaks.
    Works
    Shortens an outbreak by about 1 day vs a dummy treatment if started at the first tingle
    Cost
    About $15–40 on a private script
    How long
    1 day (2 doses), started at the tingle
    Watch for
    Headache, nausea. Dose may change with kidney problems.

    Same principle as step 3: early or not at all.

  5. Step 5 · PrescriptionDaily antiviral to prevent outbreaksFor people having many outbreaks a year, or before a known trigger (e.g. a ski trip).
    Works
    Roughly halves outbreaks: about 6 in 10 stay outbreak-free over 4 months, vs 4 in 10 on a dummy treatment
    Cost
    About $20–40 a month on a private script
    How long
    Months at a time, then review
    Watch for
    Headache, nausea. Doesn’t make you uninfectious, just less often.

    For frequent flyers, not the once-a-year cold sore.

  6. Step 6 · SpecialistEye or skin specialistFor sores near the eye, cold sores on eczema, or a weakened immune system.
    Works
    Not tested in trials; standard care that prevents serious complications such as eye damage
    Cost
    Medicare rebates with a GP referral; specialist gaps vary
    How long
    Usually short-term, sometimes urgent
    Watch for
    Any red, painful or blurry eye with a cold sore needs same-day care.

    Your eye is not the place for a home remedy.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Results are typical figures from trials, not promises.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Lysine supplementsTrials are small and mixed, with no clear benefit for treating or preventing cold sores.
  • ToothpasteNo evidence, and it can dry out and irritate the skin around a sore.
  • Essential oilsMostly lab studies and tiny trials. Tea tree on a raw lip can sting and cause allergy.
  • Antibiotic creamsCold sores are a virus. Antibiotics don’t touch it.

What you can do

  • Start treatment at the first tingle, itch or burn. Keep supplies at home.
  • SPF 30+ lip balm in sun, wind and snow.
  • No kissing babies, and no oral sex, while you have a sore.
  • Don’t share cups, cutlery, towels or lip balm.

See your GP if

  • Sores are big, spreading or not healed in 2 weeks
  • You get them often
  • You have eczema or a weakened immune system
  • You’re pregnant, especially near the due date

See a doctor the same day for a cold sore near the eye, a painful red eye, or sudden spreading sores on eczema skin. A newborn with blisters, fever or poor feeding needs urgent care. If very unwell, call 000 (Australia) or your local emergency number. From a mobile, 112 works in most countries.

Common questions

Are cold sores contagious?

Yes, most when blisters are present, but the virus can spread even without a visible sore. Most adults already carry it.

Can you get rid of the cold sore virus for good?

No. It stays dormant in nerves and can reawaken with sun, stress, illness or periods. Treatment shortens outbreaks; daily tablets can reduce how often they happen.

What is the fastest cold sore treatment?

A single-dose antiviral tablet from the pharmacist, taken within an hour of the first tingle, heals sores about 2 days faster. Creams help a little less.

Is it safe to kiss a baby if I have a cold sore?

No. Herpes can be dangerous for newborns. Wait until the sore is fully healed, and wash hands before handling babies.

How long do cold sores last?

Usually 7 to 10 days from the first tingle to the scab falling off. Antiviral creams or tablets started at the first tingle can shorten an outbreak by a day or two.

What triggers cold sores?

Common triggers are strong sun, colds and fevers, stress, tiredness, periods and injury to the lips. A lip balm with SPF 30 or more helps prevent sun-triggered cold sores.

Are cold sores an STI?

Not usually. Most cold sores are caused by herpes simplex type 1, which most people catch in childhood from kisses; about 2 in 3 people under 50 carry it. It can spread to the genitals through oral sex, so avoid oral sex while you have a sore.
