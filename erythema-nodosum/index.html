Fact sheet

Erythema Nodosum

Tender red or purple lumps under the skin, usually on both shins, caused by inflammation in the fat layer. It’s a reaction pattern rather than a disease in itself, often set off by an infection, a medicine, pregnancy or an inflammatory condition. The lumps clear in weeks without scarring.

Quick answer: Erythema nodosum is a reaction that causes tender red lumps under the skin, usually on both shins. It can be triggered by strep throat, other infections, sarcoidosis, inflammatory bowel disease, pregnancy or medicines such as the pill, though often no cause is found. Lumps fade like bruises within 3 to 8 weeks.

How common. Uncommon: about 2 to 3 people in 10,000 a year in England. It’s more common in women and most often affects people aged 25 to 40, though it can happen at any age.

Causes. No cause is found in up to half of people. Known triggers include strep throat (the most common in children), Mycoplasma chest infections, gut infections (Yersinia, Salmonella, Campylobacter), tuberculosis, sarcoidosis, Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis, pregnancy, the contraceptive pill and some antibiotics. Rarer links include Behçet’s disease and lymphoma.

Löfgren’s syndrome. A form of sarcoidosis that combines erythema nodosum, swollen ankles, fever and swollen glands in the chest seen on X-ray. It usually settles on its own within months to 2 years.

Symptoms. Firm, smooth, tender lumps 2 to 6 cm across, usually on both shins, sometimes thighs or forearms. They’re often easier to feel than see, and fade over weeks like bruises, going purple, yellow then brown. Fever, tiredness and aching, swollen ankles, knees or wrists often come first or alongside.

Look-alikes. Cellulitis (usually one leg, spreading, hot), bruises, insect bites, inflamed surface veins, and rarer types of fat or blood vessel inflammation, some of which ulcerate.

How it’s diagnosed. From the look of the lumps. Then the hunt for a cause: blood tests (blood count, inflammation markers, strep antibodies), a throat swab, a chest X-ray, and depending on your story, a pregnancy test, stool tests or a TB test. A skin biopsy is only needed if the picture isn’t clear.

What you can do

  • Rest with your legs raised and avoid long periods on your feet.
  • Use a cool, damp cloth or support stockings for comfort.
  • Take ibuprofen if it’s safe for you, or paracetamol.
  • Note any recent sore throat, tummy upset, new medicine or cough to tell your GP.

See your GP if

  • You have new tender lumps on your shins (to look for the cause)
  • Lumps haven’t gone after 8 weeks, or keep coming back
  • Pain is affecting your daily life
  • You’re pregnant

See a doctor the same day for a hot, red, spreading area on one leg, high fever or feeling very unwell, breathlessness, a cough with blood, night sweats and weight loss, or bloody diarrhoea.

Outlook. The lumps clear in 3 to 8 weeks without scarring, though aching legs and joints can last longer. About 1 in 3 people have another episode, and a few have lumps that keep recurring for months, which usually prompts a fresh look for a cause.

Common questions

What causes erythema nodosum?

It’s a reaction to a trigger: commonly strep throat, other infections, sarcoidosis, Crohn’s disease or ulcerative colitis, pregnancy, the contraceptive pill or some antibiotics. In up to half of people no cause is found.

Is erythema nodosum serious?

The lumps themselves are harmless and clear without scarring. It matters because it can be the first sign of something that needs treating, such as an infection, sarcoidosis or inflammatory bowel disease.

How long does erythema nodosum last?

The lumps usually fade within 3 to 8 weeks, looking like bruises as they go. Leg aches and joint pain can last a few weeks longer.

Is erythema nodosum contagious?

No. The lumps can’t spread to other people, although an infection that triggered them, like strep throat, can.

What tests are done for erythema nodosum?

Usually blood tests, a throat swab and a chest X-ray, plus other tests depending on your symptoms, such as a stool test, pregnancy test or TB test. A biopsy is rarely needed.

Can erythema nodosum come back?

Yes, in about 1 in 3 people, especially if the trigger returns. Recurring lumps are a reason to look again for a cause.
