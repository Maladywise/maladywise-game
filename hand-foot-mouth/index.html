Hand, Foot and Mouth Disease

Mouth ulcers, spots on the hands and feet, and a child who won’t eat. Hand, foot and mouth disease spreads quickly through childcare and usually clears by itself in 7 to 10 days. The main job is keeping your child drinking.

What it is: a viral infection, usually from a coxsackievirus. It is not related to foot-and-mouth disease in animals. It mostly affects children under 10, but adults can catch it too, usually with milder symptoms.

Symptoms: it often starts with a sore throat, fever and going off food. A few days later come mouth ulcers and a spotty rash on the hands and feet, sometimes the bottom and groin, which can turn into small blisters. Symptoms usually begin 3 to 7 days after catching it.

How it spreads: through coughs, sneezes, blister fluid and poo. People are most infectious in the first 5 days of symptoms, but the virus can stay in poo for several weeks. You can catch it more than once.

Nails: some children lose a fingernail or toenail a few weeks later. It grows back.

The treatment ladder

There’s no cure and antibiotics don’t work. Treatment is about comfort and fluids while the virus runs its course.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • AntibioticsIt’s caused by a virus. Antibiotics don’t work.
  • Popping the blistersLeave them to dry naturally. The fluid is infectious.
  • Acidic drinks and spicy foodThey sting a sore mouth and put children off drinking.

What you can do

  • Offer drinks little and often, and try ice blocks for older children.
  • Wash hands well, especially after nappy changes and wiping noses.
  • Don’t share cups, cutlery, towels or clothes.
  • Clean toys and surfaces, as the virus can live on them.
  • Childcare rules vary. In Australia, keep children home until the blisters have dried. NHS advice in the UK is to return once they feel well.
  • For mouth ulcers that aren’t from a virus, see our mouth ulcers fact sheet.

See your GP if

  • Your child is a young baby or seems quite unwell
  • It isn’t getting better after 7 to 10 days
  • Mouth ulcers are so sore your child can’t swallow
  • You’re pregnant and have caught it or been in close contact
  • Your child has eczema that flares or looks infected

See a doctor urgently if

  • Signs of dehydration: few wet nappies or little wee, a dry mouth, or unusual sleepiness
  • A fever of 38°C or more lasting 3 days or longer
  • Jerky movements, trouble walking, or unusual drowsiness or irritability
  • Fast breathing

Call 000 (Australia) or your local emergency number if your child is very hard to wake, struggling to breathe, has a fit, or seems seriously unwell. Serious complications are very rare.

Common questions

How long is hand, foot and mouth disease contagious?

People are most contagious in the first 5 days of symptoms, and can spread it a few days before symptoms start. The virus can stay in poo for several weeks, so keep washing hands after nappy changes.

When can my child go back to childcare?

In Australia, the advice is to keep children home until their blisters have dried up. UK advice is that they can return once they feel well. Check your centre’s rules.

Can adults get hand, foot and mouth disease?

Yes, though symptoms are often mild. If you’re pregnant and catch it, talk to your GP or midwife. Usually there is no risk to the baby.

Why are my child’s nails falling off?

Some children lose a fingernail or toenail a few weeks after the illness. It’s temporary, and the nail grows back.

How long does hand, foot and mouth disease last?

Usually 7 to 10 days. Mouth ulcers are often most painful in the first few days. Keep up fluids with cold drinks, icy poles and paracetamol or ibuprofen if needed.

Is hand, foot and mouth disease the same as foot-and-mouth disease?

No. Foot-and-mouth disease affects cattle, sheep and pigs and isn’t a human illness. Hand, foot and mouth disease is a mild human virus, and you can’t catch it from or give it to animals.

Can you get hand, foot and mouth disease twice?

Yes. Several different viruses cause it, so being immune to one doesn’t protect you from the others.
