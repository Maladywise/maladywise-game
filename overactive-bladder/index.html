Fact sheet

Overactive bladder

A sudden, hard-to-delay urge to pass urine, often with frequent trips and getting up at night, sometimes with leaks. Bladder training helps most people; medicines and other options help the rest.

Symptoms

  • Urgency: a sudden compelling need to go
  • Frequency: more than about 8 times a day
  • Night-time urination (nocturia)
  • Urge leaks before reaching the toilet

Check first

  • A urine test to rule out infection or blood
  • Diabetes, constipation, and medicines like diuretics
  • In men, prostate enlargement; in anyone, incomplete bladder emptying

Treatment

  • Bladder training: gradually stretching the time between toilet visits over 6 weeks or more.
  • Cut back caffeine, fizzy drinks and alcohol; drink steadily rather than in large amounts; reduce evening fluids.
  • Pelvic floor exercises to help suppress urges.
  • Medicines: anticholinergics (oxybutynin, solifenacin) or beta-3 agonists (mirabegron, vibegron). See bladder medicines.
  • Vaginal oestrogen after menopause.
  • Specialist options: botulinum toxin injections into the bladder, and nerve stimulation (tibial or sacral).

Where to get help

  • National Continence Helpline: 1800 33 00 66 (free)

Common questions

What causes an overactive bladder?

Often no single cause is found. The bladder muscle contracts before it’s full. Caffeine, alcohol, UTIs, constipation, menopause and some nerve conditions can contribute.

What is bladder training?

A program of gradually holding on for longer between toilet visits, usually over 6 weeks or more, to retrain the bladder.

What if tablets don’t work?

Botox injections into the bladder or nerve stimulation can help. Ask about a referral to a urologist or urogynaecologist.
