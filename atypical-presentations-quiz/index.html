Atypical Presentations Quiz

Twelve questions, easy to hard, on emergencies that don’t look like the textbook: who gets them, what they look like, and why they get missed.

  1. Question 1 of 12

    Roughly what share of people having a heart attack have no chest pain when they reach hospital?

    1. About 1 in 10
    2. About 2 in 3
    3. About 1 in 3
    4. About 1 in 50
    Show answer

    Answer: About 1 in 3. In a US registry of over 430,000 heart attacks, 33% arrived without chest pain. They were older, more often women and more often had diabetes.

  2. Question 2 of 12

    Which is a common non-chest-pain heart attack symptom, more often reported by women?

    1. Itchy palms
    2. Breathlessness, nausea and sweating
    3. A sore throat
    4. A rash on the chest
    Show answer

    Answer: Breathlessness, nausea and sweating. Women also more often report jaw, neck, back or arm pain and unusual tiredness.

  3. Question 3 of 12

    Why can heart attacks be ‘silent’ in diabetes?

    1. High sugar raises the pain threshold
    2. Blood vessels are wider in diabetes
    3. Metformin masks chest pain
    4. Nerve damage can blunt heart pain signals
    Show answer

    Answer: Nerve damage can blunt heart pain signals. Autonomic neuropathy means pain may be mild or absent, so ECGs are done for vague symptoms.

  4. Question 4 of 12

    An 88-year-old suddenly becomes confused and short of breath. Which test belongs early?

    1. An ECG
    2. A urine dipstick only
    3. A CT head only
    4. A memory test
    Show answer

    Answer: An ECG. Over 85, confusion, fainting or breathlessness can be the only sign of a heart attack.

  5. Question 5 of 12

    What were heart attack patients without chest pain more likely to experience?

    1. Faster treatment in ED
    2. Later treatment and higher death rates
    3. Lower death rates
    4. No difference at all
    Show answer

    Answer: Later treatment and higher death rates. They arrived about 2.5 hours later on average and had more than double the in-hospital death rate.

  6. Question 6 of 12

    An older woman with a urine infection has a normal temperature. What can still point to sepsis?

    1. A normal temperature rules it out
    2. Only a rash would suggest it
    3. New confusion, a fall or fast breathing
    4. Nothing until the fever appears
    Show answer

    Answer: New confusion, a fall or fast breathing. Older people may have no fever, or a low temperature. ‘Not themselves’ is a red flag.

  7. Question 7 of 12

    What question does Australia’s Sepsis Clinical Care Standard encourage families to ask?

    1. ‘Do they need a blood culture?’
    2. ‘Is it a urine infection?’
    3. ‘Is it safe to go home?’
    4. ‘Could it be sepsis?’
    Show answer

    Answer: ‘Could it be sepsis?’. Families often notice the change first. Asking out loud prompts a sepsis screen.

  8. Question 8 of 12

    A US study of 300,000+ cardiac arrests found women collapsing in public were…

    1. Less likely to get bystander CPR
    2. More likely to get CPR
    3. Never given a defibrillator
    4. Equally likely to get CPR
    Show answer

    Answer: Less likely to get bystander CPR. Women were about 14% less likely to receive bystander CPR and defibrillation. CPR works through clothing.

  9. Question 9 of 12

    Where can defibrillator pads go on a woman?

    1. Only on the stomach, below the ribs
    2. Over the bra, which stays in place
    3. Both pads on the back, side by side
    4. Under or beside the breast; move the bra
    Show answer

    Answer: Under or beside the breast; move the bra. Pad position is the same as for anyone: upper right chest and lower left side.

  10. Question 10 of 12

    Which stroke sign is easily blamed on an inner-ear problem?

    1. Sudden dizziness with trouble walking
    2. Spinning on rolling over in bed
    3. Hearing loss after a cold
    4. Ringing in both ears
    Show answer

    Answer: Sudden dizziness with trouble walking. Posterior circulation strokes cause dizziness, double vision and unsteadiness, and are often missed.

  11. Question 11 of 12

    For Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people, Heart Health Checks are recommended from what age?

    1. 60
    2. 45
    3. 30
    4. 18 only if symptoms
    Show answer

    Answer: 30. Heart disease and stroke come earlier. Hospital and death rates are about double those of other Australians.

  12. Question 12 of 12

    Why can a pulse oximeter mislead in people with darker skin?

    1. It always reads too low
    2. It can overestimate oxygen, hiding low levels
    3. It reads the heart rate wrong
    4. Nail polish is the only problem
    Show answer

    Answer: It can overestimate oxygen, hiding low levels. A 2020 NEJM study found hidden low oxygen about three times as often in Black patients as White patients with the same readings.
