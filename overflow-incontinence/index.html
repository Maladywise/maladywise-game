Fact sheet

Overflow incontinence

When the bladder can’t empty properly, it stays full and overflows in dribbles. It’s more common in men with an enlarged prostate, and in anyone with nerve damage, severe constipation or certain medicines.

Symptoms

  • Constant or frequent dribbling
  • Weak or slow stream, straining, stopping and starting
  • Feeling the bladder never empties
  • Frequent small amounts, getting up at night
  • Recurrent UTIs

Causes

  • Enlarged prostate or urethral narrowing
  • Severe constipation pressing on the bladder outlet
  • Nerve damage: diabetes, spinal problems, multiple sclerosis
  • Medicines: anticholinergics, some antidepressants, opioids, decongestants
  • After surgery or anaesthesia

Treatment

  • A bladder scan after passing urine shows how much is left behind.
  • Treat the cause: prostate medicines (tamsulosin, dutasteride) or surgery, treat constipation, review medicines.
  • Catheters: intermittent self-catheterisation is often the most effective and freeing option for long-term poor emptying.
  • Sudden inability to pass urine with pain is an emergency.

Where to get help

  • National Continence Helpline: 1800 33 00 66 (free)

Common questions

What is overflow incontinence?

Leaking because the bladder doesn’t empty properly, so it overflows, usually as dribbling.

Is overflow incontinence dangerous?

Long-term retention can cause infections and, rarely, kidney damage. Sudden painful inability to pass urine needs urgent care.

How is it treated?

By treating the cause (prostate, constipation, medicines) and sometimes with intermittent self-catheterisation.
