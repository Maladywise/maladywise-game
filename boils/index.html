Fact sheet

Boils and Staph Skin Infections

A painful, pus-filled lump around a hair follicle, usually caused by Staphylococcus aureus (“staph”). A carbuncle is a cluster joined under the skin. Most small boils drain and heal on their own.

The boil ladder

Most boils need patience and a warm compress. Bigger ones need draining; antibiotics only sometimes.

  1. Step 1 · Self-careWarm compress, cover, hands offA warm, damp cloth for 10–15 minutes, 3–4 times a day. Keep it covered and wash your hands after touching it.
    Works
    Not tested in trials; many small boils come to a head, drain and heal within 1–2 weeks
    Cost
    Free
    How long
    Days to 2 weeks
    Watch for
    Spreading redness, fever, or a boil on the face: see a doctor

    Squeezing isn’t a procedure. It’s a promotion for the infection.

  2. Step 2 · ProcedureDrainage by a doctorA small cut under local anaesthetic to let the pus out.
    Works
    About 7 in 10 cured by drainage alone in large trials. The main treatment for a large boil
    Cost
    GP visit: bulk-billed, or about $40–100 out of pocket
    How long
    Quick procedure; dressings for a few days
    Watch for
    Redness spreading afterwards: go back

    Ten minutes in the GP’s chair beats ten days of DIY.

  3. Step 3 · PrescriptionAntibiotic tablets (usually flucloxacillin or cefalexin)For spreading redness, fever, a carbuncle, a boil on the face, or a weakened immune system.
    Works
    After drainage, about 8 in 10 cured vs 7 in 10 without. Needed for spreading infection
    Cost
    Under the $25 PBS co-payment, about $13–25
    How long
    About 5 days
    Watch for
    Nausea, diarrhoea, thrush. Flucloxacillin: take on an empty stomach; rarely affects the liver. Tell your doctor about penicillin allergy

    Antibiotics can’t reach a sealed pocket of pus. That’s why draining comes first.

  4. Step 4 · PrescriptionDecolonisation for repeat boilsAntibiotic ointment inside the nose for 5 days plus a daily antiseptic body wash such as chlorhexidine.
    Works
    Whole household: about 5 in 10 had another boil within a year vs 7 in 10 treating one person
    Cost
    Nose ointment about $15–30; chlorhexidine wash about $10–20
    How long
    5 days, repeated if needed
    Watch for
    Dry skin from the wash. Keep it out of eyes and ears

    Staph loves a share house. Treat everyone, or it just moves rooms.

  5. Step 5 · Self-careDilute bleach baths12 mL of plain household bleach (4.2%) per 10 litres of bathwater. Soak 10 minutes, wetting head and face, and don’t rinse off.
    Works
    No clear benefit in the largest trial (17% vs 21% had a repeat infection in a year). Low risk
    Cost
    About $3 a bottle
    How long
    Daily for 5 days, then once or twice a week
    Watch for
    Never undiluted, never mixed with other cleaners. Keep it out of eyes

    About as strong as a swimming pool. Not stronger. Measure it.

Costs are rough Australian prices in October 2026 and vary by pharmacy and provider. PBS co-payment: $25, or $7.70 with a concession card. Typical results from studies. Staph doesn’t always play by the rules.

Save your money

Popular, heavily marketed or often asked about, but the evidence is weak or says no.

  • Squeezing or poppingPushes infection deeper and spreads it.
  • DIY lancing with a needle or bladeRisks deeper infection and scarring. Drainage is a job for a doctor with sterile kit.
  • Drawing pastes and ointmentsPopular, but no good trials. A warm compress does the same job for nothing.
  • Immune-boosting supplementsNo evidence they prevent boils. Treating skin problems and stopping shared towels does more.

What you can do

  • Don’t share towels, razors or bedding. Wash linen hot and dry it in the sun or a dryer.
  • For repeat boils: treat eczema or tinea, use clippers instead of razors, and cover cuts.
  • Keep any boil covered until it heals.

See your GP if

  • A boil hasn’t drained after a few days or healed in 2 weeks
  • You keep getting boils, or others at home do
  • You have diabetes or a weakened immune system

See a doctor the same day for a boil on the face, spreading redness, fever, severe pain or feeling unwell. Call 000 if very unwell.

Common questions

Should I pop or squeeze a boil?

No. Squeezing pushes infection deeper and spreads it. Use a warm, damp compress, and a doctor can drain a large boil with a small cut.

Do boils need antibiotics?

Often not. Draining is the main treatment for a large boil. Antibiotics are used if redness is spreading, you have a fever or a carbuncle, the boil is on your face, or your immune system is weak.

Why do I keep getting boils?

Repeat boils are often due to staph bacteria carried on the skin or in the nose. A course of nasal antibiotic ointment plus an antiseptic body wash, done by the whole household at once, can help.

When does a boil need a doctor the same day?

For a boil on the face, spreading redness, fever, severe pain or feeling unwell. Call 000 if very unwell.
