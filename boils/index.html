Fact sheet

Boils and Staph Skin Infections

A boil is a painful, pus-filled lump around a hair follicle, usually caused by Staphylococcus aureus ("staph") bacteria. A carbuncle is a cluster of boils joined under the skin. Most small boils drain and heal on their own.

Treatment options

Ratings show how strong the research is.

  • Strong evidenceDrainage by a doctorFor a large boil, a small cut to let the pus out is the main treatment. Often no antibiotics are needed afterwards.
  • Moderate evidenceAntibiotic tabletsIf redness is spreading, you have a fever or a carbuncle, the boil is on your face, or your immune system is weak. Usually flucloxacillin or cefalexin for about 5 days.
  • Moderate evidenceDecolonisation for repeat boilsClears staph you carry on your skin: antibiotic ointment inside the nose for 5 days plus a daily antiseptic body wash such as chlorhexidine. Best done by the whole household at once.
  • Limited evidenceDilute bleach baths12 mL of plain household bleach (4.2%) per 10 litres of bathwater. Soak 10 minutes, wetting head and face, and don’t rinse off. Daily for 5 days, then once or twice a week.
  • Not recommendedSqueezing or poppingPushes infection deeper and spreads it.

What you can do

  • Warm, damp compress for 10 to 15 minutes, 3 to 4 times a day.
  • Keep it covered and wash your hands after touching it.
  • Don’t share towels, razors or bedding. Wash linen and dry in the sun or a dryer.
  • For repeat boils: treat eczema or tinea, use clippers instead of razors, and cover cuts.

See your GP if

  • A boil hasn’t drained after a few days or healed in 2 weeks
  • You keep getting boils, or others at home do
  • You have diabetes or a weakened immune system

See a doctor the same day for a boil on the face, spreading redness, fever, severe pain or feeling unwell. Call 000 if very unwell.

Common questions

Should I pop or squeeze a boil?

No. Squeezing pushes infection deeper and spreads it. Use a warm, damp compress, and a doctor can drain a large boil with a small cut.

Do boils need antibiotics?

Often not. Antibiotics are used if redness is spreading, you have a fever or a carbuncle, the boil is on your face, or your immune system is weak.

Why do I keep getting boils?

Repeat boils are often due to staph bacteria carried on the skin or in the nose. A course of nasal antibiotic ointment plus an antiseptic body wash, done by the whole household at once, can help.

When does a boil need a doctor the same day?

For a boil on the face, spreading redness, fever, severe pain or feeling unwell. Call 000 if very unwell.
