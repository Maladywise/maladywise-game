Newborn Jaundice

Yellow skin and eyes in the first days of life. It’s very common and usually harmless, fading by the end of the first week or two. A few patterns need checking quickly, and pale poo is one of them.

What it is

  • Babies are born with extra red blood cells, which break down into a yellow substance called bilirubin. A newborn’s liver takes a few days to clear it.
  • Jaundice usually shows on day 2 or 3, starting on the face and spreading down the body.
  • It’s harder to see on darker skin: look at the whites of the eyes, the gums or the palms.
  • It’s more likely in babies born early, babies who are feeding less well, and with some blood group differences or infections.

What helps

  • Frequent feeds, about 8 to 12 in 24 hours. Bilirubin leaves the body in poo.
  • Your midwife or doctor may check the level with a skin meter or a heel-prick blood test.
  • If the level is high, phototherapy (a special blue light) breaks bilirubin down. Sunlight is not a safe substitute.
  • Very high levels are rare but can affect the brain, which is why levels are checked and treated early.

See a doctor or midwife promptly if

  • Jaundice appears in the first 24 hours after birth.
  • It lasts more than 2 weeks (3 weeks in a baby born early). This needs a check, including a blood test.
  • Your baby has pale, chalky or clay-coloured poo or dark urine. This can be a sign of a blocked bile duct (biliary atresia), which needs treatment early.
  • Your baby is very sleepy, hard to wake for feeds, not feeding well, or has few wet nappies.
  • The yellow is spreading down to the legs or getting deeper.

Common questions

Is newborn jaundice normal?

Usually, yes. Most babies have some jaundice from day 2 or 3, and it fades by the end of the first week or two. Feeding often helps it clear.

When is newborn jaundice serious?

If it starts in the first 24 hours, lasts beyond 2 weeks, comes with pale poo or dark urine, or the baby is very sleepy or not feeding. See a doctor or midwife promptly.

Why is pale poo a worry in a jaundiced baby?

Pale, chalky poo can mean bile isn’t reaching the gut, for example from biliary atresia. It’s rare, but surgery works best when done early, so it needs checking straight away.

Can sunlight treat jaundice?

No. Putting a baby in sunlight isn’t safe or reliable. If treatment is needed, it’s done with a special phototherapy light.
